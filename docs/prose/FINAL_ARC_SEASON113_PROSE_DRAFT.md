## Chapter 481 — No Debt Between Us

Tsubasa Kurokawa had expected Aya to reach him first.

She did.

He had not expected Rhen to walk past her.

Aya was still kneeling beside him when the Petals Monarch crossed the ruined champion field.

Hundreds of broken anchor points covered the basin.

Stone pillars had been cut apart.

Sections of reinforced chain lay buried in shattered ground.

Orchid petals faded where Counter-Blooms had opened moments before, and the last pressure of **Ten Thousand Blooms** still trembled faintly through the torn earth.

Across the field, Sera sat against a low ridge while Luo checked her circulation for the third time despite Rhen already having repaired the dangerous damage.

She was awake.

She was annoyed.

That reassured Rhen.

Tsubasa watched him approach.

The Shinsei leader's left side was a map of what Sera had done to him.

Three ribs fractured.

One shoulder partially torn.

Deep strain along the meridians of his weapon arm.

Qi reserve badly depleted.

Several fine circulation injuries from repeatedly forcing **One Chain Binds Heaven** through damaged anchor geometry.

Nothing irreversible.

Nothing trivial.

Aya had already begun treating him.

Rhen stopped a few steps away.

Aya looked up.

“I'm working.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you standing there?”

“I need him.”

Aya's eyes narrowed.

Tsubasa laughed once, then immediately regretted it when one of Sera's rib injuries reminded him it still existed.

“You need me?” he asked.

Rhen nodded.

“Healthy.”

Silence spread outward from the three of them.

Aya stared.

Tsubasa stared longer.

Across the basin, Sera heard him.

Her head slowly turned.

Rhen crouched beside Tsubasa.

Aya put one hand against his chest.

Not forcefully.

Firmly.

“He is my patient.”

Rhen looked at her hand.

Then at her.

“Yes.”

Aya waited.

Rhen did not move past her.

That mattered.

“What are you doing?” she asked.

“Finishing the treatment faster.”

“To send him back into combat.”

“Yes.”

Aya's expression became murderous.

Tsubasa looked between them.

“This is a strange way to negotiate.”

Rhen ignored him.

Aya did not.

“He just fought Sera.”

“I saw.”

“He is carrying multiple injuries.”

“I know.”

“He won.”

“Yes.”

“And now you want to restore him so you can fight him too.”

“Yes.”

Aya closed her eyes.

For one breath, perhaps two, the Established Paragon physician visibly considered leaving both men to their own stupidity.

Then she opened them again.

“Why?”

Rhen looked at Tsubasa.

The answer was simple enough that he seemed confused by the need to explain it.

“Because he's injured.”

Aya's jaw tightened.

“That is not an answer.”

“It is.”

Tsubasa leaned back against the broken anchor stone behind him.

“You believe fighting me now would be unfair.”

Rhen nodded.

“Yes.”

The word reached farther than Rhen intended.

Shinsei officers heard it.

Wuyue officers heard it.

Isgard soldiers at the edges of the basin heard it.

Even Sera heard it.

Her mouth twitched.

Tsubasa studied Rhen carefully.

“You understand she did this to me.”

“Yes.”

“You understand that damage is part of the outcome of our duel.”

“Yes.”

“You would erase it.”

“No.”

That answer came immediately.

Tsubasa's eyes sharpened.

Rhen gestured toward Sera.

“She already won what she won.”

Sera's gaze shifted to him.

Rhen continued.

“She forced your Ultimate. Broke your anchors. Damaged your chain network. Made you change your strategy. Made you retreat.”

Tsubasa did not disagree.

“She did that,” Rhen said. “Healing you afterward doesn't change that.”

He looked back at Tsubasa.

“It only means I don't get to borrow her damage.”

The basin became very quiet.

Sera smiled.

Small.

Tired.

Real.

Aya slowly removed her hand from Rhen's chest.

“Fine.”

Rhen looked at her.

“Thank you.”

“Do not thank me.”

“Why?”

“Because if you heal him badly, I will fix him after I fix you.”

Rhen considered that.

“Reasonable.”

Tsubasa almost laughed again.

He stopped himself.

Rhen placed two fingers lightly against his wrist.

The first thing Tsubasa felt was not power.

It was absence.

Pain disappeared from the broken ribs.

Not numbed.

Gone.

The torn tissue along his shoulder drew together with a warm pressure beneath the skin.

Meridians that had been scraped raw by repeated chain output settled, then rebuilt in clean layers.

The stubborn tremor in his right hand vanished.

Qi depletion reversed.

That part made Aya stare.

Tsubasa's reserve did not merely fill.

His circulation was restored to the condition it had occupied before the champion duel began.

No borrowed stimulant.

No false elevation.

No hidden overclock.

Simply full.

Rhen did not rush.

He went through Tsubasa system by system.

Bone.

Muscle.

Organ stress.

Meridians.

Ordinary qi depletion.

Accumulated physical exhaustion.

Small injuries from the six-month campaign that Aya had previously kept functional rather than fully erased.

Tsubasa felt each one disappear.

He opened and closed his hand.

Then rolled the shoulder Sera had nearly destroyed.

Nothing caught.

Nothing hurt.

He stood.

Aya immediately checked him herself.

Seven needles flashed into pulse points before Tsubasa could object.

He looked down.

“Aya.”

“Quiet.”

She checked the first rhythm.

Then the second.

Then the deep circulation route running toward his weapon arm.

Her face changed.

Not surprise anymore.

Acceptance.

“He's clean,” she said.

Tsubasa looked toward Rhen.

“Full reserve?”

“Yes.”

“Meridian strain?”

“Gone.”

“Fatigue?”

“Gone.”

Tsubasa flexed his fingers once more.

“What about the force Sera put into me?”

“Spent.”

“Her damage?”

“Healed.”

“You restored your enemy.”

Rhen shrugged faintly.

“You're not fighting me like that.”

Tsubasa stared at him.

“Why?”

Rhen's answer was exactly what it had been before.

“It would be unfair.”

No boast.

No challenge.

That made it worse.

Behind Tsubasa, several Shinsei officers exchanged looks.

A man they had spent months trying to understand had just restored their strongest fighter from the damage inflicted by Wuyue's Peak Paragon because he considered inheriting those wounds beneath him.

Tsubasa felt something colder than humiliation.

Clarity.

For the first time, he understood that Rhen did not perceive this as generosity.

He perceived it as calibration.

Sera stood with Luo's help.

Rhen looked at her immediately.

She lifted one hand.

“I'm fine.”

“You were unable to stand five minutes ago.”

“Five minutes ago was a different me.”

Rhen frowned.

Sera smiled.

Then looked at Tsubasa.

“Now you can't blame me when he annoys you.”

Tsubasa looked at her.

“You assume I intend to continue.”

Sera's expression softened.

“No.”

Then hardened again.

“I assume you still haven't surrendered.”

Tsubasa said nothing.

That was answer enough.

The champion engagement had ended.

The war had not.

Rhen looked toward the distant Shinsei lines.

Fifty thousand elite troops had been moved into the inner formation during the pause.

Farther beyond them, outside the central basin and well past the main engagement corridor, Shinsei's remaining Paragons were being distributed onto outer reserve lines.

Tsubasa had planned the field before Sera challenged him.

The champion duel had delayed the collision.

It had not erased it.

Rhen looked back at Tsubasa.

“You're healthy.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

Rhen turned away.

Sera waited until he reached her before speaking.

“You enjoyed that.”

“No.”

“Liar.”

“I did not enjoy healing him.”

“You enjoyed his face.”

Rhen thought about it.

“A little.”

Sera laughed.

Then winced.

Rhen immediately touched her side.

She slapped his hand away.

“Stop fixing every expression.”

“You said it hurt.”

“I did not.”

“You made a pain noise.”

“That was a dignified laugh.”

“It sounded injured.”

Sera leaned closer.

“You're sleeping on the clinic floor when this is over.”

Rhen looked genuinely concerned.

“We don't have a clinic.”

“Yet.”

Neither of them knew how literal that would become.

Behind them, Tsubasa returned to his lines at full strength.

No limp.

No torn meridians.

No depleted reserve.

No borrowed excuse.

The final comparison had been reset to zero.

## Chapter 482 — Five Years

Aya Katsuragi had spent months trying to put one number back into Shinsei's vocabulary.

Five.

Not “long-term vitality consequence.”

Not “severe life-force burden.”

Not “irreversible recovery debt.”

Five years.

Approximately.

For one standard healthy adult.

For one five-minute 3× Redline cycle.

Now that number hung on fifty thousand sealed vials.

The inner Shinsei formation waited beyond the basin in disciplined ranks.

Marquis-level fighters formed the floor.

Above them stood High Marquis, Peak Marquis, Dukes, Sovereigns, specialists, field captains and formation leaders.

People who had spent years becoming difficult to kill.

Each carried a vial that could make them terrifying for minutes and shorten the life waiting after those minutes ended.

Aya walked the first line herself.

Every seal bore the same medical strip.

**3× REDLINE — EXPECTED COMBAT WINDOW: ~5 MINUTES. APPROXIMATE FUTURE-VITALITY COST: ~5 YEARS.**

No euphemism.

No blank field.

No hidden appendix.

Five years.

A young Peak Marquis read the line twice.

Then looked at Aya.

“Is that real?”

“Yes.”

“One dose?”

“Yes.”

The woman swallowed.

“How long have you known?”

Aya did not defend herself.

“Longer than you.”

The answer hurt more because it was honest.

The soldier looked back at the vial.

Aya continued down the line.

She did not tell anyone what choice to make.

That was not the same as approving the choice.

It meant the choice finally belonged to the person paying for it.

Tsubasa stood on a raised stone platform at the center of the formation.

His black crescent rested behind him.

Fresh chain had been linked to repaired lengths from the champion field and laid across anchor wagons, earth spikes, reinforced pulleys and prepared stone fixtures.

He looked completely healthy.

That fact had already moved through the army.

The enemy had healed him.

Not because Shinsei negotiated for it.

Because Rhen had decided fighting him wounded would be unfair.

The story was destroying morale in a strange direction.

Some soldiers found it terrifying.

Some found it insulting.

Some found it impossible to stop thinking about.

Tsubasa waited until Aya finished the first line.

Then he spoke.

No amplification array.

No theatrical qi roar.

His voice carried because the formation had gone silent for him.

“The label is accurate.”

Fifty thousand faces looked up.

“A standard 3× Redline cycle may cost approximately five years of future life.”

He paused.

“Some will lose less. Some more. Existing health, age and prior exposure change the cost. Aya Katsuragi has ordered the estimate written plainly because you are the ones who pay it.”

Aya looked toward him.

Tsubasa continued.

“No one who steps out will be punished.”

That created the first movement.

Small.

A few heads turning.

A few shoulders shifting.

“If you do not accept the cost, leave the inner spearhead now.”

One soldier stepped backward.

Nobody stopped him.

Then another.

A Duke removed the vial from his belt, placed it into a collection box and walked toward the marked withdrawal lane.

A squad followed.

Then three people from another formation.

Then none for a while.

Tsubasa watched every person go.

No contempt.

No praise.

This was not absolution for what Shinsei had done before the truth was forced into the open.

It was simply the first time the institution had made the cost impossible to miss.

The number settled near fifty thousand after replacements, volunteers and formation adjustments.

Approximately fifty thousand remained.

Not disposable bodies.

Not ignorant bodies anymore.

People who had chosen badly or bravely or desperately or patriotically or fearfully for reasons that belonged to them.

That distinction mattered.

Across the allied observation ridge, Jin watched through a brass scope.

“They're telling them,” he said.

Sera stood beside him.

“The five years?”

“Yes.”

She was quiet.

Jin lowered the scope.

“Some are leaving.”

“Good.”

“Most aren't.”

Sera looked across the plain.

“Also expected.”

Rhen stood a little behind them.

He had no scope.

He did not need one.

His gaze rested farther out than the inner spearhead.

Eight separate Paragon signatures had taken positions beyond the main engagement envelope.

Kai.

Haru.

Eirik.

Hana.

Aya after she finished the medical line.

Kenji.

Jun.

Nao.

Outer reserve.

Beyond fifty miles from the inner collision point Tsubasa had chosen.

Sera followed Rhen's attention.

“He spread them.”

“Yes.”

Jin looked up.

“Smart.”

Rhen nodded.

“Tsubasa usually is.”

The structure was obvious once seen.

Tsubasa would personally anchor the inner spearhead with fifty thousand boosted elites.

The remaining Paragons would stay outside the central kill zone as reserve response:

collapse control;

flank interception;

medical support;

counter-command;

reinforcement if the first line broke.

It was exactly what a careful commander should do against an opponent whose full capabilities remained unknown.

Jin's expression hardened.

“He doesn't know the radius of what you can do.”

“No.”

“He still moved them outside it.”

“Yes.”

Jin looked at him.

“That bothers me.”

“It should.”

Sera folded her arms.

“What are you thinking?”

Rhen looked at the fifty thousand.

“Five years is expensive.”

“That is not a strategy.”

“No.”

“What are you thinking strategically?”

Rhen considered.

“I don't want them to spend it.”

Jin stared.

Sera's expression softened.

“They're going to activate anyway.”

“I know.”

“And you can't stop the years already burned after activation.”

“I know.”

Rhen's eyes stayed on the field.

“So I'll stop the rest quickly.”

Tsubasa descended from the platform.

Aya met him at the bottom.

“You don't have to take it.”

He looked at her.

“That applies to everyone.”

“It applies to you too.”

“Yes.”

Aya's jaw tightened.

“You are already the strongest one there.”

“Yes.”

“You are healthy because the man you intend to fight healed you.”

“Yes.”

“Then fight him healthy.”

Tsubasa looked toward the allied ridge.

Rhen stood there with his hands inside his sleeves.

No weapon.

No visible preparation.

“I don't think healthy is enough.”

Aya's expression darkened.

Tsubasa reached for the vial at his own belt.

Unlike the soldiers', his seal carried another line.

**SINGLE AUTHORIZED FINAL DOSE.**

Aya had written that herself.

He held the vial without opening it.

“Aya.”

“What?”

“If we survive this, end the program.”

She stared at him.

“That is not how responsibility works.”

“I know.”

“You don't get to give me a noble instruction before doing the thing I told you not to do.”

“I know.”

“You will still answer for it.”

“Yes.”

Aya's anger faltered.

Not because she forgave him.

Because he had stopped defending himself.

Tsubasa looked at the vial.

Then at her.

“Make sure nobody hides the number again.”

Aya's voice was quiet.

“They won't.”

He broke the seal.

Across the formation, fifty thousand hands did the same.

The sound was almost nothing.

Glass.

Metal.

Breath.

Five years waiting inside each vial.

## Chapter 483 — Fifty Thousand Redlines

The first thing Redline changed was not power.

It was rhythm.

Fifty thousand circulations accelerated at once.

Breathing sharpened.

Pulse rates climbed.

Qi moved through meridians faster than the body would ever sustain naturally.

Then the battlefield changed.

A Marquis who had needed three steps to reach full qinggong speed reached it in one.

A Peak Marquis formation that normally required synchronized pressure building for several breaths struck almost immediately.

Duke-level shield lines moved with the violence of charging cavalry.

Sovereign captains became streaks of disciplined motion through the center of their own units.

No one ascended a realm.

No one learned a new technique.

No one became wiser.

Every existing skill simply arrived with roughly triple the combat throughput available to force it through the world.

The result was monstrous.

Jin saw the first allied line bend.

“Back.”

The order moved before he finished the word.

Not panic.

Prepared doctrine.

Five-Minute Retreat had been built for exactly this problem months ago.

Wuyue lines gave ground in measured layers.

Isgard formations did the same.

Heavy infantry fell back through marked channels while archers and qi specialists stopped trying to overpower incoming formations and instead attacked footing, carts, terrain and command spacing.

Sera stood at the central ridge.

“Do not feed them a fixed line!”

Signal flags moved.

“Give them ground. Keep them moving. Make them spend the five minutes walking through nothing.”

The order saved thousands.

It did not make the pressure small.

A 3× Peak Marquis drove through a barricade that had stopped Sovereigns earlier in the war.

Three Dukes operating in linked formation shattered an Isgard shield wall and kept moving.

A Sovereign spear captain crossed fifty paces under missile pressure so quickly that the observers lost him between breaths.

Rhen watched.

Not impressed by Redline.

Impressed by the people using it.

“They're good,” he said.

Sera looked at him.

“They've been invading us for six months.”

“I know.”

“That wasn't praise.”

“It was accurate.”

Sera sighed.

“Unfortunately, yes.”

The Shinsei formation did not behave like drugged berserkers.

That would have been easier.

They remained disciplined.

Captains rotated high-output sections.

Units used the boost to close distance, break prepared lanes and then hand pressure to the next formation rather than wasting every second on personal glory.

Tsubasa had built the spearhead around the limited window.

He knew five minutes was short.

So he had removed wasted motion from the plan.

Outer Paragons remained distant.

Hana shifted reserve companies along the far flank without entering the central engagement envelope.

Nao coordinated defensive lanes around medical traffic.

Aya stood at an outer treatment station because somebody had to prepare for the crash that would come even if Shinsei won.

Haru watched the inner formation with an expression Aya could not read from that distance.

Eirik kept the reserve coherent.

Kai looked like a man physically restraining himself from joining the first charge.

Kenji and Jun held opposite response corridors.

Tsubasa had not stacked all his strength into one place.

He had stacked enough.

The allied center gave another half mile.

Then another.

Jin's fingers moved over the map.

“Still inside expectation.”

Lei Zhen looked at the collapsing forward ditch.

“That is your expectation?”

“Yes.”

“I hate your expectations.”

“So do I.”

Shen Rui glanced toward Rhen.

“Are we waiting for something?”

Rhen answered.

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Tsubasa.”

As if called by name, the Peak Paragon moved.

The 3× state entered him fully.

His body did not bulk.

No supernatural transformation tore through the sky.

His circulation simply accelerated until every motion he already knew became frighteningly efficient.

He lifted the black crescent.

The chain behind it came alive.

One anchor caught stone.

Then another.

Then twelve.

Then dozens.

Prepared points across the inner battlefield locked into a physical network.

Tsubasa stepped forward.

**Tethered Heaven Dominion** opened around him.

The first allied ranged qi strike entered the Domain and lost cohesion before reaching the center.

A second weakened farther.

Tsubasa's own output did not.

Body.

Hand.

Chain.

Crescent.

One continuous physical connection.

He released the weapon.

The crescent crossed the field in a black arc fast enough to tear a trench through stone.

An allied Sovereign tried to retreat after entering the close band of the Domain.

His movement met escalating resistance as he attempted to increase distance from Tsubasa.

Not gravity.

Not added mass.

A tethering martial law inside Tsubasa's qi field that made withdrawal progressively more inefficient once he had forced closeness.

Sera's face hardened.

“He's stronger than earlier.”

“Yes,” Rhen said.

“How much?”

“Approximately what the label says.”

Sera gave him a flat look.

“That was not helpful.”

Rhen watched the chain network expanding.

“It is enough that nobody else should enter.”

Kael stood nearby, completely healed from Shunto's beating and visibly furious that this recommendation included him.

“I can fight.”

Rhen looked at him.

“I know.”

“That sounded like 'stay here.'”

“Yes.”

Kael's jaw tightened.

Sera touched his shoulder.

“Let him.”

Kael looked at her.

She smiled faintly.

“I already had my turn.”

The black crescent struck another anchor.

Changed direction without losing physical momentum.

Then another.

Hundreds of feet of reinforced chain began to move as one weapon system.

Tsubasa looked across the battlefield.

Directly at Rhen.

Rhen stepped off the ridge.

No army followed him.

No Domain opened yet.

No Snowfield descended.

He simply began walking toward fifty thousand people who had each chosen to spend five years for the next few minutes.

Sera watched him go.

“Rhen.”

He looked back.

“Don't be annoying.”

Rhen considered that impossible instruction.

“I'll try.”

“You won't.”

“No.”

He turned back toward Tsubasa.

The strongest conventional cultivator alive had been healed to full condition.

Then multiplied his combat throughput threefold.

Then placed fifty thousand 3× elites around him.

Rhen walked toward all of it.

Because now, finally, nobody could say the comparison had been unfair to Tsubasa.
