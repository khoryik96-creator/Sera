# Final Arc — Season 113 Prose Draft, Part II

> Continues **Season 113 — Monarch's Winter**.

## Chapter 484 — Tsubasa at Three Times

Tsubasa did not wait for Rhen to reach him.

That would have been pride.

He had already spent enough lives on pride disguised as doctrine.

The chain moved first.

A black crescent crossed the battlefield from left to right, struck a buried iron anchor, wrapped once around reinforced stone, and changed direction without surrendering its momentum.

The return line came from above.

Rhen stepped half a pace aside.

The blade passed close enough to cut three strands of hair.

It struck another anchor.

Changed direction again.

The third line came low.

Rhen jumped.

Not high.

Just enough.

The crescent cut through the ground beneath his feet and sent stone fragments across the field.

Tsubasa's chain snapped taut.

He pulled.

The weapon reversed instantly.

Rhen turned his shoulder.

The crescent missed his back by less than an inch.

Across the allied ridge, Shen Rui stopped breathing.

Kael did not.

He swore.

Sera smiled despite herself.

“That is annoyingly good.”

Jin looked at her.

“Which one?”

“Yes.”

Tsubasa's **Tethered Heaven Dominion** pressed over the inner battlefield.

Rhen felt the first rule immediately.

Detached hostile qi weakened with distance.

That made ordinary ranged answers inefficient.

A projected strike released from far away would decay before it crossed the Domain.

Tsubasa's own weapon did not suffer the same problem because the qi remained physically connected through body, hand, chain and crescent.

The second rule waited closer.

Approach Tsubasa, and retreat became progressively harder.

A normal fighter could enter.

Leaving was the trap.

Rhen walked forward anyway.

Tsubasa watched him.

The chain moved again.

This time not one line.

Six.

The crescent struck different physical anchors in sequence while Tsubasa shortened and released chain length with terrifying precision.

The blade arrived from changing planes without ever becoming magical.

Every redirection belonged to actual chain wrapped around actual stone, iron, earth spikes or prepared battlefield structures.

At 3× throughput, the geometry happened faster than ordinary Paragon perception liked.

Rhen moved through it without activating a visible art.

Left.

Half step.

Turn.

Duck.

One foot on the moving chain.

He used it as a platform for less than a heartbeat.

Tsubasa yanked the line.

Rhen was already gone.

“Stop doing that,” Sera muttered.

Luo looked at her.

“Doing what?”

“Making impossible things look rude.”

Tsubasa planted one foot.

The chain network tightened.

**One Chain Binds Heaven** expanded.

The Ultimate did not create a new weapon.

It made the one weapon impossible to ignore.

Hundreds of physical anchor points across the prepared battlefield turned one continuous chain into a vast extension of Tsubasa's reach.

He remained the center.

One body.

One hand.

One chain.

One crescent.

But now the chain ran through trenches, walls, iron stakes, reinforced trees, buried hooks and stone pylons across miles of prepared ground.

The crescent could redirect through that network again and again while retaining real physical force.

The inner battlefield became an enormous kill geometry.

Rhen stopped walking.

Not because he had been trapped.

Because now Tsubasa had shown what Rhen was waiting to see.

Tsubasa noticed.

“So this is enough to interest you.”

Rhen looked at the chain network.

“Yes.”

That answer irritated Tsubasa more than insult would have.

He pulled.

The entire network responded.

The crescent vanished from ordinary sight.

It struck one anchor.

Two.

Five.

Nine.

Then came at Rhen from behind with force amplified by every preserved redirect.

Rhen raised one hand.

The blade struck his palm.

The ground beneath him exploded.

The shockwave threw broken stone outward in a ring.

Several nearby 3× Shinsei elites were forced to brace.

Tsubasa's eyes sharpened.

Rhen had stopped the crescent.

Not by catching the chain.

By placing one hand against the broad inner edge of the weapon and receiving the force directly.

Tsubasa's **Heaven Returns What It Receives** reacted too.

The collision had traveled through the crescent and chain back into his own martial system.

Part of the transmitted force was captured into his circulation.

The impact did not disappear.

His shoulder still took strain.

His stance still shifted.

But the next attack now carried a fraction of what Rhen had just given him.

Tsubasa released it immediately.

The chain snapped.

A second return attack arrived with combined 3× output and recycled collision force.

Rhen moved his head aside.

The crescent passed.

Its pressure tore the sleeve of his coat.

Sera's eyes widened.

Not because Rhen was in danger.

Because Tsubasa had done exactly what he was supposed to do.

He had made Rhen move.

He had made Rhen's coat tear.

He had created a visible consequence against a man whose old reputation had already exceeded the ranking system.

Tsubasa saw it too.

He did not celebrate.

He attacked again.

That was why he was Tsubasa.

The 50,000-person spearhead moved around the apex fight rather than toward it.

They were not there to mob Rhen blindly.

Their purpose was to collapse allied fronts, force command movement, seize terrain and make Rhen choose between answering Tsubasa and stopping the army.

It was strategically sound.

Three boosted formations hit the allied center.

Another pressed the Isgard left.

Two Duke companies broke through a secondary defensive ridge.

A Sovereign captain reached a signal tower and destroyed it before the defenders could evacuate the equipment.

Jin responded immediately.

“Do not hold the ridge. Give it.”

Lei Zhen looked at him.

“That is the third ridge.”

“Yes.”

“At what point do we stop giving them ridges?”

“When they stop paying five years to walk uphill.”

Lei stared.

Then laughed once.

“Fine.”

The alliance withdrew again.

Measured.

Controlled.

Still ugly.

Tsubasa saw the movement through his peripheral awareness.

“Your army is losing ground.”

Rhen looked past him.

“Yes.”

“You could stop them.”

“Yes.”

“But you're still here.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Rhen looked back at him.

“Because Sera told me not to be annoying.”

Tsubasa blinked.

The crescent nearly dipped.

From the ridge, Sera shouted:

“I SAID TRY.”

Rhen called back without looking.

“I AM.”

Even several Shinsei soldiers heard it.

For one absurd second, the battlefield became confused.

Then Tsubasa attacked again.

Harder.

Rhen smiled faintly.

Not because the war was funny.

Because Tsubasa had finally stopped looking like a commander carrying an institution and started looking like what he also was:

a martial artist at the absolute edge of the conventional world.

Rhen respected that.

Which was why he had healed him.

Which was why he had waited.

Which was why he was about to end it cleanly.

## Chapter 485 — Rhen Walks In

Tsubasa had one remaining assumption.

Everyone did.

Strength still had to travel through something.

A body.

A weapon.

A technique.

A Domain.

A reserve.

Even Rhen's old arts had followed that logic.

Frozen petals moved through space.

Healing required qi.

Containment required a field.

Every miracle still had structure.

So Tsubasa attacked the structure he could see.

The chain network tightened around Rhen's approach path.

Anchor lines crossed ahead and behind him.

Tethered Heaven made retreat increasingly inefficient once Rhen entered the close band.

The crescent came from a high redirect.

Rhen stepped inward instead of away.

That should have worsened the tether.

It did.

He simply did not retreat.

Tsubasa changed the chain length.

The crescent passed Rhen's shoulder.

Rhen caught the chain itself.

For the first time, Tsubasa's expression changed visibly.

The chain was not still.

It was moving with enough qi-reinforced force to cut stone and drag armored Sovereigns off their feet.

Rhen wrapped one hand around it.

The metal screamed.

His palm did not open.

Tsubasa pulled.

Rhen moved one step.

Only one.

That was enough for Tsubasa.

He released the stored force from **Heaven Returns What It Receives** through the next committed pull.

The chain snapped taut with a violent crack.

Rhen's boots carved two lines through stone.

Sera's expression sharpened.

Tsubasa pulled again.

Rhen let go.

Not because he had to.

Because he had understood the mechanic.

He looked at his palm.

A thin red line had appeared across it.

Blood.

One drop.

It fell.

The people who saw it reacted differently.

Shinsei soldiers shouted.

Wuyue soldiers went silent.

Kael leaned forward.

Sera smiled.

Tsubasa stared at the blood.

Rhen looked at him.

“Very good.”

Tsubasa's face became expressionless.

“You are mocking me.”

“No.”

“You're bleeding.”

“Yes.”

“And you sound pleased.”

“You made me bleed.”

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

Rhen closed his hand.

The cut healed.

“That is usually impressive.”

Tsubasa exhaled slowly.

Then laughed.

Not bitterly.

Not loudly.

A real laugh.

For one heartbeat, the leader of Shinsei was simply forty-two years old and standing across from the first opponent in years who made absurdity feel honest.

Then he lifted the crescent again.

“Fine.”

The chain network screamed into motion.

Every major anchor in the inner battlefield engaged.

The weapon began a full Ultimate sequence.

Tsubasa no longer tried to test Rhen.

He tried to defeat him.

The first redirect broke the sound barrier.

The second shattered a stone pylon after using it.

The third tore through a ridge.

The fourth turned back across the first line.

The fifth came from below.

The sixth forced Rhen sideways.

The seventh met him there.

At 3× throughput, **One Chain Binds Heaven** became the most complete expression of conventional physical weapon control anyone present had ever seen.

Hundreds of anchors.

One continuous chain.

No teleportation.

No duplicated weapon.

No spatial trick.

Just geometry, speed, strength, control and an amount of qi that would have emptied lesser Paragons rapidly even without Redline.

Rhen moved through it.

Not untouched.

His coat tore again.

A shallow cut opened along one cheek.

A chain segment struck his forearm and drove him through a stone wall.

Tsubasa immediately converted part of that collision into his next attack.

The crescent descended.

Rhen caught it between both palms.

The impact flattened the wall behind him.

Tsubasa pulled.

Rhen released.

The crescent came back.

Rhen finally struck.

One palm.

Not a named technique.

He hit the flat of the crescent as it passed.

The weapon changed direction violently.

Tsubasa's chain control saved three anchors from tearing free.

Five others failed.

The transmitted force traveled back through the chain.

**Heaven Returns What It Receives** caught part of it.

Tsubasa's body caught the rest.

His boots slid twenty feet.

Blood appeared at the corner of his mouth.

He smiled.

Rhen noticed.

“You're enjoying this.”

Tsubasa wiped the blood away.

“Yes.”

Rhen nodded.

“Good.”

Then he looked past him.

The inner 50,000 were beginning to push too deep.

Five-Minute Retreat had preserved the alliance, but the field was compressing.

Too many people were being forced into too little ground.

The longer Redline remained active, the more future life disappeared from fifty thousand bodies.

Rhen's expression changed.

Tsubasa saw it.

“You were waiting.”

“Yes.”

“For what?”

“For you to show me everything.”

Tsubasa's fingers tightened around the chain.

“And now?”

Rhen looked at the fifty thousand.

“Now they've spent enough.”

The air changed.

Not temperature.

Not yet.

Pressure.

Something enormous became precise.

Sera stopped smiling.

Aya, more than fifty miles away on the outer medical reserve, looked toward the center despite not being able to see what had changed.

Her physician's instincts told her that a circulation law had just touched the battlefield.

Rhen took one step forward.

Frost-white petals appeared around him.

Not the melancholy storm of **Petals Beneath a Frozen Moon**.

Not the stillness of **Petals in Stasis**.

These petals were smaller.

Cleaner.

Each carried the pale-violet radiance of his condensed core.

Tsubasa felt his own qi respond.

Not weaken.

Recognize something.

His expression sharpened.

Rhen looked at him.

“I wouldn't move too much for the next few breaths.”

Tsubasa lifted the crescent.

“That sounds like advice.”

“It is.”

“I don't intend to take it.”

“I know.”

The first frozen petal formed.

It did not come from Rhen.

It formed above a Shinsei soldier fifty yards away.

Then another.

And another.

Tsubasa looked up.

Rhen spoke the name quietly.

**Monarch's Winter Law.**

## Chapter 486 — Monarch's Winter Law

The first Shinsei soldier thought his Redline had failed.

His qinggong step shortened.

Not much.

Enough.

He tried to force more qi through his legs.

The reserve answered.

Then vanished somewhere before the technique completed.

Above him, a frozen petal became denser.

The soldier stumbled.

Another fighter beside him raised a qi-reinforced shield.

The reinforcement weakened.

A second petal formed overhead.

A Duke captain tried to open a large defensive art.

The technique began.

Then starved.

His petal thickened.

Across the inner battlefield, the same thing happened fifty thousand times.

One person.

One petal.

No more.

No less.

Rhen's **Monarch's Winter Law** did not count strength.

It counted selected martial circulations.

He had already mapped the inner battlefield through Domain perception.

Wuyue signatures were excluded.

Isgard signatures were excluded.

Civilians, neutral medical staff, prisoners and noncombatants were excluded.

Surrendered Shinsei fighters were excluded as soon as they were marked and removed from active resistance.

The Law did not know morality.

Rhen did.

He selected the circulations he intended to compress.

The field extended outward.

Ten miles.

Twenty.

Thirty.

Forty.

Fifty.

Then stopped.

Exactly where Rhen wanted it.

The outer Paragon reserve remained beyond it.

That was Tsubasa's own deployment choice.

Inside the radius, every selected hostile reserve began condensing into a single frozen petal.

Not symbolic energy.

Not a copy.

Their currently usable internal qi itself.

The stronger the cultivator, the denser the petal.

A Marquis produced one.

A Duke produced one.

A Sovereign produced one.

Tsubasa produced one.

His was different only in density.

A darkly luminous frost-white petal formed above him, pale violet shining around an interior so compressed it looked almost solid.

Tsubasa felt the first meaningful loss from his reserve.

He reacted instantly.

Chain attack.

The crescent moved.

Faster than almost anyone alive could follow.

But slower than it had a breath before.

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

Redline forced circulation harder.

The petal above him thickened faster.

He understood.

The 3× state was not protecting his reserve.

It was helping Rhen move it.

Tsubasa changed tactics.

He reduced output.

The petal still grew.

He tried to hold qi deep in the core.

The Law reached it.

He circulated through alternate routes.

The Law did not care which meridian carried the energy.

It was compressing the usable reserve itself.

Tsubasa looked at Rhen.

“What did you do?”

Rhen answered because there was no point hiding a rule already happening inside Tsubasa's body.

“Condensed your reserve.”

“Into that?”

“Yes.”

Tsubasa looked at the petal.

“How much?”

“All of it.”

For the first time in the final arc, Tsubasa Kurokawa lost control of his expression.

Not fear.

Calculation collapsing.

Behind him, the army felt it too.

Qinggong failed first among the lower tiers.

Then body reinforcement.

Then projected qi.

Then large techniques.

The Redline spearhead that had moved like a tidal wall began to fragment as fifty thousand fighters simultaneously lost the energy that made 3× throughput meaningful.

Axtaya could overdrive circulation.

It could not overdrive an empty reserve.

A Peak Marquis sprinted three more steps.

Then his qinggong vanished.

His fourth step was ordinary human muscle trying to continue at a speed the body could no longer sustain.

He crashed.

A Duke's qi armor dissolved from his skin.

A Sovereign captain tried to reinforce his spear.

The weapon qi flickered out.

No one died from the compression itself.

They simply became weaker by the second.

Sera stood at the allied ridge and watched fifty thousand individual petals appear over the battlefield.

Her eyes widened.

Jin forgot to write.

Lei Zhen slowly lowered his weapon.

Shen Rui said nothing.

Kael stared.

Luo's face went blank in the particular way physicians looked when they had just seen a biological assumption stop applying.

Sera whispered:

“Rhen.”

He heard her from miles away through the Domain.

He did not answer.

Tsubasa pulled the chain again.

**Tethered Heaven Dominion** flickered.

He forced more qi into it.

The petal above him became denser.

The Domain flickered harder.

Then recovered.

For one breath.

Two.

Three.

Tsubasa gritted his teeth.

The tether rule weakened.

Rhen took one step backward.

This time retreat did not resist him as strongly.

Tsubasa felt it.

The strongest conventional Domain in the era was starving in real time.

He tried **One Chain Binds Heaven**.

The anchor network responded.

Fewer sections moved.

The qi necessary to preserve full Ultimate geometry was disappearing into one frozen petal.

More chain fell slack.

Tsubasa still pulled.

Still fought.

Still recalculated.

Rhen watched him.

No contempt.

Tsubasa's petal grew denser.

Then denser again.

Until there was almost no usable qi left inside him.

His Domain collapsed.

The air did not explode.

The heavens did not crack.

A martial field simply lost its fuel.

**Tethered Heaven Dominion** ended.

The retreat-resistance vanished.

Detached qi stopped weakening because there was no Domain left to enforce the rule.

A moment later, **One Chain Binds Heaven** failed too.

Hundreds of anchor points became exactly what they had always physically been:

metal;

stone;

chain;

without the qi necessary to make them an apex martial system.

The black crescent hit the ground.

Tsubasa remained standing.

Barely reinforced.

No Domain.

No Ultimate.

No meaningful Redline throughput.

His reserve was above him.

One petal.

Rhen looked up at it.

“Very dense.”

Tsubasa laughed once.

“You are still doing that.”

“What?”

“Complimenting me while ruining my day.”

Rhen thought about it.

“Yes.”

Tsubasa looked at fifty thousand petals across the sky.

“How many?”

Rhen already knew.

He counted circulations, not visually drifting shapes.

“Fifty thousand and one.”

The number moved across the battlefield like a sentence.

Exactly fifty thousand soldiers.

Exactly one Tsubasa.

Exactly one petal each.

No matter how much power had been in the person.

The strongest man's reserve had not required a second petal.

It had only made the first one frighteningly dense.

## Chapter 487 — Fifty Thousand and One Petals

There were too many petals to look at individually.

That was why everyone tried.

Fifty thousand and one frost-white petals floated above the battlefield.

Each represented a cultivator's currently usable reserve compressed into a single physical-qi form under Rhen's control.

Some were translucent.

Some dense.

A few Sovereign petals held visible internal radiance.

Tsubasa's looked like a small winter star.

The army beneath them changed just as dramatically.

Three-times Redline users who had been tearing through fortifications now struggled to maintain reinforced posture.

Peak Marquis fighters breathed hard.

Dukes lost qinggong.

Sovereigns could still move well through trained physical conditioning, but every motion reminded them how much of their strength had always belonged to circulation and reinforcement.

Nobody had been made weak.

Their cultivation had been removed from immediate use.

That distinction was horrifying.

Tsubasa reached for the chain.

His fingers closed around ordinary metal.

It was still heavy.

He could still use it.

He was still Tsubasa Kurokawa.

Decades of training did not disappear because qi did.

He pulled the nearest length toward himself.

Without reinforcement, the movement was slower.

Still disciplined.

Still efficient.

Rhen watched him.

“Don't.”

Tsubasa looked up.

“Advice again?”

“Yes.”

“I remain bad at taking it.”

“I noticed.”

Around them, Shinsei officers tried to reorganize the inner spearhead.

Some succeeded.

A Duke captain shouted for shields to close.

The shields came together.

Physical shields still existed.

Armor still existed.

Weapons still existed.

The army was weakened.

Not yet neutralized.

Rhen looked at the petals.

He lifted one hand.

Several hundred petals at the edge of the field dissolved.

Not vanished.

Disintegrated into microscopic glittering frost particles.

**Winter Essence.**

The particles descended.

The first Shinsei soldier raised a shield.

Frost settled into the metal.

Not across the surface like ordinary ice.

Into microscopic faults.

Grain boundaries.

Stress points.

The shield became brittle.

A hairline crack spread.

Then another.

The metal failed in pieces.

A second soldier's qi armor had already vanished with his reserve.

Winter Essence touched the physical breastplate beneath it.

Localized frost stress spread through rivets and layered plates.

The armor fractured at the seams.

A weapon reinforcement charm had no qi left to feed it.

The blade itself became brittle where Essence entered repeated impact flaws.

It snapped when the soldier tried to raise it.

This was not matter deletion.

The materials failed because Rhen's frost-qi particles entered weaknesses ordinary cold could never reach and broke structural integrity from within.

Sera watched a line of Shinsei shields collapse without a single explosion.

“That is excessive.”

Kael looked at her.

“You fought him voluntarily.”

“So did you once.”

“I was younger.”

“You were stupid.”

“Yes.”

They looked back at Rhen.

Neither argued.

The inner spearhead began to understand that conventional defense was disappearing after cultivation had already gone.

Some soldiers dropped broken weapons.

Some raised empty hands.

Rhen excluded them from further targeting immediately.

Their petals stopped changing.

Other soldiers continued advancing physically.

Rhen looked across them.

He did not want fifty thousand people injured because they obeyed one more bad order after the war was already decided.

The Winter Essence thickened above the army.

Then descended more softly.

Rhen spoke:

**Winter Essence: Quiet Snow.**

The effect did not strike minds.

It settled through upper circulation.

Muscular aggression eased.

Breathing slowed.

Sensory alertness softened.

Bodies already deprived of their cultivated reserve found it increasingly difficult to maintain full wakefulness against the controlled calming of their remaining circulation.

One soldier sat down before falling.

Another dropped his broken spear and blinked twice.

Then slept.

Rows followed.

Not dead.

Not paralyzed.

Asleep.

Fifty thousand boosted elites who had spent years of future life for minutes of impossible pressure began lying down across the battlefield under quiet glittering snow.

Aya saw it from the outer reserve line and understood immediately what Rhen had chosen.

Not retaliation.

Anesthesia at battlefield scale.

Her throat tightened.

Haru stared beside her.

“They're sleeping.”

“Yes.”

“That's all?”

Aya looked at the field.

“No.”

She knew enough medicine to understand the terrifying part.

“He could have chosen something else.”

Haru said nothing.

At the center, Tsubasa remained awake.

Rhen had excluded him from Quiet Snow.

Tsubasa noticed.

“You want me conscious.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“You're still fighting.”

Tsubasa looked down at the chain in his hands.

Then at the sleeping army.

Then at the one dense petal above him containing the cultivation reserve of the strongest conventional fighter alive.

He pulled the chain once.

The black crescent dragged across stone.

Ordinary physical weight.

No qi.

No Domain.

No Ultimate.

Still a weapon.

Tsubasa lifted it.

Rhen sighed.

“You really are bad at advice.”

Tsubasa settled into stance.

“Yes.”

Rhen looked at the Winter Essence drifting between them.

The mass army slept.

The battlefield was quiet.

Only one hostile circulation inside the center still insisted on movement.

Tsubasa attacked.
