## Chapter 355 — One Day Later

The first Isgard physician thought Yun Shizhen was a dying herb merchant.

That was reasonable.

She arrived on the back of a medical caravan wearing a borrowed dark coat, a northern trader’s papers and enough dried blood that nobody looking at her would begin with politics.

The caravan had found her three hours south of the border station.

Not unconscious.

Yun had apparently objected to that description twice.

“Walking badly,” the caravan driver corrected when the border healer asked.

Yun, half-supported against the clinic wall, lifted one finger.

[[speaker:yun]]“Accurate.”

Then her knees gave way.

The healer caught her before she hit the floor.

That was how the warning entered Isgard.

Not with banners.

Not with a messenger galloping through snow.

With a woman too stubborn to collapse until somebody else’s hands were already beneath her.

They carried her into the border clinic.

The first examination took less than a minute to become serious.

Old rib damage.

New rib damage.

Deep meridian strain.

Repeated circulation interruption.

Dehydration.

Blood loss.

Fever beginning beneath exhaustion.

Multiple injuries at different stages of healing, some treated well enough to prove she had received competent medicine at some point and then been forced to keep moving anyway.

The physician looked at the pattern and stopped treating her like an ordinary traveler.

“Who did this?”

Yun opened her eyes.

[[speaker:yun]]“Later.”

“You need treatment now.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I need an officer.”

“You need a bed.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Both.”

The physician almost argued.

[[speaker:yun]]Then Yun said, “Wuyue.”

He paused.

Her accent had already told him she was foreign.

The word changed the type of foreign.

“Name?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yun Shizhen.”

Nothing.

Border physicians could not be expected to memorize every martial rank across another continent.

Yun swallowed.

[[speaker:yun]]“The Pale Venom.”

The junior healer beside him went completely still.

There.

That one had travelled farther.

The senior physician looked at the junior.

“You know her?”

“Wuyue Top Ten.”

Silence.

Yun closed her eyes again.

[[speaker:yun]]“No.10.”

The room changed.

The No.10 title did not make her more deserving of medicine. It activated a defensive pact between Wuyue and Isgard, and one of Wuyue’s public Top Ten had just crossed the border nearly dead while insisting on an officer.

The physician turned toward the door.

“Get the pact liaison.”

Yun caught his sleeve.

Weakly.

Still enough.

[[speaker:yun]]“Military liaison.”

He looked at her.

[[speaker:yun]]“Not just diplomatic.”

That changed his face again.

He nodded.

“Both.”

Good.

Yun released him.

The clinic began moving around her.

One healer cut away damaged fabric.

Another warmed fluid.

A third checked her circulation and swore under his breath.

Yun heard it.

[[speaker:yun]]“Professional.”

The healer looked embarrassed.

“Sorry.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I agree with you.”

He almost smiled.

Then his fingers found another damaged meridian line and the smile disappeared.

Yun watched his expression.

Physicians were bad liars around other physicians.

[[speaker:yun]]“How long?” she asked.

He said, “Do not talk.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Bad answer.”

“We are stabilizing you.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Also not an answer.”

The senior physician returned and leaned over her.

“We are going to try.”

Yun looked at him.

That was an answer.

Not a good one.

An honest one.

She understood her own body better than they did.

The escape medicine had carried her farther than it should have.

She had forced circulation through damaged pathways too many times.

Fever had joined blood loss.

Several internal injuries had been made survivable, then reopened by days of travel.

Nothing dramatic was happening now.

That was the danger.

Her body had simply spent too long being asked for one more mile.

Yun touched the wrap around her wrist.

[[speaker:yun]]“Cut this carefully.”

The physician frowned.

“Why?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Inside seam.”

He cut the outer wrap.

Found the tiny sealed capillary.

Held it up.

Barely visible residue stained the interior.

Yun exhaled.

Still there.

[[speaker:yun]]“Do not open it here.”

“What is it?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Axtaya.”

The name meant nothing to him.

Yet.

[[speaker:yun]]“Foreign stimulant. Military.”

The physician’s eyes sharpened.

“From where?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Shinrin.”

That name he knew as geography.

The rest had not reached Isgard yet.

The door opened.

An Isgard military liaison entered with two officers and a civil clerk behind him.

Nobody had put on ceremonial clothing.

Good.

Yun did not have time for ceremony.

The liaison looked at her once, then at the physician.

“Can she speak?”

The physician said, “Briefly.”

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said, “Long enough.”

The physician glared at her.

She ignored him.

The liaison pulled a chair close.

“Yun Shizhen?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Wuyue No.10?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“I am Commander Hallen, northern pact liaison. Whatever you tell me now goes directly to central command and Wuyue.”

Yun looked at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“Send the alert first.”

He paused.

“I need cause.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Foreign invasion preparation against Isgard.”

Hallen’s face hardened. He had heard enough.

“Confirmed?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“How soon?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Weeks possible.”

He stood immediately.

The clerk was already writing.

Hallen said, “Activate preliminary pact warning. Highest priority. State that Wuyue Top Ten officer delivered direct intelligence of imminent foreign invasion preparation.”

The second officer left at a run.

Yun watched him go.

Good.

The machinery was moving before the whole story existed.

That was what pacts were for.

Not certainty after the enemy landed.

Time before certainty became irrelevant.

The physician touched her shoulder.

“Enough for now.”

Yun shook her head.

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

“You will kill yourself finishing a report.”

She looked at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“I did not cross Shinrin to die before the useful part.”

Hallen sat again.

The physician swore under his breath.

This time Yun did not tease him.

She closed her eyes for one breath.

Then opened them.

[[speaker:yun]]“Write.”

## Chapter 356 — The Last Report of the Pale Venom

Yun gave them the war in the order that could save the most people.

Not the order she learned it.

Not the order it hurt.

The order that mattered now.

[[speaker:yun]]“Country: Shinrin.”

The clerk wrote.

[[speaker:yun]]“Ruling structure: Shinsei Guild. Guild is effectively central government, military command, judiciary and infrastructure authority.”

Hallen looked up.

“One institution?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Continue.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Axtaya.”

Yun pointed weakly toward the sealed capillary now sitting inside a padded evidence tray.

[[speaker:yun]]“Circulatory stimulant program. Multiplies combat throughput temporarily. Does not raise cultivation realm or skill.”

“Military issue?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“How strong?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Multiple tiers. I confirmed escalation beyond safe medical origin. Highest field tier can produce roughly triple throughput for a short window.”

The physician stopped writing treatment notes.

“Cost?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Future vitality. Also acute physical damage. Not the same thing.”

His expression changed.

Yun continued before he could ask more.

“Ordinary healing may repair torn meridians, muscle, organs. It does not automatically return lifespan already consumed.”

The physician looked at the capillary differently now.

Hallen said, “Army size.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Do not write one field army.”

The clerk paused.

Yun’s breathing became shallower.

[[speaker:yun]]“Wider trained apparatus exceeds two hundred thousand by conservative reconstruction. Regional forces, reserves, support-connected trained units. I did not confirm all would deploy at once.”

“Quality?”

[[speaker:yun]]“High.”

“How high?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Peak Marquis appears ordinary enough inside trained elite cohorts that Isgard must not plan around Wuyue scarcity assumptions.”

Hallen went very still.

That sentence did more than a dramatic number would have.

Isgard knew what Peak Marquis meant.

It knew how many regions treated that level as a major officer.

Yun continued.

“Shinsei has ten ranked martial Seats.”

Hallen said, “Ten commanders?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Ten Paragons.”

The room went silent.

The clerk’s brush stopped above the page.

Yun heard someone outside the curtain inhale sharply.

“Ten?” Hallen repeated.

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Confirmed?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Multiple independent medical and administrative sources. Treat all ten as Paragon until disproven.”

“Names?”

[[speaker:yun]]“I do not have a complete reliable ten-name operational list. Do not invent one from my report.”

Even dying, Yun remained Yun.

Hallen nodded.

“Known?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Shunto Takamori. No.2. High Paragon. Capture specialist.”

Her voice changed by one degree.

The physician heard it.

Hallen may have too.

Yun continued.

“Tsubasa Kurokawa. No.1. Stronger than Shunto. Exact martial mechanics unknown.”

Hallen said, “How much stronger?”

[[speaker:yun]]“I do not know.”

“Estimate?”

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

The refusal was immediate.

[[speaker:yun]]“Fear is not permission to manufacture intelligence.”

The clerk wrote that too before realizing it was not part of the report.

Yun almost smiled.

Then pain moved through her side and the smile failed.

The physician stepped closer.

“Stop.”

[[speaker:yun]]“One more section.”

“You said that three sections ago.”

[[speaker:yun]]“This is the important one.”

“They were all important.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Good physician.”

“Terrible patient.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Frequently.”

Hallen waited.

Yun looked at the map on the wall.

Isgard.

The coastline she had spent three months finding in other people’s paperwork.

[[speaker:yun]]“Target is here.”

Nobody needed clarification.

[[speaker:yun]]“Evidence: cold-weather medical staging, deep-keel transport repair, northern harbor studies, grain movement, elite training cycles, troop requisitions, leave compression, forward clinic staffing.”

The clerk’s brush moved quickly.

[[speaker:yun]]“No master order recovered. Independent systems converge on Isgard.”

“Launch timing?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Four-month readiness architecture from initial staging. I left late month three.”

Hallen’s face lost what little color remained.

“Weeks.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Could be delayed.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Could be accelerated.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

He stood.

“Send full emergency activation. Not preliminary. Defensive pact.”

The clerk looked up.

“Before central verification?”

Hallen pointed at Yun.

“A Wuyue Top Ten officer crossed a continent carrying physical evidence and a coherent logistics reconstruction while half-dead. Verification continues while the pact activates.”

Good.

Yun closed her eyes.

The room moved faster around her.

Messages.

Seals.

Runners.

A war beginning as paperwork because paperwork was how civilized people tried to gain hours before blood made decisions for them.

The physician pressed fingers to Yun’s pulse.

His face changed.

She knew that expression.

[[speaker:yun]]“Not yet,” she said.

He looked angry now.

[[speaker:yun]]“At whom?” Yun asked.

“You.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Fair.”

Hallen returned to the chair.

“You said Takamori captured you.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“How did you escape?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Insider assistance.”

“Name?”

Yun looked at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

“For protection?”

[[speaker:yun]]“For theirs.”

“Shinsei insider?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Close enough to its systems to create a route.”

Hallen accepted the boundary.

“Did Takamori obtain your evidence?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Notebook, probably. Not all meaning. Physical sample survived. Essential facts memorized. One coded packet may have left Shinrin earlier.”

“Destination?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Wuyue medical channels. Luo Wen may recognize it if it arrives.”

The physician glanced at the injuries again.

There were questions in his face Yun did not want spoken in a room full of military clerks.

She saw him choose not to ask.

Good physician.

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said quietly, “Captured means captured. Put that in the report.”

Hallen nodded.

“Anything else about Takamori?”

Yun’s eyes stayed on the ceiling.

[[speaker:yun]]“Not for this report.”

No detail.

No erasure.

There would be time for someone else to learn what happened if the people who survived her believed it mattered.

Right now Isgard needed troop numbers, Paragons and weeks.

The physician checked her pulse again.

This time he said nothing.

Yun knew.

She looked at Hallen.

[[speaker:yun]]“Has Wuyue been sent?”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Pact?”

“Activated.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Good.”

The word came out almost soundless.

Yun turned her head toward the evidence tray.

[[speaker:yun]]“Sample.”

“We have it.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Luo Wen.”

“We will make sure Wuyue medical command receives the findings and the sample as soon as transport allows.”

Yun frowned.

[[speaker:yun]]“Not medical command.”

Hallen waited.

[[speaker:yun]]“Luo.”

A tiny distinction.

A very large one to her.

Hallen nodded.

“Luo Wen.”

Yun relaxed by a fraction.

The physician took her hand.

Medicine did not require the touch. Sometimes physicians ran out of medicine before they ran out of hands.

Yun looked at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“Did I make it?”

He understood what she meant.

“The warning is moving.”

She closed her eyes.

[[speaker:yun]]“Good.”

That was the last clear word she spoke.

Her breathing slowed.

The room remained busy because wars did not pause politely for death.

Hallen sent another runner.

The clerk copied the report twice.

The physician kept working until there was no honest treatment left to try.

Yun Shizhen died after completing the objective Shunto Takamori had spent three months trying to prevent.

She had entered Shinrin as a poisoner following a pharmaceutical trail.

She left it knowing the shape of a war.

She had been hunted by Shinsei No.2.

Captured.

Violated.

Escaped.

Carried the warning across the border anyway.

Her final legend was not what was done to her.

It was what she still accomplished afterward.

By the time the physician covered her face, Isgard’s defensive-pact alert was already racing toward Wuyue.

Day 0 had begun.

## Chapter 357 — The Warning Reaches Wuyue

The warning reached Wuyue before Yun’s death felt real to the people receiving it.

That was the cruelty of military communication.

Facts arrived first.

Grief had to catch up.

Lu Weiran received the Isgard emergency packet at Quaint’s command room just after dusk.

He read the first line once.

Then again.

Sera saw his face before she saw the paper.

[[speaker:sera]]“What?”

Lu did not answer immediately.

Jin Seoryu stood over the western campaign map with two logistics officers.

Tae and Huo had been arguing near the door.

Qin Luo had just entered from the lower training yard.

Luo Wen was in the clinic.

Lu looked at Sera.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Isgard activated the pact.”

The room stopped.

Jin’s hand left the map.

[[speaker:jin]]“Cause?”

Lu held out the report.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Foreign invasion preparation. Shinrin.”

Sera took it.

Her eyes moved fast.

Not over every sentence.

Through structure.

Source.

Confidence.

Timing.

Then she reached the authentication line.

**Source: Yun Shizhen, Wuyue No.10. Delivered in person at Isgard northern border.**

Sera’s breath stopped.

One heartbeat.

Then continued.

[[speaker:sera]]“Alive?”

Lu looked at the second page.

Nobody spoke.

Sera already knew.

[[speaker:sera]]“Say it.”

Lu’s voice was quiet.

“She died after completing the report.”

Huo looked away.

Tae’s hands closed into fists.

Qin went completely still.

Jin did not move at all for several seconds.

[[speaker:jin]]Then he said, “Facts first.”

Not cold.

Necessary.

Sera nodded.

She read aloud.

[[speaker:sera]]“Shinsei Guild controls Shinrin’s state and military structure.”

Jin’s eyes sharpened.

[[speaker:sera]]“Axtaya is institutional. Combat-throughput stimulant. Multiple tiers. High tier approximately three-times throughput for short duration. Future-vitality cost.”

Huo swore.

[[speaker:qin]]“Army?” Qin asked.

Sera continued.

“Wider trained apparatus greater than two hundred thousand. Not one confirmed field army. Peak Marquis common enough within elite cohorts that normal Wuyue scarcity assumptions are unsafe.”

Silence again.

[[speaker:tae]]Tae said, “Two hundred thousand?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Lower bound.”

His expression changed.

Then Sera reached the next line.

[[speaker:sera]]“Ten ranked Paragon officers.”

Nobody interrupted her this time.

[[speaker:sera]]“Shunto Takamori. No.2. High Paragon. Capture specialist.”

Qin’s gaze sharpened.

[[speaker:sera]]“Tsubasa Kurokawa. No.1. Stronger than Shunto. Exact mechanics unknown.”

[[speaker:jin]]Jin said, “Good. She did not guess.”

Sera looked at him.

He meant it as respect.

She knew.

[[speaker:sera]]“Target: Isgard. Four-month readiness cycle. Yun left late month three. Launch window may be weeks.”

Jin moved immediately.

The strategist replaced the grieving man because both had to exist and only one could use the next hour.

[[speaker:jin]]“Activate pact mobilization protocols. Do not wait for a second message.”

Lu was already writing.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Which forces?”

[[speaker:jin]]“All assigned defensive contingencies first. No public panic order until we know transport capacity and landing options.”

[[speaker:sera]]Sera said, “Quaint prepares.”

Qin nodded.

[[speaker:tae]]Tae said, “All one thousand?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Prepare all. Move only on command.”

Huo was already halfway through the door.

[[speaker:tae]]“Where are you going?” Tae asked.

[[speaker:huo]]“To make sure your company does not pack six months of tea and no bandages.”

[[speaker:tae]]“That happened once.”

[[speaker:huo]]“Twice.”

[[speaker:tae]]“It was good tea.”

For one impossible second, Yun would have found that funny.

Then the room remembered she was dead.

Sera folded the report.

[[speaker:sera]]“Luo.”

Lu nodded.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“I will tell him.”

[[speaker:sera]]“No.”

Sera stood.

[[speaker:sera]]“I will.”

The clinic smelled like bitterroot and boiled cloth.

Luo was standing over a patient when Sera entered.

He looked up once.

Saw her face.

Finished tying the bandage anyway.

[[speaker:luo]]“Change this in six hours,” he told the apprentice. “If the swelling crosses the ink line, wake me.”

The apprentice nodded and left.

Only then did Luo turn fully toward Sera.

[[speaker:luo]]“She made it?”

Sera’s chest tightened.

Of all the questions.

Not “Is she alive?”

Not “Where is she?”

She made it.

Because he knew what Yun had gone to do.

Sera nodded.

[[speaker:sera]]“The warning reached Isgard.”

Luo closed his eyes.

One breath.

Then:

[[speaker:luo]]“And her?”

[[speaker:sera]]“She died after giving the report.”

Nothing broke dramatically.

Luo did not drop a cup.

Did not shout.

Did not fall to his knees.

He sat down on the nearest stool because standing had become unnecessary.

Sera placed the report beside him.

[[speaker:sera]]“There is an Axtaya sample in Isgard. She asked that it reach you.”

His hand moved to the paper.

Stopped before touching it.

[[speaker:luo]]“She said my name?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

That one hurt more.

Sera let the silence exist.

After a while Luo opened the report.

He read it once.

Then again.

At the capture line, his jaw tightened.

**Captured by Shunto Takamori. Escaped with insider assistance.**

No more detail.

Nothing about the locked room beyond what Yun chose to put into an emergency military report.

Luo did not invent the missing pieces.

Not yet.

He kept reading.

[[speaker:luo]]“She remembered everything.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Of course she did.”

His voice almost failed there.

Almost.

Then he reached the Axtaya notes.

Physician returned to his face.

Grief remained. So did the work Yun had spent her final strength giving him.

Luo stood.

[[speaker:luo]]“Tell Isgard not to open the capillary outside controlled ventilation. I want comparative bloodwork from any exposed users they have. And no one assumes healing restored spent vitality.”

Sera watched him.

[[speaker:sera]]“Luo.”

[[speaker:luo]]“I know.”

He looked at her.

His eyes were wet now.

[[speaker:luo]]“I know she is dead.”

Sera said nothing.

Luo looked down at the report.

[[speaker:luo]]“I can know that and still work.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Good.”

He wiped his face once with the heel of his hand, irritated by the inconvenience of tears.

[[speaker:luo]]“Then we work.”

Sera left him with the report.

On the way back to command, she passed the lower corridor leading toward Rhen’s sealed chamber.

She stopped.

Only for a moment.

One day.

Yun had reached Isgard one day after that door closed.

If the warning had come earlier, Rhen might have been standing beside her now.

If he were still in the early stage, perhaps Lu could have called him out.

But the chamber monitor already showed the condensation structure deep beyond safe interruption.

Sera knew the rule.

She had asked him to explain it until there was no ambiguity left.

She did not touch the door.

Did not order Lu to gamble Rhen’s meridians because grief wanted one impossible answer.

The timing was cruel.

Cruelty did not turn a lethal medical choice into a wise one.

Sera kept walking.

Back in command, Jin had already opened Isgard transport maps.

Lu was sending pact acknowledgements.

Qin had summoned Quaint company captains.

Tae and Huo were mobilizing stores.

Wuyue was moving.

Yun Shizhen had died.

The warning had not.
