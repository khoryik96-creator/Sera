## Chapter 361 — The First Day After the Warning

The first full day after Yun Shizhen died began with inventory.

Not mourning.

Not vengeance.

Inventory.

Wuyue had learned enough wars to know that grief could wait beside a bed.

Ships could not.

Jin Seoryu stood over the main command table before sunrise with three brushes tucked behind one ear and a fourth in his hand.

Sera had seen him fight entire campaigns with fewer weapons.

“Again,” she said.

A naval clerk swallowed.

“Forty-six deep-hull transports confirmed within nine days. Another nineteen if Stonecrown releases civilian grain carriers.”

“Do not count grain carriers twice,” Jin said.

“I did not.”

[[speaker:jin]]“You did yesterday.”

“That was another clerk.”

Jin looked at him.

The clerk amended himself.

“That was the office.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Better.”

Sera leaned against the table.

The map looked different from the night before.

The geography had not changed. Responsibility had.

Isgard sat to the north beneath fresh black pins representing ports, hospitals and military depots.

Wuyue's regions sat beneath silver counters.

Shinrin remained an absence beyond the sea with only Yun's reconstructed notes giving it shape.

More than two hundred thousand trained personnel in the wider apparatus.

Ten Paragons.

Axtaya.

A four-month readiness cycle already late in its third month.

Launch window measured in weeks.

Weeks were not time.

Weeks were what people called time when they were trying not to say **soon**.

Jin moved one counter.

[[speaker:jin]]“Medical reserve first.”

A logistics officer said, “Before troops?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Yes.”

“If the fleet launches—”

[[speaker:jin]]“If Isgard loses hospitals before we arrive, troops become decorative.”

The officer nodded.

Jin continued.

[[speaker:jin]]“Send preserved bloodroot, feverleaf, suturing silk, splints, anti-rot compounds and grain concentrate under pact priority. Isgard already moved its coastal reserve inland last night. Their bottleneck is civilian handling capacity.”

The last sentence mattered.

Isgard still had terrifying martial elites.

It did not have enough hands.

The natural epidemic after the last war had taken farmers, miners, haulers, healers, stable workers, smiths, children who would have grown into all of those things, and parents who would have taught them.

A country could possess three Paragons and still struggle to move grain from one valley to another.

Power did not harvest winterroot.

Power did not rebuild a waterwheel after the carpenter died.

Power did not make ten exhausted porters become thirty.

Sera had learned that lesson in Isgard the expensive way.

She intended not to learn it again.

A runner entered.

“Message from the northern pact office.”

Sera took it.

Isgard had already begun dispersing medicine away from its major ports.

Brynja Kharvorn was moving coastal engines.

Astrid Vardrenn had requested permission to reopen two old forest routes for emergency movement despite winter damage.

Solveig Skeldran had ordered regional commanders to stop concentrating senior officers in obvious command halls.

Good.

They were not waiting to be saved.

Sera handed the message to Jin.

[[speaker:sera]]“Reply that the first medicine convoy leaves before noon.”

[[speaker:jin]]“We have not finished escort allocation.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then finish it before noon.”

Jin looked at her.

[[speaker:jin]]“That was already the plan.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Good.”

[[speaker:jin]]“You enjoy giving orders after I have made them.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I enjoy correct plans.”

[[speaker:jin]]“That is not the same thing.”

[[speaker:sera]]“It is from my side of the table.”

For half a breath, Tae looked like he might laugh.

He thought better of it when Jin turned toward him.

[[speaker:jin]]“Tae.”

[[speaker:tae]]“No.”

Jin paused.

[[speaker:jin]]“I have not asked anything.”

[[speaker:tae]]“I know what your face means.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Move the western Quaint storehouses into hardened distribution.”

Tae frowned.

[[speaker:tae]]“That was less annoying than expected.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Give me time.”

Huo Wujin was already carrying two supply crates that ordinary men would have put on carts.

He set them down.

[[speaker:huo]]“Where do you want these?”

Lu Weiran answered without looking up from his ledger.

“Not there.”

Huo looked down.

[[speaker:huo]]“Why?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“That corridor is for outgoing medical cargo.”

[[speaker:huo]]“These are medical cargo.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“They are training stones.”

Huo looked at the crates again.

Someone had written **MEDICAL** across the side in charcoal.

Tae became suddenly interested in a wall.

Lu's eyes lifted.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Tae.”

[[speaker:tae]]“No evidence.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“You wrote it.”

[[speaker:tae]]“You cannot prove that.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“It is your handwriting.”

Sera closed her eyes.

For three seconds the room felt like home.

Then another runner entered with Isgard casualty projections if a landing struck before Wuyue's main expedition could assemble.

Nobody laughed again.

The day kept moving.

So did Wuyue.

At the public command bureau, Xie Wuchen signed his sixth routing authority as No.10.

He still looked offended every time someone addressed him by the number.

A clerk said, “Number Ten, the eastern reserve—”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Xie.”

The clerk stopped.

“Sir?”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Xie is shorter.”

“The regulation says—”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Then the regulation has too many syllables.”

He signed anyway.

Across the compound, Luo Wen did not sign anything unless it concerned medicine.

He had not slept.

Sera knew because she had asked.

Twice.

Both answers had been variations of no.

He stood over Yun's Axtaya sample with three assistants and a cup of tea that had gone cold enough to become an accusation.

“Do not heat it,” he said when one assistant reached for the kettle.

The assistant froze.

“The tea?”

[[speaker:luo]]“The sample.”

“I meant the tea.”

Luo looked at the cup as if seeing it for the first time.

[[speaker:luo]]“Oh.”

The assistant heated it.

Luo did not drink.

His hands were steady.

That worried Sera more than shaking would have.

She stood in the doorway.

[[speaker:sera]]“Anything new?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Not yet.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Anything useful?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

He tapped the sealed capillary.

[[speaker:luo]]“She was right about throughput.”

Sera waited.

Luo continued.

“Whatever else they have done to the compound, it is still forcing existing systems to spend faster. It is not creating cultivation. If somebody comes at us under Redline, treat the body like an overpressurized vessel, not a stronger realm.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Can you stop it?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Eventually.”

[[speaker:sera]]“That is not an answer.”

[[speaker:luo]]“It is the honest one.”

Sera accepted it.

Luo looked at the capillary again.

[[speaker:luo]]“She got this here.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:luo]]“She should have come with it.”

Sera did not offer comfort shaped like a lie.

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

Luo's jaw moved once.

Then he returned to work.

Outside, bells marked the opening of the central warehouses.

One hundred and twenty Quaint Hands were being selected for the northern expedition.

Not the strongest one hundred and twenty.

The most useful one hundred and twenty.

Trauma medicine.

Evacuation.

Road repair.

Disease control.

Reconnaissance.

Security.

People capable of killing when Sera authorized it and equally capable of spending twelve hours digging a drainage trench if that kept an infirmary from flooding.

The other eight hundred eighty would remain in Wuyue.

That decision had already begun arguments.

Huo wanted to go north.

Tae also wanted to go north but preferred to pretend his objections were strategic.

Qin had said nothing yet.

That was why Sera was more worried about Qin.

By afternoon the first pact medicine convoy moved.

By evening three Wuyue regions had begun troop assembly.

By midnight Jin had crossed out two shipping assumptions and replaced them with worse ones.

Day One ended with fewer illusions than it began.

Far across the sea, in Shinrin, another command room received a message.

The messenger knelt.

Tsubasa Kurokawa read the report once.

Then again.

The foreign infiltrator had escaped.

She had reached Isgard.

The warning had gone onward to Wuyue.

For several breaths he said nothing.

Haru Ishikawa stood at the far side of the table with a charcoal stick between two fingers.

Shunto Takamori stood near the window.

Tsubasa folded the report.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Then the calendar is wrong.”

Haru looked up.

Shunto said, “We can still contain—”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“No.”

Tsubasa's voice was quiet.

Not angry.

That made Shunto stop.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“The warning is out.”

Tsubasa looked toward the north wall where shipping schedules covered half the stone.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“The question is no longer whether they know.”

His eyes moved to Haru.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“It is how much time knowing buys them.”

Haru put down the charcoal.

“Not enough,” he said.

Tsubasa waited.

Haru walked toward the schedule board.

[[speaker:haru]]“But more than we should give them.”

Day One ended in three countries.

Only one of them had decided exactly how much faster the next thirteen would need to become.

## Chapter 362 — The Door Cannot Be Opened

Sera returned to Rhen's chamber after the first full war council.

She knew exactly where he was. What she needed now was an answer written without hope inside it.

Lu walked beside her.

Core came from the lower passage carrying two pulse slates and the expression of a man who had spent the day being asked whether impossible things were medically advisable.

They stopped outside the sealed stone door.

Nothing moved.

No frost leaked beneath it.

No pressure touched the corridor.

Rhen had always been good at disappearing when he wanted to.

This was simply the first time disappearance had become treatment.

Sera looked at Core.

[[speaker:sera]]“Say it again.”

Core did not ask what.

“Do not open the door.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Why?”

“The condensation has already passed the first reversible stage.”

Sera's eyes stayed on the stone.

[[speaker:sera]]“Plain language.”

Core exhaled.

“The first hours were compression. If he had stopped then, he would have been sick and furious but probably safe.”

[[speaker:sera]]“And now?”

“Now his old circulation structure is being rebuilt around the denser reserve.”

He tapped one slate.

“The meridians are not simply carrying qi. They are changing how they carry it. If the process is forced open from outside, the pressure can tear through channels that are only half stabilized.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Can he repair that?”

“Maybe.”

Sera turned.

Core held her gaze.

“You asked for plain language.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I did.”

“Maybe he repairs it. Maybe he wakes with enough of himself intact to repair the rest. Maybe the damage propagates faster than he can reorganize from inside it.”

Lu said nothing.

Core continued.

“We do not know because nobody else has ever had this problem.”

That was the medical summary of Rhen's life.

Sera almost smiled.

Almost.

[[speaker:sera]]“How long before interruption becomes safe?”

“It becomes safe when he finishes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“That was not my question.”

“It is the only honest answer.”

The corridor stayed quiet.

Sera placed one hand against the stone.

It was cool.

Ordinary stone.

Yesterday Rhen had stood on the other side of it wearing the dark green coat he refused to replace because the new ones “felt too intentional.”

Yesterday she had stitched the inside seam while he complained that her repair was uneven.

She had told him that if he wanted perfect stitching he could heal the coat himself.

He had said healing did not work on coats.

She had told him that sounded like a personal failure.

Twenty-four hours later Yun had reached Isgard dying.

There were cruelties nobody could punch.

Sera removed her hand.

Lu said, “We can leave a watch.”

[[speaker:sera]]“We already have one.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“A second.”

[[speaker:sera]]“For what?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“In case the chamber changes.”

Sera looked at him.

Lu's expression remained practical.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“If the frost pattern changes. If the seal cracks. If he communicates. If anything suggests the process has entered a stable interruption state.”

Core nodded.

“That is reasonable.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then do it.”

Lu made a note.

Sera turned away from the door.

Core watched her.

“You are not going to ask me a fourth time?”

[[speaker:sera]]“No.”

He looked surprised.

Sera glanced back at the stone once.

[[speaker:sera]]“I married a physician.”

Core waited.

[[speaker:sera]]“He told me what would happen if I opened it.”

Her voice stayed level.

[[speaker:sera]]“I believed him before I needed the answer to be different.”

That was the whole thing.

Not sacrifice.

Not noble suffering.

Trust was less decorative than either.

She started down the corridor.

Lu followed.

Core remained at the door to check the seal.

Halfway to the map room, Lu said, “Jin currently has Rhen listed as unavailable.”

Sera stopped.

[[speaker:sera]]“Currently?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“He dislikes leaving variables undefined.”

[[speaker:sera]]“He is unavailable.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then remove him from the war model.”

Lu raised an eyebrow.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Jin may object philosophically.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Jin can object while moving ships.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“What if Rhen finishes earlier?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then he can complain that we planned competently without him.”

Lu considered this.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“He may find that romantic.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Rhen finds inventory romantic.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“That is true.”

They reached the stair.

At the bottom, Tae waited with his arms folded.

Huo stood beside him.

Qin sat on the railing.

Sera looked at all three.

[[speaker:sera]]“No.”

Tae frowned.

[[speaker:tae]]“We did not ask.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You were going to.”

Huo looked at Tae.

[[speaker:huo]]“She is getting worse.”

[[speaker:tae]]“Marriage.”

Sera stared at them.

Huo corrected himself.

[[speaker:huo]]“Better.”

Qin's mouth moved faintly.

Sera continued down.

[[speaker:sera]]“Rhen is not an available asset.”

Tae's humor disappeared.

[[speaker:tae]]“How long?”

[[speaker:sera]]“We do not know.”

Huo looked up the stair toward the sealed chamber.

[[speaker:huo]]“And if Shinsei lands?”

[[speaker:sera]]“We fight.”

[[speaker:huo]]“If ten Paragons land?”

[[speaker:sera]]“We fight smarter.”

Tae said, “That sounded like Jin.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I have been near him too long.”

Qin stood.

[[speaker:qin]]“What do you need from us?”

Sera finally had the question she needed.

Sera looked at the three men who had lived beneath her roof, eaten her food, broken her training grounds, annoyed Lu and somehow become High Paragons while most of the world still thought Wuyue's strength ended at its public ranking board.

“Home,” she said.

Huo's brow furrowed.

[[speaker:huo]]“We are not leaving yet.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I know.”

[[speaker:huo]]“Then why—”

[[speaker:sera]]“Because when we do, Shinsei may discover Wuyue has a door behind the door.”

Tae's eyes narrowed.

Sera looked at Qin.

[[speaker:sera]]“Start designing what happens when they try to open it.”

Qin nodded once.

No complaint.

No pride.

Just work.

Behind them, one floor above, the sealed chamber remained silent.

By the time Rhen opened his eyes again, the world would have spent more than half a year learning what Sera did when she could not call him.

## Chapter 363 — Luo Reads What Yun Left Him

The packet arrived on Day Three.

Not the notebook.

Luo knew that before he touched it.

The field notebook had been the dangerous one.

The one Yun carried deep enough into Shinrin to fill with things no smuggler should have known.

Shunto probably had it.

Or pieces of it.

Or ash from it.

This was the smaller packet she had copied before the last part of the investigation swallowed her.

A medical packet.

Thin.

Oil-wrapped.

Ugly.

Perfect.

The courier placed it on the table.

“Recovered through a merchant relay in the western ports. Three transfers. One dead-drop failed. This copy kept moving.”

Luo looked at the seal.

It was not Yun's formal mark.

Too obvious.

Instead there were two small ink strokes beside the fold.

One straight.

One crooked.

He stared at them.

The courier waited.

Sera stood near the clinic window.

Lu stood beside her.

Neither hurried him.

Luo broke the seal.

Inside were six sheets.

The first looked like dosage notes.

The second looked like treatment reactions.

The third looked like shipping damage.

To another physician, they might have been ordinary.

To Luo, they were Yun being rude in a language only two people had spent enough years arguing to understand.

He read the first line twice.

Then a third time, because she had told him to.

**Read anything that comes back twice.**

His thumb pressed the edge of the paper.

Sera said nothing.

The code opened slowly.

Axtaya at institutional scale.

Not isolated smugglers.

Not a private clinic.

Military-linked production.

Standardized screening.

Repeated shipment categories.

A list of ten recurring medical priority marks that corresponded to high-command allocations.

Not ten names.

Not ten Domains.

Ten positions important enough to receive separate contingency medicine.

The packet confirmed what Yun's final Isgard report had later made explicit.

Ten Paragon seats.

Shunto among them.

Tsubasa above him.

Luo turned the page.

A map had been disguised as a pulse chart.

Three arterial branches were not arteries.

They were sea routes.

A cluster of fever spikes was not fever.

It was military stock movement.

A note about “northern cold sensitivity” meant Isgard.

The language was ridiculous.

It was brilliant.

It was Yun.

Lu leaned forward slightly.

“Useful?”

“Yes.”

“New?”

“Some.”

“Enough to change deployment?”

“Not yet.”

Luo kept reading.

The packet’s age mattered because it captured what Yun knew before the final flight.

It did not contain the complete two-hundred-thousand estimate.

It did not contain the four-month readiness conclusion in its final form.

It did not contain everything Yun learned after sending it.

That was exactly why it was credible.

A dead woman had not conveniently mailed the future.

She had sent what she knew when she knew it.

Luo reached the last sheet.

At the bottom was a line that did not fit any military code.

Three tiny dosage marks.

A loop.

A crossed stem.

He stopped breathing for a moment.

Sera saw it.

“What?”

Luo did not answer.

He translated silently.

**You forgot to eat again.**

Below it, in an even smaller mark:

**Eat something, idiot.**

His mouth opened.

Nothing came out.

Lu looked away.

Sera did not.

Luo read it again.

The handwriting was not handwriting.

The sentence was not written in words.

It was a private joke buried inside medical shorthand inside a military warning crossing an ocean.

Yun had been hiding from a High Paragon.

Tracing an invasion.

Moving evidence.

And somewhere inside all of that she had apparently found time to be annoyed that Luo skipped meals when worried.

He laughed once.

The sound was small and wrong.

Then his eyes filled.

He did not wipe them immediately.

Sera still said nothing.

Luo folded the paper along the original crease.

Carefully.

Too carefully.

Then he looked at the cold bowl on the side table.

Rice.

Vegetables.

Something Huo had probably stolen half the meat from earlier.

He picked up the chopsticks.

Lu exhaled through his nose.

Luo looked at him.

“Do not make this a moment.”

“I did not speak.”

“Your face did.”

“My face is neutral.”

“Your face is irritating.”

Sera said, “Eat.”

Luo glared at both of them.

Then ate.

Three bites.

Four.

On the fifth, his hand shook.

He stopped.

Put the chopsticks down.

Breathed.

Then picked them up again.

Nobody helped him.

That was kindness too.

When the bowl was empty, Luo pulled a clean sheet toward himself.

“What are you doing?” Sera asked.

“Separating the useful parts.”

“You just finished reading it.”

“Yes.”

“And?”

“And Shinsei's medical structure can tell us where it expects losses before troop reports do.”

His eyes had changed.

Not healed.

Working.

He drew three columns.

“Give Jin this. Not the private notation.”

Lu nodded.

“Obviously.”

“Give Qin the route structure.”

“Done.”

“Keep the original here.”

Sera said, “Under guard.”

Luo looked at the last line again.

“Yes.”

The packet had crossed Shinrin.

A merchant relay.

Three transfers.

A dead drop.

A sea.

It had arrived after Yun.

Too late to save her.

Not too late to keep doing what she sent it to do.

Luo placed one palm over the paper.

“Fine,” he murmured.

Sera heard.

“What?”

He looked at the empty bowl.

“Nothing.”

But when he returned to work, he moved Yun's untouched cup slightly closer to the lamp.

Not away.

Never away.

## Chapter 364 — Black Radiance Burns the Last Safehouse

Yurushi Amagiri burned the last safehouse himself.

He had built it nine years earlier.

That was the unpleasant part.

The pleasant part was that nobody was inside.

He stood in the back room while Darkness Qi swallowed the lamp and let the walls disappear around him.

The house had once belonged to a widow who sold rope.

Then to her nephew.

Then to nobody officially.

Over the years it had hidden three families from a predatory tax captain, two children marked for clan retaliation, a wounded Shinsei clerk who had discovered the wrong ledger, and one old swordsman whose greatest crime had been refusing to tell an officer where his daughter lived.

Yurushi had never called it resistance.

He had called it avoiding unnecessary problems.

That phrase had survived almost as long as the house.

Then Yun Shizhen happened.

Some words stopped working after enough blood.

He stepped into the alley.

A woman waited there with two children and a travel bundle.

“South road?” she asked.

“No.”

Her face tightened.

Yurushi continued.

“South road is watched. Take the dyers' canal west, then the old kiln track. There will be a cart at the broken shrine.”

“Whose cart?”

“You do not want to know.”

“That sounds reassuring.”

“It is not.”

She almost smiled.

Good.

Fear became easier to move when it remembered it could change shape.

Yurushi handed her a merchant chit.

“Do not show this unless someone stops the cart.”

“What if Shinsei stops it?”

“Especially then.”

She looked at the mark.

“Will it work?”

“For another day.”

“And after that?”

Yurushi looked back at the safehouse.

“After that I would not recommend returning.”

The woman understood.

She bowed once.

Not deeply.

He liked that.

Then she left with the children.

Yurushi waited until their footsteps vanished.

Only then did he begin destroying the house.

Not with a dramatic technique.

That would attract attention.

He poured lamp oil along the old timber supports.

Opened the rear shutters so the fire would draw inward before climbing.

Removed two floorboards that concealed the tunnel entrance.

Collapsed the first section with a heel strike.

Then lit the oil.

Flame took the room quietly.

He watched long enough to be certain the hidden ledger cache burned.

A voice came from the roof behind him.

“You always did hate paperwork.”

Yurushi did not turn.

“Hello, Hana.”

Hana Arakawa dropped into the alley without sound.

Her short swords remained sheathed.

That did not mean she was unarmed.

Neither of them had needed weapons to make a conversation dangerous in years.

She looked at the fire.

“Yours?”

“Temporarily.”

“Was.”

“Better.”

Hana's eyes moved over him.

“You are leaving.”

“Am I?”

“You destroyed three route caches in two days.”

“Poor storage discipline.”

“You moved forty-seven civilians out of districts Shunto is searching.”

“Coincidence.”

“You hate coincidences.”

“Growth.”

Her expression did not change.

Yurushi finally looked at her.

Four years together made silence unfairly efficient.

She knew the way he stood when he had already chosen something and was merely deciding how much of the choice to admit.

He knew the way her left thumb touched the edge of her sword guard when she wanted to stop somebody and had not yet decided whether she had the right.

Neither mentioned it.

Hana said, “Shunto knows someone helped the foreign woman.”

“He should investigate harder.”

“He is.”

“I noticed.”

“He has your old eastern corridor lists.”

That made Yurushi still.

“Which version?”

“Two years old.”

“Then he has six dead routes and one dangerous one.”

“He will still find people if you leave them.”

“I did not.”

Hana looked toward the street the woman had taken.

“No.”

Something softened in her face.

Not enough to become agreement.

“Yurushi.”

He waited.

“Was she Wuyue?”

He did not answer immediately.

Hana's eyes narrowed.

“She was.”

“She was a woman being hunted by our own people.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It was the answer when I helped her.”

“And afterward?”

Yurushi looked into the burning room.

“Afterward I learned her name.”

Hana did not ask it.

Maybe she already knew.

Maybe she did not want to make knowledge into evidence.

That was the problem between them.

Hana believed remaining inside a broken institution could still limit what it did.

Yurushi had once believed the same thing.

Then one day he had looked at all the people he was secretly moving around Shinsei's official decisions and realized his loyalty existed mostly as a cover story for resisting the institution he claimed to serve.

Yun had merely removed the last excuse.

Hana said, “If you leave, you lose what access you have.”

“If I stay, my access becomes a reason to keep lending the rest of myself to people I no longer trust.”

“You think I trust everything?”

“No.”

That answer hurt her more than accusation would have.

He continued.

“I think you believe somebody decent has to remain near the machinery.”

“Yes.”

“I think decent people can become lubrication.”

Hana's face hardened.

“And leaving makes you clean?”

“No.”

He met her eyes.

“Nothing makes me clean.”

He had finally named the thing he could never say while they were together.

He had served Shinsei sincerely.

He had carried orders.

He had helped enforce a peace that saved lives and later helped maintain a government that discovered how useful fear could be.

His civilian routes did not erase the first half.

Hana looked away first.

The fire caught the roof beam.

“Where will you go?” she asked.

“Somewhere with tea.”

Her eyes closed briefly.

“That is not a country.”

“It narrows the search.”

“Wuyue.”

Yurushi smiled faintly.

“You said it.”

“Idiot.”

“Probably.”

A bell rang three streets away.

Patrol change.

Hana's head turned.

Yurushi felt it too.

They had minutes.

She said, “The western inspection office receives the updated route notices at sunset.”

He looked at her.

Hana's expression was blank.

“That sounds like ordinary administration.”

“It is.”

“Of course.”

“If someone intended to be gone before then, the old river credentials might still pass.”

“Someone?”

“I have no idea who.”

Yurushi's smile became real for half a second.

Hana hated it.

He could tell because he had once spent four years learning which of her silences meant **do not laugh**.

He did not laugh.

“Thank you,” he said.

Her face tightened.

“Do not make this into more than it is.”

“I wouldn't dare.”

“You always dared.”

“That was different.”

“How?”

“We were younger.”

“We were together four years, Yurushi. We were not children.”

“Emotionally debatable.”

She nearly smiled.

Nearly.

Then the patrol bell rang again.

Hana stepped backward into the dark edge of the alley.

“Go.”

Yurushi looked at her.

“You know Shunto will eventually ask whether you saw me.”

“Yes.”

“What will you say?”

“That I inspected a fire.”

“That is true.”

“I am learning from you.”

“That should concern you.”

“It does.”

Then she was gone.

Yurushi waited another thirty breaths.

The roof collapsed inward.

The last safehouse became flame and falling timber.

No ledger survived.

No tunnel mouth remained obvious.

No civilian remained inside its routes.

Only then did Yurushi leave.

He did not travel toward Wuyue as a man seeking sanctuary.

He did not know whether Sera would trust him.

He did not know whether the Quaint Teahouse stories were even half true.

He knew one thing.

Yun Shizhen had crossed half the world carrying a warning for people who might never learn what it cost her.

Yurushi had spent years helping people escape through hidden doors.

For once, he intended to walk through one himself.
