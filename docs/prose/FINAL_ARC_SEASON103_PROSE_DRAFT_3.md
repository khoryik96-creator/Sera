## Chapter 388 — Wuyue Takes Prisoners

The first Shinsei soldier to surrender expected to die.

Sera knew because he said so.

He was twenty-six.

Maybe twenty-seven.

Peak Marquis.

His left thigh had been opened by an Arin swordswoman and tied badly with his own sash.

His sword lay three paces away.

Two Wuyue soldiers held him at spearpoint.

He looked at Sera when she approached and swallowed.

“Do it.”

Sera stopped.

[[speaker:sera]]“Do what?”

His mouth tightened.

“Kill me.”

The soldiers beside him glanced at one another.

Sera looked at the wound.

[[speaker:sera]]“You’re bleeding.”

He stared.

“I know.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then stop talking.”

She turned.

[[speaker:sera]]“Medic.”

The Shinsei soldier’s expression changed.

One of the Quaint trauma teams arrived within moments.

The prisoner looked between them.

“You’re treating me.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

The physician knelt.

“Because this is a leg.”

The soldier blinked.

The physician cut away fabric.

“Try not to make it a philosophy.”

That became the tone of the beachhead.

Not mercy announced from a platform.

Procedure.

Weapons removed.

Hands bound where necessary.

Wounded separated from the uninjured.

Officers identified.

Axtaya pouches collected but not opened by random soldiers.

Prisoners fed after screening for poison.

The dead recorded from both sides before burial.

Isgard troops watched at first with something close to disbelief.

Some had lost family during the first month.

Some had watched Halvek die.

Some had carried Raska’s body.

A Dravaryn sergeant stopped beside a row of captured Shinsei infantry and looked at Sera.

“They did this to us.”

Sera understood what he meant.

Not these exact men.

The army.

The invasion.

The month.

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

“And we feed them.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

The sergeant’s jaw tightened.

“Why?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Because they surrendered.”

“That’s it?”

[[speaker:sera]]“That’s enough.”

He looked at the prisoners.

One was barely nineteen.

Another had both hands burned from holding a damaged spear formation.

A third stared at the food like accepting it might be treason.

The sergeant exhaled.

Then walked away.

He did not look convinced.

He also did not stop the meal. By evening, seventy-three prisoners had become one hundred twelve.

The number kept rising as scattered Shinsei groups realized the retreat corridor remained open to those who dropped weapons.

Jin noticed immediately.

[[speaker:jin]]“They expected massacre.”

Sera looked at the growing holding area.

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jin]]“That expectation was part of their discipline model.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jin]]“If surrender means survival—”

[[speaker:sera]]“Do not turn this into a weapon yet.”

Jin looked at her.

Sera continued.

“We treat prisoners because that is what we do. If it also weakens Shinsei’s willingness to die pointlessly, good. But do not make compassion conditional on usefulness.”

Jin was quiet.

Then nodded.

[[speaker:jin]]“Fair.”

Luo spent most of the night in the prisoner hospital.

Not because he trusted them.

Because Axtaya made trust medically irrelevant.

Three surrendered soldiers carried sealed Redline doses.

Two insisted they had never used them.

One had.

Luo knew before the man admitted it.

Pulse instability.

Microtearing near the heart meridian.

Abnormal skin temperature.

A vitality signature that felt wrong in a way ordinary exhaustion did not.

“How many?” Luo asked.

The man looked away.

“One.”

“When?”

“Six days ago.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Why?”

“Formation breach.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Were you dying?”

“No.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Were your people?”

“Yes.”

Luo nodded.

That was medicine’s difficult answer.

Maybe that use had saved lives.

Maybe it had cost the man years.

Both could be true.

[[speaker:luo]]“How much did they tell you?”

The soldier frowned.

“About what?”

[[speaker:luo]]“The cost.”

“Recovery?”

Luo became still.

[[speaker:luo]]“What did they say?”

The soldier shrugged.

“Severe fatigue. Meridian strain. Several months before full vitality recovery if overused.”

The number on the page was the difference.

Not five years.

Months.

Luo looked at the sealed vials collected on the table.

He thought of Yun.

Then of Aya Katsuragi, whose old medical signatures Yun had found beneath early safeguards.

Somebody knew.

Somebody else had translated that knowledge into language soldiers could survive hearing.

Luo wrote one line in his notebook.

**They do not know.**

Then another.

**Or not all of them.**

He looked at the patient.

[[speaker:luo]]“You’re staying overnight.”

The man frowned.

“I’m a prisoner.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

“So?”

[[speaker:luo]]“So prisoners also have hearts.”

The soldier stared.

Luo sighed.

[[speaker:luo]]“Apparently.”

The next morning, a Shinsei medic among the prisoners asked to help.

An Isgard guard refused immediately.

Sera was called.

The medic stood with hands bound in front.

“I can identify Redline crash patterns.”

Luo looked at him.

[[speaker:luo]]“Training?”

“Field medicine.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Under Aya Katsuragi?”

The man hesitated.

“Her manuals.”

Luo untied one hand.

The Isgard guard looked at Sera.

She nodded.

[[speaker:sera]]“One hand. One table. Guard stays.”

The medic worked.

Carefully.

Efficiently.

He saved a Wuyue soldier from a circulation clot thirty minutes later.

Nobody applauded.

They just gave him another patient.

By noon, the beachhead had become something Shinsei had not planned for.

Not merely occupied ground.

A place where soldiers who surrendered remained alive long enough to compare what each side had told them.

That was not yet rebellion.

It was more dangerous than silence.

## Chapter 389 — Aya Keeps Jun Alive

Aya Katsuragi knew Jun Kajihara was alive before the stretcher reached her.

Hundred Pulse Dominion told her.

One pulse among hundreds.

Weak.

Irregular.

Still present.

[[speaker:aya]]“Here.”

The stretcher team moved toward her before she finished pointing.

Jun looked enormous even while unconscious.

The halberd was gone.

His chest armor had been removed.

A broad depression crossed the sternum where Kael’s final strike had landed.

One arm had swollen around an internal fracture.

Three meridian channels were partially collapsed.

Two more were bleeding internally.

Aya put her fingers against his neck.

[[speaker:aya]]“Who moved him after collapse?”

A medic raised one hand.

“I did.”

“How?”

“Spine board. No qinggong.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Good.”

The medic looked relieved.

Aya did not notice.

She was already inside the problem.

Needles.

Seven Pulse first.

One at the lower heart route.

Two to stop circulation from hammering damaged junctions.

One near the shoulder to keep swelling from cutting off the arm.

Three more to stabilize the chest.

Then White Thread Sutra.

Fine qi lines entered through the needles.

Not glowing magic.

Not instant flesh knitting.

Precise reinforcement around torn vessels and meridian walls so the body stopped making the damage worse every time the heart beat.

Jun coughed blood.

One medic flinched.

Aya did not.

“Good.”

The medic stared.

Aya looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“It came out.”

“Right.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Do not react to blood as though blood itself is failure.”

“Yes, Physician.”

Jun’s pulse dropped.

Aya changed two needles.

[[speaker:aya]]“Borrowed Beat.”

The room became quiet.

Her own breathing slowed.

Pulse aligned through contact.

For several minutes, Jun’s damaged circulation followed Aya’s stable rhythm instead of its own failing one.

That kept him alive long enough for the rest.

Hours later, he opened his eyes.

Aya was still there.

He looked at the ceiling.

[[speaker:jun]]“Lost.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

His voice was rough.

[[speaker:jun]]“Kael.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jun]]“New Paragon.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

Jun closed his eyes again.

[[speaker:jun]]“He learned.”

Aya adjusted a needle.

[[speaker:aya]]“So should you.”

One eye opened.

[[speaker:jun]]“That sounded philosophical.”

[[speaker:aya]]“It was medical.”

[[speaker:jun]]“How?”

[[speaker:aya]]“You are injured because your opponent adapted faster than you did.”

Jun considered that.

[[speaker:jun]]“Fair.”

He tried to move his arm.

Aya slapped his wrist back to the bed.

Jun stared.

[[speaker:jun]]“You hit me.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I am a Paragon.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You are currently a patient.”

He looked almost offended.

Then the corner of his mouth moved.

[[speaker:jun]]“Also fair.”

Aya looked at the chart.

[[speaker:aya]]“Several days before I let you stand in armor.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Too long.”

[[speaker:aya]]“No.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Shinsei needs—”

[[speaker:aya]]“Shinsei needs you alive more than it needs you pretending one successful treatment erased the fight.”

Jun was silent.

Aya continued.

“Your recoverable damage can be repaired.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Can?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jun]]“All of it?”

[[speaker:aya]]“With time.”

[[speaker:jun]]“How much?”

She looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“Enough that command will complain.”

Jun almost laughed.

Then winced.

Aya adjusted the chest support.

Outside, new wounded arrived.

The entire hospital shifted around the sound.

Jun heard it too.

[[speaker:jun]]“How many?”

Aya looked toward the door.

[[speaker:aya]]“Too many.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Redline?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Some.”

Jun’s expression hardened.

[[speaker:jun]]“Did they hold?”

Aya turned back.

[[speaker:aya]]“Do not ask me whether burning their futures bought enough ground to satisfy you while I am repairing them.”

Silence.

Jun looked at her.

Aya’s voice was calm.

That made it sharper.

[[speaker:aya]]“Ask command.”

He looked away.

Aya returned to the needles. This was why she had stayed.

Jun would live because she was here.

Hundreds of soldiers would survive because she was here.

And every repaired body made it easier for Shinsei to send another formation back into the war.

Aya had known that contradiction for years.

Today it felt heavier.

A clerk entered with a captured-status bulletin.

“Western corridor lost. Jun’s formation withdrew successfully. One hundred-plus personnel captured.”

Jun looked at him.

[[speaker:jun]]“Executed?”

The clerk checked.

“No.”

A pause.

“Wuyue is treating them.”

Jun’s eyes narrowed.

Aya stopped moving for half a second.

[[speaker:aya]]“Treating?”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Same standard as their own?”

“Report says yes.”

Jun stared at the ceiling.

Aya looked down at his chart.

Something small shifted.

Not allegiance.

Not yet.

Expectation.

The war had just become more complicated than the version Shinsei briefings had prepared them to fight.

Aya returned to work.

Jun would stand again.

That was her job.

Whether he should be sent back to the same battlefield was becoming a different question.

## Chapter 390 — Tsubasa Changes the Arithmetic

Tsubasa Kurokawa did not react to Jun’s defeat by becoming angry.

That would have been easier for everyone in the room.

Instead, he asked for three reports.

Losses.

Wuyue deployment composition.

Prisoner treatment.

Haru placed them on the table.

Tsubasa read all three.

Kai Moriyama stood near the window.

Shunto leaned against one support post.

Hana had returned from an internal-security briefing two hours earlier and remained near the rear wall.

Aya was still in Isgard.

Nao, Eirik, Kenji and Jun were all north.

The map looked emptier than Shinsei headquarters was accustomed to.

Tsubasa finished the prisoner report first.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“They treated them.”

Shunto shrugged.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Useful theater.”

Kai looked at him.

[[speaker:kai]]“Or they treat prisoners.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Same effect.”

[[speaker:kai]]“No.”

Shunto smiled.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Still dislike me?”

[[speaker:kai]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Comforting consistency.”

Tsubasa ignored them.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Haru.”

[[speaker:haru]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“How much did Wuyue send?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Confirmed public force approximately sixty-three to sixty-four thousand. Sera with a small private support element outside that number.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Kael?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Roughly six thousand Stonecrown.”

Tsubasa looked up.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Only six.”

“Yes.”

Their pre-war model had assumed Kael would either remain home or move a greater share of Stonecrown if Wuyue committed seriously.

Instead he had done both.

Moved personally.

Left the fortress intact.

Tsubasa’s gaze shifted to another line.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Mo Qingzhao.”

[[speaker:haru]]“Eighteen thousand Imperial personnel. Largest single public block.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“And Jin?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Concord nine thousand. He appears to be directing combined operational planning with Isgard command.”

Kai grunted.

[[speaker:kai]]“Smart.”

Shunto said, “Predictable.”

Haru looked at him.

[[speaker:haru]]“If it was predictable, why did our western corridor fall?”

Shunto’s smile thinned.

Kai looked delighted.

Tsubasa continued reading.

“Jun lost to Kael cleanly?”

[[speaker:haru]]“After a serious fight.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Domain interaction?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Yes. Kael stopped trying to mitigate Breach after contact and used Worldweight to make clean contact progressively harder.”

Tsubasa nodded.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Good adaptation.”

Hana watched him.

Not pride.

Not anger.

Assessment.

This was why Tsubasa remained dangerous.

He did not need enemies to be stupid.

He preferred accurate models.

Tsubasa looked toward Kai.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“You’re going north.”

Kai’s expression did not change.

[[speaker:kai]]“Expected that.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Jun will return, but not immediately. Wuyue now has Sera and Kael at Paragon level in theater, plus the Isgard survivors once they recover.”

[[speaker:kai]]“And me?”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“You stabilize the apex arithmetic.”

Kai walked to the map.

[[speaker:kai]]“Not chase Kael for revenge.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“No.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Good.”

Tsubasa looked at him.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“You once taught me that losing a duel did not make the person who won politically correct.”

Kai’s mouth moved.

[[speaker:kai]]“You remembered.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“I remember most useful lessons.”

[[speaker:kai]]“That one should have been useful more often.”

The room tightened.

Tsubasa did not look away.

Kai continued.

“You’re still treating this war like the conclusion is obvious if the arithmetic works.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Isgard’s material collapse is real.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“The epidemic losses are real.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Their fractured administration is costing lives.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Yes.”

Tsubasa’s voice remained calm.

“Then what exactly do you dispute?”

Kai pointed at the map.

[[speaker:kai]]“The part where being right about their roads makes those roads yours.”

Silence.

Haru looked at Tsubasa.

Hana looked at Kai.

Shunto looked bored.

Tsubasa’s fingers rested on the table edge.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“I am not annexing Isgard for roads.”

[[speaker:kai]]“No.”

Kai’s voice was blunt.

“You’re annexing them because you think your answer is better.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kai]]“At least say it properly.”

Tsubasa held his gaze.

Then nodded once.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Fair.”

That did not end the disagreement.

It made it worse.

Because Tsubasa could still hear criticism.

He simply believed the decision survived it.

Kai looked at the northern marker.

[[speaker:kai]]“When do I leave?”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Tomorrow.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Troops?”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Reinforcement elements follow separately. Your first role is command stabilization and High-Paragon pressure.”

Kai nodded.

Tsubasa turned to Haru.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Wuyue contingency.”

Haru already had the packet ready.

Hana’s expression changed by a fraction.

Good.

The operation had not been invented because Jun lost.

It had been authorized when Wuyue’s departure was confirmed.

Now it was entering execution.

Haru opened the map.

[[speaker:haru]]“Primary objective remains pressure on Sera’s support architecture. Not conquest. Not engagement with Stonecrown’s full fixed army.”

Tsubasa nodded.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“What do we know?”

Haru answered.

“Publicly, the Quaint Teahouse is associated with medicine, intelligence support and Sera’s private personnel. Rhen is unavailable. Sera and all ten public Wuyue rankings are north.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“What do we not know?”

Haru paused.

[[speaker:haru]]“The real depth of the private organization.”

Accelerating the invasion had bought time by spending intelligence.

Tsubasa looked at Hana.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“You go with Haru.”

[[speaker:hana]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Limited objective.”

[[speaker:hana]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“If the target is materially stronger than assessed?”

[[speaker:hana]]“Withdraw.”

Shunto smiled.

[[speaker:shunto]]“How cautious.”

Hana looked at him.

[[speaker:hana]]“How alive.”

Kai laughed once.

Tsubasa did not.

He looked at Haru.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“No heroics.”

Haru’s expression was perfectly neutral.

[[speaker:haru]]“Understood.”

Months later, Aya would hear that sentence again in memory and hate it.

For now, nobody in the room knew what three sealed Redline doses would cost him.

Tsubasa moved Kai’s marker north.

Then Haru and Hana’s markers west.

One war.

Two continents.

The arithmetic had changed.

And Shinsei changed with it.
