## Chapter 448 — Aya Watches Another Physician

Aya Katsuragi did not sleep the first night beneath Sanctuary of Petals.

Not because she could not.

Because she was offended by it.

That was the explanation she gave herself.

She stood at the edge of Sector Three with her sleeves rolled above the elbows and watched an Isgard physician remove old sutures from a wound that no longer needed them. The soldier on the cot had arrived with a collapsed lung, torn intercostal tissue and two channels of qi reinforcement fused badly from emergency healing performed weeks earlier.

By morning, the lung was whole.

The old fusion had been undone and rebuilt.

The soldier was asleep because his body finally believed it was allowed to be.

Aya stared at the clean skin.

[[speaker:luo]]“Offended?” Luo asked beside her.

She did not look at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“You can hear thoughts now?”

[[speaker:luo]]“No. Your face is loud.”

Aya turned.

Luo Wen looked worse than Rhen.

That was not difficult.

Rhen had emerged from six months of seclusion looking as if he had spent an afternoon napping behind the tea counter. Luo looked like a man who had been making decisions about who might die first for half a year.

The difference should have made Aya distrust Rhen.

Instead it made her distrust the universe.

[[speaker:aya]]“I am not offended,” she said.

[[speaker:luo]]“Good.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I am evaluating.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Of course.”

[[speaker:aya]]“He repaired a hidden arterial tear from across the district.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Without seeing the patient.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Your Dominion found it.”

Aya's eyes narrowed.

Luo smiled faintly.

[[speaker:luo]]“That part bothers you more.”

She looked back at the sleeping soldier.

It did.

If Rhen had simply been a greater healer, she could understand that. There was always someone faster, cleaner, stronger. Medicine had never promised equality.

What unsettled her was the way he had used everyone else.

Not as assistants.

As physicians.

Aya's **Hundred Pulse Dominion** identified collapse faster than his broad Sanctuary did at scale, so he had told her to identify collapse.

Luo understood Axtaya poisoning better than any other Wuyue physician, so Rhen had given him that lane.

Isgard surgeons knew their own long-term wound patterns, so he had asked them what had been attempted before.

Shinsei medics knew which stabilizers their prisoners had already received, so he had let them work too.

Nobody had been required to kneel to the miracle before using it.

That irritated Aya in ways she did not enjoy examining.

Luo checked the patient's pulse.

[[speaker:luo]]“Rhen wants you in the central lane.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Why?”

[[speaker:luo]]“He wants to ask you about Redline.”

Aya went still.

Luo noticed.

[[speaker:luo]]“You don't have to enjoy the conversation.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I know.”

[[speaker:luo]]“He is not Tsubasa.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I know.”

[[speaker:luo]]“He is also not me.”

Aya finally looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“What does that mean?”

Luo's gaze dropped to his own hands.

[[speaker:luo]]“That you shouldn't decide what he will say before he says it.”

Aya understood the warning.

Luo had spent months resisting the same instinct with Yun.

She followed him.

The central lane had changed overnight.

Yesterday it had been mud, stretchers and shouted numbers.

Now recovered soldiers were walking out under supervision while fresh patients moved in. Some needed canes only because their balance had not caught up with newly repaired limbs. Others kept touching healed ribs, shoulders, throats, faces.

One man cried because his hand no longer shook.

Another sat in complete silence staring at fingers that had been numb since the first month of the invasion.

Rhen stood beneath the open sky.

The Sanctuary still covered the horizon.

Frost-white petals drifted through it in impossible numbers, each carrying that faint pale-violet radiance.

He was eating an apple.

Aya stopped.

Luo did not.

[[speaker:luo]]“You're late,” he said.

Rhen looked at Aya.

[[speaker:rhen]]“She was working.”

Luo's mouth tightened.

[[speaker:luo]]“Do not encourage her.”

Aya folded her arms.

Rhen finished the apple.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Your Dominion is very good.”

Aya had prepared herself for many things.

That was not one of them.

She stared.

Rhen looked mildly concerned.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Was that rude?”

Luo walked away.

[[speaker:luo]]“I'm leaving.”

Aya watched him go.

Then looked back at Rhen.

[[speaker:aya]]“You're serious.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You can feel sixty thousand damaged circulations under this field.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“More than that now.”

[[speaker:aya]]“And you're telling me my perception art is good.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Why?”

Rhen blinked.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Because it is.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You are extremely irritating.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Sera says that.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I understand her better already.”

Neither did he.

[[speaker:aya]]“What part?”

[[speaker:aya]]“The beginning was not three-times Redline.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I know.”

Not defensive.

Precise.

Breath.

[[speaker:aya]]“Not from me.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“No.”

It did not come.

The shift wrong-footed her.

[[speaker:aya]]“Paragons?”

[[speaker:aya]]“That is convenient.”

Rhen looked toward the nearest hospital row.

[[speaker:rhen]]“You treated people because they were in front of you.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Even when you hated why they were hurt.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“That is what Luo does.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“That is what Yun did too,” Rhen said.

[[speaker:rhen]]“You knew her?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Her work?”

Not yet.

Pulse slowed.

A fourth redirected the collapsing channel.

**Borrowed Beat.**

Aya linked her controlled rhythm into the failing circulation just long enough to stop the body from chasing its own instability.

The tear sealed.

The stimulant residue purged.

Heart rhythm normalized.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Very good.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You make it sound simple.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Not easy.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“No.”

A different threshold.

Something he had never promised anyone.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Not this,” he said.

[[speaker:aya]]Then Aya said, “I started writing the numbers into every Redline record.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Command objected.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I heard that too.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I am telling the soldiers now.”

She almost smiled back.

Almost.

Then the Sanctuary marked three more critical patients.

Aya turned.

Rhen turned with her.

They went back to work.

By the end of the second day, Aya stopped watching Rhen as if he were a medical phenomenon.

She started using him like another physician.

That was exactly what he wanted.

## Chapter 449 — Isgard Stands Again

For three days, Isgard talked about the same person.

Not Rhen.

The Petals Monarch.

The name moved through mess lines, bridge crews, hospital queues and night watches with the peculiar certainty reserved for things people had once believed were already history.

Older soldiers remembered the previous Wuyue war directly.

Some had fought beneath banners that opposed him.

Some had carried wounded from the final battlefield after Sera's Domain fell and the two Paragons facing her discovered that the ranking system had no useful place to put the man who stepped in next.

Younger soldiers knew the same scene from veterans, academy lectures and arguments around winter fires.

Details changed depending on who told it.

The ending never did.

Frozen petals.

One Paragon punched through.

Another broken by a Transcended art.

The war stopped.

One old Isgard shieldman answered a younger lancer's question by telling him the stories had made the Petals Monarch smaller, not larger.

That answer traveled almost as quickly as the title itself.

Five years earlier, many Isgard soldiers had said Petals Monarch with anger.

Now they said it while waking beneath repaired roofs, testing limbs that should still have been broken and watching their own Paragons prepare to stand again.

History had not become friendship.

It had become context.

The man who ended Isgard's last war had returned to Isgard by healing it.

Then Aldric Veyrhald woke angry.

This reassured everyone.

## Chapter 450 — Rhen Does Not Take Command

Sera was right.

It was not easy.

Wuyue occupied one side of the long Isgard table.

Isgard occupied the other.
