## Chapter 368 — Fourteen Days

The first Shinsei sail appeared at dawn on Day Fourteen.

Brynja Kharvorn saw it before the lookout bell.

Not because her eyes were better.

Because she had spent the last thirteen days staring at an empty horizon until emptiness itself had become suspicious.

The sail was small at first.

A pale cut beneath the cloud line.

Then another.

Then five.

Then enough that counting stopped being useful.

Brynja lowered the viewing glass.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Ring it.”

The lookout beside her did not move.

He was young.

Too young to remember Isgard before the epidemic.

Old enough to remember carrying his mother's body to a winter trench.

“Commander?”

[[speaker:brynja]]“The bell.”

He blinked.

Then moved.

Iron thunder rolled across the coastal fortress.

One strike.

Two.

Three.

**Fleet.**

Below the wall, people who had spent two weeks pretending preparation could postpone fear stopped pretending.

Carts turned inland.

Medicine chests were loaded.

Children disappeared into cellar routes.

Infantry companies moved toward prepared terraces.

Ox teams pulled the last mobile engines out of the lower yard.

There were fewer ox teams than the fortress plans assumed.

Of course there were.

The old plans had been written before the epidemic.

Before one valley lost half its haulers.

Before smith families became one old master and two apprentices trying to maintain equipment meant for six workshops.

Before grain collection required soldiers because there were not enough civilian hands to move sacks before snow.

Isgard still had people who could split boulders.

It had fewer people who could repair a wheel.

That difference had never looked more dangerous than it did while Shinsei sails multiplied across the sea.

Brynja pointed toward the lower road.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Move the second engine now.”

An officer said, “The draft team is still coming.”

[[speaker:brynja]]“Use infantry.”

“That will exhaust them before—”

[[speaker:brynja]]“Use infantry.”

He ran.

Behind Brynja, another officer unfolded the coastal order from Solveig.

It had arrived the night before.

No heroic language.

No promises.

**Do not defend every beach. Deny the ports that matter. Preserve the roads that feed the interior. If a position becomes a grave, leave before it earns the name.**

Brynja had approved.

Mostly.

She intended to make one beach extremely inconvenient first.

The fleet came closer.

Now the sails showed a symbol.

A broken circle joined by one horizontal line.

Shinsei.

The mark meant unity to the people who carried it.

Brynja had read Yun Shizhen's report twice.

To her it currently meant twenty-five thousand trained invaders and an unknown number of things stronger than common sense.

A runner climbed the stairs too fast.

“Western tower confirms additional hulls.”

[[speaker:brynja]]“How many?”

“Twenty-two visible. More behind fog.”

[[speaker:brynja]]“Landing craft?”

“Mixed.”

[[speaker:brynja]]“Paragon signs?”

The runner swallowed.

“None confirmed.”

Brynja looked back at the sea.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Then stop looking for fireworks.”

“Commander?”

[[speaker:brynja]]“A Paragon who wants to arrive unnoticed will not announce himself because our report form has a box.”

The runner nodded.

Good boy.

Terrible day to learn.

At the inland command post, Solveig Skeldran received the first bell relay twelve minutes later.

She was already standing.

She had slept in armor.

Again.

Varok sat across from her with one boot unlaced and a bowl of porridge he had not touched.

Astrid Vardrenn had three forest maps spread across the floor.

Halvek Veyrhald stood near the main road board.

Alive.

For now.

None of them knew which names would be missing from the room by the end of the month.

Solveig read Brynja's first count.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Begin inland dispersal.”

Halvek said, “Already moving.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Primary roads?”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Open.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Secondary?”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Two washed out. Astrid has alternatives.”

Astrid pointed at the floor without looking up.

[[speaker:astrid]]“Not alternatives. Goat paths with ambition.”

Varok tied his boot.

[[speaker:varok]]“Can cavalry use them?”

[[speaker:astrid]]“No.”

[[speaker:varok]]“Can wounded?”

[[speaker:astrid]]“If they enjoy regretting birth.”

Solveig looked at her.

Astrid sighed.

[[speaker:astrid]]“Yes. Slowly.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Then mark them.”

The room moved.

Not panic.

Work.

A second message arrived.

Then a third.

The fleet had split.

Good.

Bad.

Both.

Shinsei was testing more than one landing point.

Solveig moved counters.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Brynja holds the main western port. Do not reinforce every false landing.”

Varok said, “I can take mobile reserve south.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“You take center until we know which landing is real.”

[[speaker:varok]]“I hate waiting.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“I know.”

[[speaker:varok]]“That was not agreement.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“It was sympathy.”

Halvek almost smiled.

The door opened again.

This messenger wore Wuyue colors on one sleeve.

Pact liaison.

Solveig's head turned.

[[speaker:solveig]]“From south?”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Army?”

The messenger's face answered before his mouth.

“Still assembling.”

Nobody cursed.

That would have wasted time.

“What has moved?” Solveig asked.

“Medicine. Preserved food. Surgical supplies. Two fast naval escorts. Wuyue says the main expedition is not ready to sail without becoming a supply failure halfway north.”

Varok muttered something impolite.

Solveig ignored him.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Sera?”

“Still in Wuyue.”

That hurt more.

Not because Solveig blamed her.

Because everyone in Isgard knew what Sera could do if she were standing on this coast.

The messenger continued quickly.

“She says the pact is active. They are coming.”

Solveig looked at the map.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Tell her to arrive with an army that can still eat when it gets here.”

The messenger blinked.

“Those exact words?”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Yes.”

Varok said, “Add something inspiring.”

Solveig looked at him.

He reconsidered.

[[speaker:varok]]“Never mind.”

The messenger left.

At sea, the first Shinsei transport lowered boats.

Brynja saw them spread.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Range?”

“Not yet.”

She waited.

The hardest part of coastal defence was accepting that the enemy could be seen long before he could be killed.

Closer.

Closer.

The landing boats passed the outer rock marker.

Brynja raised one hand.

Every engine crew on the wall watched it.

She dropped the hand.

Isgard's coast answered.

Heavy bolts crossed the grey morning.

Stone shot followed.

One landing craft split.

Another turned.

A third kept coming.

Then a man standing in the prow raised a shield.

Not dramatically.

Just enough.

A siege bolt struck it.

The boat should have broken around him.

Instead the man's body lowered, shoulder settling, rear foot bracing against the deck as if he were standing on stone instead of moving water.

The force passed through shield rim.

Arm.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Leg.

Into the hull.

The boat dipped.

Did not break.

Brynja's expression changed.

“Paragon?” the lookout whispered.

She watched the man lower the shield.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Maybe.”

The figure turned toward the other boats and made one short hand signal.

They changed formation immediately.

Not merely troops obeying a strong fighter.

Troops obeying a commander they trusted.

Brynja felt something colder than fear.

Recognition without a name.

[[speaker:brynja]]“Send the shieldwork description inland.”

The lookout looked at her.

“Why?”

[[speaker:brynja]]“Because somebody older than me may hate it for a reason.”

The first Shinsei boots touched Isgard before noon.

Fourteen days after Yun died.

Wuyue was moving.

Isgard was fighting.

And the man with the old shield stepped onto the shore of the country that had spent twenty years believing him dead.

## Chapter 369 — Five Paragons Step Ashore

Shinsei's first landing did not look like an army of monsters.

That made it worse.

They formed ranks.

Counted boats.

Carried wounded inland.

Established latrine trenches before half the senior officers had disembarked.

Assigned water points.

Marked civilian structures that were not to be burned without command authorization.

Twenty-five thousand highly trained troops behaved like people who expected to live in the place after winning it.

Isgard understood the insult immediately.

The five Paragons arrived separately.

Not because they wanted dramatic entrances.

Because they had jobs.

Aya Katsuragi stepped ashore beside the second medical ship.

She did not look at the battlefield first.

She looked at the wounded.

[[speaker:aya]]“Who was struck by the coastal bolt?”

A medic raised one hand.

“Third boat. Four crushed ribs, one open fracture, two concussions.”

“Redline?”

“None.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Keep it that way.”

Aya knelt beside a soldier coughing blood.

Seven fine needles appeared between her fingers.

No speech.

No aura display.

She pressed two into the chest wall, one near the shoulder, another at the lower rib, then guided the soldier's breathing until the panic stopped eating what little air he had.

A captain approached.

“Lady Aya, command asks whether the medical line can move closer to—”

“No.”

He stopped.

[[speaker:aya]]“The wounded come here. I do not move the hospital into siege range because a map wants symmetry.”

“Yes, Lady Aya.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Also stop calling me Lady in a treatment tent.”

“Yes, Lady—”

He left before finishing.

Aya closed her eyes for one second.

Then returned to the patient.

Two beaches south, Kenji Narukami jumped from a boat before it finished grounding.

The landing officer shouted after him.

Kenji landed in ankle-deep water.

Thunder Qi snapped around his calves.

He turned.

[[speaker:kenji]]“What?”

“The ramp!”

[[speaker:kenji]]“I have legs.”

“You also have regulations!”

Kenji looked genuinely offended.

[[speaker:kenji]]“Why?”

A soldier laughed despite himself.

Kenji grinned.

Then he saw the first Isgard body near the dune.

The grin faded.

He had grown up on stories where Shinsei arrived and wars ended.

Roads became safe.

Clans stopped burning villages over old banners.

The Thousand-Step Trial had made him famous because he crossed a mountain disaster again and again until people stopped dying where he could reach them.

He liked movement because movement meant arriving in time.

The body in the sand had not been arrived at in time.

Kenji looked away.

“Form on the ridge,” he said.

No joke this time.

Jun Kajihara disembarked at the main western landing with his halberd balanced against one shoulder.

His troops had already begun measuring the first Isgard fortification line.

One officer said, “Stone face. Reinforced gates. Layered stakes.”

Jun looked once.

[[speaker:jun]]“Do not waste men proving the wall is thick.”

The officer waited.

[[speaker:jun]]“Find where the road makes them defend it.”

Jun's reputation had been built at Thirteen Gates.

A siege expected to last months.

Three days.

Thirteen sequential breaches.

No sack afterward.

The people inside had fought because commanders ordered them to.

Jun had seen no virtue in punishing civilians for surviving the wrong flag.

He studied Isgard's road network with the same expression now.

Not hatred.

Problem.

That distinction would matter later.

Nao Shibasaki came ashore last among the lower three.

She paused at the waterline.

The sea pulled around her boots.

A young Shinsei soldier stood nearby gripping his spear too tightly.

Nao noticed.

[[speaker:nao]]“First campaign?”

He stared straight ahead.

“No, Lady Nao.”

[[speaker:nao]]“You are lying to somebody who grew up running a bathhouse.”

The soldier blinked.

Nao looked at his hands.

[[speaker:nao]]“People only hold things like that when they are afraid the object may escape.”

His grip loosened by instinct.

She smiled faintly.

[[speaker:nao]]“Better.”

He swallowed.

“Are we going to win?”

Nao looked toward the Isgard hills.

[[speaker:nao]]“Ask me something I can know.”

The soldier hesitated.

“Will you be here?”

[[speaker:nao]]“Yes.”

That answer steadied him more than a lie would have.

Nao moved inland.

Years earlier she had stood between two Shinsei forces carrying contradictory orders and activated Stillwater Dominion until neither side could sustain escalation long enough to begin a civil battle.

The Two Flags Incident had become a story about restraint.

Now she walked inside an invasion.

The contradiction was not lost on her.

She simply had not yet decided what to do with it.

The fifth Paragon had already been ashore for hours.

Eirik Voss stood over the western command map with the same shield from the first landing resting beside him.

Isgard steel.

Old pattern.

Repaired many times.

Shinsei officers had offered to replace it.

He had declined.

A commander did not discard a shield because the paint had become politically inconvenient.

Eirik traced Halvek's road system with one finger.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Do not push center yet.”

Jun looked at him.

[[speaker:jun]]“The road is the center.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“The road is what Halvek wants us to believe the center is.”

Jun considered.

Eirik moved his finger east.

[[speaker:eirik]]“He will trade ground for movement. If we chase the ground, he keeps the movement.”

Nao entered.

[[speaker:nao]]“Civilians?”

Eirik pointed at three valleys.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Likely clearing inland.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Orders?”

[[speaker:eirik]]“No targeting evacuation columns unless they contain active military formations using them as cover.”

Jun nodded.

Kenji arrived a moment later wet to the knee.

Eirik looked at him.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Ramp.”

Kenji stared.

[[speaker:kenji]]“How did you know?”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Your officer complained before you arrived.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Betrayal.”

Nao said, “Regulation.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Worse.”

For one moment they looked almost ordinary.

Then Aya entered carrying a casualty sheet.

[[speaker:aya]]“Coastal batteries killed forty-three before landing. Another seventy-one injured.”

Eirik took the sheet.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Can they move?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Some.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Redline?”

Aya's eyes sharpened.

[[speaker:aya]]“Not for a landing we already secured.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“I did not ask for it.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Good.”

Eirik handed the sheet back.

[[speaker:eirik]]“What do you need?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Clean water. More cloth. Less command curiosity inside my hospital.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Done.”

Aya left.

Kenji watched her go.

[[speaker:kenji]]“She likes you.”

Eirik looked at him.

“No.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Respect?”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Possibly.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Same thing at your age.”

Nao covered a smile.

Jun looked as if considering whether a halberd counted as disciplinary procedure.

Eirik ignored them.

He looked north.

Five Paragons.

Twenty-five thousand troops.

Enough to break a continent already missing too much of the ordinary machinery that kept armies alive.

Not enough to guarantee anything.

Tsubasa had chosen speed over perfect preparation.

Now the people on the shore had to make that choice successful.

Eirik touched the old shield.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Move before evening.”

Jun nodded.

Nao's expression tightened.

Kenji rolled his shoulders.

The first real inland advance began.

Isgard watchers reported five Paragon-grade presences.

No Domain explanations.

No convenient biographies.

Only names gathered from captured signals and banners.

Aya Katsuragi.

Kenji Narukami.

Jun Kajihara.

Nao Shibasaki.

And one commander whose name had not yet been confirmed.

The oldest Isgard officers did not need the name.

They had started recognizing the way he held his shield.

## Chapter 370 — Isgard Recognizes Its Dead King

The first person to say Eirik Voss's name was seventy-two years old.

He said it like a curse.

Then like a prayer.

Then as if neither was safe.

“Voss.”

The command tent went silent.

Solveig looked at the old officer.

Captain Orven had served under three governments and complained about all of them with equal commitment.

He had been young when Eirik disappeared.

Not young enough to forget.

“What did you say?” Solveig asked.

Orven stared at the shieldwork report from Brynja's coast.

“Read the brace again.”

A clerk did.

“Impact received at shield rim. Force distributed through shoulder, hip and rear leg rather than direct arm absorption. Commander remained planted despite unstable deck.”

Orven's face had gone pale.

Varok said, “That is a defensive method.”

“No.”

Orven looked at him.

“That is **his** defensive method.”

Solveig felt the room change.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Eirik Voss is dead.”

Orven laughed once.

It contained no humor.

“So we believed.”

Another old officer stepped closer.

“What else?”

The clerk read.

“Short battle signal. Three downward fingers, one lateral sweep. Landing boats shifted staggered shield coverage immediately.”

The second officer sat down.

Varok looked between them.

[[speaker:varok]]“Explain.”

Orven closed his eyes.

“That was the old King's Guard landing order.”

Nobody used the title **king** officially anymore.

Not the way they once had.

That did not stop the word from entering the room like a ghost.

Eirik Voss had once ruled enough of Isgard that people who loved him called him king and people who hated him used worse words.

He had standardized taxes across regions that previously stole from each other at border posts.

Forced rival clans to share road maintenance.

Built common courts.

Created winter-grain redistribution.

Tried to limit private martial wars.

Many ordinary people had loved him for it.

Many powerful houses had not.

Then assassination attempts came.

Retaliation came.

Two sides prepared to fight a civil war **for him**.

Eirik disappeared.

Evidence suggested death.

The war that might have carried his name never fully ignited.

People built twenty years on the belief that removing himself had been his final act.

Solveig looked at the coast marker.

[[speaker:solveig]]“If he lived, why Shinsei?”

Orven answered quietly.

“Maybe because they built what he failed to.”

Nobody liked that answer.

It sounded too possible.

A messenger entered at speed.

“Contact at the western inland road.”

Solveig stood.

[[speaker:solveig]]“Who?”

“Shinsei command delegation under flag.”

[[speaker:solveig]]“Terms?”

“They requested Aldric.”

The room went colder.

Aldric Veyrhald was already on his way.

Of course he was.

Nobody had successfully convinced Aldric to remain behind a map because an enemy requested him by name.

The meeting took place on a broad frozen field between two advance lines.

No hidden archers.

No ceremonial furniture.

Two banners.

Isgard's iron-grey.

Shinsei's broken circle.

Aldric walked out with spear in hand.

Not leveled.

Carried.

Maedra stood twenty paces behind with Crimson Road pressure held close to her skin.

Sigrun remained farther back near reserve troops.

She was new to Paragon.

Nobody had yet tested what that meant against Shinsei.

Across the field, a man approached with sword and shield.

Older than the portraits.

Broader through the shoulders.

Grey at the temples.

Alive.

Aldric stopped.

For a moment all cultivation language became irrelevant.

No High Paragon.

No Established Paragon.

No Dominion.

Just a dead man walking toward his old country beneath a foreign flag.

Aldric said his name.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Eirik.”

The other man stopped.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Aldric.”

Behind the Isgard line, somebody began crying.

Not everyone had hated Eirik.

That was the problem.

An elderly quartermaster near the rear whispered, “He opened the east winter road.”

A younger soldier looked at him.

“What?”

“Before him, two clans charged tolls on the same bridge.”

Another older woman said, “My village ate because of his grain law.”

A third voice, harsher:

“My uncle disappeared during one of his emergency levies.”

History refused to simplify itself for the convenience of battle.

Eirik heard some of it.

His face did not change.

Aldric's did.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You chose Shinsei.”

Eirik looked toward the broken-circle banner.

“Yes.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Why?”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Because I spent half my life trying to make Isgard stop bleeding through the cracks between its own authorities.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“And so you came back with foreigners.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“With a system that ended the same disease in Shinrin.”

Aldric's grip tightened on the spear.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You think occupation is medicine.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“No.”

Eirik's answer was immediate.

[[speaker:eirik]]“I think disorder can kill more people than occupation if everyone involved is too proud to admit the arithmetic.”

Maedra's eyes hardened.

Aldric said, “Then you learned the wrong lesson from surviving us.”

Eirik's face finally shifted.

Pain.

Small.

Real.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Look at Isgard.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“I am.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Look at the farms.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“I have.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“The mines.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“The medicine routes.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“The epidemic emptied valleys you still expect soldiers to defend.”

Aldric stepped forward one pace.

[[speaker:aldric]]“And you saw that weakness and brought an army.”

Eirik did not deny it.

“No.”

That surprised people.

He continued.

[[speaker:eirik]]“I saw a country I once failed to stabilize breaking under problems it cannot solve with stronger champions.”

Aldric's eyes became very cold.

[[speaker:aldric]]“So you will decide for us.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“If the alternative is another generation dying because every region believes sovereignty means refusing coordination—”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Stop.”

The word crossed the field.

Aldric lowered the butt of his spear into the frozen ground.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You left.”

Eirik went still.

“You left because people were going to kill each other in your name.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“And now you return with twenty-five thousand soldiers to explain that our right to choose became inconvenient.”

Eirik said nothing.

Aldric continued.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You may be right about every road.”

A pause.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Every tax.”

Another.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Every shortage.”

The wind moved between them.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You may even be right that our government is worse at some things than yours.”

Eirik's eyes remained on him.

Aldric's voice hardened.

[[speaker:aldric]]“That does not make Isgard yours.”

No cheer followed.

Good.

This was not theatre.

Eirik looked past Aldric toward the grey banners.

Toward old soldiers who remembered him.

Toward young ones who only knew stories.

“Submit the ports to joint Shinsei administration,” he said. “Open military inventory. Accept unified coastal command. No reprisals against local officers who comply. Existing civil councils remain provisionally in place.”

Aldric almost smiled.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Provisionally.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“There it is.”

Eirik's jaw tightened.

[[speaker:eirik]]“I am offering a route that does not require these fields to fill with bodies.”

Aldric lifted his spear.

Not attacking.

Answering.

[[speaker:aldric]]“So am I.”

Eirik looked at the spear point.

Then at Aldric.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Wuyue will come.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“You trust them after the last war?”

[[speaker:aldric]]“I trust what they did after winning.”

That landed.

Wuyue had withdrawn.

No annexation.

No permanent occupation government.

No attempt to turn military victory into ownership.

Eirik understood why Aldric chose that comparison.

His expression became sad.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Tsubasa believes Wuyue will commit cautiously.”

Aldric said, “Then Tsubasa does not know Sera.”

For the first time, Eirik almost smiled.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Perhaps.”

He lifted his shield.

Not into guard.

Yet.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Last chance.”

Aldric's spear settled into both hands.

“No.”

One syllable.

The thing Tsubasa's system had become increasingly bad at hearing.

Eirik closed his eyes briefly.

When he opened them, the old ruler was still there.

So was the Shinsei commander.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Then we meet tomorrow.”

Aldric nodded.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Tomorrow.”

They turned away from each other.

Only after Eirik returned beneath the broken-circle banner did an Isgard intelligence officer finally confirm the Shinsei seat attached to his name.

**Eirik Voss.**

**Shinsei No.5.**

**High Paragon.**

The dead king had come home stronger than he had ever been alive.

And tomorrow, Aldric Veyrhald intended to tell him no with a spear.
