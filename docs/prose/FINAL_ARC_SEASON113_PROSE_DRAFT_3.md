# Final Arc — Season 113 Prose Draft, Part III

> Completes **Season 113 — Monarch's Winter**.

## Chapter 488 — Winter Essence

Tsubasa attacked without qi.

That sentence should have made him ordinary.

It did not.

The black crescent was still a heavy weapon built for a man who had trained with it for years.

The chain was still real.

His stance was still refined enough that even without reinforcement, every step placed weight where it belonged.

The first swing came low.

Rhen stepped over it.

Tsubasa shortened the chain by hand and reversed the weapon on a tighter arc.

Rhen leaned back.

The crescent passed beneath his chin.

No Domain.

No 3× throughput.

No Supreme force stored for the next strike.

Just technique.

Rhen respected it enough not to smile.

Tsubasa pulled the chain again.

Winter Essence descended between them.

The first particles settled on the crescent's reinforced outer rim.

Tsubasa felt the difference immediately.

The weapon's physical integrity had survived Sera's Ultimate, repaired links, six months of war and a full 3× sequence.

Now microscopic frost-qi entered old stress lines.

The metal whitened.

A crack appeared near the inner crescent.

Tsubasa changed grip and struck anyway.

The crack widened.

A second impact would break it.

Rhen did not provide one.

Winter Essence drifted across Tsubasa's remaining armor instead.

He had already lost qi reinforcement when his reserve entered the petal above him.

Now the physical plates themselves began failing.

A shoulder guard split at the seam.

Chainmail rings turned brittle and snapped when he twisted.

The reinforced bracer around his weapon arm fractured in three places.

Tsubasa looked down once.

Then tore the damaged armor off himself.

Rhen watched.

“That was expensive.”

Tsubasa gave him a flat look.

“Are you worried about my equipment?”

“A little.”

“You destroyed it.”

“Yes.”

“That makes the concern difficult to appreciate.”

Rhen considered.

“Fair.”

Tsubasa moved again.

Faster than an ordinary man his age.

Slower than a Paragon.

Still precise.

He swung the chain horizontally.

Rhen caught it.

This time there was no qi behind the pull.

Only Tsubasa's body.

Rhen could have ended the fight there.

Instead he let go.

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

“You keep allowing me another attack.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Rhen looked at him.

“You're still choosing one.”

Tsubasa's jaw tightened.

He changed stance.

The crescent lifted.

Rhen looked at the Winter Essence circling around the dense petal above Tsubasa.

Then he made a different choice.

The glittering frost changed direction.

Not toward Tsubasa's mind.

Not toward his thoughts.

Toward his remaining motor circulation.

Rhen spoke quietly.

**Winter Essence: Calamity.**

Tsubasa felt the captured qi return.

Only a fraction.

Not enough to restore his cultivation.

Not enough to reopen his Domain.

Enough to enter the motor meridians driving his current attack.

His mind remained completely his own.

He still intended to cut Rhen.

His body did not complete the motion the way he intended.

The shoulder rotated half a degree too far.

The wrist turned inward.

The chain tightened across his own stance.

The crescent that should have passed outside his left side crossed back toward him instead.

Tsubasa reacted on instinct.

He released the weapon before it could cut deep.

The blade scraped across his remaining chest plate and tore it open.

His rear foot planted to recover.

Calamity routed the next pulse of motor qi through the wrong physical line.

His own stance folded beneath him.

One knee hit stone.

Tsubasa looked up sharply.

He understood at once.

“You're not controlling my thoughts.”

“No.”

“My movement.”

“Your motor circulation.”

Tsubasa's expression darkened.

“Using my own qi.”

“A little of it.”

“The qi you took.”

“Yes.”

Tsubasa laughed once.

There was no humor in it.

“That is unpleasant.”

“I know.”

“You can make me strike myself.”

“Yes.”

“Break my own body.”

“Yes.”

“Kill myself.”

Rhen's face went still.

“If I chose that severity.”

Tsubasa held his gaze.

“You won't.”

“No.”

“Why?”

Rhen looked across the field.

Fifty thousand soldiers slept under Quiet Snow.

“No reason to.”

Tsubasa stared at him for a long moment.

Then reached for the chain again.

Rhen sighed.

“You are very committed to this.”

“You healed me to full strength.”

“Yes.”

“You let me take Redline.”

“Yes.”

“You let me use everything.”

“Yes.”

“And now you're surprised I won't stop because you removed it?”

Rhen thought about that.

“No.”

Tsubasa wrapped the chain around his forearm manually.

Calamity remained waiting inside the small amount of motor qi Rhen had routed back.

Tsubasa changed how he moved.

Shorter actions.

Less circulation.

More pure muscle.

He denied Rhen as much of the returning motor pathway as he could.

Rhen's eyes sharpened.

Even now, Tsubasa adapted.

That was the part of him Rhen respected most.

Tsubasa picked up the damaged crescent.

The weapon was cracked.

His armor was failing.

His cultivation reserve floated above him in one petal.

His army slept.

His Domain was gone.

His Ultimate was gone.

He took one breath.

Then another.

And stood.

Across the field, Sera watched him rise.

She did not mock him.

Neither did Kael.

Neither did Jin.

Nobody who had fought at the top of the martial world mistook refusal to collapse for stupidity in that moment.

The strategic war was over.

Tsubasa simply had not accepted the sentence yet.

He lifted the damaged crescent.

Rhen looked at him.

“Last one?”

Tsubasa settled into stance.

“No promises.”

Rhen almost smiled.

Then Tsubasa charged.

## Chapter 489 — Tsubasa Without Heaven

Without cultivation, Tsubasa Kurokawa was still dangerous.

That mattered.

It mattered to him.

It mattered to Rhen.

It mattered to every soldier watching from the allied ridge who had spent years using qi as if it were synonymous with martial skill.

Tsubasa crossed the distance through ordinary movement.

No qinggong.

No tether pressure.

No chained battlefield geometry.

He used footwork.

Real footwork.

Decades of it.

The damaged crescent came from his right.

Rhen moved left.

Tsubasa had expected that.

The chain wrapped around his own forearm shortened the weapon's return path.

The second cut came immediately.

Rhen raised one wrist.

Metal struck skin.

The cracked crescent broke.

Half the blade spun away.

Tsubasa did not pause.

He used the remaining inner curve like a short hooked weapon and drove it toward Rhen's ribs.

Rhen caught his wrist.

Tsubasa headbutted him.

The impact landed.

Rhen blinked.

Sera's mouth opened.

Kael made a sound somewhere between a laugh and disbelief.

Tsubasa tried to use the surprise to pivot behind Rhen.

Rhen still held his wrist.

The pivot stopped.

Tsubasa drove a knee upward.

Rhen turned his hip.

The knee slid past.

Tsubasa elbowed.

Rhen blocked.

For several seconds, the strongest conventional cultivator of the era fought without any of the cultivation that had made the title possible.

And he was still excellent.

Rhen let him be.

Not out of pity.

Because this was the cleanest part of the entire fight.

No army.

No drug.

No Domain.

No Ultimate.

No excuse.

Two trained bodies at arm's length.

Tsubasa's broken weapon dropped.

He switched to empty hand.

Palm.

Elbow.

Shoulder.

A low sweep.

Rhen stepped over it.

Tsubasa caught the edge of Rhen's coat and tried to pull him into a knee.

Rhen allowed the coat to tear rather than resist the fabric.

Tsubasa's knee missed.

“Another coat,” Sera muttered.

Jin glanced at her.

“How many has he lost?”

“Too many.”

“Is that a strategic concern?”

“Yes.”

Jin looked back at the fight.

“I'll add it to reconstruction.”

Sera smiled.

At the center, Tsubasa attacked again.

Rhen finally answered physically.

One palm to the chest.

Gentle compared with what he could do.

Not gentle by ordinary standards.

Tsubasa flew backward.

He hit the ground, rolled, and came up to one knee.

Blood left his mouth.

Not from old injuries.

Rhen had healed those.

This damage belonged entirely to Rhen.

That distinction was important.

Tsubasa wiped his mouth.

“You were holding back.”

“Yes.”

“How much?”

Rhen looked uncomfortable.

“That question never helps.”

Tsubasa laughed.

Then stood again.

His legs shook.

Not fear.

Physical exhaustion.

Without qi reinforcement, even a Peak Paragon's body was still a body.

Redline had already begun its physical crash, although the absence of usable reserve left the drug with very little martial throughput to magnify.

His heart beat too fast.

Muscles trembled.

Future vitality had already begun to be spent during the seconds before Rhen captured his reserve.

Those seconds would not be refunded.

Tsubasa knew it.

He still stepped forward.

Rhen looked at him.

“You proved it.”

Tsubasa stopped.

“What?”

“That you're strong.”

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

“I did not come here for your approval.”

“I know.”

“Then don't offer it.”

Rhen nodded.

“Fine.”

Tsubasa took another step.

Rhen sighed.

“You're difficult.”

“Your wife said the same thing about you.”

Rhen paused.

Sera shouted from the ridge:

“I HEARD THAT.”

Tsubasa looked toward her.

That was the mistake.

Rhen stepped in.

One hand touched Tsubasa's shoulder.

The other caught his elbow.

A tiny turn of hips.

No named art.

No Transcended technique.

No Supreme technique.

Tsubasa's balance disappeared.

He hit the ground flat on his back.

The breath left him.

Rhen put one foot lightly on the chain beside his hand.

Not on Tsubasa.

Never on Tsubasa.

The message was enough.

Tsubasa looked up at the pale-violet petals overhead.

Then at the sleeping army.

Then at Rhen.

For the first time, he stopped trying to stand.

Rhen waited.

Tsubasa's breathing slowed.

The Redline crash began settling through him.

“You healed me,” Tsubasa said.

“Yes.”

“You let me take the drug.”

“Yes.”

“You let me use the Dominion.”

“Yes.”

“The Ultimate.”

“Yes.”

“The army.”

“Yes.”

“And then you took the one thing all of it required.”

“My turn.”

Tsubasa laughed weakly.

“That's almost arrogant.”

Rhen considered.

“Sorry.”

“You're not.”

“No.”

Tsubasa closed his eyes.

Not unconscious yet.

Just tired.

Rhen crouched beside him.

“Don't sleep from the drug.”

Tsubasa opened one eye.

“You put fifty thousand people to sleep.”

“Different sleep.”

“Of course it is.”

Rhen placed two fingers against his wrist.

He did not restore the spent lifespan.

He did not return the captured qi yet.

He simply stabilized the immediate physical crash enough that Tsubasa would not die while the battlefield decided what came next.

Tsubasa felt the correction.

“You're healing me again.”

“Yes.”

“This is becoming insulting.”

“Sorry.”

“You're still not.”

“No.”

Tsubasa's mouth moved faintly.

Then his eyes closed properly.

Alive.

Stable.

Defeated.

The strongest conventional cultivator beneath heaven had fallen at full health, after 3× Redline, with his complete Domain, Ultimate, Supreme passive and fifty-thousand-person spearhead available to him.

Nobody could blame Sera's wounds.

Rhen had erased them first.

Nobody could blame an incomplete reserve.

Rhen had restored it first.

Nobody could say Tsubasa had been denied his apex.

Rhen had waited for it.

The comparison had been clean.

That was why the silence afterward felt so final.

Then the outer reserve started moving.

Eight Paragon signatures crossed the distant lines.

Not all of them with the same intention.

Rhen stood.

Sera saw them too.

“Aya isn't attacking.”

“No.”

“Hana?”

“No.”

“Nao?”

Rhen watched the remaining signatures.

“No.”

Sera's eyes narrowed.

“Haru?”

Rhen waited.

One signature remained still.

“No.”

Four moved forward.

Kai.

Eirik.

Kenji.

Jun.

Each carried a sealed vial.

The war had one argument left.

## Chapter 490 — The Last Argument

The outer reserve had been more than fifty miles away when **Monarch's Winter Law** opened.

That was why the count had remained exact.

Fifty thousand inner elites.

One Tsubasa.

Fifty thousand and one petals.

Now the central pressure had ended.

The sleeping spearhead lay across the field.

Tsubasa was down but alive.

Rhen no longer used Monarch's Winter Law as the answer to what approached from beyond the engagement envelope.

The remaining Shinsei Paragons crossed toward the center.

Eight had been deployed outside.

Four came to fight.

Four did not.

Kai Moriyama broke his seal first.

The High Paragon looked at Tsubasa on the ground.

Then at Rhen.

Then drank.

3× Redline entered **Crimson Crucible Dominion** circulation before the drug had fully settled.

Heat began building around him.

Eirik Voss looked toward the Isgard banners on the allied ridge.

His face tightened.

He had once ruled the land behind them.

Now he stood in Shinsei armor preparing to force one more exchange after its invasion had already failed.

He knew what that looked like.

He took the vial anyway.

Kenji Narukami broke his seal.

Lightning-like Thunder Qi sharpened through **Stormstep Dominion**.

Jun Kajihara hesitated only once.

Then activated his own dose.

Four Paragons.

Four Redlines.

Across the reserve line, Aya did not move.

Her vial remained sealed.

Hana took hers from her belt.

Looked at it.

Then placed it on the ground.

Nao did the same.

Haru held his longer.

Aya watched him.

She did not speak.

She did not need to.

Haru remembered lying on a medical table after three same-day Redlines.

Five years.

Then ten.

Then twenty.

Roughly thirty-five years gone because he had kept insisting that losing and dying were the same thing.

Aya's voice returned from that day anyway.

**You weren't dying. You were losing.**

Haru looked at Tsubasa.

Defeated.

Alive.

Then at the sleeping army.

Then at the vial.

He dropped it.

The glass did not break.

It simply landed in the dirt.

Aya exhaled.

Hana looked at him.

Haru did not look back.

“I already paid enough for being stupid.”

Nao's mouth moved faintly.

“That may be the smartest thing you've said.”

“Don't ruin it.”

For once, Hana smiled.

The four who had activated 3× moved toward Rhen.

Kai first.

Heat built fast.

At three times normal throughput, **Crimson Crucible Dominion** would have turned a prolonged fight into a furnace.

Eirik opened **Crownless Dominion** beside him.

Ambient qi recovery vanished for selected enemies within the field.

Kenji's **Stormstep Dominion** sharpened his burst geometry.

Jun's **Breach Dominion** prepared to punish fixed defenses.

Four Paragons.

Two High.

One Established.

One Stable.

All at 3× throughput.

It would have been one of the most dangerous conventional assault groups assembled anywhere in the known world.

Rhen looked tired.

Not physically.

Emotionally.

Sera knew the difference.

He did not want to beat them.

They had already lost.

They simply had not found a way to stop moving yet.

Kai raised his saber.

Rhen lifted one hand.

Frost-white petals appeared.

Different from Monarch's Winter Law.

Warmer at the edges.

The cold of an ending giving way rather than an ending refusing to happen.

Rhen spoke:

**Winter Ends, Spring Blossoms.**

Kai's Redline circulation changed first.

The drug had forced his body into an artificial borrowed state.

Triple throughput.

Accelerated reserve expenditure.

Vitality converted into present performance.

Rhen's Supreme art did not erase the seconds already spent.

It did not return future years already consumed.

It forced the distorted state toward its proper conclusion.

Kai's 3× circulation fell.

Not gradually.

Decisively.

The artificial overclock completed and ended.

His body returned toward its natural High-Paragon state.

The same happened to Eirik.

Kenji.

Jun.

Four Redline surges died before they could become a sustained attack.

Kai staggered.

His Dominion flickered from the sudden transition.

“What did you—”

“Ended it,” Rhen said.

Eirik checked his own pulse.

The 3× state was gone.

The vial had been used.

Some future vitality had already been spent in the short interval before Rhen stopped it.

That cost remained.

Aya saw the result from the outer line and closed her eyes.

Not relief exactly.

Something close.

Rhen had stopped the process.

He had not lied about its cost.

Kai looked furious.

“You can cancel Redline.”

“No.”

Rhen's correction was immediate.

“I can end an artificial state.”

Kai stared.

“That distinction matters to you?”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because if I call it something it isn't, somebody will try to use the wrong answer later.”

Luo, hearing from the ridge, nodded despite himself.

Physician language.

Even now.

Jun tightened his grip on the halberd.

The Redline was gone.

His natural Paragon cultivation remained.

“So we fight normally.”

Rhen looked at him.

“No.”

Jun's eyes narrowed.

Rhen had already decided he was finished hurting people who had lost.

The temperature dropped.

Not across fifty miles.

Not as another mass compression law.

A different old terror returned.

The moon seemed irrelevant even though it remained above the horizon.

Frost spread across stone.

Blood inside hostile circulations cooled.

Kai stopped.

Eirik stopped faster.

He knew the art by reputation.

So did Isgard.

So did Wuyue.

Rhen's voice carried through the field.

**Moonless Snowfield.**

The Supreme battlefield-control art settled over the four attacking Paragons.

Rhen selected them deliberately.

Not Aya.

Not Hana.

Not Nao.

Not Haru.

Not sleeping soldiers.

Not allied fighters.

The same qi-selection principle remained.

No magical morality.

No mind reading.

Only terrifyingly precise control of the circulations Rhen intended to immobilize.

Kai felt blood begin to freeze along the outer circulation routes of his limbs.

Not enough to kill him while still.

Enough that forcing high-output motion through it would be catastrophic.

Kenji felt the same warning through his legs.

Jun's grip tightened around the halberd.

Eirik did not move at all.

Rhen looked at them.

“Don't move.”

Kai's jaw tightened.

Rhen continued.

“If you force circulation through blood that is freezing, you can rupture the vessels immediately.”

Jun looked at his hand.

He could feel it.

Every Paragon there could.

This was not a threat requiring faith.

Their own bodies confirmed it.

Rhen did not ask them to kneel.

He did not tell them they were weak.

He did not remind them that Tsubasa had already fallen.

He simply waited.

Kai looked toward Tsubasa.

The Shinsei leader was alive.

Stable.

Defeated.

The fifty-thousand-person spearhead slept.

The four Redlines had been ended in seconds.

The four who refused Redline stood outside the immobilization field watching.

No reinforcement was coming.

No hidden technique remained.

No calculation produced victory.

Eirik Voss closed his eyes.

For years, he had told himself he understood the difference between a ruler and a commander.

This was simpler.

He understood surrender.

He opened his eyes.

“Enough.”

Kai looked at him.

Eirik's voice remained steady.

“Shinsei stands down.”

Jun's grip loosened.

Kenji lowered his sword as far as the frozen circulation safely allowed.

Kai stared at Rhen.

Then at Tsubasa.

Then at the sleeping army.

His saber lowered.

Rhen waited one more breath.

Two.

He felt the hostile commitment leave their circulations.

Moonless Snowfield released them immediately.

Warmth returned.

Blood moved normally.

No punishment.

No delayed injury.

No humiliation.

Kai flexed his fingers.

Rhen looked toward Aya.

“You should check them.”

Aya stared at him.

“Of course you say that now.”

“They took Redline.”

“I know.”

“It's bad for you.”

Aya's expression became dangerous.

Rhen looked at Sera.

“She is still offended by me.”

Sera laughed.

“Good.”

Aya walked onto the field with her needles already in hand.

Hana followed.

Nao followed.

After a moment, Haru picked up the unbroken vial he had dropped and carried it with him.

Not to use.

To hand to Aya.

Behind them, the final Shinsei formations began lowering weapons.

Isgard soldiers did not cheer immediately.

Neither did Wuyue.

Six months of war had taught everyone to distrust sudden silence.

They waited.

One banner lowered.

Then another.

Then another.

The surrender signal moved across Shinsei's lines.

Only then did sound begin returning to the battlefield.

Not victory screams.

Crying.

Laughter.

People sitting down because they had been standing through too much history.

Sera walked toward Rhen.

He looked at her.

“Done?”

She stopped beside him.

“For today.”

Rhen frowned.

“You said that yesterday.”

“I was wrong yesterday.”

“And today?”

Sera looked across the sleeping army, the lowered banners, the physicians already moving, and Tsubasa alive on the ground.

Then she took Rhen's hand.

“Today we're closer.”

Rhen looked at their joined hands.

Then at the battlefield.

He did not say Shinsei had lost when he woke.

He did not say Tsubasa had never had a chance.

He did not need to.

Everyone who had watched understood the actual truth.

Wuyue and Isgard had carried the war for more than six months without him.

Sera had pushed the strongest conventional cultivator alive until he bled, retreated and exhausted everything he knew.

Then Rhen had healed that man back to full health.

Given him his reserve back.

Let him choose 3× Redline.

Let him deploy fifty thousand boosted elites.

Let him open his Dominion.

Let him use his Ultimate.

Let him use every conventional excuse before taking any of them away.

Tsubasa had not lost because he was wounded.

Rhen had healed him.

He had not lost because Sera weakened him first.

Rhen had erased the recoverable weakness.

He had not lost because he lacked his army.

Fifty thousand had stood with him.

The mathematics simply had no place for Rhen.

That was the difference.

The war had spent six months calculating strength.

Then an anomaly woke up.

Season 113 ended with Shinsei's weapons on the ground.

And physicians from both sides walking forward together.
