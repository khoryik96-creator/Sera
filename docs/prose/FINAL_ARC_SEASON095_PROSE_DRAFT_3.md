## Chapter 308 — He Thought It Was Temporary

The second man came voluntarily.

That made him harder to question.

He spoke readily enough; embarrassment was what made him difficult.

His name was Dae Junseo, thirty-seven, High Marquis by cultivation and dock foreman by profession. He had spent fifteen years loading cargo in an eastern Wuyue port, six years running security for merchant warehouses, and exactly one evening believing a smiling broker who told him that a glass ampoule could make him fight above his station for ten minutes.

He sat in the Quaint clinic with both hands wrapped around a cup of tea he had not touched.

Sera sat opposite him.

Luo stood near the treatment cabinet.

Yun sat on the cabinet despite being told not to.

Lu had positioned himself at the edge of the room with a ledger and the patient manifest.

Rhen leaned against the wall.

Dae looked at all of them.

Then at the door.

“Can I leave?”

Sera nodded.

“Yes.”

He looked surprised.

“Now?”

“Yes.”

“Before I tell you anything?”

“Yes.”

Dae frowned.

“That feels like a trap.”

“It is not.”

“People said the Pale Orchid was terrifying.”

Yun said, “She is.”

Sera looked at her.

Yun smiled.

Dae did not.

Sera rested both hands on her knees.

“You came here because Lu found your name on a purchase ledger and you agreed to be examined. That does not make you a prisoner.”

Dae's fingers tightened around the cup.

“What if I bought something illegal?”

Lu answered without looking up.

“You bought mislabeled medicine from a licensed broker.”

Dae glanced at him.

“That sounded suspiciously precise.”

“It is.”

“Am I in trouble?”

“Possibly with your wife.”

Dae closed his eyes.

“She knows?”

“No.”

“Oh.”

Lu finally looked up.

“But if you die because you bought secret miracle strength from a man at the docks, I assume she will become informed.”

Dae looked stricken.

Sera rubbed her forehead.

“Lu.”

“What?”

“Less helpful.”

“I was motivating honesty.”

Rhen said, “It worked.”

Sera looked at him.

He looked back.

Dae inhaled slowly.

“I thought it was temporary.”

The room settled.

Luo stepped closer.

“What did they tell you it did?”

“Opened the meridians. Increased circulation. Made your body use everything at once.”

“Did they say it raised cultivation?”

Dae hesitated.

“They said it would feel like I was one level stronger.”

Yun's mouth flattened.

“Careful wording.”

Dae nodded.

“The broker said it wasn't a breakthrough. Just... access.”

“To what?” Luo asked.

“Everything I already had.”

Rhen's gaze sharpened slightly.

That was closer to the truth than the broker probably understood.

Sera asked, “Why did you take it?”

Dae looked ashamed.

“My younger brother got into debt with a dock gang.”

Nobody interrupted.

“I was going to pull him out. They had six men. Two were Marquis. I knew I could beat one. Maybe two. Not six.”

“So you bought strength,” Yun said.

Dae looked at her.

“Yes.”

“Did it work?”

His expression changed.

“For a while.”

He described it carefully.

The ampoule had been red glass, sealed with black wax.

He bit through it because the broker told him the compound absorbed fastest through the mouth.

Within breaths, his pulse surged.

His limbs felt light.

Qi moved with almost no hesitation.

Every technique he knew arrived faster than habit.

He crossed the warehouse floor before the first guard finished drawing a blade.

He broke a table with one kick.

Threw a Marquis through a storage wall.

Disarmed another before the man understood he had moved.

“I thought I was brilliant,” Dae said quietly.

Luo's eyes stayed on him.

“And then?”

“My left hand stopped closing.”

Yun sat straighter.

“During the effect?”

“Yes.”

“Then?”

“My hearing went strange. Like everyone was underwater.”

“Then?”

“I threw up.”

“Still fighting?”

“Yes.”

Luo looked at Yun.

The delayed suppression layer.

Dae continued.

“I remember knowing I should stop. I remember thinking that very clearly.”

His fingers trembled around the tea cup.

“But I didn't feel like I had to.”

Yun's expression turned cold.

The distinction Yun had been waiting for surfaced at last.

Not mind control.

Worse in a quieter way.

The body screaming stop while the chemical system lowered the volume of the scream.

“How long?” Sera asked.

Dae swallowed.

“Seven minutes, maybe eight.”

“Then?”

“I woke up the next afternoon.”

Luo asked, “What were you told recovery would be?”

“Two days tired. Maybe nausea.”

“Anything about future vitality?”

Dae frowned.

“No.”

“Lifespan?”

His face changed.

“No.”

“Long-term organ stress?”

“No.”

“Permanent depletion?”

“No.”

The last answer came sharper.

Dae looked from Luo to Rhen.

“Permanent?”

Rhen stepped away from the wall.

“May I?”

Dae held out his wrist.

Rhen examined him.

The damage was older than the first patient's.

Most of it had healed naturally.

Poorly in places.

A faint ridge remained along one meridian branch. The heart carried evidence of stress. Two muscle groups had compensated for tissue that never recovered evenly.

And beneath it all sat the same wrong thinness.

Less severe.

Still there.

Rhen withdrew his hand.

“When did you take it?”

“Four months ago.”

“Any second dose?”

“No.”

“Any other stimulants?”

“No.”

Rhen looked at Luo.

“The absence remains.”

Dae went pale.

“What absence?”

Sera intervened before fear filled the gap with something worse.

“We do not know exactly yet.”

“That sounds bad.”

“It is why we are asking.”

Yun slid off the cabinet.

“What did the broker ask before selling it?”

Dae frowned.

“Money.”

“Besides money.”

“Weight.”

Everyone looked at him.

Dae noticed.

“What?”

Yun stepped closer.

“Exact wording.”

“He asked how much I weighed.”

“Height?”

“Yes.”

“Age?”

“Yes.”

“Resting pulse?”

Dae blinked.

“He counted it himself.”

Luo's face changed.

“Where?”

“Wrist.”

“What else?”

“He asked if I had heart weakness. If my father died young. If I had ever coughed blood after cultivation. If I used fire qi.”

Yun looked at Luo.

“That is screening.”

Luo nodded once.

Not street screening.

Medical screening.

Dae watched them.

“What does that mean?”

Yun answered.

“It means whoever designed the sale protocol knew enough medicine to understand who might die before the product finished working.”

Dae's face lost color.

Sera glanced at Lu.

“Do we have the broker?”

“Under observation.”

“Do not move yet.”

“I was not going to.”

Dae looked confused.

“You know who sold it?”

“We know which company touched your purchase.”

“Then arrest him.”

Lu's tone stayed calm.

“If we arrest the middle of a chain, the beginning disappears.”

Dae looked down.

“I could identify him.”

“That helps.”

Yun asked, “Did the ampoule have writing?”

Dae frowned.

“Not writing I could read.”

“What kind?”

“Small marks near the base.”

Lu slid a blank page toward him.

“Draw them.”

Dae took the charcoal.

His first attempt was useless.

His second had shape.

By the fourth, three marks appeared repeatedly.

Yun looked at them.

“Not Wuyue script.”

Luo leaned over her shoulder.

“Not Isgard either.”

Lu took the page.

“Merchant shorthand?”

“Maybe.”

Dae added one more detail.

“The broker had a chart.”

Everyone looked at him again.

“A chart?” Luo asked.

“Different colors. Different weights.”

“How many?”

“I don't know. Rows.”

“Did he choose your ampoule from the chart?”

“Yes.”

Yun exhaled once through her nose.

That was worse than a recipe.

Recipes made substances.

Charts made systems.

Body weight.

Pulse.

Risk factors.

Dose categories.

Somewhere, someone had standardized the act of asking a cultivator's body how much abuse it could survive.

Sera stood.

“Dae.”

He looked at her.

“You are staying tonight.”

He stiffened.

“Prisoner?”

“Patient.”

His shoulders loosened.

“Rhen is going to repair what he can. Luo and Yun will run tests. You can leave tomorrow if you want.”

Dae nodded.

Then looked at Rhen.

“Can you give back what I lost?”

Rhen did not lie.

“I do not know.”

Dae absorbed that.

Then finally drank the tea.

It had gone cold.

Rhen warmed it with two fingers.

Dae stared at the cup.

“People said you were the world's greatest healer.”

Rhen looked at him.

“People say many things.”

Dae took a careful sip.

“Are they wrong?”

Rhen considered.

“No.”

Sera covered a smile.

Yun looked delighted.

Luo muttered, “There it is.”

Rhen looked at all of them.

“What?”

Nobody answered.

On the table, Lu folded the charcoal drawing of the foreign marks and placed it beside the shipping invoices.

One voluntary user.

One standardized chart.

One lie about temporary cost.

The problem was becoming less mysterious.

And far more deliberate.

## Chapter 309 — The Wind That Would Not Stay

The broker ran at noon.

Lu considered that rude.

Not surprising.

Rude.

The surveillance team had spent four days doing everything correctly.

No obvious martial presence.

No interrogation.

No sudden seizure of records.

They watched the merchant office open, close, receive cargo, send clerks to lunch, bribe a customs inspector in a way that was almost charmingly ordinary, and move three sealed ledgers between buildings.

Then someone in the port authority asked the wrong question too loudly.

The broker left through a second-floor window.

Lu received the signal while standing beside Sera near a warehouse full of dried seaweed.

He closed his eyes.

“One instruction,” he said.

Sera looked at him.

“I gave them one instruction.”

“Do not spook him?”

“Yes.”

“Did we spook him?”

“He is currently crossing a roof.”

“That sounds spooked.”

Lu stared at her.

Sera smiled apologetically.

Shen Rui arrived from the eastern quay at the same moment, white-blue robes moving around him in the salt wind.

He had come because the broker's shipping company also serviced one of Azure's medicine routes, and Rui had learned the hard way that medical corridors became dangerous when martial people treated them as someone else's problem.

“Where?” he asked.

Lu pointed.

A man in brown leapt from one tiled roof to another.

Fast for a Marquis.

Not fast enough.

Sera stepped forward.

Lu caught her sleeve.

She looked at him.

“Records first.”

“He is escaping.”

“He is middle.”

“You want to follow him.”

“I want to know where middle runs when middle is frightened.”

Sera stopped.

Rui nodded.

“Reasonable.”

Then three men with hooked blades emerged from an alley ahead of the broker.

Lu's expression changed.

“Not ours.”

The broker saw them too.

He turned.

Two more appeared behind him.

Sera's eyes narrowed.

“They are not extracting him.”

“No,” Rui said.

The first hooked blade went for the broker's throat.

A gust of wind struck sideways.

Not a blast.

Not a dramatic wall of qi.

One sharp compression of air altered the attack by half a handspan.

The hook missed.

The broker stumbled.

A masked man landed between him and the killers.

Grey-white outer robe.

Short sword at his hip.

Wind moving around him so cleanly that the robe barely fluttered.

Rui stopped breathing for one beat.

Sera noticed.

“You know him.”

The masked man moved.

One step.

He vanished from the first attacker's expectation without vanishing from the street.

Wind-Qi compressed beneath his foot, carrying him along a line too fast for ordinary eyes to track cleanly.

His hand touched the attacker's wrist.

The hooked blade left the man's fingers.

Second step.

He appeared beside another assassin before the first blade hit the ground.

No wasted motion.

No show.

A shoulder strike folded the second man.

The third tried to retreat.

The masked man's sword came out halfway.

Only halfway.

A thin arc of compressed wind cut the man's sleeve and the leather strap holding his weapon.

The weapon fell.

The sword returned to its sheath.

Rui's expression had gone very still.

Sera looked at him.

“Definitely know him.”

Rui did not answer.

The fourth assassin charged the broker instead.

The masked man turned.

For the first time, his circulation opened enough that Rui could feel it clearly.

Wind.

Not Azure wind.

Older.

Sharper at the transition between stillness and motion.

Rui's memory went back more than a decade.

A younger Shen Rui.

A compromised intelligence report.

Three supposed smugglers running through a mountain pass.

Rui had been certain they were enemy couriers.

A masked swordsman had stood between him and them.

Rui had demanded he move.

The man had refused.

They fought.

Rui lost.

Badly enough to remember.

Later, he learned the three “couriers” were witnesses fleeing a corrupt regional official who had fed Azure false intelligence.

Rui had never found the masked swordsman again.

Until now.

The fourth assassin hit the ground.

The fifth did not attack.

He ran.

The masked man let him.

Lu said, “Interesting.”

Sera looked at him.

“You say that when you are about to become annoying.”

“He left one.”

Rui had already understood.

“To follow.”

The masked man's head turned slightly.

He had heard them.

Of course he had.

Sera stepped into the street.

“Xie Wuchen.”

The masked man froze.

The name was right. That was why he froze.

Rui looked at Sera.

“You know his name?”

“Lu does.”

Lu looked offended.

“I know everyone's name.”

The masked man turned fully.

He removed the cloth covering the lower half of his face.

Thirty-three.

Fine features hardened by years outdoors.

Clear eyes that looked younger when relaxed and much older when deciding whether someone was dangerous.

Xie Wuchen sighed.

“That is unfortunate.”

Sera smiled faintly.

“You sound like Rhen.”

“I do not know who that is.”

Rui said, “Liar.”

Xie's eyes moved to him.

For the first time, recognition sharpened them.

“Oh.”

Rui's expression darkened.

“Oh?”

“You got older.”

“So did you.”

“Yes.”

“That is all?”

Xie considered.

“You improved.”

Rui almost smiled despite himself.

“Enough to beat you?”

“No.”

The smile vanished.

Sera covered her mouth.

Lu did not bother.

Rui looked at both of them.

“This is not funny.”

“It is slightly funny,” Sera said.

Xie glanced toward the broker.

The man had collapsed against a wall, staring at everyone around him as though his career decisions had become unexpectedly complicated.

Xie pointed at him.

“He was about to be killed.”

“We noticed,” Lu said.

“So I helped.”

“Why?”

Xie looked at him.

“Because he was about to be killed.”

Lu nodded.

“Good answer.”

Xie's gaze shifted toward the alley where the fifth assassin had escaped.

“I assume you are following that one.”

“Yes.”

“Then I am done.”

He turned.

Sera said, “You always leave this fast?”

Xie stopped.

Rui's expression changed slightly.

He remembered the mountain pass.

Xie had left there too.

No name.

No thanks.

No attempt to explain why he had risked himself for strangers.

Xie looked back over one shoulder.

“I helped.”

“You did.”

“The problem is no longer immediate.”

“No.”

“Then I am leaving.”

Sera tilted her head.

“That is a very specific philosophy.”

“It has worked.”

“Has it?”

Xie's eyes narrowed.

Rui looked at Sera.

He recognized the tone.

She was not recruiting.

Not yet.

She was testing where someone placed the line between conscience and responsibility.

Xie stepped onto the edge of the street.

Wind gathered under his feet.

Rui spoke before he moved.

“Tempest Hall.”

Xie stopped again.

The words landed differently.

Rui continued.

“Last successor?”

Xie's face lost its lightness.

“Yes.”

Rui nodded.

He knew enough of Tempest Hall's old history to understand what that meant.

A school gone.

One inheritor left.

A martial lineage traveling without a place to return to.

Sera heard the answer too.

Xie looked between them.

“I am still leaving.”

Sera shrugged.

“I did not stop you.”

“You were about to.”

“No.”

“You have the face.”

“What face?”

“The one people use before saying responsibility.”

Lu said, “She does have that face.”

Sera looked at him.

Lu looked away.

Xie stepped backward.

“Goodbye.”

The wind compressed.

He was gone from the street a heartbeat later, moving over the roofline in a sequence of real, brutally fast steps.

No teleportation.

No vanishing art.

Just a Sovereign whose Wind-Qi made distance look briefly dishonest.

Rui watched until he disappeared.

Sera turned to him.

“You really lost to him?”

Rui closed his eyes.

“Do not.”

“How badly?”

“Do not.”

Lu was already writing.

Rui looked at him.

“What are you writing?”

“Xie Wuchen. The Unseen Gale. Tempest Hall. Newly Sovereign.”

“How do you know he is newly Sovereign?”

Lu looked up.

“I know everyone's name.”

Rui stared.

Sera laughed.

Then the broker made a small sound from the wall.

Everyone remembered him.

Lu closed the ledger.

“Now,” he said, “we ask why someone preferred him dead before we could ask where the boxes came from.”

The humor left the street.

Rui looked once more toward the roofline where Xie had vanished.

Years ago, the man had protected three strangers from a mistake and disappeared before anyone could thank him.

Today, he had done the same thing.

Perhaps that was virtue.

Perhaps it was fear of belonging anywhere long enough to be needed twice.

Rui did not know yet.

He only knew the wind had returned.

## Chapter 310 — Axtaya

The broker knew the name.

He did not know what it meant.

That distinction disappointed Yun.

“Say it again.”

The broker swallowed.

“Axtaya.”

The word sounded wrong in Wuyue speech.

Not difficult.

Foreign.

The first syllable clipped. The last softer than the middle. Something transliterated from a language none of them used daily.

They sat in one of the mountain interview rooms three days after the port attack.

The broker had not been tortured.

Sera had made that clear before the first question.

He had been arrested for trafficking mislabeled dangerous medicine and cooperating with attempted murder. The charge alone kept him in custody.

The fact that Huo occasionally walked past the door had increased his willingness to answer questions without anyone requesting the service.

Yun sat across from him.

Luo beside her.

Lu occupied the end of the table with four ledgers.

Sera stood near the window.

Rhen had declined to attend because the broker was medically stable and, in his words, “too frightened already.”

Huo had been offended.

The broker looked at Yun.

“That is what it was called on the invoices.”

“By whom?”

“I don't know.”

“You sold it.”

“I brokered sealed cargo.”

“To cultivators.”

“Yes.”

“Using weight charts.”

“Yes.”

“Pulse screening.”

“Yes.”

“Risk questions.”

“Yes.”

“And you did not know what it did?”

The broker's face tightened.

“I knew what I was told.”

Yun leaned closer.

“What were you told?”

“Temporary combat amplification.”

Luo's expression did not change.

“Not cultivation breakthrough.”

“No.”

“Not permanent strengthening.”

“No.”

“Recovery?”

“One or two days.”

“Long-term cost?”

The broker hesitated.

Yun saw it.

“So there was one.”

“I did not know the number.”

“That was not my question.”

He looked at the table.

“The supplier said repeated use was dangerous.”

Luo's voice went cold.

“Repeated.”

“Yes.”

“What about one dose?”

“They said healthy users recover.”

“Recover what?”

Silence.

Luo looked at him for a long moment.

The broker looked away first.

Lu opened a ledger.

“Your company received six shipments over nine months.”

The broker nodded reluctantly.

“Three went north through Isgard intermediaries. Two remained in Wuyue. One disappeared from your books.”

“I told you—”

“You told me the warehouse burned.”

“It did.”

“The warehouse burned two weeks before the shipment arrived.”

The broker stopped breathing.

Lu turned one page.

“This is why dates matter.”

Sera hid a smile.

Yun did not.

“I like him.”

Luo looked at her.

“You have known Lu for years.”

“I am rediscovering it.”

Lu continued.

“Where did the sixth shipment go?”

The broker swallowed.

“Military buyers.”

The room changed.

Sera's face went still.

“Whose military?”

“I don't know.”

“Wuyue?”

“No.”

“Isgard?”

“No.”

“How do you know?”

“Different payment notes. Different script. Foreign weights.”

Lu leaned forward.

“Show me.”

The broker pointed to one of the copied invoices.

A column near the edge held numbers none of them had bothered interpreting because the units were unfamiliar.

Lu turned the page sideways.

Yun looked over his shoulder.

“Body mass?”

Luo frowned.

“Could be cargo.”

The broker shook his head.

“No. Those match the screening charts.”

Yun's eyes sharpened.

“There.”

One column.

Body-weight categories.

Another.

Pulse ranges.

Another.

Color-coded ampoule classes.

The system was more complete than Dae remembered.

Luo traced the categories without touching the paper.

“This is medical.”

Sera looked at him.

“You keep saying that.”

“Because it matters.”

His voice stayed controlled.

“A poisoner can discover a lethal dose. A physician builds exclusion criteria.”

Yun nodded.

“Whoever made the original framework expected to keep some users alive.”

Sera looked at the broker.

“Supplier.”

He shook his head.

“I never met them.”

“Route.”

“Sea.”

“From where?”

“I don't know.”

Lu placed another document in front of him.

“What is this mark?”

The broker looked.

A broken circle joined by a horizontal line.

His expression changed.

Not recognition exactly.

Familiarity.

“I saw that on outer crates.”

Yun looked at Lu.

“Company?”

“Could be.”

The broker said, “They told us not to copy it into local manifests.”

“Why?”

“I don't know.”

“Another useful sentence,” Yun muttered.

Lu ignored her.

He slid the charcoal copy of Dae's foreign ampoule marks beside the symbol.

Then one old sea-insurance form.

Then a merchant route table.

Different documents.

Different intermediaries.

One repeated cluster of foreign transliteration.

Axtaya.

And beneath it, in a route-reference column, another word.

Lu read it once silently.

Then aloud.

“Shinrin.”

The broker nodded too quickly.

“Yes.”

Everyone looked at him.

He realized the mistake.

Yun smiled without warmth.

“You know that one.”

He closed his eyes.

“Only as a place-name.”

“Where?”

“Far east by sea routes. Merchant networks. Not direct from here.”

Sera looked at Lu.

“Do we know it?”

“Barely.”

Lu went through memory.

Old trade notes.

Foreign herb catalogs.

A ship captain once complaining about Shinrin customs fees.

Nothing strategic.

Nothing that had ever required Wuyue's leaders to care.

“A distant continent or major state,” he said. “Depending on which merchant map you trust.”

“Government?”

“Unknown.”

“Martial strength?”

“Unknown.”

“Army?”

“Unknown.”

Sera exhaled.

Yun rested both forearms on the table.

“So at the moment we know a dangerous medically designed stimulant moves through a foreign route touching a place called Shinrin.”

Luo added, “We know users are lied to about cost.”

Lu added, “We know the supply chain uses legitimate medical commerce and standardized screening.”

Sera looked at the broker.

“And we know someone tried to kill the middleman.”

The broker looked miserable.

“I am beginning to feel underappreciated.”

Yun stared at him.

He lowered his eyes.

Sera turned toward the window.

Outside, evening light lay across the mountain training courts.

Tae was correcting a company captain's stance.

Huo was eating something he probably had not paid for.

Qin walked beside Lu's deputy through a line of returning medical caravans.

Above them, the crooked sign leaned over a tea room full of people who had no reason to know the word Shinrin.

Not yet.

Sera looked at Yun.

“What do you need?”

Yun did not answer immediately.

Her instinct was already moving ahead.

Foreign poison.

Foreign medicine.

A system built by someone who understood physiology well enough to classify bodies before dosing them.

The route was too distant for one local arrest to solve.

But they were not at the point of invasion.

Not at the point of ten Paragons.

Not at the point of a foreign government.

They did not know any of that.

At this moment, the problem remained what it had earned the right to be.

A pharmaceutical network.

A dangerous one.

Yun looked down at the word.

“Axtaya.”

Luo watched her.

“What?”

“It sounds like medicine trying to have a prettier name than it deserves.”

Lu closed the ledger.

“Most products do.”

Sera looked at the route mark beneath it.

“Shinrin.”

The word meant almost nothing to her.

That was what made it dangerous.

An unknown country did not automatically imply an army. What mattered was that somewhere beyond Wuyue's habits, somebody had built a medical system sophisticated enough to make a High Marquis fight above himself for minutes and pay a cost he had never been told existed.

Sera touched the page once.

“Find the road.”

Yun's eyes lifted.

For the first time all evening, she smiled for real.

“Now that sounds fun.”

Luo sighed.

“It absolutely does not.”
