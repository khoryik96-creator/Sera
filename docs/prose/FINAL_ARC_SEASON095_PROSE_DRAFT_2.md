## Chapter 305 — Physician and Poisoner

Yun Shizhen believed every medicine should be interrogated before it was trusted.

Luo Wen believed medicine should not have to survive interrogation by someone holding three knives.

Their disagreement had lasted most of the morning.

“It is not three knives,” Yun said.

Luo looked at the table.

There were three knives.

[[speaker:luo]]“One is a scraper.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It has an edge.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That does not make it a knife.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is shaped like a knife.”

[[speaker:yun]]“So are you when you are tired.”

Luo stared at her.

Yun smiled and leaned back over the workbench.

The sealed residue from the Isgard patient sat beneath a glass hood in the lower laboratory, divided into four samples so nobody could destroy the entire clue by being clever.

That division had been Lu's idea.

He had delivered the jars, labeled them, checked the labels twice, and left immediately after Yun asked if he wanted to smell one.

Luo had approved of his survival instinct.

The laboratory itself occupied one of the cooler rooms beneath the mountain clinic. Shelves held dried herbs, antidote stocks, mineral powders, sealed toxin cabinets and enough instruments that Huo had once walked in, looked around, and said the entire room seemed hostile.

Yun had taken it as a compliment.

She lowered a thin strip of treated paper into one diluted sample.

The paper darkened at the edge.

Luo watched.

“Circulation opener.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Partly.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Fast absorption.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Adrenal response.”

Yun made a face.

[[speaker:yun]]“Crude word.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Useful word.”

[[speaker:yun]]“We are not discussing goats.”

[[speaker:luo]]“We are discussing a man who tried to break a wagon axle with his shoulder.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That does support your argument.”

Luo took the next sample.

He did not touch it directly.

Neither of them was stupid.

Yun was merely less respectful of appearances.

He added a drop of neutral carrier fluid.

Nothing happened.

Then, several breaths later, a pale thread formed through the mixture.

Luo's eyes narrowed.

[[speaker:luo]]“Delayed release.”

Yun nodded.

[[speaker:yun]]“That is the interesting part.”

[[speaker:luo]]“The interesting part is that there are several interesting parts.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That is why I like it.”

Luo looked at her.

Yun looked back.

After a moment she added, “Professionally.”

“Of course.”

They had been together long enough that neither needed to perform affection every time they were alone.

It showed in smaller things.

Luo automatically moved the lamp when Yun leaned closer.

Yun automatically passed him the instrument he was reaching for before his hand arrived.

He knew which poisons made her impatient.

She knew which medical puzzles would keep him awake until sunrise unless someone physically removed the notes from his hands.

Sometimes that someone was her.

Sometimes she made tea first.

Neither had admitted this counted as domesticity.

Luo wrote three lines in the notebook.

[[speaker:luo]]“First layer forces circulation.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Agreed.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Second suppresses pain feedback.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Not only pain.”

Yun tapped the glass.

“Warning responses. Tremor. breath distress. vascular pressure. The body begins screaming and something tells it to lower its voice.”

Luo's expression hardened.

[[speaker:luo]]“That is not medicine.”

Yun tilted her head.

[[speaker:yun]]“It could have started as medicine.”

He looked at her.

She continued.

[[speaker:yun]]“Emergency battlefield stimulant. Collapse rescue. Shock response. A physician could have built the first principle.”

[[speaker:luo]]“And someone kept going.”

“Yes.”

Luo looked again at the pale thread inside the sample.

Medicine often required violence.

A broken bone had to be set.

An abscess cut.

A failing heart forced through another minute.

The difference was purpose.

Medicine hurt a body so it could continue being a body.

This compound seemed designed to make survival secondary to output.

“What is the third layer?” he asked.

Yun smiled.

[[speaker:yun]]“There you are.”

He ignored her.

She reached for one of the knives.

[[speaker:yun]]“The body begins to fail after the first forced surge. Whoever made this expected that.”

She scraped a nearly invisible residue from the side of the jar onto a ceramic tile.

[[speaker:yun]]“Here.”

Luo leaned closer.

The material had separated into two textures after drying.

One crystalline.

One waxy.

“Release coating,” he said.

Yun nodded.

[[speaker:yun]]“The second component arrives after the first damage begins.”

[[speaker:luo]]“To do what?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Buy time.”

Luo did not like the answer.

[[speaker:luo]]“How?”

Yun's expression lost its amusement.

[[speaker:yun]]“By making the body spend something it would normally refuse to spend.”

The room became quieter.

Luo thought of the patient upstairs.

The torn meridians.

The strange absence Rhen had not yet named.

He looked at Yun.

[[speaker:luo]]“You think vitality.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I think the compound is not satisfied with qi.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Vitality is not a jar of oil.”

“I know.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You cannot simply pour it into a technique.”

“I know.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Then how?”

Yun rested both hands on the workbench.

[[speaker:yun]]“That is what I want to know.”

Luo's gaze dropped to her fingers.

One had a shallow cut from earlier.

He reached across, took her hand and turned it over.

Yun looked down.

[[speaker:yun]]“That is extremely dramatic.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You cut yourself.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is a line.”

[[speaker:luo]]“It is contaminated work.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I was wearing gloves.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You removed one.”

[[speaker:yun]]“To write.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You have another hand.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That is discriminatory.”

Luo cleaned the cut anyway.

Yun let him.

When he finished, she curled her fingers around his wrist.

[[speaker:yun]]“You are worried.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“About me?”

[[speaker:luo]]“About your terrible laboratory habits.”

[[speaker:yun]]“So me.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Unfortunately.”

She smiled.

The line belonged to Rhen.

Half the household had stolen it by now.

Yun released him and returned to the sample.

[[speaker:yun]]“Let us say the original designer was a physician.”

Luo sat beside her.

“Why?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Because of the screening logic.”

[[speaker:luo]]“We do not have screening logic.”

[[speaker:yun]]“We have the body.”

Yun pointed toward the notes.

“The patient's damage clusters around thresholds. Whoever made the compound expected variance in meridian width, cardiovascular tolerance and body mass.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That could be trial and error.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Then there would be more random failure.”

Luo considered that.

Yun continued.

[[speaker:yun]]“This is ugly, but it is not ignorant.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That may be worse.”

“Yes.”

For several breaths neither spoke.

Then Yun reached toward the patient chart.

Luo moved it away.

She looked at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“You are being difficult.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You have not eaten.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I ate.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Dried plum is not breakfast.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I had five.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That is not a stronger argument.”

Yun leaned back.

[[speaker:yun]]“If I say six, does it become one?”

“No.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Cruel.”

Luo stood.

[[speaker:luo]]“Come upstairs.”

[[speaker:yun]]“We are working.”

[[speaker:luo]]“We can work after food.”

Yun looked at the samples.

Then at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“You know I can poison you.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“And yet you command me.”

[[speaker:luo]]“I am asking.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That sounded like command.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You are free to refuse.”

Yun smiled slowly.

[[speaker:yun]]“And if I refuse?”

[[speaker:luo]]“I bring food here.”

She stared.

[[speaker:yun]]“That is unfair.”

[[speaker:luo]]“I learned from you.”

They left the laboratory together.

Behind them, four sealed jars sat beneath glass.

No label yet.

No origin.

No name.

Only a compound built by someone who understood exactly how far a human body could be pushed before it began to break.

And, perhaps more frighteningly, how to make it keep going after it should have stopped.

## Chapter 306 — Something Was Spent

Rhen healed the man completely.

Then waited for him to recover.

He did not.

That was the problem.

The first patient sat upright in the clinic bed three days after arrival, breathing normally, pulse steady, meridians repaired, organs restored and muscles no longer carrying the deep tearing left by the five-minute surge.

By every ordinary measure, he should have looked exhausted but healthy.

Instead he looked older.

Not visibly by years.

Nothing so theatrical.

The skin beneath his eyes held a faint hollowness. His recovery pulse lacked resilience. When he stood, his body found balance correctly but seemed reluctant to spend effort doing it.

Rhen watched him walk from the bed to the window.

The patient looked back.

“Am I dying?”

“No.”

The answer came quickly enough that the man believed it.

Then Rhen added, “Sit.”

The man sat.

That part worried him more.

Sera leaned against the far wall.

Luo and Yun stood near the treatment table. Qin had joined them because resonance gave him a different way to hear circulation, though he had made clear he was there as an observer and not as a physician.

Rhen placed two fingers against the patient's wrist.

The pulse was normal.

He closed his eyes.

Healing qi entered.

The patient inhaled sharply.

Warmth spread through his arm, chest and abdomen as Rhen searched for what remained broken.

Nothing.

He increased the depth slightly.

Muscle.

Healthy.

Organs.

Healthy.

Meridian walls.

Restored.

Heart.

Stable.

Blood.

Normal.

The emptiness remained.

Rhen withdrew his hand.

Yun watched his face.

“That is annoying you.”

“Yes.”

The patient swallowed.

“That is not comforting.”

Rhen looked at him.

“You are alive.”

“That part was comforting.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“You are not currently deteriorating.”

“Better.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I cannot restore what is missing.”

The room went still.

The patient stared.

“What is missing?”

Rhen did not answer immediately.

He disliked naming things before he understood them.

Luo stepped closer.

“Vital reserve?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Something adjacent to it.”

Yun folded her arms.

“You said the body is repaired.”

“It is.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Then this is not damage.”

“No.”

Sera pushed away from the wall.

“What is the difference?”

Rhen looked at her.

“If you cut someone, there is a wound. I can close it.”

He looked back at the patient.

[[speaker:rhen]]“If someone empties a cup, repairing the cup does not put the water back.”

The patient looked at his hands.

“What did they take from me?”

Rhen's jaw tightened slightly.

“I do not know yet.”

Yun did.

Or at least suspected.

“Not they,” she said.

The man looked at her.

[[speaker:yun]]“You spent it.”

His expression changed.

Yun did not soften the point, but neither did she make it cruel.

“The drug made your body spend something it normally protects.”

“I didn't agree to that.”

“No.”

That word mattered.

The patient looked away.

Sera asked, “Can he recover naturally?”

Rhen considered.

“Some things, yes. Ordinary exhaustion. Qi. physical condition.”

[[speaker:sera]]“And the missing part?”

“I do not know.”

Qin spoke for the first time.

“His circulation sounds younger than his recovery.”

Everyone turned toward him.

The patient blinked.

Qin tilted his head.

“The pulse rhythm is correct. The resonance behind it is thin.”

Luo looked interested.

“Thin how?”

[[speaker:qin]]“Like a bell with the right shape but less metal.”

Yun's eyes sharpened.

“That is useful.”

Qin smiled faintly.

“Good. I was worried being blind had become decorative.”

Sera ignored the tone.

“Can Echo Dominion read it more clearly?”

Qin shook his head.

“I am not activating a Domain inside a clinic to listen to one recovering man's pulse.”

Rhen nodded approvingly.

“Reasonable.”

Sera looked at both of them.

“I hate when you agree.”

The patient almost smiled.

That helped.

Rhen returned to the bedside.

“What were you told before you took it?”

The man hesitated.

“That it would make me stronger.”

“For how long?” Luo asked.

“Minutes.”

“What cost?”

“Sickness after.”

“How much sickness?”

“A day. Maybe two.”

Yun's face went flat.

The man continued.

“They said the body would feel emptied because the drug burns qi fast.”

“It does burn qi fast,” Luo said.

The patient looked hopeful.

Luo did not let him misunderstand.

“That is not all it burns.”

The hope disappeared.

Sera crossed her arms.

“Who gave it to you?”

“A broker.”

“Name.”

He gave one.

Lu, waiting outside the room with a ledger, wrote it down before anyone called him in.

Sera heard the scratch of the pen.

Of course he was there.

Rhen looked again at the patient's face.

“How old are you?”

“Thirty-two.”

“Any chronic illness?”

“No.”

“Family heart weakness?”

“No.”

“Previous stimulants?”

The man shook his head.

Rhen asked questions for several more minutes.

Nothing explained the gap.

When the patient finally lay back, Rhen drew a thin blanket over him.

“You are not dying today.”

The man looked at him.

“Tomorrow?”

Rhen's face stayed calm.

“I do not make jokes about mortality with frightened patients.”

“That was not a joke.”

“I know.”

Rhen rested one hand on the bedframe.

“You are stable. Eat. sleep. rebuild your qi normally. We will continue measuring what returns and what does not.”

The man nodded.

When they stepped into the corridor, Sera shut the door behind them.

“What do you think?”

Rhen looked toward the clinic window.

“Something was spent.”

Yun nodded.

“Future vitality.”

Luo's mouth tightened.

“That phrase is too broad.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is still the nearest one.”

Rhen looked at Yun.

“What makes you think future?”

“Because the compound delays collapse by pulling from somewhere the body normally refuses to access. If it were only present qi, you could restore the deficit by restoring qi.”

Rhen said nothing.

That logic matched what his healing sense found.

Luo asked the more important question.

“How much?”

Nobody answered.

The scale mattered.

One day of vitality was ugly.

A month was criminal if concealed.

A year would be monstrous.

They did not know.

Sera looked at Rhen.

“Can your strongest healing restore it?”

He understood what she meant.

Not ordinary tissue repair.

Not the routine version of Spring's evolved lineage.

The deeper arts he almost never needed.

Rhen shook his head once.

“Not safely without knowing what I am restoring.”

Yun lifted one eyebrow.

“That is a very Rhen answer.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“It is a physician answer.”

[[speaker:yun]]“You say that like those are different.”

Rhen looked at her.

“Sometimes.”

Sera glanced back through the clinic door.

The patient had closed his eyes.

A man had been told he was buying five minutes of strength and a day of sickness.

Whatever had actually been taken from him was not visible enough to scare anyone at the moment of purchase.

That made it easier to sell.

And worse.

“Find the source,” Sera said.

Lu's voice came from behind them.

“I am.”

They all turned.

He held up the original shipping manifest.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“And I need Yun to stop contaminating my evidence table.”

Yun looked offended.

“I have never contaminated your evidence table.”

Lu stared at her.

She considered.

[[speaker:yun]]“Intentionally.”

Luo closed his eyes.

Rhen walked away before he was asked to mediate.

Sera followed him.

At the end of the corridor, she caught his sleeve.

He stopped.

[[speaker:sera]]“You are worried about something else.”

Rhen looked at her.

She touched his wrist.

[[speaker:sera]]“The refill.”

He exhaled softly.

His reserve was full again.

The healing he had just performed should have left a measurable absence.

It had not.

“Faster again?” she asked.

“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“How fast?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Too fast.”

That was the first time he used those words.

Sera's fingers tightened around his sleeve.

Rhen covered her hand with his.

“I am watching it.”

[[speaker:sera]]“So am I.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I noticed.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You were supposed to.”

They stood there for a moment while the clinic breathed around them.

One mystery inside a patient.

Another inside Rhen.

Neither belonged to the other.

Not yet.

## Chapter 307 — Lu Follows the Box, Not the Poison

Lu Weiran hated mysteries that people insisted were mysterious.

Most were not.

Most were paperwork with better marketing.

The Axtaya sample did not have a name yet.

The route did.

Or several routes, which was better.

Lu spread six manifests across the long table in the mountain archive and weighted the corners with tea cups because Huo had borrowed the proper ledger stones and not returned them.

He had not asked what Huo needed ledger stones for.

Experience had taught him that some information damaged the person who learned it.

Sera sat opposite him.

Qin stood near the wall, listening to a junior courier recite dates.

Luo and Yun had been banned from touching anything until Lu finished copying it.

Yun had called this tyranny.

Lu had thanked her.

“Start again,” he told the courier.

The young woman swallowed.

“Crate entered Isgard at South Fen relay on the ninth day of early spring.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Origin?”

“Listed as Greywater medical consortium.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Does Greywater exist?”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Does the consortium?”

“No.”

“Good.”

The courier blinked.

Sera said, “He means useful.”

“I said good.”

The courier continued.

“Seal matches an Isgard epidemic clinic destroyed by fire eight months earlier.”

Lu tapped the manifest.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Meaning the seal itself could be genuine.”

Sera leaned closer.

[[speaker:sera]]“Stolen before the fire?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Or recovered after. Or copied from old cargo. The point is not the seal.”

[[speaker:sera]]“What is?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“The box.”

Sera stared at him.

Lu stared back.

Qin's mouth moved slightly.

Sera noticed.

[[speaker:sera]]“Do not encourage him.”

[[speaker:qin]]“I said nothing.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You enjoyed it.”

[[speaker:qin]]“That is private.”

Lu moved the first manifest aside.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Medical cargo is boring. That is why it is useful.”

Sera waited.

He continued.

“Medicine moves on predictable schedules. Temperature-controlled compounds use certain wagons. Certain routes. Certain rest intervals. Controlled toxins require declared handlers. If someone wants to hide a dangerous substance inside legitimate medical traffic, they still have to obey enough of the boring rules to keep the cargo stable.”

Sera nodded slowly.

[[speaker:sera]]“And those rules leave a trail.”

“Yes.”

Lu pointed at three route marks.

“The first crate entered Isgard through South Fen. The second through a western grain exchange. The third came by sea disguised as fever medication.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Different origins.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Officially.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Actually?”

Lu slid three invoices together.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“All three used the same wax supplier.”

Sera looked at him.

[[speaker:sera]]“That is it?”

“No.”

[[speaker:sera]]“The same rope?”

“No.”

[[speaker:sera]]“The same carpenter?”

Lu's face changed by half a degree.

Sera pointed at him.

[[speaker:sera]]“It is the carpenter.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“It is not the carpenter.”

Qin said, “You look disappointed.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“I am surrounded by children.”

Lu drew one invoice forward.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Same wax supplier. Same ceramic ampoule maker. Same transit insurer.”

Sera's amusement faded.

[[speaker:sera]]“Those are not accidental.”

“No.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Who?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Intermediaries.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Useful ones?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“One is already dead.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Of course.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“One relocated.”

“Where?”

“Unknown.”

[[speaker:sera]]“And the third?”

Lu looked toward the archive door.

A Quiet Hand operative entered carrying a sealed packet.

Lu took it, opened it and read.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Still operating.”

Sera's eyes sharpened.

“Where?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Port city. East coast.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Wuyue?”

“Yes.”

That changed the problem.

The source being Wuyue was less important than the route touching home.

Lu handed the packet to Sera.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Registered as a merchant brokerage. Mostly medical equipment, preserved herbs and sea transport contracts.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Mostly?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Enough legitimate business to survive inspection.”

Qin turned his head.

“Meaning if we kick the door in, half the evidence disappears into lawyers.”

Sera looked at him.

“Since when do you say lawyers?”

[[speaker:qin]]“Lu taught me.”

Lu looked pained.

“I did not.”

[[speaker:qin]]“You did. Repeatedly. Usually with hatred.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“That is not instruction.”

Sera smiled.

Then looked back at the manifest.

[[speaker:sera]]“Quiet surveillance.”

Lu nodded.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“No arrests yet.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Agreed.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“No Tae.”

Sera looked offended.

[[speaker:sera]]“I was not going to send Tae.”

Lu said nothing.

She narrowed her eyes.

[[speaker:sera]]“I was not.”

Qin smiled.

The door opened again.

Yun entered.

Lu looked at her hands first.

Both empty.

He relaxed slightly.

She noticed.

[[speaker:yun]]“You wound me.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“I am adapting.”

Luo came in behind her carrying a notebook.

[[speaker:luo]]“We have another component.”

Lu held up one finger.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Do not put it on the table.”

Luo stopped.

Yun looked delighted.

[[speaker:yun]]“I told you.”

Luo sighed.

Sera stood.

[[speaker:sera]]“What did you find?”

“Not origin,” Luo said. “Function.”

Yun answered more directly.

[[speaker:yun]]“The compound suppresses the body's own refusal.”

Lu looked at her.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“That sounds philosophical.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is physiological.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Better.”

Luo opened the notebook without setting it down.

“We think the first layer drives circulation. Another suppresses distress. A delayed component extends the high-output state after tissue begins failing.”

“How long?” Sera asked.

“Unknown.”

“Cost?”

“Unknown.”

Lu's expression remained flat.

“Excellent.”

Yun leaned against a cabinet.

“You are welcome.”

Sera pointed to the invoices.

“We have three medical transport chains converging through the same suppliers and one Wuyue broker.”

Yun's posture changed.

[[speaker:yun]]“Local production?”

“Not necessarily.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Then import.”

“That is what I think.”

Luo looked at the route map.

[[speaker:luo]]“From where?”

Lu tapped the sea lane.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Still buried under intermediaries.”

Yun stepped closer.

Lu immediately moved the manifests two inches away from her.

She stared at him.

He stared back.

[[speaker:yun]]“I am not poisonous.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“No. Your hobbies are.”

Yun looked at Luo.

[[speaker:yun]]“Defend me.”

“No.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Coward.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Alive.”

Sera rubbed her forehead.

The room felt absurdly normal.

That was useful.

Because beneath the jokes, everyone understood what the route implied.

A compound sophisticated enough to burn through a cultivator's body for temporary output was moving inside real medical logistics.

Not sold in one alley.

Not brewed by one reckless poisoner.

Packed.

Insured.

Routed.

Relabeled.

Someone had built a supply chain around it.

Lu traced one finger from Isgard to the eastern Wuyue port.

Then farther out across the blank space of the map.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“That is what bothers me.”

Sera looked at him.

“What?”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“The route does not begin anywhere.”

Yun's expression sharpened.

Lu continued.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Every record I have is middle.”

“Meaning?” Luo asked.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Meaning somebody did not merely hide the cargo.”

He tapped the far edge of the sea lane.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“They hid the country it came from behind enough legitimate trade that nobody bothered asking.”

The room went quiet.

Sera looked toward the blank eastern margin.

Wuyue knew the northern continent.

It knew Isgard because blood had forced them to learn each other.

Beyond the older trade routes lay names most people treated as merchant trivia.

Remote ports.

Foreign states.

Places whose politics had never mattered enough to become Wuyue's problem.

Perhaps one of them had just arrived in a sealed medical crate.

Lu gathered the manifests.

“I follow the boxes,” he said.

Yun smiled faintly.

[[speaker:yun]]“And I follow the poison.”

Luo looked between them.

[[speaker:luo]]“I follow both of you because neither respects sleep.”

Sera looked at Qin.

[[speaker:sera]]“What do you follow?”

Qin lifted his tea.

[[speaker:qin]]“Good judgment.”

Lu said, “Then you are in the wrong room.”

For once, everyone agreed.
