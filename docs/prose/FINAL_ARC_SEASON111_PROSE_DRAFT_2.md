## Chapter 465 — Three Times

Kael saw the first attack.

That was the problem.

He saw it.

Understood it.

Moved correctly.

And still arrived late.

Shunto crossed twenty paces before the mud displaced beneath his first visible step.

One hook flashed toward Kael's throat.

Kael leaned back.

The edge missed skin.

The guard ring touched his collarbone.

Lock.

Shunto's elbow arrived before Kael finished leaning.

Lock.

His broken wrist—reinforced now by Redline-driven qi hard enough to make the damaged bones scream—struck Kael's forearm.

Lock.

Palm to chest.

Lock.

Shoulder to ribs.

Lock.

Five contacts in less than one breath.

Kael threw a punch.

Shunto was already elsewhere.

Worldweight caught the force from his departure step.

Kael tried to redistribute it.

Shunto had crossed the road, touched a bridge rail, vaulted a wagon and returned from the opposite side before the pressure completed its route.

Kael turned.

Too slow.

A hook caught the back of his coat.

**Wolf Hook.**

The pull ripped him sideways.

Shunto's knee met his stomach.

Kael folded.

A hand struck the side of his neck.

Lock.

His right arm tingled.

Kael planted anyway.

Worldweight surged through the road.

He sent burden into the section beneath Shunto.

Shunto was already airborne.

Kael snarled.

“Annoying.”

Shunto landed on the side of a cart.

The wood exploded from the force.

Then he was gone again.

Luo tracked the motion with his eyes and hated every number he saw.

Three-times throughput did not make Shunto three times wiser.

It did not refine Hundred Locks Dominion.

It did not create skill he had not earned.

It simply removed the usual spaces between his decisions.

A High Paragon who normally needed a fraction of a breath to reset his stance now reset almost immediately.

A lock that normally required one clean entry could be followed by three more before the opponent had finished reacting to the first.

The body was being driven as though time owed it something.

Luo counted silently.

Nine seconds.

Twelve.

Fifteen.

Shunto struck Kael's left thigh.

Kael's knee dipped.

Another touch reached the same region.

A second lock stacked.

Kael forced circulation through the leg.

That purged one.

It cost concentration.

Shunto punished the concentration.

Hook across cheek.

Not the blade.

The flat guard.

Contact.

Lock.

Kael's jaw went numb.

Shunto smiled.

“You were enjoying yourself before.”

Kael spat blood.

“I still am.”

“That is stupid.”

“Yes.”

Shunto attacked.

Kael gave ground for the first time.

One step.

Two.

Three.

The corridor soldiers watching from Bridge Two understood what that meant.

Kael Veyran did not retreat easily.

Not because he was reckless.

Because his entire art rewarded connection with terrain.

Every backward step surrendered prepared ground.

Shunto was forcing him off his own world.

Luo saw Kael glance once toward the medical sheds.

Toward the two injured scouts.

Toward Luo.

Then Kael changed direction.

He stopped retreating toward them.

He began circling away.

Drawing Shunto with him.

Luo's expression tightened.

Of course.

Kael was not trying to improve his position.

He was trying to move the fight farther from the people behind him.

Shunto noticed too.

“You think you're leading me?”

Kael shrugged.

“You followed.”

Shunto's smile vanished.

He accelerated again.

Kael slammed both palms downward.

The road ruptured.

Not randomly.

Worldweight pushed accumulated pressure into three connected fault lines beneath the valley floor.

Stone heaved upward in staggered slabs.

Shunto could not simply sprint straight through.

He cut left.

Kael had prepared that.

A sand-packed retaining wall collapsed.

Shunto used the falling wall as a stepping surface.

Kael cursed.

High Paragon.

Every answer demanded another answer.

Shunto came over the wall upside down, hooks crossed.

Kael caught both wrists.

For one instant their bodies locked.

That was Kael's range.

Worldweight surged through his planted feet.

He drove Shunto downward.

Shunto touched Kael's wrists.

Locks.

Kael ignored them.

He twisted.

Shunto's body hit the road hard enough to split stone.

Kael lifted him again.

Shunto's knee struck Kael's ribs.

Lock.

Kael slammed him again.

The road cratered deeper.

Shunto's heel struck Kael's knee.

Lock.

Kael lifted a third time.

Shunto's hooked blade caught Kael's belt.

Wolf Hook pulled him off balance.

Shunto rotated.

Both men reversed.

Kael hit the ground instead.

Shunto's boot came down.

Kael rolled.

The boot shattered stone beside his ear.

Three-times force.

Kael swept Shunto's supporting leg.

Shunto hopped.

Kael smiled despite himself.

Too light.

No stored burden.

He had learned.

Luo counted.

Forty-two seconds.

Shunto's pulse was already wrong.

Not failing.

Wrong.

Too much pressure through damaged structures.

Broken wrist reinforced rather than healed.

Ribs stressed every breath.

Shoulder joint repeatedly forced beyond safe recovery.

Redline was letting him spend tomorrow's consequences today.

Luo began remembering every compensation.

Kael took another lock to the shoulder.

Then one to the calf.

His movement deteriorated.

Shunto's did not.

Not yet.

Kael tried to purge.

Shunto gave him no time.

**Closed Meridian Cage.**

Five rapid contacts hit Kael's left arm from shoulder to wrist.

Circulation through the limb nearly vanished.

The arm dropped.

Kael looked at it.

“That's inconvenient.”

Shunto drove a hook into his guard.

Kael blocked one-handed.

The impact threw him through a fence.

Wood snapped around him.

Shunto followed.

Kael kicked a fence post into his path.

Shunto cut it.

Kael used the cut pieces to hide his step.

His right fist hit Shunto's damaged ribs.

Hard.

Shunto grunted.

Kael hit the same place again.

Redline could drown pain.

It could not erase fractures.

Shunto's torso buckled.

Kael drove his shoulder through him and sent both men into the ditch.

Mud erupted.

For half a breath they were chest to chest.

Kael's one working hand found the back of Shunto's neck.

Shunto's fingers found Kael's sternum.

Lock.

Kael headbutted him.

Shunto's nose broke.

Kael laughed once.

Shunto's face changed.

He struck Kael's throat.

Not enough to crush it.

Enough to lock circulation around the neck and make breath hitch.

Kael's laugh ended.

Shunto drove an elbow into his temple.

Kael's vision flashed white.

Then another.

Kael blocked late.

Another lock.

The fight changed.

Not because Shunto became stronger.

Because Kael's usable body was disappearing piece by piece.

One arm.

Half a leg.

Fingers.

Shoulder.

Jaw.

Neck.

Hundred Locks Dominion's identity became brutally clear.

Every touch removed another part of the opponent's fighting capacity.

At normal speed, Kael could trade damage for those touches.

At three-times throughput, Shunto was collecting them faster than Kael could make the payment hurt enough.

Luo counted one minute and eighteen seconds.

Shunto looked at him again.

This time longer.

Luo understood.

The next phase had begun.

## Chapter 466 — Luo Becomes the Real Target

Shunto stopped looking at Kael as the problem.

That frightened Luo more than being looked at directly.

It meant Shunto had understood the battlefield.

Kael was the wall.

Luo was the reason the wall might eventually matter.

Shunto feinted right.

Kael moved with him.

Shunto did not attack.

He reversed mid-step, touched a broken bridge support, vaulted over Kael's shoulder and accelerated straight toward the medical wagons.

Toward Luo.

Kael's locked left leg responded late.

He was three paces behind before he even turned.

“Luo!”

Luo did not move.

That was not bravery.

Moving randomly in front of a High Paragon was suicide.

He watched Shunto's shoulders.

Right hook low.

Left hand open.

Pulse elevated.

Weight slightly forward because the damaged ribs disliked full rotation.

Luo stepped half a pace left.

Shunto adjusted.

Exactly.

A silver needle appeared between Luo's fingers.

He threw.

Shunto cut it out of the air.

Luo had expected that.

The needle was not poisoned.

Shunto's eyes narrowed.

Then Kael hit him from the side.

Not cleanly.

Desperately.

Shoulder to hip.

Both men crashed through a medical cart.

Kael landed badly.

His locked knee folded.

Shunto rolled to his feet almost instantly.

Kael did not.

Shunto moved toward Luo again.

Kael grabbed his ankle.

Shunto looked down.

Kael looked up from the broken cart.

“No.”

Shunto kicked him in the face.

Kael's head snapped back.

Blood sprayed across the wheel.

His grip loosened.

Shunto stepped free.

Kael caught the ankle again.

Shunto stared.

“You're making this tedious.”

Kael's mouth was full of blood.

“Good.”

Another kick.

Kael rolled with it and used the motion to pull Shunto off-line.

Not enough to topple him.

Enough to buy Luo one breath.

Luo used it.

He moved behind a stone supply marker rather than farther away.

Shunto saw.

“Still studying?”

“Yes.”

“You think poison will bridge a realm?”

“No.”

Shunto paused.

Luo continued.

“I think bodies remain bodies.”

Shunto laughed.

“Yun taught you that?”

The name stopped Kael more than Luo.

Only for a fraction.

Luo's expression did not change.

“Yes.”

Shunto watched him.

Something ugly entered his smile.

“She taught you poorly.”

Kael stood.

Slowly.

His left arm still hung useless.

One eye was swelling.

Blood ran from his mouth.

“Careful,” he said.

Shunto glanced at him.

“With what?”

Kael's voice lost every trace of humor.

“My patience.”

Shunto almost laughed.

Kael attacked.

Not to win.

Luo saw that immediately.

Kael attacked to occupy.

Every movement forced Shunto to answer him instead of crossing the remaining distance.

A kick that Shunto had to check.

A shoulder drive that had to be redirected.

A slab of road kicked upward to block line of sight.

A burst of Worldweight into the exact ground Shunto needed to push from.

Kael began spending himself deliberately.

Luo hated him for it.

He also used every second.

**Undertaker's Measure** had been running since the first exchange.

Not a glowing spell.

Not supernatural diagnosis from nowhere.

Luo watched.

Listened.

Measured.

Pulse visible at throat.

Breathing interval.

Recovery after heavy burst.

How Redline drove heat toward the skin.

How Shunto's injured wrist forced compensation through elbow and shoulder.

How Hundred Locks Dominion synchronized contact with tiny circulation pulses through the fingers.

How Shunto purged foreign qi after Kael's earth pressure entered too deeply.

How often his High-Paragon body corrected itself automatically.

Luo needed a pattern.

Shunto gave him hundreds.

Two minutes.

Redline window nearly half spent.

Shunto realized it too.

His aggression sharpened.

He stopped wasting touches on Kael's already deadened left arm.

He targeted what remained.

Right shoulder.

Right knee.

Core circulation.

Neck.

Kael blocked the hook and took a palm to the chest.

Lock.

His breath staggered.

Shunto slipped past.

Kael threw himself sideways.

He did not have time to plant.

No Worldweight answer.

Just body.

His shoulder caught Shunto's waist.

They hit the ground.

Shunto rolled on top.

His fist struck Kael's face.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Kael covered.

Shunto touched through the guard.

Locks compounded across both forearms.

Kael's hands slowed.

Shunto stood and kicked him in the ribs.

A cracked rib became a broken rib.

Kael grunted.

Shunto stepped around him toward Luo.

Kael's hand closed around his coat.

Shunto looked down again.

“Really?”

Kael pulled himself up using Shunto's body.

“Yes.”

Shunto backhanded him with the hook guard.

Kael went down.

He got up.

Shunto's face tightened.

Luo watched Kael's pulse now too.

Too fast.

Internal bleeding increasing.

Knee unstable.

Concussion likely.

Multiple locks interfering with compensation.

Kael needed to stop.

Kael could not stop.

Because if he stopped, Shunto reached Luo.

Luo reached into his sleeve.

A needle touched his palm.

Not yet.

He needed more.

Shunto's purge pattern was too clean.

A mistimed toxin would be noticed and removed.

Kael looked back at him once.

One eye barely open.

Luo understood the question.

How long?

Luo held up two fingers.

Not minutes.

Two more exchanges.

Kael nodded.

Shunto saw the gesture.

His expression changed.

“What was that?”

Kael smiled through blood.

“Nothing.”

Shunto's eyes went to Luo.

Then back.

He understood enough.

The next attack was not aimed at Kael at all.

Shunto threw one hook.

Not at Luo.

At the stone marker beside him.

Wolf Hook caught the iron ring embedded in it.

Shunto pulled.

The entire marker ripped sideways.

Luo had to move.

That was the point.

Shunto launched through the opening.

Kael was too far.

His locked knee failed on the first step.

He fell.

Shunto crossed the gap.

Luo saw the hook coming.

He raised one needle.

He knew it would not stop a High Paragon.

Then Kael's body hit Shunto from behind.

Kael had not run.

He had used Worldweight to collapse the ground beneath himself forward, turning the fall into a brutal sliding launch.

It was ugly.

Desperate.

Effective.

Shunto missed Luo by less than a handspan.

All three men hit the mud.

Kael wrapped both arms around Shunto's waist despite the left barely functioning.

Shunto drove his elbow backward.

Kael's cheekbone cracked.

He held on.

Another elbow.

Kael's nose broke.

He held on.

A third.

Kael's forehead opened against the hook guard.

He held on.

Luo moved away.

Not fleeing.

Creating distance for the next measurement.

Shunto roared and threw Kael over his shoulder.

Kael hit the road on his back.

The impact knocked breath from him.

Shunto looked at Luo.

Luo looked back.

“Two exchanges,” Shunto said.

Luo said nothing.

Shunto smiled.

“I heard.”

Then he turned back to Kael.

The smile disappeared.

“I only need one.”

## Chapter 467 — Number One Stands Here

Shunto broke Kael's right arm first.

Not by accident.

Not through accumulated strain.

Deliberately.

He caught the wrist with one hook, trapped the elbow with the other, and rotated in opposite directions.

Kael heard the bone go before he felt it.

A clean wet crack beneath armour and sleeve.

The forearm bent.

Kael's face tightened.

Shunto kicked the broken limb aside and drove a palm into the shoulder.

Hundred Locks sealed what circulation remained.

The arm dropped uselessly.

Kael now had no reliable arms.

Shunto looked at Luo.

“One.”

Kael headbutted him.

Shunto staggered half a step.

Kael laughed.

It sounded wrong through the blood in his mouth.

“Still one.”

Shunto's expression went blank.

Then he grabbed Kael by the hair and back of the neck.

He drove his face into the road.

Stone cracked.

Kael's forehead struck first.

Then cheek.

Shunto lifted him.

Slammed him again.

The second impact cratered the already broken surface.

Kael's vision disappeared for a moment.

When it returned, the world was sideways.

Mud.

Blood.

Boots.

Luo twenty paces away.

Shunto's hand still in his hair.

“Stay down.”

Kael tried to stand.

Shunto kicked him in the face.

The blow rolled him across the road.

One tooth came loose and disappeared into the mud.

Kael stopped on his side.

His body did not answer immediately.

Shunto walked past him.

Toward Luo.

Kael moved one leg.

The locked knee shook.

He planted the other.

Pushed.

Nothing.

Shunto kept walking.

Kael dragged himself up using the broken bridge rail.

One arm hung wrong.

The other barely closed.

His face was almost unrecognizable beneath swelling and blood.

He took one step.

The compromised knee buckled.

He caught himself against the rail with his shoulder.

Shunto glanced back.

Annoyance replaced surprise.

“You cannot stop me.”

Kael spat red into the road.

“No.”

“Then why are you standing?”

Kael looked at Luo.

Luo had a needle between two fingers now.

Still waiting.

Kael looked back at Shunto.

“Because he's not done.”

Shunto's eyes narrowed.

He turned fully.

“You are Wuyue's Number One.”

Kael smiled with half his mouth.

“That's why I'm standing here.”

Shunto crossed the distance instantly.

His kick hit Kael's chest.

Ribs broke.

Kael flew backward through the bridge rail.

Wood exploded.

He hit a support stone and slid down.

Something inside him tore.

Luo felt cold.

Internal bleeding.

Severe.

Shunto walked to Kael.

Not Luo.

Not yet.

He had become angry enough to need the obstacle to understand that it had lost.

That was useful.

Luo hated himself for thinking it.

He used the seconds anyway.

Shunto seized Kael by the front of his coat and hauled him upright.

“Look at yourself.”

Kael's left eye opened.

“Seen worse.”

Shunto punched him in the stomach.

Kael folded.

A second punch hit the damaged ribs.

Then a knee.

Then Shunto threw him to the ground.

Kael hit shoulder-first.

The joint dislocated.

Shunto stomped the compromised knee.

The joint collapsed sideways.

Kael screamed.

The sound was brief.

Controlled almost immediately.

Shunto stared down at him.

“There.”

Kael breathed in broken pieces.

“There what?”

“You finally sound human.”

Kael laughed.

It became a cough.

Blood touched his lips.

“I've always been human.”

Shunto kicked him in the face again.

Kael rolled.

Another kick struck the side of his head.

His body went loose.

Luo's heart stopped for one beat.

Kael did not move.

Shunto turned.

Luo lowered his needle slightly.

Not surrender.

Preparation.

Shunto walked toward him.

Five paces.

Four.

Three.

Behind him, Kael's fingers moved.

Shunto did not notice.

Luo did.

Two paces.

Kael rolled onto his stomach.

One pace.

Shunto raised a hook.

Kael caught his ankle.

Shunto froze.

Not because the grip was strong.

It was barely a grip.

Two numb fingers and the crook of a ruined arm around the boot.

But it stopped the step.

One fraction.

Luo's needle moved.

Shunto kicked backward without looking.

His heel struck Kael's face.

Kael's grip broke.

But the needle had already passed.

Shunto turned his hook and cut it out of the air.

Metal clicked.

The needle spun into the mud.

Shunto smiled.

“Missed.”

Luo looked at the discarded needle.

“Yes.”

Shunto's smile faded.

Something in Luo's tone bothered him.

He looked down at his own sleeve.

A second needle was embedded near the elbow.

Not deep.

Barely through cloth and skin.

Shunto ripped it out instantly.

His qi surged through the region.

Purge.

Luo watched.

There.

The exact route.

The same correction he had seen six times already.

Shunto burned the foreign substance out before it entered anything important.

He tossed the needle aside.

“You waited all this time for that?”

Luo's voice remained quiet.

“No.”

Shunto took one step toward him.

Luo's eyes went to the pulse in his throat.

Redline had three minutes and forty-seven seconds behind it.

The damaged ribs were forcing slightly asymmetrical breath.

The broken wrist was being held together by reinforcement and stubbornness.

The shoulder was compensating.

The purge had just routed blood and qi through exactly the channel Luo needed confirmed.

Behind Shunto, Kael moved again.

Not up.

Not yet.

But alive.

Luo looked at him.

Kael's one open eye found his.

Luo gave the smallest nod.

Done.

Kael understood.

Shunto saw the exchange.

His face changed.

He turned on Kael with pure fury.

“You.”

Kael smiled from the ground.

“Me.”

Shunto crossed back to him and seized his coat.

Luo did not stop him.

Not yet.

Shunto hauled Kael upright again.

Kael's legs barely supported him.

Shunto drove him into the bridge pillar.

Once.

Twice.

The back of Kael's head struck stone.

Blood marked it.

A hook caught his side and ripped armour open.

Shunto's knee hammered into the exposed ribs.

Kael sagged.

Shunto hit him again.

And again.

Then threw him face-first into the road.

His broken arm folded beneath him.

Shunto kicked him in the ribs.

Once.

Twice.

Three times.

Redline kept the breath powerful.

It could not make it quiet anymore.

Luo heard every asymmetry.

Every compensation.

Every clue.

“Now.”

“Now what?”

At Kael.

One knee destroyed.

One arm broken.

Other arm functionally locked.

Multiple ribs broken.

Shoulder out.

Internal bleeding.

Severe head trauma.

Circulation torn apart by overlapping Hundred Locks.
