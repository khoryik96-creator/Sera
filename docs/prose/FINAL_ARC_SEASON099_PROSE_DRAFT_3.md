## Chapter 348 — Hana Moves One Patrol

Hana Arakawa knew Yurushi was lying before he finished the first sentence.

Not because he was bad at lying.

Because he only became excessively precise when hiding something important.

“The eastern medical corridor has an unnecessary double patrol between third and fourth bell,” he said.

Hana looked up from the deployment ledger.

They had not been alone in a room like this for almost two years.

That fact sat between them without needing help.

Her office overlooked one of Shinsei’s inner administrative courtyards. Wind moved through the open lattice shutters, lifting the corner of a map.

Hana pressed it down.

“You came here to discuss patrol efficiency.”

“Yes.”

“No.”

Yurushi considered.

“Partly.”

“Still no.”

He stood across from her desk in a dark travel coat that had been repaired at the shoulder by someone with poor matching thread.

Hana noticed because she had once repaired his sword wrappings for four years and therefore possessed the deeply irritating ability to recognize his maintenance habits at a glance.

He noticed her noticing.

Neither commented.

Hana said, “What do you want?”

“One patrol moved.”

“Which?”

“Eastern medical corridor. Third bell rotation.”

“Why?”

Yurushi was quiet.

There.

The real conversation.

Hana leaned back.

She was Shinsei No.6 now.

Established Paragon.

Enough authority to alter a patrol assignment without asking anyone above her, provided the change remained within ordinary command logic.

Also enough responsibility to know when someone was trying to borrow that authority without explaining why.

“Yurushi.”

He looked at her.

“Someone is being held wrongly.”

Hana’s expression changed.

“Wrongly how?”

“I am not telling you everything.”

“That is a terrible opening.”

“I know.”

“Shunto?”

He did not answer.

That was answer enough.

Hana looked toward the courtyard.

Below, clerks crossed carrying dispatch boxes.

Shinsei worked.

Always.

That had once been what she loved most about it.

No matter which clan name someone carried, there was a road office, a bridge crew, a medicine storehouse, a chain of responsibility.

Yurushi had once loved that too.

Then he started asking what happened when the chain itself became the thing hurting people.

She had answered that decent people had to remain inside it.

He had answered that remaining could become permission.

Four years of shared meals had survived missions, wounds and long absences.

That argument did not.

Hana looked back at him.

“Is this person dangerous?”

“Yes.”

“At least you are honest.”

“Dangerous is not the same as deserving whatever is being done.”

“Foreign?”

“Yes.”

“Spy?”

“Probably.”

Hana closed her eyes for one second.

“You have improved nothing.”

“I am trying not to make you responsible for information you cannot unknow.”

“That is very generous of you.”

“It is also practical.”

“There you are.”

A faint smile touched his face and disappeared.

Hana hated that she remembered exactly what that expression looked like across a breakfast table.

She picked up the patrol ledger.

“What do you know for certain?”

Yurushi answered carefully.

“A foreign woman was captured alive after a long pursuit. She is badly injured. She is being held under Second Seat’s personal security chain inside a medical ward. Documentation has been narrowed. Staff have been instructed not to record everything they observe.”

Hana’s hand stopped.

Yurushi continued.

“I saw enough to know something crossed a line.”

“What line?”

His eyes met hers.

He did not need to say it.

Hana understood.

The room became colder despite the open wind.

She looked down at the ledger.

Shunto was difficult.

Useful.

Brilliant at capture.

Trusted too often because his results ended conflicts cleanly on paper.

Hana had disliked him for years without possessing one clean event strong enough to force the institution to confront what he was.

Now Yurushi stood in her office asking for one patrol.

Not a confession.

Not a rebellion.

One patrol.

Hana said, “Who is she?”

“I do not know exactly.”

That surprised her.

“You are helping someone whose name you do not know.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Yurushi’s face softened in a way she remembered too well.

“Because knowing her name should not be the price of deciding this is wrong.”

Hana looked away first.

There it was.

The reason she had loved him.

Also the reason they had broken.

He made moral decisions before institutions gave permission.

She feared what happened when everyone did that.

He feared what happened when nobody did.

Neither fear had become less reasonable.

Hana opened the patrol ledger.

“The east medical corridor double-rotation exists because the north service yard is under structural inspection.”

“I know.”

“If I move one unit, the gap is nine minutes.”

“Enough.”

“You said ten.”

“Plans improve.”

She almost smiled.

Did not.

“Where do I send them?”

“West archive inspection.”

“That is too obvious. Shunto will see the connection.”

Yurushi considered.

“South bridge inventory?”

“Better.”

Hana made one notation.

Routine manpower correction due to overlapping inspection duty.

Plausible.

Boring.

Deniable.

She sanded the ink.

Then looked at him.

“This is one patrol.”

“Yes.”

“One.”

“I heard you.”

“I am not joining whatever network you built after leaving.”

“I did not ask.”

“I am not helping a foreign intelligence operation against Shinrin.”

“I did not ask.”

“I am moving one patrol because a medical-security chain is being used in a way I cannot justify without investigation.”

Yurushi nodded.

“Understood.”

Hana hated how easy he made it to preserve the boundary.

She wanted him to argue.

Maybe then the old argument would feel simpler.

Instead he stepped back.

“Thank you.”

“Do not.”

He paused.

“Do not thank me like this is absolution for either of us.”

Yurushi’s expression went still.

“I would not.”

Good.

He turned toward the door.

Hana spoke before he reached it.

“Yurushi.”

He looked back.

“If Shunto asks, I moved the patrol because the structural inspection duplicated manpower.”

“Yes.”

“And if that person is more than you think—”

“She probably is.”

Hana exhaled.

“Of course she is.”

For the first time, he smiled properly.

Then left.

Hana stared at the ledger after the door closed.

One patrol.

Nine minutes.

A decision small enough to hide inside administration.

Large enough, perhaps, to change a continent.

She did not know that yet.

All she knew was that someone had been hunted wrongly, Yurushi had asked once, and she still trusted the part of him that had never learned how to ignore a locked door.

## Chapter 349 — She Escapes on Her Own Feet

The restraint opened on the second try.

Yun hated that.

The first try had taken twenty-three seconds and nearly snapped the improvised shim.

The second took nine.

Nine was acceptable.

Her right wrist came free at third bell plus four minutes.

The patrol gap began at plus six.

Two minutes.

Yun removed the left restraint, stood and nearly fell.

Her hip failed once.

She caught the bedframe.

Breathed.

Tried again.

Own feet.

That had been the rule.

Not because being carried was shameful.

Because Yurushi needed both hands and full attention for routes, and Yun needed enough control over her body to choose what happened next.

She took the hidden medicine.

Not enough to numb pain.

Enough to keep circulation from collapsing during movement.

Then she retrieved the Axtaya capillary from the wrist brace.

Still intact.

She wrapped it beneath a fresh bandage.

Physical proof.

The notebook remained with Shunto.

Unfortunate.

Not fatal.

The coded packet might already be moving.

Memory remained complete.

Three layers had become two and a possibility.

Enough.

The door clicked.

Not opened.

Unlocked.

Yun waited.

One second.

Two.

No savior appeared.

Good.

She opened it herself.

The corridor was empty.

Nine-minute patrol gap.

Yun stepped out.

Pain changed her gait.

She adjusted.

Not normal.

Normal was impossible.

She aimed for medically plausible: injured patient moved under administrative confusion.

At the first junction, a nurse looked up.

Yun lifted a folded treatment sheet Yurushi had left beneath the tray.

The nurse glanced at it.

Then at Yun.

“Transfer?”

Yun nodded tiredly.

“South annex.”

The nurse pointed the wrong direction.

“Other hall.”

“Thank you.”

Yun went the correct wrong way.

First junction cleared.

At the service stair, a guard appeared early.

Of course.

Plans failed at eight minutes.

Yun had told Yurushi.

The guard frowned.

“Where is your escort?”

Yun coughed.

Real cough.

Pain helped.

“Physician sent me downstairs.”

“Which physician?”

Yun gave the name of the midnight doctor.

The guard knew him.

Bad.

“He is not on shift.”

“Then he should stop signing forms while sleeping.”

The guard took the sheet.

Yun let him.

His eyes dropped.

That was the opening.

She touched two fingers to the inside of his wrist.

Tiny contact.

Tiny toxin.

Not enough to injure.

Enough to create sudden pins-and-needles across the hand.

He jerked.

Yun caught the sheet.

“Circulation problem,” she said professionally. “You should get that checked.”

He stared at his hand.

Yun walked past.

Not ran.

Running confessed.

At the stair turn, she ran.

The service gate below was open exactly twelve finger-widths.

Yurushi had promised no miracles.

Apparently he had promised inconvenient architecture.

Yun turned sideways and forced herself through.

Her ribs protested violently.

She did not scream.

Outside, rain hit her face.

Good.

Weather complicated tracking.

A shadow moved under the eave.

Yurushi stepped from it.

Not teleportation.

He had been physically pressed behind a storage buttress where the darkness concealed him.

“Late,” he said.

“Guard.”

“Problem?”

“His hand tingles.”

Yurushi stared.

Yun kept moving.

“He will recover.”

“I was not asking medically.”

“Then ask better questions.”

He almost laughed.

They crossed the yard.

No dramatic sprint.

Two workers rolled a medicine cart past.

Yurushi took the handle from one as though expected.

The worker did not object.

Yun climbed into the covered rear compartment among empty herb crates.

“Humiliating,” she muttered.

“Alive.”

“Both can be true.”

The cart moved.

At the outer yard, a clerk checked the manifest.

Real seal.

Old credential.

No forgery required.

Yurushi complained about spoiled feverroot for thirty seconds until the clerk became eager to make him somebody else’s problem.

The gate opened.

They left.

Yun counted turns inside the cart.

One.

Two.

Three.

At the fourth, she climbed out before Yurushi told her.

He looked back.

“What are you doing?”

“Changing route.”

“This is the route.”

“It was. The second guard felt toxin. When he reports, medical transport becomes obvious.”

Yurushi considered for half a second.

Then abandoned the cart without argument.

Good.

They moved through a dye alley.

Yun chose the rear door because the front had fresh mud without footprints.

Inside, the owner looked at Yurushi’s seal and swore.

“Again?”

“Sorry.”

“You are never sorry.”

“True.”

The man opened a freight panel.

No questions.

Yun noticed he never looked directly at her face.

Professional mercy.

They crossed into the next lane.

A patrol bell sounded behind them.

Early.

Shunto knew.

Yun said, “How far to the old clan path?”

“Three districts.”

“Too far.”

“Alternative?”

“Clinic exchange yard.”

“That is monitored.”

“Not the waste channel.”

Yurushi looked at her.

“You mapped that?”

“Three months.”

“Fair.”

They changed direction.

This was the point that mattered.

Yurushi was not rescuing a passive body through his perfect route.

Yun was using what she knew.

He was using what he knew.

The plan changed every five minutes because reality refused to respect authorship.

At the clinic yard, Yun contaminated one pursuit path with a harmless but alarming red-reactive powder.

The first Shinsei team stopped rather than risk unknown toxin.

Procedure.

Shunto’s own improvements now bought her time.

Yun nearly smiled.

“His training helps,” she said.

Yurushi understood immediately.

“That is rude.”

“Yes.”

They crossed the waste channel.

Yun slipped once.

Yurushi reached for her.

She flinched before she could stop herself.

His hand stopped in the air.

He did not touch her.

Did not apologize too loudly.

Did not make the moment about his feelings.

He simply said, “Wall on your left.”

Yun caught the wall herself.

“Thank you.”

They continued.

Yurushi watched her for several steps.

Not the blood.

Not only the damaged circulation marks around her wrists.

The whole picture.

The too-careful way she kept one side away from contact. The medicinal smell of a detention compound. The bruising that did not match a clean arrest. The exhaustion beneath the poisoner's discipline. And the reflex that had stopped his hand before either of them chose it.

His face went still.

“What did Takamori do?”

Yun's eyes met his.

For one moment, something behind them became very cold.

Then she looked toward the route north.

“Later.”

He did not move.

Yun added, “If there is one.”

That answered enough.

Not everything.

Enough.

Yurushi's jaw tightened.

Yun pointed north.

“Isgard first.”

He nodded once.

No demand for details.

No promise to avenge her.

No attempt to turn her escape into his anger.

“Isgard first,” he agreed.

By dusk, they reached the forgotten ridge path.

Yun’s breathing had become shallow.

Her right leg dragged slightly.

Yurushi noticed.

“Rest.”

“No.”

“You collapse here, the route dies with you.”

That was a better argument.

Yun sat for four minutes.

Exactly four.

She drank water.

Checked the bandage around the capillary.

Still intact.

Then stood.

Yurushi did not offer a hand.

Good.

They moved north.

Behind them, Shinsei patrols began closing roads.

Ahead, the ridge disappeared into rain.

Yun escaped on her own feet.

Not alone.

Those were different truths.

## Chapter 350 — Yurushi Stays Behind

At the last cache, Yurushi gave Yun his coat.

She refused.

“You need it.”

“So do you.”

“I am going back.”

“That is an argument for keeping your coat.”

“I have another.”

“Where?”

“Cache.”

Yun looked around the tiny stone shelter.

One blanket.

Medicine.

Dried food.

Two civilian travel papers.

No coat.

“You are lying.”

“Yes.”

She was too tired to fight properly, so she took it.

The coat was warm.

That annoyed her.

They had reached the northern ridge beyond Shinsei’s ordinary administrative road net.

From here, an old civilian-medical trail descended toward territory with Isgard-linked trade contacts.

Not safe.

Safer.

Yun sat on a stone bench while Yurushi checked the next trail marker.

Her hands shook now even when she was not cold.

Too much forced circulation.

Too little food.

Injuries layered over injuries.

The escape medicine had bought function.

It had not created health.

Yurushi returned.

“Marker is clean.”

“For now.”

“Yes.”

“How long to Isgard contact?”

“Two days if healthy.”

Yun looked at him.

He corrected.

“Three to four.”

“Better.”

“You should take five.”

“I do not have five.”

He did not argue.

The four-month readiness window hung over everything now.

Yurushi still did not know the full scale.

Yun had told him only what he needed for the escape: Shinsei was preparing external military action toward Isgard and the warning had to leave.

He had not asked for more while they were moving.

Now he did.

“How bad?”

Yun looked south.

Shinrin lay beyond the ridges.

Roads.

Clinics.

Granaries.

Ten Paragons.

More than two hundred thousand trained apparatus.

Axtaya.

A state built to end one kind of violence preparing another.

“Bad enough that Isgard needs the warning before Shinsei knows it has been warned.”

Yurushi nodded once.

No demand for numbers.

Good.

Yun said, “Come with me.”

He looked at her.

“You already asked.”

“I am asking again because now Shunto knows the escape was internal.”

“Yes.”

“He will hunt whoever built the corridor.”

“Yes.”

“He may find Hana’s patrol change.”

Yurushi’s face became still.

Yun noticed.

“Hana.”

He did not answer quickly enough.

“Interesting.”

“Not important.”

“Very important to your face.”

“This is not the time.”

“Probably not.”

Yun leaned back against the wall.

Then more seriously:

“If you stay, you become a target.”

“If I leave, everyone attached to these roads becomes the target without anyone here to close them.”

“You cannot protect all of them.”

“No.”

“Shunto is High Paragon.”

Yurushi looked at her.

For the first time, Yun felt him stop hiding completely.

Not an aura burst.

Not pressure.

Just the absence of pretense.

The qi inside him was dense.

Very dense.

High Paragon dense.

Yun stared.

“You are joking.”

“No.”

“You let me think you were merely suspicious.”

“I was trying humility.”

“It does not suit you.”

“Agreed.”

Yun laughed once and immediately regretted it because her ribs hurt.

“High Paragon?”

“Yes.”

“Ranked?”

“No.”

“Why?”

“I dislike what rankings become when governments own them.”

There was history again.

Yun let it rest.

A High Paragon changed the risk.

Did not remove it.

Shunto was still No.2, backed by the state, and Yurushi’s goal was not to fight him openly.

“Then survive,” Yun said.

“I plan to.”

“You sound like someone who thinks planning matters.”

“It occasionally helps.”

Outside, a bird startled from the lower trees.

Yurushi moved to the doorway.

Listened.

No immediate pursuit.

He returned and placed three packets on the bench.

“Pain control. Circulation support. Fever prevention.”

Yun checked each.

“Good.”

“I know.”

“Arrogant.”

“Also true.”

He gave her one of the civilian travel papers.

The identity described a widowed herb buyer from a northern hill district.

Yun read it.

“Forty-one?”

Yurushi looked at her injuries.

“You currently look tired enough to be fifty.”

She stared.

He backed toward the door.

“High Paragon reflexes,” he said. “Useful.”

Yun considered poisoning him.

Not enough time.

She folded the paper.

“What happens when Shunto finds you?”

“He will ask questions.”

“And?”

“I will disappoint him.”

That sounded familiar.

Yun understood why she liked him.

Annoying.

She stood.

Her knees nearly failed.

Yurushi did not move to catch her.

He waited until she stabilized.

Then pointed north.

“Follow the old stone markers until the third broken shrine. Do not take the river bridge even if it looks open.”

“Why?”

“Inspection post.”

“Of course.”

“After the shrine, take the goat trail west. It meets a medical caravan road used by Isgard-linked traders.”

“Names?”

He gave two.

Yun memorized them.

Then looked at him.

“Yurushi.”

“Yes?”

“If I make it—”

“When.”

She ignored him.

“If I make it, this becomes larger than your routes.”

“I know.”

“Shinsei may go to war.”

“I know.”

“You helped cause the warning.”

Yurushi looked south.

“No. Shinsei caused the warning.”

Yun held his gaze.

Then nodded.

Good answer.

She left.

The first hundred steps were manageable.

The next hundred were worse.

By the time she reached the tree line, her vision had begun narrowing at the edges.

She stopped.

Breathed.

Counted pulse.

Too fast.

She took the circulation support, not the pain medicine.

Pain could stay.

Function mattered more.

Behind her, Yurushi remained at the shelter until she disappeared over the ridge.

Then he turned south.

By nightfall, Shunto stood inside the abandoned medical ward staring at an open restraint brace.

The door lock showed no forced damage.

The patrol ledger showed one routine reassignment.

The service gate had opened under a legitimate maintenance credential.

A medical cart passed under a valid old seal.

Nothing looked like one betrayal.

That was the problem.

Someone understood the institution well enough to make its own ordinary systems carry an escape.

Shunto picked up a tiny strip of black paper from beneath the bed.

Most of it had burned.

One corner remained.

Dark paper.

His face changed.

Not recognition of a person.

Recognition of method.

Someone inside—or close enough to inside—had helped her.

“Find the corridor,” he said.

A guard bowed.

“And the person?”

Shunto looked at the open door.

“Yes.”

Far north, Yun walked alone beneath Yurushi’s coat.

The warning was moving again.

So was the damage inside her.

And for the first time since entering Shinrin, there was no locked room behind the next door.
