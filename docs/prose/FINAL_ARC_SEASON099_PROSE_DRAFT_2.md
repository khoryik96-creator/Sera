## Chapter 345 — Black Radiance Notices

The second packet of medicine was different.

Yun knew before opening it.

The first had been cautious.

Anti-inflammatory powder.

Gauze.

Circulation salve.

Useful, but deniable as a clerical mistake.

The second contained a tiny folded strip of black paper.

Nothing written on it.

Just black.

Yun stared at it for a long time.

Then smelled the medicine.

Same quality.

Different compound.

This one treated restraint bruising and meridian irritation without sedating the patient.

Whoever sent it understood that sedation would make an escape harder.

That was not random kindness.

It was operational kindness.

More dangerous.

Yun did not use it immediately.

She tested a grain against the skin of her forearm.

Waited.

No burning.

No numbing agent.

No hidden tracer she could detect.

She dissolved another grain in basin water and watched for reactive color.

Nothing.

Then she waited another hour because paranoia had become a professional requirement.

Still nothing.

Finally, she used half the dose.

The pain around her shoulder eased without dulling thought.

Good.

Someone knew medicine.

Or knew someone who did.

The next help came as a late door.

At the fourth bell, the corridor guard normally opened Yun’s room for physician inspection.

That morning, the lock turned eleven seconds late.

Eleven seconds was not much.

It was enough for Yun to move the basin pipe fitting half a turn and return to bed before the door opened.

The guard entered angry.

“Key stuck.”

The physician shrugged.

Yun looked at the lock.

No visible damage.

Interesting.

The third help came as a missing patrol.

During a scheduled walk to the washroom, two corridor guards escorted Yun through the eastern service hall.

Normally a secondary patrol crossed the junction halfway through.

That day, nobody came.

Yun counted the empty seconds.

Nine.

Ten.

Eleven.

The same imprecision again.

Not exact.

Close enough to feel deliberate.

She glanced toward the service stair.

Open.

The guard beside her pulled the restraint lead.

“Move.”

Yun moved.

She did not take the opening.

A convenient savior was often just a more creative captor.

That evening, she found the black paper moved.

Yun had hidden it beneath the mattress seam.

Now it lay two finger-widths to the left.

Someone had entered while she was out.

Not searched.

Marked.

She picked it up.

On the reverse, in pale gray powder, one short line had appeared.

**Do not trust the north stair.**

Yun almost smiled.

At least the mysterious helper shared her hatred of convenient exits.

She burned the paper over the fixed lamp’s protected vent as best she could.

The ash disappeared into the grate.

The next day, Shunto did not visit.

The day after, the corridor changed.

Two more guards.

Different physician.

The north stair was sealed for “maintenance.”

Yun looked at the sign as she passed.

So the warning had been real.

Or beautifully self-confirming.

Still not enough.

She needed to know who was moving the pieces.

The answer arrived three nights later carrying feverroot.

Yun heard the argument before she saw the man.

“I have authorization.”

“You have old authorization.”

“Old medicine works on new fevers.”

“That is not how authorization works.”

“It should be.”

A guard sighed with the exhaustion of someone who had lost this argument before.

The door to Yun’s corridor opened.

A tall man entered carrying two wooden medicine boxes.

Dark hair.

Travel-worn black-gray coat.

No Shinsei seat insignia.

No official rank marker.

He moved like a person deliberately trying not to move like a cultivator.

That was the first suspicious thing.

The second was that his shadow did not quite match the hallway lamp.

Only for a fraction of a second.

Then it did.

Yun watched him through the small observation gap as he argued with the clinic steward.

“Feverroot goes to the lower ward.”

“That corridor is under security restriction.”

“So fever agreed to stay downstairs?”

The steward looked murderous.

The man smiled pleasantly.

Not charming.

Annoying.

Yun approved cautiously.

He passed her door without looking at it.

Good.

Then one box slipped.

Not dropped.

Slipped.

The lid shifted open.

Inside were packets wrapped in the same paper as the medicine Yun had received.

The man caught the box.

Closed it.

Kept walking.

Yun’s pulse changed.

There.

He did not look back.

That was also good.

Later, the evening physician called him by name while complaining about inventory.

“Amagiri, if you keep bringing outside stock into secured wards—”

Yun stored it.

Amagiri.

The name meant nothing to her yet.

The man answered from the hallway.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Then people will keep receiving medicine.”

“You are not Shinsei medical command.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I noticed.”

“You are not Shinsei anything.”

A pause.

Then:

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“That is mostly why I sleep well.”

The physician muttered something rude.

Footsteps receded.

Yun lay back.

Not Shinsei anything.

Yet allowed into a restricted medical facility with old authorization, enough local familiarity to irritate staff by name and enough cultivation control to make his shadow behave incorrectly.

Interesting.

At midnight, Yun found a fresh strip of black paper beneath her tray.

One line.

**Second Seat moves you in two days.**

No request for trust.

No dramatic promise.

Just information.

Yun burned it.

Then began building a test.

If Amagiri wanted to help, he would have to survive being treated like a possible enemy first.

That was fair.

## Chapter 346 — Yun Does Not Trust a Convenient Savior

Yun’s test was a lie about poison.

A small one.

The next medicine packet contained circulation salve and clean binding cloth.

Yun used the cloth to hide a harmless yellow powder beneath the edge of the basin.

Then she left a coded mark on the wall where only someone deliberately checking the room would notice it.

The mark meant nothing.

The powder meant less.

What mattered was what the helper did after finding both.

If the next packet contained antidote, the helper was watching too closely.

If guards suddenly searched the basin, the helper was Shinsei.

If nothing changed, perhaps the channel was genuinely one-way.

The next packet arrived at dusk.

No antidote.

No warning.

One folded black strip.

**Your yellow powder is insulting.**

Yun stared at it.

Then laughed despite the pain.

Quietly.

One point to Amagiri.

Beneath the sentence:

**Also harmless. If you are going to test me, use something interesting.**

Two points.

She burned the note.

The next day, he appeared during physician rounds.

This time he carried no feverroot.

He carried a repair ledger.

Apparently his talents included being where paperwork allowed him to be.

Yun watched through the door gap.

Amagiri stopped at the basin pipe outside her room and argued with a maintenance worker about water pressure.

Then he crouched.

His shadow stretched beneath the door.

Too far.

No teleportation.

No vanishing.

Just a physical dark route cast under the angle of the hallway lamp, manipulated by Darkness Qi enough to carry concealment where it should not naturally exist.

Yun felt a tiny object slide along the floor inside the shadow and stop beneath her bed.

Amagiri stood.

The shadow returned to normal.

He left.

Yun waited five minutes before retrieving the object.

A key fragment.

Not a full key.

A molded impression of the restraint brace’s left-side pins.

Useful.

Not sufficient.

Good.

A savior would have sent a key and expected gratitude.

A professional sent one ingredient.

Yun began to reconsider him.

That night, she wrote on the back of a medicine label:

**Need right-side pin pattern. Need service-gate cycle. Do not open door.**

She left it beneath the tray.

The message vanished before morning.

No reply came for two days.

That was better than instant competence.

Then the physician who used the alternate key arrived during the midnight round.

His keyring had changed.

One key missing.

He cursed for three minutes.

The guard blamed laundry staff.

The physician blamed everyone.

Yun kept her face blank.

The next morning, a sliver of wax appeared inside the hem of a clean medical wrap.

Right-side pin impression.

Amagiri had not manufactured a miracle.

He had stolen access long enough to copy it.

Now they could work.

Yun spent the day shaping a release shim from basin metal and wax measurements.

The restraint would not open completely.

It did not need to.

She only needed one wrist free for twelve seconds.

At evening meal, a shadow paused beneath the door.

This time a voice came with it.

Very low.

“Can you walk?”

Yun did not answer immediately.

The voice waited.

Good.

[[speaker:yun]]“Define walk.”

A soft breath that might have been laughter.

“Own feet. No carrying.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Then yes.”

“Fast?”

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

“Fight?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Briefly.”

“Poison?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Always.”

“That was the answer I expected.”

Yun moved closer to the door.

[[speaker:yun]]“Name.”

“Yurushi Amagiri.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Affiliation.”

A pause.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Complicated.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Bad answer.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Independent.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Better.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Formerly close enough to Shinsei that old doors still remember me. Far enough away that current officers dislike it.”

Yun considered.

[[speaker:yun]]“Why?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Why help?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

Silence.

Then Yurushi said, “Because I saw you after they brought you in.”

Yun’s hand tightened.

He continued before she could speak.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I do not know who you are.”

True, perhaps.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I know Second Seat has a badly injured foreign prisoner hidden inside a medical security ward. I know the treatment is not ordinary detention. I know staff were told to document less than they observed.”

His voice stayed level.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“And I know wrong when I see it.”

Yun said, “That is not enough reason to risk a High Paragon.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“For some people it is.”

She hated that answer because part of her wanted to believe it.

So she did not.

Not yet.

[[speaker:yun]]“What do you want from me?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Nothing.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Everyone wants something.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I want you out before Takamori moves you somewhere my old doors do not remember.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That is something.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Fine. I want fewer locked rooms.”

Yun rested her head briefly against the door.

That answer sounded like history.

Not performance.

She said, “I have evidence.”

Yurushi became quiet.

“Of what?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Not telling you yet.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Good.”

That surprised her.

He continued.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“If you told me everything through a door after three notes, I would reconsider helping.”

Yun almost smiled.

Three points again.

She said, “The evidence cannot be left behind.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Where?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Some on me. Some may already be elsewhere. One notebook may be in Takamori’s possession.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Then the notebook is not tonight’s priority.”

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

Yun liked that he understood immediately.

“Your priority?” Yurushi asked.

[[speaker:yun]]“Get me far enough north that I can reach an Isgard-linked route.”

There was a pause.

Longer this time.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Isgard.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“That makes this larger than a prison escape.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Are you going to tell me how much larger?”

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Fair.”

Yun sat on the floor beside the door.

They built the first real plan in whispers.

Not Yurushi’s plan.

Not Yun’s.

A joint construction.

He knew guard bureaucracy, maintenance access, forgotten service corridors and which patrols were staffed by people likely to obey procedure instead of improvising.

Yun knew her own body, Shunto’s pursuit logic, poison dispersal, which medical compounds in the ward could become obstacles and exactly how much circulation she could force before collapse.

Yurushi proposed a west service exit.

Yun rejected it because Shunto would expect an outsider to prefer civilian density.

Yun proposed a drainage route.

Yurushi rejected it because the lower channel had been sealed after flood repairs last year.

He suggested the old export clinic.

Yun asked for three exits.

It had four.

Better.

At the end, Yurushi said, “I can create ten minutes.”

Yun answered, “I need seven.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Why not take ten?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Because if you promise ten, something will happen at eight.”

A pause.

“Fair.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Plan for seven.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Done.”

The shadow withdrew.

Yun returned to bed before the next guard passed.

She still did not trust Yurushi Amagiri.

Not completely.

Trust was not a door one walked through once.

It was a series of accurate predictions.

So far, he had made several. Enough to build with.

## Chapter 347 — The Roads He Built for Other People

The first cache predated Yun’s investigation, changing what the entire route network meant.

Yurushi showed her the ledger after she asked the obvious question.

“How long have you had this route?”

They were not escaping yet.

Not officially.

Yun had been moved from the secure medical room to a temporary treatment annex through a forged maintenance error Yurushi arranged to last less than one hour.

The annex had one blind corner.

They used it.

Yurushi stood beside a cabinet while Yun sat on a stool pretending to be too injured to move.

He opened a false panel.

Inside were dried medicine packets, two merchant seals, a child’s winter cloak, three blank travel slips and a small pouch of coins from at least four regions.

Dust had settled on the back edge.

Not new.

Yun looked at him.

“This existed before me.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“How long?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Some parts eight years. Some two. The road changes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Who for?”

Yurushi closed the panel.

“People who need to leave places stronger people insist they stay.”

Yun’s eyes narrowed.

“Political dissidents?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Sometimes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Criminals?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Sometimes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That is broad.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Life is inconveniently broad.”

He handed her a merchant seal.

Yun examined it.

Real.

Old, but real.

Yurushi continued.

“Mostly civilians caught between policy and punishment. Clan families whose local enemies learned to use Shinsei law as a weapon. Healers accused of withholding stock. Villagers trapped behind security closures. People who were technically guilty of crossing the wrong authority at the wrong time.”

[[speaker:yun]]“You decide who deserves extraction?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“No.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Someone does.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“People decide whether they want to leave. I decide whether I can move them without getting everyone else killed.”

Yun looked at him differently.

There was arrogance in that too.

Of course there was.

Anyone who built secret routes through a continental government had to believe their judgment mattered.

The question was whether the judgment had limits.

“How many?” she asked.

Yurushi shrugged.

“Over years?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Enough that I stopped counting because counting started to feel like collecting virtue.”

That answer irritated her less than a number would have.

He opened another compartment.

Medical wrappers.

All from different districts.

“Some caches are mine. Some belong to old clan physicians. Some to merchants. Some to people who do not know the full route and should not.”

“Compartmentalized.”

“Exactly.”

“Shunto will break pieces once he knows.”

“Yes.”

“You are risking years of infrastructure for me.”

Yurushi’s face changed slightly.

“No.”

Yun waited.

“I am risking one corridor for you. The rest stays dark if we do this correctly.”

Good.

No hero speech.

No claim that Yun mattered more than everyone else.

That was what she needed.

Yurushi handed her a strip of cloth.

“Wrap your left wrist. The brace mark is distinctive.”

She did.

“Can you hide your cultivation?”

“Enough for clerks. Not for Paragons.”

“Good. Clerks are tonight’s problem.”

Yun looked at him.

“You are strong.”

Yurushi’s expression stayed neutral.

“Sometimes.”

“How strong?”

“Strong enough that using it openly turns an escape into a military incident.”

“That was evasive.”

“Yes.”

Yun filed it away.

The shadow trick alone suggested more than ordinary rank.

But he had not used force once.

That told her more about his strategy than any title.

Yurushi moved to the window.

“Come here.”

Yun stood slowly.

Her body objected.

The medicine helped.

Not enough.

Good medicine should not make a person forget damage existed.

Outside, the rear yard looked empty.

Yurushi pointed.

“See the wall?”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Behind it is an old dye lane. Three businesses share freight credentials. One owner owes me nothing and dislikes me. Best kind of contact.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Why?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“He will not lie for me. He will simply honor a real document if the seal is valid.”

Yun nodded.

“After the dye lane?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Merchant wagon.”

[[speaker:yun]]“After wagon?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Clinic exchange.”

[[speaker:yun]]“After clinic?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Old clan path.”

[[speaker:yun]]“After path?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Civilian medical route north.”

Yun looked at him.

“The same corridor Shunto used to catch me near.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Not the same road.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Explain.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Shinsei built over older roads. Governments like to think they invented geography.”

That almost earned a laugh.

Yurushi continued.

“The official transfer route follows the valley floor. Before unification, three hill clans moved medicine along the ridge because they taxed one another below. Shinsei closed two paths and forgot the third because the new road was better.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Forgot?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Administratively.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Meaning?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“The path still exists. It is simply absent from current route planning.”

Yun understood.

Not invisible.

Not magic.

Bureaucratically dead.

Useful.

[[speaker:yun]]“How many civilians have used it?”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Recently? Six families during last year’s security closures. Two wounded clan mediators. One physician who made the mistake of accusing a district official of stealing medicine.”

[[speaker:yun]]“So if Shunto searches it—”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“Other people suffer.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

Yurushi’s jaw tightened.

“That is why I stay behind after you leave.”

Yun stared at him.

He had already decided.

[[speaker:yun]]“You assume I leave.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I am trying optimism.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Dangerous habit.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“So I hear.”

The annex bell rang once.

Their hour was ending.

Yurushi closed the false panel.

Yun adjusted the borrowed wrap.

Before leaving, she touched the cabinet.

Old wood.

Old dust.

Old routes.

Proof that Black Radiance had not invented a moral conscience because a Wuyue woman appeared in his prison.

He had been doing this for years.

Quietly.

Imperfectly.

Without a title.

That made his help more believable.

It also made the risk heavier.

Yun said, “If I get out, Shunto will look for who helped.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I know.”

[[speaker:yun]]“He will find something.”

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“I know.”

[[speaker:yun]]“You could leave with me.”

Yurushi looked toward the false panel.

“Then the first people he questions are everyone who ever used one of these roads.”

Yun understood.

The escape was not one corridor.

It was years of people who trusted darkness to remain dark.

She nodded once.

[[speaker:yun]]“Then we do not waste it.”

Yurushi smiled faintly.

“Now you sound like me.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Do not be insulting.”

The bell rang again.

They separated before the guard returned.

Outside, Shinsei’s roads remained orderly, mapped and controlled.

Underneath them, older roads remembered people the government had stopped seeing.
