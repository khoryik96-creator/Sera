## Chapter 445 — North Without an Army

Rhen ate because Lu stood in front of the door.

There were many ways around Lu Weiran.

Rhen knew all of them.

He also knew that using a Supreme movement art to escape breakfast would produce consequences more irritating than breakfast.

So he sat.

The kitchen had not been prepared for the most dangerous man alive to wake before noon.

It had, however, been prepared for Huo.

This meant there was enough food.

Rhen finished one bowl of rice porridge, two steamed buns, half a plate of pickled greens and something Tae insisted was medicinal beef despite smelling strongly of pepper and bad judgment.

Lu watched the entire process.

“This is excessive,” Rhen said.

“You were sealed in stone for six months.”

“I was not starving.”

“You were not eating.”

“My body was maintaining itself.”

“That sentence is exactly why normal people don't trust cultivators.”

Rhen looked across the table at Qin.

“Do people trust physicians?”

Qin took a sip of tea.

“Not this one.”

Tae laughed into his bowl.

Huo tried to steal another bun from Rhen's plate.

Rhen moved it six inches without looking.

Huo's hand closed on air.

He stared.

Then tried again.

The bun moved back.

Huo looked delighted.

“You're using cultivation to defend breakfast.”

“Yes.”

“Respect.”

Lu put both palms on the table.

“Can we please behave as if a continental war exists for ten consecutive breaths?”

Everyone stopped.

Lu counted.

At seven, Huo reached for the bun again.

Tae slapped his hand.

Lu closed his eyes.

Rhen finished eating.

Then he stood.

The room changed with him.

Not because his aura expanded.

It did not.

That remained the most unsettling part.

There was no great pressure announcing the Petals Monarch. No mountain-deep reservoir leaking from behind suppression. Rhen simply rose, tied the old dark-green coat closed, checked the leather bracer on his right forearm, and looked exactly like the wandering physician who used to leave the shop for herbs.

Only the petals betrayed him.

One drifted past the window.

Frost-white.

Pale violet beneath the surface.

Tae watched it disappear.

“You really don't know how strong you are now?”

Rhen considered the question one last time.

“I know approximately.”

“That is different from what you said earlier.”

“I have had breakfast since then.”

Tae stared.

Rhen walked away before the argument could mature.

The mountain base gathered without being summoned.

Not all one thousand Quiet Hands. Most were working, training, moving medicine or holding the scattered Wuyue network together. But enough people found excuses to be near the western ridge that the path looked suspiciously busy.

A Sovereign company captain stood beside a stack of empty baskets he had no reason to inspect.

Two Duke lieutenants were checking the same saddle for the fourth time.

A row of Peak Marquis apprentices had apparently discovered a deep professional interest in sweeping clean stone.

Rhen stopped.

Every broom froze.

“Good work,” he said.

The apprentices bowed so quickly one struck another with the handle.

Rhen continued.

Huo leaned toward Tae.

“He knows.”

“Obviously he knows.”

“They're bad spies.”

“They are healers carrying brooms.”

“Exactly.”

At the ridge, the northern sky was clear.

Wuyue spread beneath the mountain in layers of autumn green and distant road haze. Somewhere beyond all of that lay the coast. Beyond the coast, the Black Current. Beyond the Current, Isgard.

Sera.

Rhen looked at Lu.

“The packet?”

“Already moving through the first courier handoff.”

“Good.”

“You will not reach Sera before it does.”

Rhen raised an eyebrow.

Lu paused.

Then amended, “You should not reach Sera before it does.”

“That is different.”

“Yes.”

“Which one did you mean?”

Lu glared at him.

Rhen smiled faintly.

Yurushi stood farther back with Qin. Tae and Huo had both followed despite pretending they had not.

Rhen looked at the three High Paragons.

“Stay.”

Huo sighed dramatically.

“We heard you the first time.”

“I know.”

“Then why say it again?”

“Because you followed me to the ridge.”

“We were saying goodbye.”

“That is suspiciously healthy.”

Tae pointed at Rhen.

“You don't get to use that line after what Lu said to you.”

Rhen ignored him.

He looked at Yurushi.

“If Shinsei moves here again?”

“I know how they think.”

“At least how they used to.”

Yurushi nodded.

Rhen looked at Qin.

“Central command?”

“Mine.”

“Tae?”

“Mobile west and Stonecrown support.”

“Huo?”

Huo grinned.

“Wherever Sera lets me break things.”

“That is not a command assignment.”

“It has worked so far.”

Tae sighed.

Rhen's eyes moved over them one last time.

Then to Lu.

“And you?”

“I will continue doing everyone's actual work.”

“Good.”

Lu's mouth twitched despite himself.

Rhen turned north.

The world seemed to wait.

He took one step.

Nothing exploded.

There was no tear in space, no doorway, no distortion in distance.

His foot simply touched stone.

Then his body ceased to be where ordinary eyes could follow it.

**Drifting Petals.**

The art did not teleport him.

It was movement refined until the intervals between one physical placement and the next became too small for ordinary perception to hold. His qi carried through legs, spine, breath and terrain in a seamless chain of acceleration. Each contact with earth gave him another. Each release carried him farther than the last.

The mountain vanished behind him.

Forest became a dark-green smear beneath his feet.

A road appeared, then was gone.

He crossed a river on three touches so light the water broke only after he had reached the opposite bank.

By the time the sound arrived, he was already beyond the next ridge.

He did not run at maximum output.

That was important.

The new reservoir answered movement differently.

Before seclusion, large acceleration had required the body to release visible quantities of qi, even if Rhen made those quantities look effortless.

Now he touched only a fraction.

A fraction of a fraction.

The result carried him farther than expected.

Rhen adjusted downward.

Then downward again.

A pale-violet petal appeared beside him.

The wind shredded every ordinary leaf around it.

The petal remained intact.

Rhen frowned.

“Too much.”

He reduced output again.

The next step landed without disturbing dust.

Better.

He continued north.

People saw almost nothing.

A farmer looked up from a field when frost-white petals crossed the sunlight and disappeared before falling.

A merchant caravan felt a sudden cold breeze from the wrong direction.

At a military checkpoint, three soldiers straightened because the road bell rang once though nobody had passed through the gate.

Rhen was already twenty miles beyond them.

The coast arrived before afternoon.

Black Current shipping filled the horizon.

Wuyue war transports moved north under escort. Medical ships returned south. Supply vessels rode low with grain, timber, salt and replacement armour. The war had turned the sea itself into a road.

Rhen stopped on the roofbeam of a signal tower.

Nobody below noticed.

The invisibility folded naturally around him after the movement art, not bending light through magic, but suppressing the cues by which ordinary perception caught his body—presence, qi disturbance, breath rhythm, sound and the small physical signatures a cultivator normally left behind.

Far out on the water, an Isgard-bound hospital ship laboured against the current.

Rhen watched it.

Then stepped from the tower.

His foot touched the sea.

Once.

Twice.

By the third step the coast was behind him.

He crossed the Black Current alone.

No banner.

No escort.

No army.

A Wuyue naval officer on the hospital ship looked over the rail when a line of tiny frost crystals appeared across the water fifty yards away.

“Did you see that?” she asked.

The sailor beside her squinted.

“See what?”

The crystals were gone.

Rhen continued north.

For six months, tens of thousands of people had crossed this sea carrying steel, medicine, orders, grief and food.

He crossed carrying one repaired coat and the knowledge that Sera had been doing his share of the work too.

That thought stayed with him longer than the coastline.

By evening, Isgard rose against the northern horizon.

Rhen slowed.

Not because he was tired.

Because he could feel the war before he could see it.

Smoke sat over the coast in layers.

The land carried too many damaged circulations.

Thousands.

Tens of thousands.

Some sharp and newly torn.

Some old, badly knitted, reopened, reinforced and damaged again.

Some dim enough that the body holding them had no business remaining awake.

Rhen stopped on a high black ridge overlooking the allied rear territory.

The first thing he saw was not an army.

It was a hospital field.

Rows of canvas.

Rows behind those.

And beyond them, more.

His face changed.

Lu had shown him numbers.

Numbers had edges.

This did not.

A cart creaked along the road below carrying six wounded soldiers. One was Wuyue. Two were Isgard. Three wore stripped Shinsei grey and had prisoner cords around their wrists.

The same physician walked beside all six.

Rhen watched until the cart disappeared between tents.

Then he looked farther north.

Somewhere beyond the command lines was Sera.

He released the last layer of invisibility.

A single frost-white petal appeared beside him.

Pale violet moved through it like trapped dawn.

Below, a sentry looked up.

The spear fell from his hand.

## Chapter 446 — Sera

The sentry did not recognize Rhen's face.

That was understandable.

Most of Wuyue did not.

He recognized the petal.

That was worse.

For five years, frozen petals had belonged to stories people told with lowered voices. A healer's miracle. A battlefield disappearing beneath winter. A man whose name had become less useful than the title attached to it.

The sentry stared at the pale-violet light inside the frost-white petal.

Then at Rhen.

Then back at the petal.

“Sir,” he managed.

Rhen looked down the slope.

“Where is Sera?”

The sentry pointed so quickly his shoulder popped.

Rhen winced.

“You should have that looked at.”

“Yes, sir.”

“Now.”

“Yes, sir.”

The sentry ran toward the hospital instead of the command post.

Rhen nodded approvingly.

News outran him anyway.

By the time he reached the allied command district, people were already stepping out of tents.

A Silver Horizon swordswoman froze halfway through tying her hair.

An Imperial officer stopped with a spoon in his mouth.

Two Isgard guards looked ready to challenge him until a Wuyue captain grabbed both by the sleeves.

Nobody cheered.

The reaction was stranger than cheering.

The war simply paused around him in small pieces.

A stretcher team slowed.

A clerk forgot the report in his hand.

A physician came out of a tent, saw Rhen, and began crying before she seemed to understand why.

Rhen did not know what to do with that.

So he kept walking.

The command post stood farther inland behind three defensive rings and a line of black pines that had somehow survived artillery, qi shock and six months of people cutting branches for firewood.

Rhen reached the first ring.

The guards moved aside without being told.

The second did the same.

At the third, Arin Vale stood waiting.

Her silver hair was shorter than he remembered.

One leg carried a reinforced brace beneath the trousers. Her face looked leaner. The old elegance remained, but war had carved away anything decorative around it.

She looked at him for a long moment.

Then said, “You took your time.”

Rhen nodded.

“I was busy.”

Arin's eyes narrowed.

“Sleeping?”

“Apparently.”

She almost smiled.

Almost.

Then she stepped aside.

“She is in there.”

Rhen stopped before the command tent.

Arin's voice softened.

“Don't make a joke first.”

Rhen looked at her.

“Why?”

“Because she has spent six months threatening people who try to take care of her.”

“That sounds normal.”

“It is not normal when she can flatten a hill.”

“Fair.”

“And Rhen?”

He waited.

Arin looked at him with the tired seriousness of someone who had watched Sera carry too many lists.

“She did well.”

Rhen's expression changed.

“I know.”

He entered.

Sera was standing over a map.

Of course she was.

There were six people in the tent with her: Jin Seoryu at the far end, Luo Wen beside an open medical ledger, Solveig Skeldran in Isgard grey, Xie Wuchen near the rear pole, Mo Qingzhao with two dispatches in hand, and one exhausted courier waiting to be dismissed.

Sera had one palm planted on the table.

Her other arm was wrapped from shoulder to wrist.

A dark bruise disappeared beneath the collar of her clothing. There was a fresh cut at one temple and the subtle stiffness of someone compensating for internal damage every time she breathed.

She was thinner.

Not dramatically.

Enough that Rhen noticed before he noticed anything else.

Sera looked up.

The room stopped.

Jin did not speak.

Luo's eyes closed briefly.

Solveig looked between Sera and the stranger at the entrance and understood from everyone else's faces that she was missing something enormous.

Rhen looked at Sera.

Sera looked at Rhen.

Six months fit into the space between them.

Then Rhen said, “You look terrible.”

Arin, outside the tent, whispered something unprintable.

Sera's eyes widened.

Jin lowered his face into one hand.

Rhen realized perhaps half a second too late that advice had been given for a reason.

Sera walked around the table.

Not quickly.

The injured side prevented that.

She crossed the distance anyway.

Rhen prepared for a slap.

He got punched in the chest.

It was not a Peak-Paragon punch.

It was barely a cultivator's punch.

He still accepted it.

“You,” Sera said.

Rhen nodded.

“Me.”

“You slept through a war.”

“Yes.”

“Six months.”

“Twelve days.”

Her face changed.

“You counted?”

“Lu did.”

“Of course he did.”

Then she grabbed the front of his coat and pulled him down into her.

Rhen's arms closed around her immediately.

The command tent ceased to exist.

Not literally.

Jin was still there.

So was Luo.

So was Solveig, who had the courtesy to turn toward the map and become intensely interested in a coastline she had memorized twenty years ago.

Sera pressed her face into Rhen's shoulder.

He felt the way she avoided putting weight through her injured side.

He felt the unevenness in her circulation.

Three partially healed internal tears.

One damaged shoulder channel.

Old exhaustion layered under newer exhaustion.

A pattern of qi depletion that no amount of stubbornness could disguise from him.

Rhen's jaw tightened.

Sera felt it.

“Don't,” she said into his coat.

“I didn't say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was going to say hello.”

“No, you weren't.”

Rhen considered lying.

Sera pulled back enough to look at him.

He did not lie.

“You're hurt.”

“There it is.”

“Several times.”

“Yes, doctor.”

“You haven't been sleeping.”

“I sleep.”

“How much?”

Jin quietly moved toward the far side of the tent.

Sera glared at him.

“Do not.”

“I said nothing.”

“You were going to.”

Rhen looked between them.

“I like this council.”

Sera's mouth twitched.

Then her eyes dropped to the petal near his shoulder.

The humor vanished.

She touched it.

The frost should have burned.

It did not.

The petal rested against her fingertip as lightly as cold silk.

Pale violet shone beneath its surface.

Sera looked at Rhen.

“What happened to you?”

“It worked.”

“That is not an answer.”

“Tae said that too.”

“Then Tae was right.”

“He will be unbearable when I tell him.”

“He already is.”

Rhen smiled.

Sera studied him more carefully.

She was one of very few people alive who had spent enough time close to Rhen to understand what suppression felt like.

Before, standing beside him had always carried a contradiction. Nothing visible. Nothing aggressive. Yet some part of her cultivation knew there was a depth behind the silence.

Now there was no depth.

No measurable pressure.

No hidden ocean.

She reached for his wrist.

Rhen let her.

Her fingers settled over the pulse point.

One beat.

Two.

Her brow furrowed.

“Your qi is…”

“Compressed.”

“I know what compressed feels like.”

“More compressed.”

Sera looked up.

“How much more?”

Rhen hesitated.

Sera narrowed her eyes.

“Rhen.”

“Enough that we should not test it here.”

The tent became very quiet.

Xie Wuchen, who had been saying nothing at the rear, slowly looked toward the canvas walls as if calculating their insurance value.

Jin's eyes sharpened.

Solveig finally turned back.

Luo stared at the pale-violet petal.

Sera released Rhen's wrist.

“Of course.”

Rhen looked mildly defensive.

“What?”

“I spend six months becoming increasingly unreasonable, and you come back with a new reason for everybody else to be unreasonable around you.”

“I didn't choose the color.”

“That is the part you object to?”

“It is very purple.”

“Violet.”

“Worse.”

Sera laughed.

It came out more tired than she wanted.

Rhen heard that too.

His expression softened.

“I missed you.”

Sera stopped.

The room politely became interested in other things again.

She looked at him.

“You were unconscious.”

“I was cultivating.”

“You were a decorative rock.”

“I still missed you.”

Sera's eyes shone.

She covered it by grabbing his coat again.

This time she kissed him.

Not long.

Long enough that Jin looked at the ceiling.

Long enough that Luo turned a page he had already read.

Long enough that Solveig decided Wuyue's command culture was stranger than advertised.

When Sera stepped back, Rhen looked unfairly calm.

Only the faint pink at the top of one ear betrayed him.

Sera noticed.

That improved her mood considerably.

“Good,” she said.

Rhen blinked.

“Good?”

“You still do that.”

“Do what?”

“Nothing.”

Rhen looked suspicious.

Jin cleared his throat.

Sera turned.

The war returned to the room.

Jin gestured at the map.

“We can give you the current state in an hour.”

“Lu gave me six months on the way out,” Rhen said.

Jin glanced at Sera.

“Lu did?”

“He used names first.”

Jin nodded once.

“Good.”

Rhen looked toward Solveig.

“Acting War Commander?”

Solveig's eyebrows rose.

“You know who I am.”

“Lu told me to remember your name.”

Solveig looked as though she did not know whether to be honored or offended.

Rhen extended a hand.

“Thank you for keeping Isgard alive.”

Solveig stared at his hand.

Then took it.

“We're not done yet.”

“I know.”

Rhen looked around the room.

Then back at Sera.

“How many wounded within thirty miles?”

Luo answered before she could.

“Depends what you call wounded.”

“Anyone whose body is limiting combat, work, sleep or survival because of recoverable damage.”

Luo gave him a long look.

“Too many.”

“Number.”

“Directly under allied care? More than forty thousand active cases. Add recovering troops, civilians, prisoners and people still on restricted duty, and you pass sixty thousand easily.”

Rhen nodded.

Aya's work. Luo's work. Hundreds of physicians. Six months of keeping bodies together with insufficient time.

Sera saw the decision form.

“No.”

Rhen looked at her.

“You don't even know what I'm going to say.”

“You're going to heal everyone.”

“Not everyone.”

“Rhen.”

“Only the recoverable ones.”

“That is what I meant.”

He considered this.

“Then yes.”

Sera pinched the bridge of her nose.

“You just woke up.”

“I ate.”

Jin looked at Luo.

Luo looked at Jin.

Somewhere, Lu Weiran's influence had crossed a sea.

Sera stared at Rhen.

“Can you even do that after what you just did to yourself?”

Rhen looked at the pale-violet petal beside him.

Then at the hospital fields beyond the tent.

“Yes.”

No boasting.

No drama.

Just certainty.

Sera knew that tone.

She exhaled.

“Then you're not doing it alone.”

Rhen looked at her bandaged arm.

“You are first.”

“No.”

“Sera.”

“No.”

“You have internal tearing.”

“I know.”

“You are compensating through the left shoulder and lower ribs.”

“I know.”

“You are extremely annoying.”

Sera smiled.

“I know.”

Rhen looked at Luo.

“Is she always like this now?”

“Worse.”

“Traitor,” Sera said.

Luo turned another page.

Rhen's mouth twitched.

“Fine.”

Sera narrowed her eyes.

“That was too easy.”

“I will heal you when the field starts.”

“That is still first.”

“You will be standing near me.”

“That is manipulation.”

“I am a physician.”

Luo actually laughed.

Sera looked around the room and realized she had lost.

For the first time in six months, she did not mind.

## Chapter 447 — Sanctuary of Petals

Rhen did not heal sixty thousand people by himself.

That was the first thing he made clear.

By dawn the next morning, every physician within the allied rear command had been ordered into one of twelve triage sectors.

Aya Katsuragi received the order with suspicion.

Luo Wen received it with relief.

Sera received it while Rhen was repairing the torn circulation beneath her ribs and therefore lacked the leverage to object.

“This is cheating,” she said.

Rhen's hand rested lightly against her side through layers of clothing and bandage.

“This is medicine.”

“You waited until I couldn't move.”

“You can move.”

Sera tried.

A soft pressure in her meridians stopped her from sitting up.

She stared at him.

Rhen looked innocent.

“Temporarily.”

“I hate you.”

“No, you don't.”

“Currently evaluating.”

Rhen's qi moved once.

The internal tear that had resisted three weeks of field rest sealed cleanly.

Sera's breath caught.

Not from pain.

From the absence of it.

The ache had been there so long she had stopped counting it as injury.

One second it existed.

The next it did not.

Rhen moved to the shoulder channel.

Sera went quiet.

“Better?” he asked.

“Don't look smug.”

“I am not.”

“You have a smug face.”

“I have the same face as six months ago.”

“That is also irritating.”

He finished.

Sera sat up.

Her body answered without hesitation for the first time in months.

She flexed the injured arm.

Full strength returned smoothly from shoulder to fingertips.

No catch.

No compensating route.

No buried scar tissue pulling the qi sideways.

Sera looked at him.

Rhen's expression softened.

“One patient,” he said. “Now help me with the others.”

That was why she did not argue again.

Outside, the allied hospital district had become a city of medicine.

Jin reorganized supply roads so stretchers moved in one direction and recovered patients left through another. Solveig assigned Isgard soldiers to crowd control. Mo's Imperial officers counted beds. Arin put Silver Horizon swordswomen on prisoner-sector security because they were disciplined enough not to turn old hatred into revenge.

Luo coordinated poison, circulation and trauma teams.

Aya stood opposite him with Shinsei physicians who had crossed under medical truce flags.

The arrangement was uncomfortable.

It was also necessary.

Rhen walked to the center of the district.

There was no raised platform.

He refused one.

Instead he stood in a patch of trampled grass between three hospital lanes while thousands of people tried not to stare.

Sera stood beside him.

Jin at his left.

Luo and Aya several steps forward.

Solveig farther back with the sector commanders.

Rhen looked at the physicians.

“Nothing about this replaces triage.”

Aya's eyes sharpened.

Rhen continued.

“I can stabilize many people at once. I can repair recoverable damage very quickly. I cannot decide who is bleeding behind a tent wall if nobody tells me they are there.”

He looked across the gathered medical staff.

“You still matter.”

Some shoulders lowered.

Not all.

Rhen pointed to the twelve sectors.

“Critical first. Then severe. Then restricted-duty and long recovery. Civilians and prisoners use the same medical priority rules as soldiers.”

A Wuyue officer raised a hand.

“What about Shinsei prisoners?”

Rhen looked at him.

“I said prisoners.”

The officer lowered his hand.

Aya watched closely.

Rhen turned to her.

“Your Hundred Pulse can identify circulation collapse faster than most teams can report it.”

Aya nodded cautiously.

“Yes.”

“Use it.”

“You know what it does?”

“Luo explained.”

Luo did not look up from his board.

Rhen continued.

“Mark anyone whose circulation will fail before a courier can reach us. Don't waste time proving you can treat them alone.”

Aya's expression changed.

Most powerful healers made medicine into hierarchy.

Mine is better.

My art is higher.

My patient survives because I arrived.

Rhen treated power as plumbing.

Whichever pipe carried the water fastest should be used.

“What are you going to do?” she asked.

Rhen looked up at the morning sky.

“Make the area easier to survive in.”

He raised one hand.

The first petal appeared above his palm.

Then ten.

Then a hundred.

Frost-white petals lifted into the air without wind.

Pale violet light moved inside them.

The hospital district fell silent.

Rhen breathed in.

And the world opened.

**Sanctuary of Petals.**

There was no explosion.

A translucent arc of frost-petal light expanded from the center with terrifying gentleness.

One mile.

Five.

Ten.

Twenty.

Thirty.

The hemisphere reached its maximum approved span over hospital fields, allied camps, prisoner wards, civilian shelters, supply roads and stretches of ruined Isgard earth where people had been sleeping under patched canvas because no building remained.

Petals covered the sky like a second weather system.

No wall blocked movement.

No person was trapped.

But inside the Sanctuary, hostile external force met a protective field so dense that the nearest soldiers instinctively lowered their weapons.

More important was what happened inside bodies.

Rhen's qi did not simply pour into everyone equally.

That would have been wasteful and dangerous.

The Sanctuary established contact first.

Hundreds of thousands of circulation points registered at the edge of his awareness—not thoughts, not identities, only living structures under stress.

Rhen selected the treatment field.

The physicians provided priority.

Aya's **Hundred Pulse Dominion** spread through the critical sectors.

Her perception touched pulse, circulation and meridian behavior across the living bodies around her with terrifying precision.

She lifted one hand.

“Sector Three. Bed line seven. Third patient from the west. Now.”

A marker flashed.

Rhen found the body through the Sanctuary.

A young Isgard soldier had an internal arterial tear hidden behind a rib injury. The man had been talking five minutes earlier. He was dying now.

Rhen touched the connection.

The tear closed.

Blood pressure stabilized.

The soldier's eyes opened.

Aya stopped breathing for half a second.

“Sector Nine,” Luo called. “Poison cascade. Wuyue female, Duke-level, two antidotes already failed.”

Rhen shifted.

The toxin separated from circulation in a pale shimmer, moved toward harmless collection points and broke down under controlled qi pressure.

“Sector One!”

“Sector Six!”

“Prison ward four!”

The calls began coming faster.

Rhen answered.

A shattered meridian network reconnected.

A collapsed lung reinflated.

An infected wound shed dead tissue and sealed from clean edges.

A Shinsei prisoner whose abdomen had been opened two days earlier stopped dying under the hands of an Isgard physician who had lost a brother to the invasion.

The physician stared at the healed wound.

Then went immediately to the next bed.

That mattered more than staring.

Hours passed.

Rhen did not move from the center.

Sera did.

Fully restored, she spent the morning walking sectors, resolving arguments and forcing commanders to release soldiers whose bodies were being repaired but whose minds had not slept properly in weeks.

At noon, Jin found her carrying two bowls of broth.

“One is for him?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

“One is for me.”

Jin looked genuinely impressed.

“Historic.”

Sera kicked him lightly in the shin.

He accepted the strategic loss.

Rhen ate without dropping the Sanctuary.

That bothered Aya more than anything else she had seen.

By evening, the critical list had shrunk by more than half.

Not vanished.

Half.

There were too many bodies, too much damage and too many years of bad healing layered under newer wounds for even Rhen to repair an army in one gesture.

The Sanctuary could hold tens of thousands at once.

Treatment still had order.

Still had attention.

Still had time.

Rhen preferred it that way.

At sunset, a Wuyue captain approached Sera.

“How long can he keep this up?”

Sera looked toward Rhen.

Frost-white petals still covered the sky.

The pale violet within them had not dimmed.

“I don't know,” she said.

The captain swallowed.

Sera smiled tiredly.

“That's new for me too.”

Night settled over Isgard.

The Sanctuary remained.

For the first time in six months, thousands of wounded soldiers slept beneath a sky they did not have to watch.
