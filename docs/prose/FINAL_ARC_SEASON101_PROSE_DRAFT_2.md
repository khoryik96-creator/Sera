## Chapter 365 — Tsubasa Moves the Calendar

Haru Ishikawa erased twenty-three days from a wall with one piece of charcoal.

The sound was almost nothing.

A dry scrape.

Grey dust.

Three weeks of preparation disappearing beneath his hand.

Tsubasa Kurokawa watched from the center of the command chamber.

“Again,” he said.

Haru did not complain.

He redrew the sequence.

Shinrin's northern ports.

Isgard landing corridors.

Food reserve.

Occupation medicine.

Axtaya inventory.

Horse transport.

Replacement chain.

Fortification timber.

The invasion had never been four months away.

That was the mistake outsiders might have made from the phrase **four-month readiness cycle**.

The cycle had already been running.

Shinsei was late in month three.

Final stores were moving.

Ships were being concentrated.

Garrison rotations had already begun freeing assault formations.

Yun Shizhen had found them near the end.

Then she had escaped.

Then she had reached Isgard.

Then Wuyue had received the warning.

The remaining preparation window was no longer measured in comfortable weeks.

It was measured in what those weeks could buy the enemy.

Haru tapped the northern route.

[[speaker:haru]]“Fourteen days is possible.”

Shunto Takamori leaned against the far table.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Possible and advisable are different words.”

Haru looked at him.

[[speaker:haru]]“I know.”

Shunto smiled faintly.

[[speaker:shunto]]“You sounded almost disappointed.”

[[speaker:haru]]“I am disappointed whenever somebody explains vocabulary to me.”

Kai Moriyama barked one laugh from near the door.

He had been invited because Tsubasa trusted him to object without caring whether objection was fashionable.

Kai folded his arms.

[[speaker:kai]]“So what breaks?”

Haru turned back to the wall.

[[speaker:haru]]“Redundancy first.”

He marked one column.

[[speaker:haru]]“Two reserve grain schedules become one. If the first chain is disrupted, replacement stock arrives later.”

Another mark.

[[speaker:haru]]“Occupation medicine lands closer to the assault wave instead of preceding it.”

Another.

[[speaker:haru]]“Winter equipment screening becomes regional sampling instead of complete inspection.”

Another.

[[speaker:haru]]“We lose time for additional intelligence on Wuyue.”

That one made the room quieter.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]Tsubasa asked, “How much do we actually know?”

Haru answered without pretending certainty.

“Enough to know the defensive pact is real.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Armies?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Rebuilt, but unevenly.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Kael Veyran?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Stonecrown remains homeland-heavy. His last war made him cautious about emptying fixed defence.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Public Top Ten?”

[[speaker:haru]]“Strong. Several advanced in the rebuilding interval. Exact private training unknown.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Quaint?”

Haru paused. The blank space in the schedule had become impossible to ignore.

[[speaker:haru]]“Fragmentary.”

Tsubasa's fingers rested against the black crescent blade lying beside the map table.

Not gripping.

Thinking.

Wuyue had a public hierarchy.

That was easy to study.

Quaint did not behave like one.

A tea house with assassins.

A medical network with hidden companies.

A strategist called Sera who refused a public number despite having enough strength to make numbers inconvenient.

A physician named Rhen whose reputation was absurd enough that reports became less useful the more carefully they were written.

Tsubasa disliked unknowns.

He disliked giving them time more.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“How much does fourteen days cost our estimate of Wuyue?”

[[speaker:haru]]Haru said, “A lot.”

Shunto's smile thinned.

[[speaker:shunto]]“How precise.”

Haru ignored him.

[[speaker:haru]]“To improve our model materially, we need more agents, more port observation, more time around Stonecrown, and actual visibility on Quaint's inner structure. Fourteen days gives us almost none of that.”

[[speaker:kai]]Kai said, “So we're choosing to fight people we haven't measured.”

Haru glanced at him.

[[speaker:haru]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Bad habit.”

[[speaker:haru]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Then why do it?”

Haru looked at Tsubasa. He had the answer, but the decision belonged to Tsubasa.

Tsubasa stood.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Because every day we spend measuring them is a day they spend loading ships.”

Kai's jaw tightened.

Tsubasa continued.

“Yun's warning changes the balance. Before it, Isgard had no reason to expect the landing point or scale. Wuyue had no reason to mobilize under the pact.”

He touched the coast.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Now both do.”

Haru nodded.

Tsubasa looked at the readiness board.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“If we wait for perfect intelligence, we arrive against prepared ports, Wuyue formations already north, dispersed medical stores and coordinated command.”

[[speaker:kai]]Kai said, “And if we rush?”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“We arrive against an enemy that knows we're coming but has not finished becoming ready.”

[[speaker:kai]]“While we also are not finished.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes.”

Kai stared at him for several breaths.

Then at the map of Isgard.

[[speaker:kai]]“Still think annexing them is saving them?”

Nobody moved.

Shunto's eyes shifted between the two men.

Haru became very still.

Tsubasa did not take offense.

That was one of the reasons Kai had stayed beside him this long.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes,” Tsubasa said.

Kai's expression hardened.

[[speaker:kai]]“Dangerous answer.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“I know.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Do you?”

Tsubasa met his eyes.

Isgard had lost too many civilians to epidemic after the last war.

Its apex cultivators had become stronger while farms weakened.

Mining output had fallen.

Medicinal harvests had fallen.

Support families had vanished from valleys that still possessed martial banners.

Shinsei planners looked at the north and saw a country with extraordinary swords and too few hands behind them.

Resources.

Land.

A strategic coast.

And, in Tsubasa's mind, an old political disease he recognized too well.

Fragmented authority carrying too much power inside too little social capacity.

He had watched that disease destroy Shinrin once.

He would not pretend his fear was imaginary simply because his answer had become dangerous.

“I think Isgard cannot sustain another decade like the last two years,” he said.

[[speaker:kai]]Kai said, “Then trade with them.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“We tried.”

[[speaker:kai]]“Not the same as conquering them.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“No.”

[[speaker:kai]]“You used to know that difference faster.”

The room went silent.

Tsubasa looked at Kai for a long moment.

When he had been twenty-three, Kai had beaten him and asked whether peace that required killing everyone who said no was meaningfully different from conquest.

Tsubasa had returned later with negotiated terms.

He had learned.

That memory was why Kai still spoke like this.

It was also why the question hurt.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]Tsubasa said, “I remember.”

Kai's answer was immediate.

[[speaker:kai]]“Good.”

No resolution.

No conversion.

Just a line placed in the room where nobody could pretend it had not been spoken.

Tsubasa turned to Shunto.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“The infiltrator.”

Shunto straightened slightly.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Yun Shizhen.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“You confirmed her identity after escape.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“How did she leave your custody?”

Shunto's expression remained controlled.

[[speaker:shunto]]“External assistance inside our route structure. Likely a senior covert operative or someone with access to old patrol gaps.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Likely?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“I am narrowing it.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Was she medically stable when she escaped?”

A very small pause.

Haru noticed.

Aya would have noticed more.

Tsubasa noticed enough to ask again.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Shunto.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“She was injured.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“From capture?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Capture and interrogation.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Anything outside authorized detention procedure?”

Shunto looked directly at him.

[[speaker:shunto]]“No.”

The lie entered the room quietly.

Tsubasa accepted it.

It was not stupidity. Shunto had ended rebellions without massacres.

Captured commanders alive.

Removed dangerous men from cities that would otherwise have burned.

Because Tsubasa believed dangerous tools could be managed by the person holding them.

That belief would cost him later.

For now, he nodded.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Find the insider. Do not disrupt civilian districts to do it.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Of course.”

Shunto bowed slightly.

Haru looked back at the board.

Fourteen days.

He moved shipping lanes.

Changed three port sequences.

Cut a secondary reserve depot.

Then stopped.

[[speaker:haru]]“Tsubasa.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes?”

[[speaker:haru]]“If Wuyue commits more than the cautious aid model, the first month becomes dangerous.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“How dangerous?”

[[speaker:haru]]“If they move the public Top Ten and a major continental force, this stops being an Isgard annexation campaign.”

Haru's charcoal touched the line between two continents.

[[speaker:haru]]“It becomes a two-continent war.”

Tsubasa looked at the same line.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Then we prevent them from arriving before the first position is secure.”

Haru nodded.

Not agreement.

Execution.

That was what made him valuable.

Tsubasa looked around the room.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Fourteen days.”

[[speaker:kai]]Kai said, “You are accepting the intelligence gap.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kai]]“The reserve gap.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kai]]“And the occupation gap.”

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“Yes.”

Kai's eyes stayed on him.

[[speaker:kai]]“Then remember later that you chose those gaps.”

Tsubasa did not look away.

[[speaker:tsubasa]]“I will.”

Outside the command chamber, bells began calling northern port masters to emergency readiness.

The invasion had not been moved from four months away.

It had already been close.

Tsubasa simply took the remaining weeks and decided his enemies would receive fourteen days of them.

## Chapter 366 — Sixty Percent Does Not Mean Sixty Percent of the Men

Wuyue's Black March activated at sixty percent on Day Five.

Three clerks immediately misunderstood what that meant.

Jin nearly killed none of them.

This was considered growth.

“Again,” he said.

The youngest clerk stared at the deployment board.

“Sixty percent of continental combat strength—”

[[speaker:jin]]“No.”

“Sixty percent of available soldiers—”

[[speaker:jin]]“No.”

“Sixty percent of—”

Jin put down his brush.

The clerk stopped talking.

Sera sat at the side table drinking tea and enjoying this more than she should have.

Jin pointed at the board.

[[speaker:jin]]“Strategic defence weight.”

“Yes, Lord Jin.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Not bodies.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Not every sixth man out of ten.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Not a mystical number that forces armies to become equal sizes.”

The clerk hesitated.

[[speaker:tae]]Tae whispered, “He was about to ask.”

Huo nodded gravely.

The clerk decided survival mattered.

“Yes.”

Jin continued.

“Stonecrown sends six thousand.”

Kael Veyran stood beside the western map with both arms folded.

A younger Kael would have bristled at the number.

Six thousand sounded small beside the army carrying his name.

The current Kael had buried enough soldiers to understand why thirty-eight thousand staying home could be the stronger decision.

“Most of Stonecrown's fixed defence remains,” he said.

Jin nodded.

[[speaker:jin]]“Exactly.”

[[speaker:jin]]“White Vein sends six.”

Liang Yue adjusted one of his unnecessary cloaks.

[[speaker:liang]]“Appropriate.”

Kael looked at him.

[[speaker:kael]]“You have three cloaks.”

[[speaker:liang]]“We are discussing war.”

[[speaker:kael]]“You are wearing three cloaks during war.”

[[speaker:liang]]“Preparedness.”

[[speaker:sera]]Sera said, “Continue before they become married in public.”

Both men turned toward her.

Jin did not react.

He had survived too much.

[[speaker:jin]]“Concord sends nine thousand.”

His own force.

No pride in the number.

Just weight.

[[speaker:jin]]“Stormreach eight.”

Lei Zhen nodded.

[[speaker:jin]]“Azure eleven.”

Shen Rui was already reading road estimates.

[[speaker:jin]]“Crownless four.”

Ilyra Serath smiled in a way that made the nearest logistics officer uncomfortable.

[[speaker:ilyra]]“Only four?”

Jin looked at her.

[[speaker:jin]]“Four thousand people, Ilyra.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I know.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Not knives.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I know.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Do you?”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“No.”

Sera drank tea.

[[speaker:jin]]“Imperial Army eighteen thousand.”

The Imperial Army was not the strongest formation per person.

It did not need to be.

Eighteen thousand disciplined troops could become road security, reserve line, siege labour, port control, wagon escort, replacement infantry and the boring connective tissue that allowed legends to spend their attention on places where legends actually mattered.

[[speaker:mo]]“Eighteen stays.”

[[speaker:mo]]“My ministers will complain.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:mo]]“My quartermasters will complain more.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:mo]]“Good.”

Mo smiled.

[[speaker:mo]]“If the quartermasters are not complaining, somebody forgot to move an army.”

Mo looked pleased to receive the highest available logistical blessing.

[[speaker:jin]]“Silver Horizon: eighteen hundred.”

Two thousand one hundred field-ready swordswomen existed after rebuilding.

No apology.

**63,800.**

Public personnel.

Not counting Sera.

Not counting the 120 Quaint Hands she had selected.

Not counting the hidden High Paragons remaining behind.

The room changed.

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

“I did not mean—”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“It is.”

[[speaker:sera]]“That means some people will hate their number.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Good.”

Sera continued.

“People who enjoy deployment numbers have not buried enough soldiers.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Approximately eighty-three thousand eight hundred public combat-ready personnel remain distributed across Wuyue.”

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Eight hundred eighty,” Lu said.

[[speaker:qin]]“Plus us.”

Tae groaned.

[[speaker:sera]]“You are staying.”

[[speaker:tae]]Tae said, “We have not reached that agenda item.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Also denied.”

“Rhen is unavailable.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Kael's fixed defence cannot be everywhere.”

At Huo.

[[speaker:sera]]“We do not know whether Shinsei will attack Wuyue directly.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Fine?”

Huo pointed at him.

[[speaker:huo]]“You labeled training stones as medical cargo.”

The room turned.

Tae slowly looked at Huo.

[[speaker:tae]]“Traitor.”

Lu made a note.

“What are you writing?” Tae demanded.

[[speaker:luweiran]]“Evidence.”

For one breath, generals laughed.

Then Jin rolled out the shipping schedule.

The room returned to war.

Sixty percent became roads.

Wagons.

Horses.

Grain.

Ship berths.

Arrow bundles.

Medicine chests.

A thousand arguments over what could move first.

No black sky appeared.

No mystical drum shook the continent.

The Black March looked less impressive than legend.

Mostly paperwork.

Mostly wheels.

Mostly people saying goodbye.

That was what made it real.

## Chapter 367 — The Man Who Says He Has Not Joined

Sera decided she might kill him.

Not because he was Shinsei.

Because the criticism was specific.

[[speaker:yurushi]]“The leaves are good,” Yurushi said.

Sera stared at him across the table.

[[speaker:black_radiance]]“The water was too hot.”
