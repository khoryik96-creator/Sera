## Chapter 341 — Captured Alive

Yun woke with six meridians closed.

That was the first useful fact.

The second was that she was not drugged.

The third was that whoever tied her hands understood poisoners.

No exposed rope fibers.

No metal buckle within finger reach.

No untreated wood she could scrape for splinters.

Her wrists were bound inside layered medical cloth, then secured through a rigid brace that limited rotation without cutting circulation.

Annoyingly considerate.

Yun opened her eyes.

Stone ceiling.

White plaster walls.

One high window.

One door.

No decorative objects.

No lamp within reach.

A basin fixed to the wall.

A narrow bed beneath her.

Not a dungeon.

A controlled medical holding room.

Of course.

Shunto had spent three months learning her.

He was not going to put Pale Venom in a cell full of interesting surfaces.

Yun tested her left shoulder.

Nothing below half strength.

Right side worse.

Hip channels restricted.

Lower-back circulation segmented.

Jaw free.

Breathing normal.

She could speak.

She could think.

She could not produce enough coordinated output to break the restraints without tearing her own meridians first.

A competent lock.

She hated it.

Footsteps approached.

Yun closed her eyes again.

The door opened.

Two people entered.

One physician.

One guard.

No Shunto.

Interesting.

The physician checked her pulse through the restraint opening.

Gloves.

Prepared.

The guard remained beyond kicking range.

Also prepared.

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said, “You forgot breakfast.”

The physician’s hand paused.

“You are awake.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I had noticed.”

“You have internal strain from forced circulation.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Your Second Seat caused it.”

The physician did not answer.

Not approval.

Not denial.

Institutional silence.

He examined the bruise along Yun’s shoulder and the swelling near her knee.

Then reached for a vial.

Yun’s eyes sharpened.

[[speaker:yun]]“What is that?”

“Anti-inflammatory.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Name.”

He named it.

Yun recognized the formula.

Safe enough.

[[speaker:yun]]“Dose?”

He told her.

[[speaker:yun]]“Too high for my weight.”

The physician frowned.

Then recalculated.

Reduced it.

Good.

Not everyone here was stupid enough to punish a patient through medicine. That fact also made Shinsei harder to simplify.

The physician administered the lower dose.

Yun allowed it.

The guard watched her mouth carefully afterward.

They knew about hidden capsules now.

Wonderful.

When the physician finished, he said, “You will be questioned.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I was hoping for a holiday.”

“No unnecessary movement.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Does your government issue that sentence with every room?”

The guard almost smiled.

The physician did not.

They left.

Yun waited until the lock clicked.

Then began inventory.

Room dimensions.

Ventilation.

Guard interval.

Meal timing unknown.

Physician likely every four hours based on swelling management.

Window too high but not impossible if lower-body circulation returned.

Door hinge internal.

No obvious drainage access.

Basin pipe perhaps useful if she could reach it.

She shifted her wrist.

Pain.

Not enough.

The brace held.

Good design.

Yun relaxed.

A bad prisoner wasted strength proving restraints were strong.

A useful prisoner learned when they became less strong.

She reviewed memory instead.

Shinsei.

Axtaya.

More than two hundred thousand trained apparatus.

Ten Paragon seats.

Shunto No.2.

Tsubasa No.1.

Isgard.

Four-month readiness.

Notebook.

Sample.

The notebook had been inside her clothing when captured.

Had Shunto taken it?

Probably.

The wrist brace remained.

She felt the tiny pressure seam against her skin.

The Axtaya capillary was still there.

Either they had missed it or found it and left it deliberately.

Both possibilities were dangerous.

Yun did not react.

Evidence only mattered if the other side did not know which evidence mattered.

Another hour passed.

Maybe two.

Without sunlight angle it was difficult to tell.

Then the door opened again.

Shunto entered alone.

Yun looked at his left hand first.

He noticed.

Flexed the fingers once.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Still works.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Disappointing.”

He closed the door.

No blades.

No gloves this time.

That was deliberate.

A statement that the room had already removed her options.

Yun disliked statements.

Shunto stood beyond reach anyway.

[[speaker:shunto]]“How are the locks?” he asked.

[[speaker:yun]]“Amateurish.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You tried them.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Of course.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Six closed lines.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I counted.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Good.”

He pulled the room’s only chair closer but stopped outside kicking range.

Still learning.

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said, “You rehearsed the road for how long?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Twenty-seven days.”

She blinked once.

[[speaker:yun]]“Obsessive.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You poisoned my arm.”

[[speaker:yun]]“For eleven minutes.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Still sensitive?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“No.”

Lie.

Tiny.

Yun saw the tension in his ring finger.

Interesting.

She stored it.

[[speaker:shunto]]Shunto said, “You knew the road was a trap.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“And took it.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Why?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Because you did your job well.”

Yun continued.

“You did not catch me because I forgot how to think. You removed enough alternatives that the bad route became the least bad route.”

Shunto’s expression remained unreadable.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Is that praise?”

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Sounded like it.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I can respect competence while disliking the person using it.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Useful distinction.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It keeps the world from becoming childish.”

For a moment neither spoke.

Then Shunto’s eyes shifted toward her wrist brace.

One glance.

Too small for most people.

Yun saw it.

So he had noticed the brace.

Did he know about the capillary?

Unclear.

Good.

The information war remained alive.

[[speaker:shunto]]Shunto said, “Where is the rest?”

Yun smiled faintly.

[[speaker:yun]]“There it is.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“The material you collected.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Define material.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Records. Samples. Notes. Names.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Laundry invoice.”

His eyes narrowed.

Yun continued.

“Very revealing laundry invoice.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You think this is funny.”

[[speaker:yun]]“No. I think you prefer me afraid.”

That landed.

Shunto leaned back.

Yun’s smile disappeared.

[[speaker:yun]]“You captured me alive,” she said. “Congratulations. That means you won the immediate contest.”

She looked directly at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“You did not capture everything I know.”

Shunto said nothing.

Yun settled against the wall.

The locks hurt.

Her body was injured.

Her routes were gone.

Her notebook might already be in his possession.

None of that changed the central fact.

Shunto had captured a person.

Not the warning.

## Chapter 342 — She Does Not Give Him the War

Shunto placed Yun’s notebook on the table.

That answered one question.

Not the important one.

The notebook looked unchanged.

Water stain at the corner.

Repaired spine.

Cheap leather.

He had not cut it apart.

Yet.

Yun stared at it without giving him the satisfaction of looking away.

Shunto sat opposite her.

This room was different from the holding room.

Larger.

Two chairs.

One table bolted to the floor.

A narrow strip of sunlight from a high window.

Her wrists remained restrained.

Four circulation lines were closed now instead of six.

Enough to walk under guard.

Not enough to fight properly.

Progress.

Shunto tapped the notebook.

[[speaker:shunto]]“What does three circles mean?”

Yun nearly laughed.

Of course he chose the dinner symbol.

[[speaker:yun]]“Bad cooking.”

His face did not change.

[[speaker:shunto]]“That is not a joke.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is exactly a joke.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Three circles appears beside military estimates.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Luo cooks badly under stress.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Luo Wen.”

Yun’s expression did not move.

Inside, something hardened.

Shunto knew the name.

Not surprising.

Wuyue’s medical figures were public enough.

The important question was whether he knew the relationship.

He watched her.

Yun watched back.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Your physician,” he said.

[[speaker:yun]]“Many people are physicians.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Your partner.”

There.

Yun felt the word land and allowed nothing to show.

Shunto continued.

“You built the notation together.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Did I?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You write differently in ordinary medical records.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Perhaps I became creative abroad.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Three circles.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Burned fish.”

He stared.

Yun stared back.

Eventually Shunto looked down at the notebook.

He had decoded structure.

Not meaning.

Good.

“Who is waiting for this?” he asked.

[[speaker:yun]]“No one.”

Lie.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Who expects you back?”

[[speaker:yun]]“My landlord.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Wuyue.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Eventually.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“When?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“So they know something is wrong.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Probably.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Who?”

Yun shrugged as much as the restraint allowed.

[[speaker:yun]]“A very judgmental landlord.”

Shunto’s jaw tightened.

He slid the notebook toward her.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Read this.”

Yun looked down.

A page of coded medical shorthand.

The troop estimate.

He pointed to the disguised weight notation.

[[speaker:shunto]]“What is two hundred thousand?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Large patient.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Yun.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Shunto.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Excellent summary.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Isgard.”

That silence was intentional.

Too much denial would confirm it.

Too much sarcasm would do the same.

[[speaker:shunto]]“You know.”

Yun looked bored.

He leaned forward.

[[speaker:shunto]]“What do you know?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Your tea is bad.”

The sound was small.

Hard.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Where is the physical sample?”

Yun’s pulse remained steady.

So he did not know.

Or wanted her to think he did not.

Either way, same response.

[[speaker:yun]]“What sample?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Axtaya.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Interesting name.”

Too cute.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Do not.”

Good.

Emotion changed timing.

His right hand near the notebook.

Left hand slightly looser.

Ring finger still stiffer than the others.

Not weakness enough to matter in a fight.

Information enough to matter later.

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

Good.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Most.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Your word, not mine.”

Once.

Twice.

The packet might have left.

No Domain made interrogation omniscient.

No High Paragon technique turned another person into an open ledger.

Shunto had questions.

Yun still controlled some answers.

That was not victory.

But it was not surrender either.

## Chapter 343 — What Shunto Chose

The worst thing Shunto did was not losing control.

It was choosing control for the wrong purpose.

Yun understood that afterward.

Before, there was only the room.

Night, probably.

No sunlight through the high window.

One lamp fixed behind protective glass.

Her restraints had been changed after the interrogation.

Both wrists secured.

Circulation restricted at shoulder, hip and lower spine.

Enough movement to eat.

Not enough to fight.

The door opened.

Shunto entered without a physician.

Without a guard.

Yun looked up.

[[speaker:yun]]“No notebook?”

He closed the door.

Something in the way he did it changed the room.

Yun felt it immediately.

Not killing intent.

Not interrogation.

Entitlement.

She stood as far as the restraint line allowed.

[[speaker:yun]]“Open the door.”

Shunto said nothing.

Yun’s pulse accelerated.

She hated that he could probably feel it.

Good.

Let him.

Fear was evidence of danger, not consent.

He stepped closer.

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said, “No.”

Clear.

No sarcasm.

No ambiguity.

[[speaker:yun]]“No.”

Shunto stopped for one breath.

Then continued.

Yun moved first.

She drove her shoulder into him, forced qi through a partially closed line and tried to turn the restraint chain into leverage.

Pain tore across her back.

Shunto caught the movement.

Not roughly at first.

Efficiently.

That made it worse.

He knew exactly how much control his cultivation gave him over her damaged circulation.

Yun tried a second route.

He closed it.

She struck him.

He restrained her.

She said no again.

He heard her.

He understood.

He chose to continue.

The narrative did not owe that choice beauty.

There was none.

It did not owe him passion.

There was none.

What followed was assault: deliberate, coercive and enabled by the power he had already used to imprison her.

Yun resisted until resistance stopped changing the immediate outcome.

Then she survived it.

That distinction belonged to her.

Not him.

Later, the lamp was still burning behind glass.

Shunto stood near the door.

Yun sat against the far wall with her knees drawn up because it hurt less that way.

Neither spoke for several breaths.

Her face felt distant.

Her body did not.

That was inconvenient.

Pain returned in small places first.

Shoulder.

Wrist.

The old rib injury.

Her circulation felt scraped raw from forced restraint.

She focused on those things because they were measurable.

Measurable things could be treated.

Shunto looked at her.

Yun looked back.

Whatever he expected to see, she refused to perform it for him.

[[speaker:shunto]]He said, “You should have—”

[[speaker:yun]]“Do not finish that sentence.”

Her voice was quiet.

He stopped.

Good.

At least one instruction still worked.

Yun continued.

“You chose this.”

Shunto’s jaw tightened.

[[speaker:yun]]“Do not make it about the chase. Do not make it about the notebook. Do not make it about me poisoning your arm.”

She swallowed once.

[[speaker:yun]]“You chose this.”

He opened the door.

[[speaker:yun]]Yun said, “Look at me.”

He did.

Her eyes were dry.

Her body had not yet decided what to do with the urge to cry.

[[speaker:yun]]“You are still not getting the war.”

Shunto’s expression changed.

A flicker.

Anger, perhaps.

Or shame.

Yun did not care which.

He left.

The door locked.

Only then did her hands begin shaking hard enough that she had to press them against her knees.

She breathed.

Not slowly.

Not beautifully.

Just enough.

One breath.

Another.

Another.

She counted pulse intervals because Luo had once told her counting was useful when the body mistook memory for current danger.

He had been talking about battlefield shock.

Close enough.

Yun counted to sixty.

Then again.

At some point tears came.

She let them.

There was no victory in refusing them.

No weakness in having them.

The room stayed locked.

Shunto remained powerful.

The assault remained real.

And none of those facts converted what he did into ownership of anything inside her.

Not the notebook’s meaning.

Not the warning.

Not her judgment of him.

Not her name.

Yun pressed her forehead against her knees.

She would survive the next minute.

Then the next. She had enough data for tonight.

## Chapter 344 — The Prison Is Still a Laboratory

By morning, Yun had four new data points.

The first was that the night guard changed twelve minutes earlier than the day guard.

The second was that the physician assigned after midnight used a different key sequence on the circulation brace.

The third was that the eastern wall pipe vibrated every time someone opened the service gate below.

The fourth was that Shunto’s left hand went numb when he came back.

That one pleased her.

Not enough.

Nothing would have been enough.

But it pleased her.

He entered near midday with two guards this time.

Good.

Cowardice or procedure.

Either worked.

Yun sat on the bed while the physician changed the dressing on her wrist.

Shunto remained near the door.

He looked tired.

His left hand hung slightly open.

Not useless.

Not like Chapter 328.

But the ring and little finger were slow.

Yun noticed before he did.

Then he tried to close them.

The ring finger hesitated.

His eyes dropped.

Yun smiled.

Small.

Ugly.

Satisfied.

Shunto looked at her.

[[speaker:shunto]]“What?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Nothing.”

He flexed again.

Numbness climbed into the outer palm.

Recognition reached his face.

Yun leaned back against the wall.

[[speaker:yun]]“You really should stop touching poisoners.”

Both guards moved.

Shunto lifted his right hand.

They stopped.

[[speaker:shunto]]“When?” he asked.

Yun shrugged.

That hurt.

She did it anyway.

[[speaker:yun]]“Could have been the road.”

His eyes narrowed.

[[speaker:yun]]“Could have been interrogation.”

Silence.

[[speaker:yun]]“Could have been later.”

The room became very still.

Yun’s smile vanished.

She looked directly at him.

[[speaker:yun]]“You will never know exactly which moment you gave me.”

That was the point.

The toxin was subtle.

Not lethal.

Not dramatic.

A delayed nerve-circulation irritant that built through repeated exposure to a treated thread hidden inside one restraint seam.

Yun had found the thread the first night by touch.

The original purpose was medical padding.

She had contaminated it using residue from a damaged poison capsule worked into the edge of her nail.

Almost nothing.

Then waited.

Shunto’s hands had adjusted the restraint more than once.

Enough.

Not a victory over a High Paragon.

A reminder.

Even imprisoned, she was still working.

[[speaker:shunto]]Shunto said, “Physician.”

The doctor examined his hand.

Yun watched professionally.

[[speaker:yun]]“Delayed peripheral toxin,” she said.

The physician glanced at her.

Shunto did not.

“Treatment?” he asked.

“Reduce circulation pressure. Do not purge through the shoulder. You will spread it.”

Shunto finally looked at her.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Why tell me?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Because I am a poisoner, not an idiot. Dead captor creates lockdown. Numb captor creates paperwork.”

Shunto’s mouth tightened.

Yun continued.

“Also your doctor was about to choose the wrong route.”

The physician looked offended.

“Was not.”

[[speaker:yun]]“You were.”

“I was assessing.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Slowly.”

The guard nearest the door looked down to hide something suspiciously like amusement.

For ten seconds, the room almost felt normal.

Then Yun’s body reminded her it was not.

Pain shifted when she breathed.

Her hands tightened beneath the blanket.

She did not let the moment erase the night.

She also did not let the night erase the moment.

Both could exist.

Shunto left with the physician.

The guards followed.

The door closed.

Yun waited.

Then stood.

Her legs shook once.

She waited for them to stop.

The prison was still a laboratory.

Suffering had not become interesting. Observation mattered because it returned choices.

She checked the basin pipe.

Three vibrations since dawn.

Service gate cycle perhaps every two hours.

She checked the restraint brace.

The midnight physician used key pressure from left to right.

That meant internal pins were not symmetric.

Lower hinge carried more weight.

Not useful yet.

Guard footsteps.

Seven regular.

One dragged the left foot.

Medicine delivery after fourth bell.

Food after sixth.

Shunto’s visit irregular.

Good.

Irregular people still depended on regular systems.

Yun sat and reconstructed the room from everything she could not see.

Hall outside.

Medical station two doors down based on herb smell.

Service stairs beneath eastern wall.

Main guard post farther south because shift noise arrived late through the corridor.

No label.

Not standard issue.

Good quality.

Very good quality.

Convenient medicine was still convenient.

Someone had helped her.

Or someone wanted her to think they had.

Yun looked toward the locked door.

The prison was still a laboratory.

Now it had introduced a new variable.
