## Chapter 401 — Jun Returns Before He Is Finished Healing

Jun Kajihara hated being told he was seventy-eight percent recovered.

Not because the number was low.

Because Aya Katsuragi had measured it.

[[speaker:jun]]“How precise is seventy-eight?”

Aya did not look up from the chart.

[[speaker:aya]]“Precise enough to irritate you.”

[[speaker:jun]]“That is not medicine.”

[[speaker:aya]]“It is today.”

Jun sat on the edge of the treatment cot in partial armor.

His chest still hurt when he breathed too deeply.

The damaged arm had full gross movement and incomplete high-output stability.

Three repaired meridian junctions were structurally intact but still vulnerable to prolonged Breach Dominion strain.

He looked functional.

That was not the same as finished.

Aya checked his pulse again.

[[speaker:aya]]“You can fight.”

Jun nodded.

[[speaker:aya]]“You should not maintain full Dominion output longer than necessary.”

He nodded again.

[[speaker:aya]]“If your chest pain sharpens, stop.”

A pause.

[[speaker:aya]]“If your left hand begins to numb, stop.”

Another.

[[speaker:aya]]“If you taste blood—”

[[speaker:jun]]“I know.”

Aya looked at him.

Jun corrected himself.

[[speaker:jun]]“Stop.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

He reached for the halberd resting beside the cot.

Aya’s hand closed around the shaft first.

Jun stared.

She did not release it.

[[speaker:aya]]“You are not healed because command needs you.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I know.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You are being returned early because command needs you.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Those are different sentences.”

Jun’s expression softened by a fraction.

[[speaker:jun]]“I know.”

Aya let go.

Good.

That was why she stayed.

Jun would have returned anyway.

Without Aya, he would have returned with unstable meridians, hidden internal bleeding and enough untreated qi disruption to turn the next clean clash into a death sentence.

She had spent days repairing him because leaving good people to bad medicine did not become moral merely because the institution using them was wrong.

People died when physicians abandoned the room first.

Aya believed that.

She still did.

That was becoming the problem.

Outside the treatment pavilion, Redline crates were moving.

More than before.

Aya noticed immediately.

[[speaker:aya]]“Where?”

The quartermaster stopped.

“Forward Third and Sixth.”

[[speaker:aya]]“How many?”

He hesitated.

Aya looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“How many?”

“Formation allocation.”

[[speaker:aya]]“That is not a number.”

The quartermaster glanced toward his officer.

The officer approached.

“Physician Katsuragi.”

[[speaker:aya]]“How many 3× seals?”

“Operationally restricted.”

Aya’s face went still.

[[speaker:aya]]“I designed the medical screening protocol for the drug you are carrying.”

“This is command authorization.”

[[speaker:aya]]“That did not answer me.”

Jun remained near the pavilion entrance.

Watching.

The officer lowered his voice.

“Several thousand.”

Aya’s hand tightened around the chart.

[[speaker:aya]]“For emergency distribution?”

“Formation activation.”

The words landed badly.

Not individual catastrophic necessity.

Not selected breach response.

Formation.

Aya stepped closer.

[[speaker:aya]]“No.”

The officer blinked.

“Excuse me?”

[[speaker:aya]]“No mass same-window 3× activation.”

“Command approved it.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I am objecting medically.”

“I will record that.”

[[speaker:aya]]“And then?”

The officer said nothing.

Aya understood.

Then anyway.

Jun looked at the crates.

[[speaker:jun]]“How critical?”

The officer answered him.

“Wuyue is preparing another push. If Third and Sixth break, the coastal supply spine becomes vulnerable.”

Jun’s jaw tightened.

Aya turned to him.

[[speaker:aya]]“Do not.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I didn’t say anything.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You were about to say necessity.”

Jun looked at her.

[[speaker:jun]]“Sometimes it is.”

Aya stared.

He did not say it lightly; Jun had watched delayed action kill people as a child.

His entire command philosophy grew from the belief that refusing necessary violence often increased the final body count.

Aya knew that.

She also knew what several thousand 3× doses meant inside living bodies.

“Necessary to whom?” she asked.

Jun did not answer.

Good.

He should not have had an easy one.

The crates continued toward the front.

Aya watched them go.

Jun lifted the halberd.

[[speaker:jun]]“I’ll be careful.”

She looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“No, you won’t.”

A faint smile.

[[speaker:jun]]“Fair.”

Then he returned to war before she had finished healing him.

Aya stood in the doorway until the last Redline crate disappeared.

Seventy-eight percent.

Several thousand seals.

The numbers felt unrelated.

They were about to become the same problem.

## Chapter 402 — Thousands Open the Same Seal

Jin Seoryu knew something was wrong because the Shinsei line stopped retreating.

Not slowed.

Stopped.

The allied push had been working for nineteen minutes.

Mo’s Imperial infantry held the center.

Arin’s swordswomen had broken the left screening force.

Isgard heavy troops were reclaiming a low ridge they had lost three weeks earlier.

The Shinsei formation should have yielded another two hundred paces before stabilizing.

Instead, every officer in the front rank touched the same place on their belt.

Jin’s face changed.

[[speaker:jin]]“Signal withdrawal.”

The aide stared.

“We’re winning.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Signal withdrawal.”

A horn began.

Too late.

Thousands of seals broke at once.

The battlefield did not explode.

That would have been easier to understand.

Bodies changed.

Breathing sharpened.

Circulation roared.

Already elite Peak-Marquis and Duke-class soldiers suddenly moved with throughput far beyond what their formations had displayed minutes earlier.

Not new cultivation.

A Peak Marquis remained a Peak Marquis.

But a Peak Marquis spending everything at roughly triple combat output for five minutes was still a nightmare for anyone who had built a plan around normal endurance.

The Shinsei center hit Mo’s infantry like a gate dropped downhill.

Three shield lines collapsed.

Not because the defenders became weaker.

Because the people striking them had borrowed tomorrow’s body to pay today’s impact.

Arin saw her left flank disappear into speed.

[[speaker:arin]]“BACK.”

Her swordswomen moved.

One formation reacted late.

Seven women died before the second horn finished sounding.

Jin tore his original plan in half.

Literally.

The paper ripped between his hands.

The aide stared.

“New orders?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Everything changes.”

“What about the ridge?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Give it back.”

“The beachhead?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Keep only the hospital road.”

“We just spent—”

[[speaker:jin]]“I know what we spent.”

Jin’s voice cut harder.

“Do not spend it twice because pride wants interest.”

Orders ran.

Some commanders obeyed immediately.

Others hesitated because winning lines were psychologically harder to abandon than losing ones.

Sera appeared beside the central relay.

[[speaker:sera]]“What?”

Jin pointed.

[[speaker:jin]]“Mass 3×.”

[[speaker:sera]]“How many?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Thousands.”

Her face hardened.

[[speaker:sera]]“Window?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Luo said roughly five minutes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Then we don’t fight five minutes.”

Jin looked at her.

There.

Same conclusion.

Sera entered Orchid Dominion only across the hospital-road sector where withdrawal pressure threatened to become massacre.

Not the whole battlefield.

No reason to waste Peak-Paragon output everywhere.

Every serious Shinsei offensive commitment inside her authority created a Counter-Bloom.

A boosted spear captain lunged.

His attack opened the opposite shoulder.

Sera bloomed a needle strike there and killed the arm without killing the man.

A sword formation committed forward.

Counter-Blooms opened along abandoned defensive lines.

She used concentrated Orchid force to break knees, wrists, grips.

Not enough.

There were too many.

And Redline did not make their attacks stupid.

It made good soldiers horrifyingly capable of executing them harder and faster.

Sera stopped one collapse.

Could not be everywhere.

That was the point.

Kael tried to hold the central road.

Worldweight Dominion punished every Redline charge through the ground.

But the boosted soldiers simply spent more to break burden.

They could.

For five minutes.

The battlefield became a lesson in why temporary power was still power.

A Peak-Marquis formation that would normally tire under repeated Worldweight pressure instead shattered its own sustainable reserve to keep moving.

Kael stopped three waves.

Fourth forced him back.

Jin watched the timer.

[[speaker:jin]]“One minute.”

Aide shouted:

“Which one?”

[[speaker:jin]]“Since first activation.”

“Not simultaneous!”

Jin swore.

Of course.

Not every seal had opened on the same breath.

Some ten seconds later.

Some thirty.

The window was smeared.

Still usable.

Luo arrived at the command line.

[[speaker:luo]]“Watch skin temperature and hand tremor. Crash starts unevenly.”

Jin looked at him.

[[speaker:jin]]“How uneven?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Enough to get people killed if you treat five minutes like a bell.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Helpful.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Medicine is rude.”

The first Shinsei soldier collapsed at four minutes forty-two.

Not dead.

His legs simply stopped obeying the output his circulation was still demanding.

Then another.

Then six.

The wave began losing coherence.

Jin saw it.

[[speaker:jin]]“Now.”

Wuyue counter-formations moved.

Not full attack.

Controlled.

They hit the crash instead of the peak.

For thirty seconds it worked beautifully.

Then another boosted unit arrived from reserve.

Fresh seals.

Fresh five-minute windows.

Jin’s expression went flat.

Shinsei had not merely armed soldiers with Redline.

It had begun designing operations around lifespan as a consumable resource.

The battle lasted another hour.

Wuyue held the hospital road.

Lost the ridge.

Lost two temporary positions.

Saved most of the army because Jin had abandoned a winning plan fast enough.

From a distance, the result looked indecisive.

Inside the casualty tents, it did not.

Hundreds of Shinsei bodies came back carrying years they no longer possessed.

Aya Katsuragi was waiting.

## Chapter 403 — Hundred Pulse Hospital

Aya opened Hundred Pulse Dominion over the hospital and immediately wished she had not.

Not because the Domain hurt.

Because it told the truth.

Hundreds of living circulations appeared to her at once.

Not visually.

Pulse.

Pressure.

Meridian behavior.

Vessel strain.

Every body became a medical statement written in motion.

And the statements were screaming.

[[speaker:aya]]“Triage one through twelve, severe meridian tearing.”

Assistants moved.

[[speaker:aya]]“Thirteen to twenty, internal bleeding but stable core.”

Moved.

[[speaker:aya]]“Twenty-one—stop him walking.”

A soldier protested.

“I’m fine.”

Aya pointed without looking.

[[speaker:aya]]“Your left femoral meridian is splitting.”

He stopped.

Mostly because three medics grabbed him.

Aya moved deeper.

White Thread Sutra.

Needles.

Pressure stabilization.

One patient’s collapsed qi channel could be repaired.

Another’s heart rhythm could be calmed.

A third had torn enough internal tissue that ordinary medicine would have failed.

Aya could still save him.

That was not the nightmare.

The nightmare was beneath all of it.

A quiet absence in vitality.

Not injury.

Missing future.

Hundred Pulse Dominion could feel the body compensating around something that no longer existed.

Years already spent.

Aya moved from cot to cot.

Repairable.

Repairable.

Repairable.

Gone.

Repairable.

Gone.

Gone.

Gone.

Her breathing changed.

One assistant noticed.

“Physician?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Continue.”

Aya placed Seven Pulse Needles into a young soldier’s chest.

Twenty-two, maybe.

His meridians were torn but salvageable.

His future vitality loss was not.

“How many doses?” she asked.

“One.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Duration?”

“Four minutes something.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Prior use?”

“No.”

Aya stabilized the pulse.

The body would live.

The body might live very well.

Five future years could still be gone.

No ordinary healing would put them back.

Not hers.

Not Luo’s.

Not even Rhen’s ordinary healing, if the reports were right.

Aya looked around the hospital.

Hundreds.

Not all had lost the same amount.

Bodies varied.

Duration varied.

Baseline vitality varied.

But the pattern remained.

This was not emergency medicine used too often by accident.

It was industrialized expenditure.

A command decision had taken years from hundreds of people in one afternoon.

A colonel entered.

“Physician Katsuragi.”

Aya did not look up.

[[speaker:aya]]“What?”

“Command requests readiness estimate for surviving Redline formations.”

Her hand stopped.

[[speaker:aya]]“Readiness.”

“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“When?”

“Tomorrow morning.”

Aya looked at him.

The colonel took one step back.

[[speaker:aya]]“Tomorrow.”

“Yes.”

She pointed at the room.

[[speaker:aya]]“Do you see them?”

He swallowed.

“Yes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“No. You see wounded soldiers.”

Aya’s voice remained quiet.

“I see circulation.”

She touched one patient’s chart.

[[speaker:aya]]“This man can walk tomorrow.”

Another.

[[speaker:aya]]“This one could fight in three days if I were willing to be irresponsible.”

Another.

[[speaker:aya]]“This one may recover completely from tissue damage.”

The colonel said nothing.

Aya looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“None of those sentences restore what Redline spent.”

“I understand.”

[[speaker:aya]]“No.”

For the first time, anger entered her voice.

[[speaker:aya]]“You know the phrase. I am not convinced you understand it.”

The room had gone quieter.

Soldiers were listening.

Good.

Aya looked around.

One dose.

Five minutes.

Five years, roughly.

The actual number varied.

The principle did not.

A physician could repair torn flesh.

Could not repair time already burned.

Aya turned back to the colonel.

[[speaker:aya]]“Readiness estimate tomorrow.”

“Yes?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Alive.”

He blinked.

[[speaker:aya]]“That is the estimate.”

Then she returned to work.

Hundred Pulse Dominion remained open.

For the next six hours, Aya felt every recoverable wound she repaired and every irrecoverable absence she could not.

By midnight, she had stopped thinking of the battlefield as a victory or loss.

It was a room full of people whose future had been converted into minutes.

And Shinsei had called that arithmetic.

## Chapter 404 — They Told Him Months

The nineteen-year-old soldier was afraid to ask Aya the question.

She knew because his pulse accelerated every time she came near.

Not pain.

Anxiety.

Aya finished adjusting the needle in his shoulder.

[[speaker:aya]]“What?”

He looked at her.

“Nothing.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Your pulse disagrees.”

He almost smiled.

Then didn’t.

“How long?”

Aya understood the question as recovery.

[[speaker:aya]]“Meridian tearing? Six to eight weeks before full unrestricted combat if you follow instructions.”

The soldier stared.

“That’s all?”

Aya paused.

[[speaker:aya]]“What did you think?”

“They said months.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

“So I got lucky.”

Something cold moved through Aya.

[[speaker:aya]]“What exactly were you told about Redline?”

The young man frowned.

“That it’s an emergency stimulant.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

“Three-times output for around five minutes.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

“Severe fatigue. Meridian damage. Recovery can take months. Repeated use is dangerous.”

Aya’s hands went still.

[[speaker:aya]]“Lifespan.”

He looked at her.

“What?”

[[speaker:aya]]“What were you told about lifespan?”

The soldier stared.

Nothing in his face was performative.

He genuinely did not understand the question.

Aya felt his pulse through the Dominion.

Honest confusion.

“No one said anything about lifespan.”

The hospital disappeared around her for one second.

Not literally.

Aya remembered every safety memorandum she had written.

Every dosage chart.

Every phrase negotiated with military administration.

**Severe long-term vitality consequences.**

**Extended recovery burden.**

**Repeated exposure contraindicated.**

Precise language.

Medical language.

Language that command could preserve while removing the thing ordinary soldiers actually needed to know.

Years.

Aya looked at the boy.

[[speaker:aya]]“One standard 3× exposure of roughly five minutes may cost approximately five years of future life.”

His pulse stopped accelerating.

For half a beat, it almost slowed too much.

“What?”

Aya did not soften it.

[[speaker:aya]]“Five years. Approximate. Individual cost varies.”

The soldier looked at his hands.

“No.”

Aya said nothing.

“They said months.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I know.”

“No, they said recovery could take months.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I know.”

He looked at her.

“Did you know?”

The question had finally arrived. Aya could have explained.

Could have told him she objected to higher dosing.

That she stayed to reduce harm.

That early medical doctrine contained more warnings.

That command language had changed.

That she had personally rejected unsuitable soldiers.

That people were alive because she kept control of parts of the system.

All true.

None answered him.

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

The boy looked away.

Aya felt his pulse break into anger.

Good.

He was entitled to it.

“Then why didn’t you tell us?”

Aya’s throat tightened.

[[speaker:aya]]“I thought the warnings remained in operational briefing.”

“That isn’t an answer.”

No.

It wasn’t.

Aya looked down at her own hands.

[[speaker:aya]]“I stayed because I believed if the military was going to use Axtaya, someone had to keep it from becoming worse.”

The soldier said nothing.

[[speaker:aya]]“I believed being inside meant I could control dosage, screening, recovery, repeat use.”

“And?”

Aya looked around the hospital.

[[speaker:aya]]“And this is worse.”

Silence.

She removed one damaged needle and replaced it.

The soldier flinched.

“Sorry,” she said.

Not for the needle.

He knew.

Aya stood.

At the records table, she asked for every standard Redline briefing currently issued to frontline formations.

Three versions arrived.

None contained an approximate lifespan number.

One used **vitality burden**.

One used **long-term recovery consequences**.

One said only **high medical risk**.

Aya read them all.

Then took out a brush.

On the first page she wrote:

**3× REDLINE: APPROXIMATELY FIVE MINUTES OF TRIPLE COMBAT THROUGHPUT. STANDARD HEALTHY-ADULT FUTURE-LIFE COST MAY APPROXIMATE FIVE YEARS. REPEAT USE COMPOUNDS SHARPLY.**

The clerk beside her stared.

“Physician—”

[[speaker:aya]]“Copy it.”

“Command has not approved—”

[[speaker:aya]]“Copy it.”

Her voice did not rise.

The clerk moved.

Aya opened the medical eligibility roster.

Twenty-eight soldiers marked marginal became ineligible.

Then thirty-one.

Then forty-six.

A supply officer arrived.

“What are you doing?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Medicine.”

“You’re cutting Redline availability.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

“On whose authority?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Mine.”

“You cannot block command issue.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I can classify bodies medically unfit.”

“This many?”

Aya looked at him.

[[speaker:aya]]“Yes.”

The officer’s jaw tightened.

“This will affect battlefield readiness.”

Aya looked back at the nineteen-year-old soldier.

His future had already been affected.

[[speaker:aya]]“Good.”

That was the moment Aya stopped merely reducing harm quietly.

She began resisting the machine using the authority the machine had given her.

## Chapter 405 — Five Minutes

Luo Wen put six watches on Jin Seoryu’s table.

Jin looked at them.

[[speaker:jin]]“Why?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Because you keep saying five minutes as if war respects clocks.”

[[speaker:jin]]“I do not.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You did twice.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Those were estimates.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Good. Then learn the estimate properly.”

Luo opened his notes.

Captured Axtaya.

Prisoner pulse records.

Shinsei casualty timing.

Wuyue observations.

One voluntary test using a non-Redline lower stimulant analogue under controlled medical conditions.

Jin frowned at that line.

[[speaker:jin]]“You tested it?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Not 3×.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Good.”

[[speaker:luo]]“I’m reckless, not stupid.”

Sera, sitting beside the table, said nothing.

Luo looked at her.

[[speaker:luo]]“What?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Nothing.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That was judgement.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Yes.”

He ignored her.

[[speaker:luo]]“Standard 3× active peak is roughly five minutes. But onset varies. Crash varies. Body mass, prior fatigue, circulation quality, dose absorption, temperature, injury.”

Jin nodded.

[[speaker:jin]]“So no exact bell.”

[[speaker:luo]]“No.”

[[speaker:jin]]“But tactical window.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

Luo moved the watches.

[[speaker:luo]]“First minute, don’t contest unless terrain forces you.”

Second.

[[speaker:luo]]“Minutes two to four, make them move for nothing.”

Third.

[[speaker:luo]]“Force direction changes. Deny fixed targets. Make them spend qinggong, formation acceleration, weapon reinforcement.”

Fourth.

[[speaker:luo]]“Near expected crash, do not rush too early. Some users hold longer.”

Fifth.

[[speaker:luo]]“When tremor, temperature drop after peak, gait instability and circulation noise appear—then punish.”

Jin studied the arrangement.

[[speaker:jin]]“Retreat as offense.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Not rout.”

[[speaker:luo]]“No.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Structured denial.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

Jin smiled.

Luo looked suspicious.

[[speaker:luo]]“What?”

[[speaker:jin]]“I like it.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That is rarely comforting.”

Jin stood and moved to the map.

[[speaker:jin]]“Five-Minute Retreat.”

Luo stared.

[[speaker:luo]]“You named it already.”

[[speaker:jin]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That is terrible.”

[[speaker:jin]]“It is accurate.”

[[speaker:luo]]“It sounds like soldiers are taking a break.”

[[speaker:jin]]“They’ll remember it.”

[[speaker:sera]]Sera said, “He’s right.”

Luo looked betrayed.

Jin began designing.

Forward lines required premarked retreat lanes.

No unit could withdraw through another’s medical corridor.

Rear formations had to know whether they were receiving a controlled retreat or a collapse.

Signal horns needed a pattern distinct from ordinary withdrawal.

Cavalry could not countercharge during the peak.

Archers should force Redline users to spend movement rather than trade shots.

Worldweight units could punish planted force without trying to stand directly in front of boosted bodies.

White Vein specialists could preserve efficiency while moving.

Quaint recon could identify red-vial distribution before activation where possible.

Sera watched the plan become real.

[[speaker:sera]]“How much ground do we give?”

Jin answered.

[[speaker:jin]]“Whatever costs less than the people.”

Good.

That was the correct doctrine.

Not heroic ground worship.

Not endless retreat.

Trade land for time only when time had measurable value.

Luo tapped the watches.

[[speaker:luo]]“One more thing.”

Jin looked at him.

[[speaker:luo]]“Some crash hard enough to die if you hit immediately.”

[[speaker:jin]]“And?”

[[speaker:luo]]“If they surrender, treat them.”

Jin nodded.

[[speaker:jin]]“If they keep attacking?”

[[speaker:luo]]“Stop them.”

Simple.

Medicine did not require passivity.

Sera stood.

[[speaker:sera]]“When do we test it?”

Jin looked at the newest Shinsei formation report.

[[speaker:jin]]“Tomorrow.”

Luo collected five watches.

Left one.

Jin looked at it.

[[speaker:luo]]“For you.”

[[speaker:jin]]“I own one.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You forget to use it when excited.”

Jin stared.

Sera’s mouth twitched.

Luo walked out.

On the table, one cheap watch ticked.

Five minutes had never sounded so long.
