## Chapter 454 — Aya Writes the Years

Aya Katsuragi changed one sentence and made an entire command office angry.

The old line read:

**3× Redline: emergency combat stimulant. Severe long-term vitality consequences possible. Repeated use strongly discouraged.**

Aya crossed it out.

Underneath, in black ink, she wrote:

**3× Redline: approximately five minutes of triple combat throughput. Standard healthy-adult projected cost: approximately five years of future lifespan. Cost varies by condition. Repeat use compounds sharply.**

She sanded the ink.

Closed the file.

Opened the next one.

Wrote the same thing again.

By the ninth report, a medical clerk stopped pretending not to stare.

By the twelfth, the deputy logistics physician arrived.

By the fifteenth, a colonel appeared.

Aya kept writing.

The colonel stood opposite her desk for several breaths.

“Lady Katsuragi.”

Aya did not look up.

“That title is not necessary in a field hospital.”

“The wording is.”

“Yes.”

“It is causing concern.”

“Yes.”

“That was not praise.”

“I know.”

Aya finished a number.

The colonel placed both hands on the desk.

“Five years is an estimate.”

“Yes.”

“Not a guaranteed cost.”

“Yes.”

“Then writing it like that is misleading.”

Aya finally looked at him.

“No. ‘Severe long-term vitality consequences’ was misleading.”

He stiffened.

“That language came from medical command.”

“From my office.”

Aya did not look away.

“That is why I am correcting it.”

The colonel's jaw tightened.

“Soldiers do not need a lifespan estimate in the middle of active war.”

“Why?”

“Because it affects willingness.”

Aya's expression became very still.

“That is the purpose of informed consent.”

“This is an army.”

“They still possess bodies.”

The colonel exhaled sharply.

“Redline is issued for catastrophic battlefield necessity.”

“Then catastrophic battlefield necessity can survive the truth.”

He stared at her.

Aya opened the next report.

The patient had been twenty-four.

One dose.

Approximately four minutes active.

Severe cardiac strain.

Three torn meridian junctions.

Recovered.

Projected lifespan cost uncertain but substantial.

Aya wrote:

**Estimated future-life expenditure: four to five years.**

The colonel watched the pen move.

“Command will challenge this.”

“Then command may come here.”

“They will.”

“Good.”

He left.

The clerk waited until the tent flap stopped moving.

Then whispered, “Was that wise?”

Aya kept writing.

“No.”

The clerk blinked.

Aya looked up.

“It was necessary. Those are not the same thing.”

By afternoon the correction had spread through three hospitals.

Not officially.

Physicians were faster than officials when frightened enough.

A Shinsei medic copied the new wording onto a casualty board.

Another wrote the dosage model inside his own field notebook.

A third added a line beneath the sealed Redline inventory:

**DO NOT REISSUE SAME DAY WITHOUT PHYSICIAN AUTHORIZATION. SECOND DOSE COST MAY APPROXIMATELY DOUBLE.**

Aya saw it and did not erase it.

Tsubasa summoned her before dusk.

His consolidated command camp had finished taking shape around the northern theater.

The official Ten were now physically close enough to be called together in minutes.

Aya hated that she found the arrangement efficient.

She entered the command pavilion.

Tsubasa sat at the center table.

Kai stood nearby.

Haru occupied a chair beside the wall, one boot tapping once every few breaths.

Hana was absent.

Shunto was not.

Aya's gaze touched him and moved on.

Tsubasa held one of her amended reports.

“You changed the standard language.”

“Yes.”

“Without approval.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because it was dishonest.”

Shunto's mouth curved faintly.

“Dishonest.”

Aya looked at him.

“Do you need the word defined?”

Haru stopped tapping his boot.

Kai looked at the floor.

Tsubasa did not react to the insult.

He lifted the report.

“Is five years medically defensible?”

“Yes.”

“As an average?”

“For a healthy adult at standard 3× Redline exposure of roughly five minutes. Individual cost varies.”

“Could it be less?”

“Yes.”

“More?”

“Yes.”

“Substantially more?”

“With prior damage, poor baseline vitality, redosing, age, or overdraw—yes.”

Tsubasa nodded.

“Then the number stays.”

Shunto turned toward him.

Aya did too.

Tsubasa set the report down.

“An estimate is not made safer by hiding the magnitude.”

Shunto's eyes narrowed.

“It weakens discipline.”

Tsubasa looked at him.

“Truth does not weaken discipline. It reveals whether discipline was built on consent or ignorance.”

The room changed.

Aya watched him carefully.

This was why Tsubasa remained difficult to hate cleanly.

He could say the right thing.

Understand the right principle.

Then still preserve the machine that made the principle necessary.

Tsubasa continued.

“However, issuance doctrine remains unchanged for now.”

There it was.

Aya's jaw tightened.

“Tsubasa.”

“Say it.”

“You cannot acknowledge the real cost and continue pretending one sealed 3× dose per expeditionary soldier is ordinary emergency doctrine.”

“I am not pretending it is ordinary.”

“You issue it ordinarily.”

“Because we are in a war where five minutes can decide whether a formation is annihilated.”

“Then tell them five minutes may cost five years before you hand them the vial.”

Tsubasa held her gaze.

“Yes.”

Aya paused.

He continued.

“Effective immediately, all new issue requires the lifespan estimate to be included in briefing. Existing deployed units receive amended notice through medical officers.”

Shunto's expression hardened further.

Haru looked at Aya.

She looked back at Tsubasa.

“And same-day redosing?”

“Prohibited without direct field-commander and physician authorization.”

“Second-dose cost?”

“Included.”

“Third?”

A silence.

Haru's face changed.

Tsubasa did not look at him.

“Classified as gross medical emergency and command violation outside imminent collapse.”

Aya folded her arms.

“That should have been true before.”

“Yes.”

No excuse.

No defense.

Just yes.

Haru stood.

The movement was abrupt enough that everyone looked at him.

He stared at the report on the table.

“Put thirty-five years on mine.”

Aya's chest tightened.

“Haru.”

“Put it there.”

“Your actual cost cannot be measured exactly yet.”

“Then write the estimate.”

Tsubasa watched him.

Haru's voice stayed steady.

“Five. Ten. Twenty.”

The numbers everyone already knew.

He touched one finger to the report.

“If the file says I used three, then the file should say what three means.”

Aya looked at him for a long time.

Then nodded.

“I will.”

Haru left.

Shunto watched the tent flap fall shut.

“Wonderful,” he said softly. “We are turning medical records into confession.”

Aya turned on him.

“No.”

Her voice was quieter now.

“Into records.”

Tsubasa's gaze moved between them.

Shunto smiled without warmth.

Then looked away.

Aya understood something she did not yet have words for.

The war had not changed because one line in a report changed.

But institutions were made from lines like that.

What they permitted people to hide.

What they forced people to name.

That night, every Shinsei field hospital received an amended Redline notice.

Some officers complained.

Some soldiers cursed.

Some laughed because five years sounded impossible when written on cheap paper.

Others stopped laughing.

Aya walked between cots until after midnight.

At one bed, a nineteen-year-old soldier held the amended notice with both hands.

“Five years?” he asked.

Aya stopped.

“Yes.”

He looked at the sealed vial tied inside his emergency pouch.

“They told us recovery could take months.”

“I know.”

“Did they know?”

Aya did not lie.

“Some did.”

The soldier looked at her.

“Did you?”

Aya's throat tightened.

“Yes.”

He stared for several breaths.

Then looked away.

Aya did not ask forgiveness.

She stayed until he asked his next question.

## Chapter 455 — The Sealed Account

Lu Weiran did not trust messages that mattered.

A message could be stolen.

A courier could be followed.

A seal could be copied.

A cipher could become someone's retirement project.

So when Yurushi Amagiri finally told him he was ready to give Sera the complete account of Yun Shizhen's captivity, Lu did not reach for paper.

He reached for tea.

Yurushi looked at the cup.

“This is serious.”

“Yes.”

“And you're making tea.”

“Yes.”

“Rhen has infected you.”

Lu's face remained calm.

“No. Rhen makes bad tea when distracted.”

“That sounded personal.”

“It is logistical.”

Yurushi sat.

The mountain base was quieter than usual.

Tae had left that morning to inspect a regional company rotation.

Huo was somewhere below ground shouting at trainees who were apparently failing to be hit correctly.

Qin sat on an upper terrace listening to three separate courier briefings at once.

The hidden organization that had once been two hundred killers and healers under Sera's authorization now moved a thousand people without public banners.

Lu had built most of the system that made that possible.

He poured.

“Start at the point you are certain,” he said.

Yurushi's humor disappeared.

“The final detention site.”

Lu nodded.

Yurushi began.

He did not dramatize.

That made it worse.

He described the holding rooms.

The circulation locks.

The way Shinsei guards changed shift when Shunto personally entered.

The fact Yun had already poisoned Shunto once and therefore later restraints had been adapted specifically to prevent skin contact and poison delivery.

He described the escape route he opened.

The checkpoint Hana altered without ever admitting why.

The condition Yun was in when he found her.

Then he stopped.

Lu waited.

Yurushi stared at the tea.

“When I reached her,” he said, “her injuries did not match interrogation alone.”

Lu said nothing.

“I asked her whether Shunto had done it.”

His jaw tightened.

“She did not answer directly.”

Lu's eyes lowered.

“She did not need to.”

“No.”

The room became very still.

Yurushi's hands stayed flat on the table.

“She wanted the military evidence moved first. Ships. Troop estimates. Redline storage. Paragon sightings. Everything.”

“Of course she did.”

“She was dying and still correcting my route priorities.”

A faint, broken smile touched Yurushi's mouth.

“She told me I was wasting time on the wrong crate.”

Lu looked down.

“That sounds like her.”

“You knew her well?”

“No.”

Lu's answer was quiet.

“But I knew people who did.”

Yurushi nodded.

Neither said Luo's name.

They did not need to.

Lu finally slid a blank sheet across the table.

“Now we write.”

Yurushi frowned.

“You said you don't trust messages.”

“I don't.”

“Then?”

“We are not writing one message.”

Lu divided the page into four sections.

“Military facts go in one packet. Your direct observations in another. Medical inference in a third. Names and route compromise in a fourth.”

“Why split them?”

“So stealing one does not reveal all of them.”

He took out four different seals.

“Two packets move north through ordinary Quaint medical supply traffic. One goes by a Wuyue military courier who does not know the other two exist. The final packet stays with a person.”

Yurushi looked at him.

“What person?”

Lu sipped tea.

“You don't need to know.”

Yurushi stared.

Lu added, “Neither does the person until the final exchange point.”

“You are unsettling.”

“Yes.”

They worked for three hours.

Lu asked the same question five different ways whenever timing mattered.

He separated what Yurushi knew from what Yurushi believed.

He refused to let inference become fact merely because the inference was likely correct.

When they reached the assault, Lu stopped writing for the first time.

“Do you know?”

Yurushi's eyes hardened.

“Yes.”

“Or infer?”

“Know enough.”

Lu held his gaze.

Yurushi's voice dropped.

“She refused him.”

The rest did not need description.

Lu wrote only what Sera needed.

**Shunto Takamori sexually assaulted Yun Shizhen while she was held under Shinsei custody. Clear refusal. Abuse concealed from official report. Yurushi personally observed aftermath and received Yun's confirmation sufficient to remove ambiguity.**

He sanded the ink.

Yurushi looked at the words.

“They look too small.”

“They are supposed to.”

“Why?”

“Because the truth does not become more true if I decorate it.”

Yurushi closed his eyes.

Lu folded the sheet.

Then burned the draft beneath it.

Only the final packet remained.

The route began before dawn.

A medicine cart left the mountain base carrying feverroot, bone-set resin and three crates of ordinary bandages.

Inside one bandage crate was a false base.

Inside the false base was a lacquer tube.

The tube contained only the first packet.

The second traveled with a courier who believed he carried bridge requisitions.

The third moved through an Isgard-bound prisoner-exchange ledger.

The fourth never entered a bag.

Lu memorized it.

Then dictated it two days later to a trusted Quaint captain at a relay point who memorized only half.

Another captain received the other half.

They traveled separately.

Nobody held the whole account except Lu, Yurushi and eventually Sera.

When the first packet reached Isgard, Sera was reviewing hospital release numbers with Jin.

A Quaint medic placed the lacquer tube on the table.

“From Lu.”

Sera's hand stopped.

Jin noticed.

“What?”

“Private.”

He closed his notebook immediately.

“I'll leave.”

Sera looked at him.

“You don't know what it is.”

“No.”

“Then why—”

“Because Lu does not send lacquer tubes for grain counts.”

He left.

Sera broke the first seal.

Military facts.

Most were confirmations.

Then came the second packet.

Yurushi's direct observations.

The third.

Medical inference.

Sera's breathing changed.

The final two-part memorized account arrived after sunset.

The captains delivered their halves separately.

Sera reconstructed them alone.

Then read the final line twice.

Her fingers tightened against the paper.

There was no dramatic explosion of qi.

No Orchid Dominion.

No broken table.

That would have been easier for everyone outside the room.

Sera simply sat very still.

Rhen entered without knocking because he had spent too many years pretending doors in their life applied to him.

He stopped when he saw her face.

“Sera.”

She folded the paper.

Rhen did not ask to see it.

That mattered.

“Yurushi told me everything,” she said.

Rhen's expression changed.

Not much.

Enough.

“Everything?”

“Yes.”

A long silence.

Sera looked at him.

“She got out anyway.”

Rhen nodded.

“She carried the evidence anyway.”

“Yes.”

“She reached Isgard anyway.”

“Yes.”

Sera's jaw trembled once.

Then stopped.

Rhen sat beside her.

He did not touch her until she leaned into him first.

When she did, his arm went around her.

For several breaths she said nothing.

Then:

“I need to tell Luo.”

Rhen's hand tightened slightly at her shoulder.

“Yes.”

“I hate that I have to.”

“Yes.”

“I hate that he deserves to know.”

“Yes.”

Sera closed her eyes.

“Don't say yes to everything.”

Rhen looked down at her.

“I can say no if you want.”

She gave a small, broken laugh despite herself.

“Idiot.”

“Yes.”

She hit him lightly in the ribs.

He let her.

## Chapter 456 — Sera Tells Luo

Luo Wen knew something was wrong because Sera asked him to close the door.

Not the tent flap.

The door.

They were using an old Isgard command house near the hospital quarter, one of the few stone buildings that still had intact shutters and no blood stains on the walls.

Luo entered carrying three patient charts.

Sera stood beside the window.

No guards.

No Rhen.

No Top Ten.

He put the charts down.

“Who died?”

Sera looked at him.

The question hurt more because of how normal it sounded.

“No one today.”

Luo's shoulders lowered by a fraction.

“Then what?”

Sera gestured to the chair.

He did not sit.

Neither did she.

“Lu got the complete account from Yurushi.”

Luo went still.

“The escape.”

“Yes.”

“The detention site?”

“Yes.”

“Shunto?”

Sera's silence answered too quickly.

Luo's eyes sharpened.

“What did he do?”

Sera looked at the floor once.

Then back at him.

She did not soften the truth into something easier to hear.

“While Yun was held in Shinsei custody, Shunto sexually assaulted her.”

Nothing happened.

That was the frightening part.

Luo did not move.

Did not blink.

Did not breathe visibly.

Sera continued because stopping would make him force the words out of her one by one.

“Yurushi saw the aftermath. Yun's response removed any ambiguity. She refused him. He concealed it from the official custody record.”

Luo's hand lowered to the edge of the table.

His fingers rested there.

Not gripping.

Just touching wood.

Sera watched him.

“Luo.”

“Where is Shunto Takamori?”

His voice was completely calm.

Sera had expected anger.

This was worse.

“Tsubasa has consolidated the official Ten in Isgard.”

“Where?”

“We do not have exact current camp position yet.”

“Who does?”

“Jin has likely sectors. Lu is working routes. Yurushi's old intelligence helps.”

Luo nodded once.

“Good.”

Sera stepped closer.

“Do not disappear.”

Luo looked at her.

“I am not Rhen.”

“That is not reassuring.”

“I am a Sovereign.”

“That is exactly why it isn't reassuring. Shunto is a High Paragon.”

Luo's eyes did not change.

“I know.”

“Then say what you are thinking.”

Silence.

Sera waited.

Finally Luo looked toward the patient charts.

“I am thinking Yun would be angry if I died stupidly.”

Sera's chest loosened by one breath.

“Yes.”

“She would call it inefficient.”

“Yes.”

“She would probably correct my poison ratios at the funeral.”

Sera almost smiled.

Almost.

Luo's gaze dropped.

The calm finally cracked—not outward, but inward.

His face tightened around something he refused to let become sound.

“She didn't tell me.”

Sera's throat closed.

“She was dying.”

“I know.”

“She prioritized the invasion evidence.”

“I know.”

“She may not have wanted her last minutes to become—”

“I know.”

The third answer broke.

Luo turned away.

Sera did not touch him.

Not yet.

He stared at the wall.

“She knew I would ask.”

Sera said nothing.

“She knew I would know something was missing.”

His shoulders moved once.

“She still spent the time on troop counts.”

“That was Yun.”

“Yes.”

Luo laughed once.

It was not humor.

“She corrected my silverroot notation before leaving.”

Sera remembered the tiny note in the notebook.

Tea owed.

Ordinary nonsense beside a war.

Luo closed his eyes.

“Did Yurushi save her?”

“He helped her escape.”

“That wasn't the question.”

Sera looked at him.

Luo opened his eyes.

“Did he save her from him?”

“He got her out after.”

Luo nodded.

“He did what he could.”

“Yes.”

“Then I owe him.”

“You do not owe him revenge.”

“I did not say revenge.”

“No.”

Sera studied him.

“That worries me more.”

Luo finally looked directly at her.

“What do you want me to do?”

“Keep being a doctor.”

His face went blank.

Sera continued.

“I am not asking you to forgive Shunto.”

“Good.”

“I am not asking you to forget.”

“Good.”

“I am asking you not to let him choose what you become next.”

Luo looked away.

The silence stretched.

Then he asked, “Did Rhen tell you to say that?”

Sera's eyebrows rose.

“No.”

“He would.”

“He'd say it worse.”

That earned the faintest movement at the corner of Luo's mouth.

Sera stepped closer.

“When the time comes, you will not face him alone.”

Luo's gaze sharpened.

“Sera.”

“No.”

“You don't even know what I was going to say.”

“Yes, I do. You were going to tell me this is personal.”

“It is.”

“So was Yun.”

He stopped.

Sera held his gaze.

“And she belonged to more people than your grief.”

Luo's face changed.

Not anger.

Pain accepting a shape it disliked.

Sera continued quietly.

“Let us help you carry it.”

For a long moment Luo said nothing.

Then he sat.

Not because the conversation was over.

Because his legs had finally remembered he was tired.

Sera sat opposite him.

They stayed there without speaking.

After several minutes, Luo reached into his robe and took out Yun's notebook.

He always carried it now.

He opened to a page near the back.

One of her old poison diagrams sat beside his physician corrections.

Two handwriting styles arguing across paper.

Luo traced one line with his thumb.

“She hated this version.”

“Why?”

“She said I was thinking like a doctor.”

Sera blinked.

“You are a doctor.”

“Yes. She found that disappointing whenever poison was involved.”

Sera laughed softly.

Luo did too.

This one was real enough to hurt.

Then he closed the notebook.

“Where is Shunto Takamori?” he asked again.

Sera did not mistake the repetition for obsession.

This time it was a practical question.

“We'll find out.”

Luo nodded.

“And until then?”

Sera glanced at the patient charts.

“You have three people waiting for you.”

Luo looked down.

The first chart was a Shinsei prisoner with Redline-related kidney damage.

The second was a Wuyue cavalry officer.

The third was an Isgard child injured in a supply-road collapse.

Luo picked them up.

His hands were steady.

“Fine.”

He stood.

At the door he stopped.

“Sera.”

“Yes?”

“Thank you for telling me yourself.”

She nodded.

Luo opened the door.

Then paused one last time.

“When we find him, do not let Rhen erase the problem in one punch.”

Sera looked at him.

Despite everything, something almost wicked entered her eyes.

“I wasn't planning to.”

Luo nodded.

“Good.”

He returned to the hospital.

The first patient he treated was Shinsei.
