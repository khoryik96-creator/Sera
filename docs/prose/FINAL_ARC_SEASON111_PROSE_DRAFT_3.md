## Chapter 468 — Undertaker's Measure

Luo Wen had spent most of his adult life learning how much poison was enough.

Too little was useless.

Too much was lazy.

A physician who overdosed every patient until something happened was not a physician.

A poisoner who did the same was not much better.

Yun had laughed the first time he said that.

Then made him measure the same tincture twelve times until his hands stopped assuming that close enough was a number.

Now Shunto Takamori stood twenty paces away on 3× Redline.

High Paragon.

Hundred Locks Dominion active.

Broken wrist forcibly reinforced.

At least two fractured ribs.

Shoulder recently dislocated and reset.

Nasal fracture.

Three minutes and fifty-two seconds into a five-minute overclock.

Pulse too fast.

Respiration asymmetrical.

Body temperature rising.

Microtremor beginning in the damaged hand despite reinforcement.

Luo looked at him.

And measured.

**Undertaker's Measure.**

The art was not a mystical verdict.

It was disciplined perception refined until tiny things became useful.

Pulse visible through skin.

Blood volume inferred from colour and recovery.

Qi pressure inferred from the delay between movement and tissue response.

Toxin resistance inferred from purge speed.

Exhaustion inferred from what compensation the body chose when it no longer had enough clean options.

Luo did not need to know how much poison killed a High Paragon.

That would have been a stupid question.

He needed to know how much poison disrupted **this High Paragon**, at **this minute**, while **this damaged body** was forcing **this Redline cycle** through **this Dominion**.

Shunto took another step.

Luo watched the left shoulder.

Compensation increased.

Good.

Not for Shunto.

For the diagnosis.

Behind him, Kael did not move.

Luo refused to look again.

If Kael had died, looking would not fix it.

If Kael was alive, looking would waste the thing Kael had nearly died to buy.

Shunto raised one hook.

“You're very calm.”

Luo reached into his sleeve.

“I'm working.”

“On me?”

“Yes.”

Shunto smiled.

“Then work faster.”

He moved.

Luo threw three needles.

Not together.

One high.

One low.

One late.

Shunto cut the high needle.

Dodged the low.

The late one grazed his coat.

No penetration.

Shunto laughed.

Luo stepped back.

The ground behind him ended at the broken medical shed.

Shunto knew it.

No more room.

He came in with the right hook.

Luo turned sideways.

The blade missed his abdomen by inches.

He used those inches.

A needle touched Shunto's forearm.

Barely.

Shunto backhanded Luo across the face.

Luo hit the shed wall.

Pain flashed through his jaw.

He almost dropped.

Sovereign against High Paragon.

That was the difference.

Shunto could kill him with ordinary contact if Luo pretended cultivation gaps were optional.

Luo did not pretend.

He moved before the second strike.

A hook tore his sleeve instead of his arm.

Luo planted a second needle into the cloth wrapped around Shunto's damaged wrist.

Shallow again.

Shunto ripped it out.

Qi surged.

Purge.

Luo watched the route.

Same as before.

Excellent.

Shunto's eyes sharpened.

“You want me to purge.”

Luo said nothing.

That confirmed too much already.

Shunto stopped purging.

Interesting.

Luo adjusted.

A toxin that depended on purge was useless if the patient refused the purge.

Unless refusing created its own problem.

Shunto attacked again.

Luo ducked beneath the first hook.

The second caught his shoulder.

Not the blade.

The guard.

Pain and numbness exploded down his arm.

Hundred Locks.

Luo stumbled.

Shunto grabbed his throat.

The world stopped.

Luo's feet left the ground.

Shunto held him one-handed.

“You're not Yun.”

Luo's face changed for the first time.

Not grief.

Not rage.

Recognition.

“No,” he rasped.

Shunto tightened his grip.

“There was only one Pale Venom.”

Luo's mouth moved.

For one impossible instant, Shunto thought he was smiling.

“Yes.”

A silver needle punched through the webbing of Shunto's thumb.

He dropped Luo instinctively.

Luo hit the ground and rolled.

Third placement.

Shunto ripped the needle free.

This time he did not purge.

He stared at it.

Then at Luo.

Luo coughed once and pushed himself upright with his good arm.

Three tiny placements.

Forearm.

Damaged wrist.

Thumb webbing.

Not enough toxin to hurt a Paragon.

That was the point.

They were markers.

Small disruptions in three circulation branches Shunto's body kept using to stabilize hook control and Hundred Locks output.

Shunto understood part of it.

He shifted his circulation away from the hand.

Luo saw where it went.

There.

The compensation route joined the rib-protective cycle.

That was the missing line.

Luo inhaled.

“Four minutes, nine seconds.”

Shunto's expression tightened.

“You keep counting.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“Because your body is.”

Shunto stepped forward.

His damaged wrist trembled once.

Tiny.

Almost invisible.

Luo saw it.

Shunto saw Luo see it.

The former No.2 attacked with fury.

Luo moved behind the stone marker.

Shunto cut through it.

Luo threw a paper packet into the dust.

Shunto held his breath.

Correct.

Luo had expected that too.

The packet contained nothing toxic.

Only powdered silverroot.

Yun's favourite teaching irritant because it smelled strong enough to make students overreact.

Shunto realized a moment later.

“You're wasting time.”

“No.”

Luo looked at his throat pulse.

Four minutes, twenty-six seconds.

“You are.”

---

At the alliance command hall, the warning arrived almost forty minutes too late.

Sera opened Lu's encoded sheet.

Read the first line.

Then the second.

Her face emptied.

Rhen noticed immediately.

“What?”

Sera handed him the sheet.

Shunto escaped.

Likely route reconstructed through still-live supply clearance.

Target probability highest: Greywater corridor.

Kael Veyran present.

Luo Wen present.

Warning already sent to corridor.

No confirmation received.

Rhen folded the paper once.

“Distance?”

Jin answered from the map.

“Too far for ordinary reinforcement.”

Rhen was already standing.

Sera looked at him.

“Go.”

No argument.

No speech about command.

This was not taking over the war.

This was a physician going where two people might be dying.

Frost-white petals gathered around Rhen's feet.

Pale violet flashed through them.

**Drifting Petals.**

He left.

Not by teleportation.

The road, ridge and frozen plain simply began passing beneath him at impossible speed.

Sera looked back at Lu's message.

Her finger tightened on the page.

“Be alive,” she whispered.

She did not specify which man.

## Chapter 469 — False Cure

Shunto finally purged at four minutes and thirty-three seconds.

Not because Luo forced him.

Because his damaged hand stopped closing properly around the hook.

Redline was demanding three-times throughput from a wrist that had been broken before the dose began.

The small placements made the compensation route less reliable.

Shunto had two choices.

Accept reduced control.

Or purge.

He chose purge.

Of course he did.

Shunto Takamori had built his entire martial identity around removing flaws from the opponent's body.

He could not tolerate one in his own.

Qi surged from core to shoulder.

Shoulder to elbow.

Elbow to wrist.

Then split exactly where Luo expected.

The first symptoms vanished.

Shunto's hand steadied.

He smiled.

Luo watched the smile.

“Better?”

Shunto flexed his fingers.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Shunto's smile faded.

Luo raised one finger.

**False Cure.**

The purge was the treatment Shunto chose.

The treatment was the trigger Luo had designed around.

The tiny foreign irritants did not resist being expelled.

They encouraged it.

As Shunto forced circulation down the obvious cleansing route, the second phase shifted into the compensating channels he had opened to protect his broken wrist and damaged ribs.

Not enough to poison his whole body.

Enough to make the next correction expensive.

Shunto took one step.

His right knee hesitated.

One fraction.

He looked down.

Luo watched.

There.

Shunto forced the knee stable.

The correction pulled qi from the opposite hip.

Luo saw that too.

Shunto's eyes lifted.

“You—”

Luo's second finger rose.

**Pulse Debt.**

The toxin load was tiny.

Embarrassingly tiny by old poison-school standards.

But Pulse Debt did not care about volume as much as behaviour.

Every time Shunto forced another high-output cycle through Redline, numbness and unreliability accumulated in the already stressed channels.

The harder he tried to overpower it, the more useful the poison became.

Shunto understood.

He stopped moving.

Luo almost admired him.

Almost.

A High Paragon knew when aggression fed the enemy.

For one breath Shunto stood perfectly still and began a controlled internal purge.

That would work.

Given time.

Luo had built the entire fight around the fact that Shunto no longer had time.

Four minutes, forty-one seconds.

Redline's own cycle continued whether Shunto moved or not.

His heart did not pause because he chose caution.

His organs did not stop processing the overclock.

His damaged ribs did not become whole.

Luo waited.

Shunto realized what waiting meant.

He looked toward the road behind Luo.

Then toward Kael.

Kael still lay motionless.

Shunto's jaw tightened.

He could spend the final seconds purging.

Or finish the two men before the crash.

Luo knew which man Shunto was.

Shunto attacked.

“Three,” Luo said.

Shunto crossed ten paces.

Luo's hand closed.

**Three Pulse Reversal.**

The three earned placements answered together.

Not with explosion.

With mistiming.

Shunto's hand circulated for hook control a fraction before the shoulder stabilized.

His rear leg discharged qinggong before the forward hip had completed weight transfer.

The chest tried to support Redline pressure while the rib-protective compensation route was still purging False Cure.

Three pulses.

Three rhythms.

Out of synchronization.

At ordinary output, Shunto could have corrected it.

At High-Paragon output, it would have been difficult.

At 3× Redline, the mistake multiplied itself before his body could slow down.

His first step overshot.

The hook line went wide.

The second leg tried to compensate too hard.

His stance crossed itself.

Shunto slammed one hook into the ground and stopped his fall.

Stone cracked.

He looked up at Luo with murder in his eyes.

Luo did not move.

“Four minutes, forty-eight seconds.”

Shunto ripped the hook free.

His circulation corrected.

Partially.

He had seven, perhaps twelve usable seconds before the crash began destroying fine control.

Enough for a High Paragon to kill a Sovereign.

Shunto knew it.

Luo knew it.

Shunto moved.

This time he abandoned technique.

No elegant Five Lock Hand.

No careful hook geometry.

Pure direct force.

Kill the physician.

Luo saw the decision form before the body completed it.

Because he had watched that body for five minutes.

Because Kael had bought him those minutes with bone and blood.

Because Yun had taught him that poison did not begin when it entered the body.

It began when you understood what the body would do next.

Luo raised his hand.

His voice was almost gentle.

“Diagnosis complete.”

**Final Diagnosis.**

The Supreme art did not add a new poison from nowhere.

It used everything already earned.

Observation.

Minor doses.

Purge habits.

Compensations.

Redline strain.

Injury routes.

The next major circulation cycle converged exactly where Luo had predicted.

Shunto's body tried to drive maximum output into the final charge.

False Cure redirected the purge compensation.

Pulse Debt punished the forced throughput.

Three Pulse Reversal kept the major channels from synchronizing.

The entire combat circulation failed at once.

Not permanently.

Not enough to kill a healthy High Paragon by itself.

Enough.

Shunto's right leg stopped receiving full qi reinforcement.

His hook arm lagged.

His core output reached the chest and had nowhere clean to go.

He stumbled.

One step.

Two.

Still moving.

Still terrifying.

Luo's eyes widened.

High Paragon.

Even systemic failure did not mean immediate collapse.

Shunto forced himself forward through it.

The hook rose.

Luo had no defense left.

Then the road moved.

Kael Veyran opened one eye.

Worldweight Dominion had never fully vanished.

It had weakened with him.

Collapsed inward.

But six minutes of battle had filled the connected terrain with pressure Shunto and Kael had both imposed.

Kael had almost nothing left in his body.

The road still remembered.

His fingers twitched against the mud.

The stored burden moved.

Every broken stone beneath Shunto's final approach answered at once.

Pressure surged into the exact stance his failed circulation could no longer correct.

Shunto's rear leg buckled.

He turned toward Kael in disbelief.

Kael dragged one destroyed knee beneath himself.

Pushed with the one leg that still worked.

Worldweight returned the battlefield's own stored force through him.

Kael launched forward.

Not graceful.

Not clean.

Shoulder first.

He hit Shunto in the chest.

The impact carried both men across the road.

Shunto's hook flew away.

Kael stayed attached long enough to drive him into the shattered bridge pillar.

Stone boomed.

Shunto's head snapped back.

The failed circulation finally lost the argument.

His body went slack.

Kael slid down with him.

For one second both men sat against the broken pillar like exhausted travellers.

Kael looked sideways.

Shunto's eyes were open but unfocused.

Kael breathed once.

Pain tore through everything.

He looked toward Luo.

“Did that count?”

Luo ran toward them.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Kael fell over.

## Chapter 470 — Live Long Enough

Luo reached Kael first.

That decision cost him nothing.

Shunto was unconscious.

Kael was dying.

Physician triage did not care who deserved what.

Luo's fingers found Kael's neck.

Pulse.

Weak.

Irregular.

Breathing shallow.

One side of the chest moved badly.

Internal bleeding severe.

Skull trauma.

Broken right arm.

Dislocated left shoulder.

Destroyed knee.

Multiple rib fractures.

Hundred Locks residue interfering with already compromised circulation.

Luo's hands moved.

**Lantern Before Dawn.**

Soft controlled qi entered Kael's circulation.

Not enough to heal everything.

Luo was not Rhen.

It stabilized the failing rhythm.

Kept the damaged heart from following the rest of the body into collapse.

Opened just enough circulation around the lungs for breath to continue.

Luo tore Kael's armour open.

Blood had pooled beneath it.

Too much.

“Kael.”

No response.

Luo pressed two fingers below the ribs.

“Kael.”

One eyelid moved.

Good.

Bad.

Consciousness was not required.

Breathing was.

Luo placed three needles.

Then four more.

Bleeding slowed.

Not enough.

He looked toward the corridor.

“Medic!”

The first teams had already started forward the moment Shunto fell.

They ran now.

Luo pointed without looking up.

“Chest kit. Splints. Blood-support tonic. Do not move his neck.”

The Isgard medic dropped beside him.

“What about the other one?”

Luo looked at Shunto.

The former No.2's chest moved.

Barely.

Redline crash had arrived on top of Final Diagnosis, broken ribs, damaged wrist and the final impact.

He was not immediately dying.

He would be soon.

Luo looked back at Kael.

“Kael first.”

They worked.

Two minutes.

Three.

Kael's pulse remained.

Ugly.

But present.

Only then did Luo move to Shunto.

A corridor soldier grabbed his sleeve.

“Doctor.”

Luo looked at him.

The soldier stared at Shunto.

Everyone there knew enough now.

Knew Yun.

Knew some version of what Shunto had done.

Knew Kael lay broken because he had kept Shunto away from Luo.

The soldier's hand tightened.

“You don't have to.”

Luo looked at the hand.

The soldier released him.

Luo knelt beside Shunto.

“No,” he said.

The soldier frowned.

Luo checked Shunto's pulse.

“I do.”

Shunto's circulation was a disaster.

Redline crash.

Pulse Debt.

False Cure.

Three Pulse Reversal residue.

Final Diagnosis had collapsed the combat cycle but had not been designed as execution.

Luo knew exactly what he could do now.

He could wait.

Not touch anything.

Shunto would probably die.

Maybe in three minutes.

Maybe ten.

The war would call it consequence.

Many people would call it justice.

Luo thought of Yun at a tea table correcting the way he held a dropper.

He thought of her saying poison was only medicine with a different intention.

He thought of the line he had promised himself never to cross.

There was only one Pale Venom.

He would not wear her name by becoming worse.

Luo placed the first stabilizing needle.

Shunto's pulse steadied slightly.

Second needle.

The dangerous purge cycle stopped chasing itself.

Third.

Luo used Lantern Before Dawn to support the heart through the crash.

Shunto's eyes opened.

Not fully.

Enough.

He looked at Luo.

Confusion.

Then recognition.

“You.”

Luo kept working.

Shunto swallowed.

It hurt.

“Why?”

Luo said nothing.

Shunto's eyes shifted toward Kael.

The Wuyue No.1 lay surrounded by medics.

Blood everywhere.

Then back to Luo.

“You could let me die.”

“Yes.”

Shunto breathed shallowly.

“Then do it.”

Luo finally looked at him.

“No.”

Something like anger returned to Shunto's eyes.

Luo leaned closer.

His voice remained quiet.

“You are going to live long enough to answer for her.”

Shunto stared at him.

Luo finished the stabilization.

Not healing.

Not restoration.

Enough to prevent immediate death.

Enough to ensure a tribunal had a living defendant.

Then he stood and went back to Kael.

The first white custody banners appeared on the ridge nine minutes later.

Shinsei riders.

Weapons sheathed.

Hands visible.

The corridor troops raised bows anyway.

The Shinsei captain stopped well outside range.

“Shunto Takamori acted without Shinsei authority!” he shouted. “We request joint custody pending medical stabilization and formal judgment!”

Nobody answered.

Luo did not care.

Kael's pulse had weakened again.

“Come on.”

His hands were bloody now.

“Do not make me explain to Sera why Wuyue No.1 died because he refused to sit down.”

Kael did not answer.

One medic looked toward the road.

Frost-white petals had appeared on the far ridge.

At first only three.

Then dozens.

Pale violet shimmered through them.

The medic stopped breathing.

“Doctor.”

Luo did not look up.

“Busy.”

“Doctor.”

“What?”

The air changed.

Rhen stepped onto the road.

Not from nowhere.

His coat snapped once behind him from the speed he had carried down the last slope.

Drifting Petals dispersed around his boots.

His eyes moved once across the scene.

Shunto unconscious under guard.

Broken road.

Destroyed bridge supports.

Blood.

Kael.

Luo.

Rhen was beside them before anyone spoke.

He knelt.

Luo's shoulders dropped one fraction.

Not relief.

Permission to stop being the best option available.

“Internal bleeding,” Luo said immediately. “Multiple ribs. Right arm. Left shoulder. Knee destroyed. Head trauma. Hundred Locks residue. Pulse unstable but supported.”

Rhen placed two fingers against Kael's chest.

Frost-white petals gathered.

“Good work.”

Luo almost laughed.

“Fix him.”

Rhen looked at him.

“I will.”

**Sanctuary of Petals.**

Not thirty miles this time.

One patient.

Focused.

The pale-violet field folded around Kael's body.

Broken ribs aligned.

Bone knit.

The collapsed knee rebuilt ligament by ligament, cartilage by cartilage, fractured edges drawing together beneath the skin.

The right arm straightened.

The left shoulder settled back into the socket and repaired around itself.

Hundred Locks residue dissolved as damaged circulation was restored rather than merely forced through.

Internal tears closed.

Blood where it should not be was broken down and cleared.

The swelling around Kael's face receded.

The deep head trauma stabilized.

Luo watched because he was a physician and physicians watched even when the answer offended them.

Several minutes later, Kael looked like a man who had lost a fight rather than a body somebody had tried to dismantle.

Then even that began fading.

Rhen stopped only when every medically recoverable injury was structurally restored.

Kael remained unconscious.

Rhen checked his pulse.

“Alive.”

Luo exhaled.

“He'll wake?”

“Yes.”

“Can he fight again?”

Rhen looked at him as if the question were obvious.

“Yes.”

Luo nodded.

That mattered.

Not today.

Later.

Kael would never be able to say Shunto took the choice away from him.

Rhen looked toward Shunto.

The Shinsei custody riders went very still.

Luo followed his gaze.

“He's stable.”

Rhen's expression did not change.

“You treated him.”

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Luo looked at him.

“You aren't going to ask why?”

Rhen glanced at him.

“You're a physician.”

Luo looked away.

That was somehow worse than praise.

Rhen stood.

“Do not restore Shunto to combat condition,” Luo said.

“I wasn't planning to.”

“He can survive as he is.”

Rhen checked him from a distance with the Sanctuary's edge.

“Yes.”

The Shinsei captain called again.

“We request joint custody.”

Rhen looked at Luo.

“Your decision?”

Luo stared.

“Mine?”

“You were here.”

Luo looked toward the corridor commander.

Then toward the white banners.

Then at Shunto.

“Wuyue and Isgard guards until Sera and Solveig approve transfer terms. Shinsei may provide physicians under observation.”

Rhen nodded.

“Reasonable.”

The corridor commander repeated the order.

Nobody looked at Rhen for strategy.

That pleased him.

Kael made a sound behind them.

Everyone turned.

His eyes opened slowly.

He looked at the sky.

Then Rhen.

Then Luo.

His voice came out rough.

“Did we win?”

Luo stared at him.

“You were clinically dead-adjacent five minutes ago.”

Kael blinked.

“So yes?”

Rhen smiled faintly.

Luo closed his eyes.

“Yes.”

Kael relaxed.

“Good.”

Then, after a moment:

“My face hurts.”

Rhen frowned.

“It shouldn't.”

Kael considered that.

“Then I remember it hurting.”

“That seems likely.”

Kael looked toward the ruined bridge.

Engineers were already staring at him.

He sighed.

“I owe them a bridge.”

Luo sat down in the mud.

For the first time since Yun died, a laugh escaped him before he could stop it.

Small.

Exhausted.

Real.

Kael looked at him.

“Was that a laugh?”

“No.”

“It was.”

“You have head trauma.”

“Rhen fixed it.”

“Then perhaps he missed something.”

Rhen looked offended.

Kael smiled.

Across the road, Shunto remained alive beneath guard.

Not forgiven.

Not free.

Alive.

The former Second Seat had wanted one last proof that strength still entitled him to judgment.

Instead Wuyue's Number One had broken his body to protect a physician.

And the physician had chosen to keep Shunto alive for judgment anyway.

Season 111 ended with Kael Veyran lying beneath an Isgard sky, completely capable of fighting again one day.

He simply did not yet know whether he wanted to.
