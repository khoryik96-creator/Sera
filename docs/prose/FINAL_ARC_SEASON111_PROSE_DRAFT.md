## Chapter 461 — A Disgraced Number Two

Shunto Takamori counted to three hundred and twelve before the first old habit saved him.

Not a technique.

Not a Dominion.

A habit.

The guard who brought his water still knocked twice before opening the outer hatch.

Two knocks.

The old signal used for a senior internal-security prisoner inspection.

The man realized what he had done only after the second knock landed.

His face changed.

Shunto smiled.

The hatch opened three fingers wide.

“Water.”

Shunto remained seated on the narrow cot.

“No title?”

The guard's jaw tightened.

“You have been stripped of command.”

“I heard.”

The cup slid through.

Shunto did not reach for it.

“How long have you served?”

The guard said nothing.

“Eight years?”

Silence.

“Nine.”

The guard looked at him.

That was enough.

Shunto leaned back against the wall.

“For nine years, if I told you to open a corridor, you opened it.”

“You do not command me now.”

“No.”

Shunto's gaze dropped to the man's hand.

The hand holding the hatch lever.

“Your body still thinks I do.”

The guard shut the hatch hard.

Shunto listened to his breathing outside.

Too fast.

Not fear.

Conflict.

That was the problem with authority.

A seal could be removed in one sentence.

Years of obedience took longer.

Shunto resumed counting.

Three hundred and thirteen.

Three hundred and fourteen.

The second habit came at four hundred and six.

A shift supervisor arrived to verify that Shunto had no hidden communication needles.

The supervisor brought two soldiers.

Both from internal security.

Both had trained under Shunto's office.

One still called him “sir.”

The supervisor corrected him immediately.

The mistake had already happened.

Shunto saw the soldier's embarrassment.

He saw the supervisor's anger.

He saw which man would hesitate if procedure broke.

He stored that.

At six hundred and eighty-one, a messenger arrived outside the detention block carrying the next revocation sheet.

Shunto heard the clerk arguing with the gate captain.

“Central authorization has removed Redline access.”

“I have that.”

“Prisoner-system clearance too.”

“I have that.”

“Supply routing?”

The captain paused.

“No.”

“Then sign.”

Paper scratched.

Shunto closed his eyes.

Supply routing was still alive.

At nine hundred and twelve, somebody changed the guard rotation early.

That was Tsubasa.

Shunto almost respected it.

Almost.

He stood.

The cell was built for a Paragon.

Not to contain one forever.

Nothing made by men could promise that.

It was built to make breaking out expensive enough that everyone outside had time to respond.

Layered ironwood.

Qi-damping seals worked into the frame.

Four physical locks.

Two senior guards beyond the first door.

A second containment corridor twenty paces farther out.

Shunto touched the wall.

He did not activate **Hundred Locks Dominion**.

That would announce everything.

He waited instead.

The next water check came seven minutes early.

A new guard.

Young.

Not internal security.

Better.

The hatch opened.

Shunto dropped to one knee.

The guard stiffened.

“What happened?”

Shunto let his shoulder sag.

“Chest.”

The guard did not move.

“Call medical.”

Shunto breathed shallowly.

“Redline withdrawal.”

That phrase did what he expected.

The guard's uncertainty became procedure.

There was no standard Redline withdrawal protocol for a High Paragon who had not officially dosed.

Which meant the guard had to ask someone.

The hatch shut.

Footsteps moved.

Shunto stood immediately.

Three breaths later, the outer seal weakened.

Not opened.

Weakened.

Somebody outside was allowing a physician to inspect through the barrier.

Shunto struck once.

Not the door.

The hinge pin.

**Five Lock Hand.**

One compressed fingertip impact entered the point where metal, seal and frame shared stress.

The hinge did not explode.

It shifted half an inch.

Enough.

Shunto struck the lower hinge.

Then the center seam.

Then the first physical lock.

The guard shouted.

Shunto drove his shoulder into the door.

The qi-damping seal flared.

The already-shifted hinge tore free.

The door folded inward.

The young guard raised a spear.

Shunto caught the shaft with one hand.

He could have killed him.

He did not.

He twisted, stepped inside the guard's balance, and struck the upper arm once.

The limb went numb.

The spear fell.

The second guard reached for the alarm bell.

Shunto kicked the bell stand through the wall before the hammer landed.

Then he ran.

The camp reacted faster than most armies in the world could have.

But it was reacting to its former No.2.

That mattered.

“Stop him!”

Some soldiers moved instantly.

Some looked around for confirmation.

Some recognized Shunto's face before they recognized the order.

A captain stepped into his path.

“Former Second Seat, halt!”

Shunto almost laughed at the title.

He vaulted the wagon beside him, crossed a roof, dropped into the next lane and reached a restricted supply corridor before the camp fully understood which direction he had taken.

The supply officer at the checkpoint held up a hand.

“Seal.”

Shunto showed the old travel token on instinct.

The man looked at it.

His eyes flicked to the revocation board.

The board had not updated yet.

That half-second was all Shunto needed.

He passed.

Behind him, somebody shouted his name.

The supply officer turned.

Shunto was already inside the next building.

He knew the room.

Emergency field stores.

Bandages.

Coagulants.

Qi stabilizers.

Three locked Redline boxes reserved for command-break emergencies.

His authority over them had been revoked.

The physical lock did not know that yet.

Shunto opened one with the old command key.

Inside lay six black-sealed vials.

He took one.

Only one.

He stared at it for a moment.

The new labels Aya had forced onto the stock were impossible to miss.

**3× REDLINE. APPROXIMATE FUTURE-VITALITY COST: FIVE YEARS.**

Shunto's mouth tightened.

“Five years,” he murmured.

Then slipped the vial into his coat.

The alarm finally rolled across the whole camp.

Not bells now.

Signal gongs.

Three short.

One long.

Internal breach.

Shunto moved faster.

He knew where the horse lines were.

He knew which gates still accepted old supply-routing clearance.

He knew which quartermaster would check a seal before checking a face.

He knew exactly how Shinsei worked because he had helped teach it how to work.

That was the third habit that saved him.

By the time Tsubasa Kurokawa reached the detention block, Shunto had already crossed the outer camp road.

Kai stood beside the broken cell door.

Hana arrived seconds later.

Nao behind her.

Tsubasa looked at the hinge.

Then at the unconscious guard.

“Alive?”

Aya was already kneeling beside him.

“Yes.”

Tsubasa's jaw tightened.

Hana looked toward the south gate.

“He is not heading for Shinrin.”

“No.”

“Yurushi?”

Tsubasa shook his head.

“Too far.”

Kai studied the routing table dragged from the gate office.

“Then what does he want?”

Tsubasa did not answer immediately.

Because he knew Shunto.

Knew the way shame became accusation inside him.

Knew that being stripped would feel worse to Shunto than being wounded.

A messenger ran into the lane.

“Supreme Commander.”

Tsubasa looked at him.

“Former Second Seat used still-live supply authorization at West Three.”

“Destination?”

“Unclear. The route grants access toward the western recovered corridors.”

Hana went still.

“Wuyue.”

Kai's eyes sharpened.

Tsubasa looked toward the distant mountains.

“Who is out there?”

The messenger unfolded a slate.

“Several rotating commands. Wuyue No.1 Kael Veyran is recovering near the Greywater corridor. Luo Wen is inspecting recovered medical stores there.”

Silence.

Tsubasa closed his eyes once.

Then opened them.

“Send warning to Wuyue command.”

“Already moving.”

“Send our own pursuit.”

Kai stepped forward.

“I'll go.”

“No.”

Kai frowned.

Tsubasa continued.

“If you leave now, every allied scout sees another Shinsei High Paragon racing toward their No.1 and assumes coordinated attack.”

“Then Hana.”

“Same problem.”

Hana understood first.

“You want normal pursuit.”

“Yes.”

“Too slow.”

“Yes.”

Tsubasa looked toward the route Shunto had taken.

The cost of doing this lawfully was suddenly measured in distance.

“Send riders under white custody banners,” he ordered. “Signal every Wuyue relay that Shunto Takamori is acting without Shinsei authority.”

Aya stood.

“And if they don't believe us?”

Tsubasa's expression did not change.

“They would be reasonable not to.”

## Chapter 462 — The Corridor

Kael Veyran hated recovering.

Luo Wen hated Kael Veyran recovering.

These were related problems.

“Sit.”

Kael kept walking.

Luo looked up from the crate he was inspecting.

“Did you misunderstand?”

“No.”

“Then sit.”

“I am walking.”

“Yes. Incorrectly.”

Kael stopped.

The Greywater corridor stretched through a narrow valley where thaw runoff had turned half the road into brown slush. Wuyue engineers had rebuilt two bridges in six days. Isgard teams were reopening the western supply lane. Prisoner wagons moved south under marked medical escort while military traffic used the eastern fork.

It was ugly.

Muddy.

Efficient.

Kael liked it.

Luo did not care.

“You tore the same intercostal line yesterday.”

“It healed.”

“It closed.”

“Same thing.”

“No.”

Kael folded his arms.

The movement pulled slightly at his left side.

Luo noticed.

Kael noticed Luo noticing.

“This is why physicians are unpopular.”

“Incorrect. Physicians are unpopular because patients are stupid.”

A nearby Isgard medic coughed into her sleeve to hide a laugh.

Kael looked at her.

She became very interested in a box.

Luo returned to the captured medical stores.

The reason he was here was legitimate.

Shinsei had abandoned three supply sheds during the last contraction. Two contained ordinary medicine. One contained Axtaya precursors, field stabilizers and documentation written in a shorthand that mixed military dosage codes with Aya's original medical notation.

Luo wanted every page before someone decided it was only inventory.

Kael was here because the corridor ran through Stonecrown's current northern responsibility and because Kael, according to Kael, was “fine.”

According to every physician who had touched him, Kael was not fine.

Rhen's **Sanctuary of Petals** had not reached this region when the mass restoration began. Kael had refused relocation while the corridor was unstable, and Luo had been unwilling to move several critical patients simply to put one stubborn Paragon under a better healer.

So Kael had recovered the old-fashioned way.

Mostly.

His ribs were set.

His shoulder was functional.

The worst meridian abrasions from Kai had closed.

He was perhaps eighty-five percent.

Kael called that one hundred.

Luo called it evidence that numbers should require licensing.

Kael leaned over the open crate.

“Anything?”

“Yes.”

“What?”

“A reason for you to stop breathing on my notes.”

Kael straightened.

“You've gotten less pleasant.”

Luo's hands stopped for one fraction of a second.

Then resumed.

“Probably.”

Kael looked at him.

There were things the two men did not discuss.

Yun was one of them.

Not because Kael did not care.

Because Luo had made it clear that pity was not useful medicine.

Kael respected that.

Mostly.

A scout entered the shed.

“Lord Kael.”

Kael turned.

“What?”

“Western ridge patrol missed a signal window.”

Luo looked up.

Kael's expression changed immediately.

No complaint.

No laziness.

No recovery.

Wuyue No.1 returned to his face like a door closing.

“How long?”

“Seven minutes.”

“Weather?”

“Clear.”

“Other relays?”

“Normal.”

Kael walked outside.

Luo followed.

The corridor commander met them halfway.

“Could be equipment failure.”

Kael looked toward the western ridge.

“No.”

The commander frowned.

“You know that?”

“No.”

“Then—”

“I dislike the timing.”

Kael pointed.

“Pull noncombatants behind Bridge Two. Prisoners too.”

“Lord Kael, that will jam the road.”

“Jam it.”

The commander saluted and ran.

Luo looked at Kael.

“You felt something.”

Kael's eyes stayed on the ridge.

“No.”

“That is reassuring.”

“I said I dislike the timing.”

“Your instincts are not medicine.”

“They have kept me alive.”

“Your physicians have kept you alive.”

Kael almost answered.

Then the first body fell from the ridge.

Not dead.

A Wuyue scout.

He hit the road hard, rolled twice and stopped near the ditch.

His right arm hung uselessly.

Kael moved before anyone else.

He caught the next falling shape.

Another scout.

Alive.

Breathing.

Both arms numb.

Luo dropped beside the first.

One touch to the wrist.

His expression sharpened.

“Circulation locks.”

Kael looked up.

A man stood on the ridge.

Dark travelling coat.

Paired hooked blades hanging low.

No Shinsei banner.

No escort.

Shunto Takamori stepped off the ridge and landed on the road.

Mud barely splashed.

Kael straightened.

“Interesting.”

Luo's face went still.

Shunto looked past Kael.

His eyes found Luo.

Recognition came slowly.

“The physician.”

Luo stood.

Kael moved half a step between them without looking back.

Shunto noticed.

“Protective.”

Kael rolled his shoulder once.

“What happened to your army?”

“I no longer require one.”

“That sounds like something men say shortly before regretting it.”

Shunto's mouth tightened.

He drew both hooks.

The corridor soldiers raised weapons.

Kael lifted one hand.

“Back.”

Nobody moved.

He looked over his shoulder.

“Did I become unclear while recovering?”

The commander swallowed.

“Back!”

Troops withdrew toward Bridge Two.

Luo did not.

Kael looked at him.

“No.”

Luo folded his sleeves higher.

“I wasn't asking.”

“Shunto is a High Paragon.”

“I know.”

“You are a Sovereign.”

“I know.”

“Then why are you still here?”

Luo looked toward the two injured scouts.

“Because I am their physician.”

Kael stared at him.

Luo added, “And yours.”

“That was not comforting.”

“It was not intended to be.”

Shunto took one step forward.

Kael's qi settled into the road.

Earth.

Sand.

Stone.

Pressure moved outward through everything physically connected beneath his feet.

The valley seemed to become attentive.

Shunto smiled faintly.

“So the new Paragon has learned to stand.”

Kael's expression flattened.

“Come find out.”

**Worldweight Dominion** opened.

Shunto answered.

**Hundred Locks Dominion** spread across the road.

## Chapter 463 — Worldweight Against Hundred Locks

Shunto's first mistake was treating the road like ground.

Every wagon that had crossed it.

Every stone hammered down by engineers.

Every patch of packed sand beneath the mud.

Stored it.

Returned burden through the same connected network.

Not gravity.

Not invisible magic.

Shorter step.

Less force.

Only touched.

“Stop forcing circulation through it.”

“I need it.”

Lock.
