## Chapter 391 — Two Paragons Enter Sera’s Home

**Days earlier, while Wuyue’s main expedition was still crossing north...**

Haru Ishikawa looked at the Quaint Teahouse and decided the intelligence report was incomplete.

Hana Arakawa looked at it and decided the intelligence report was probably terrible.

The building was too ordinary.

That was the first problem.

A low roof.

Dark wood.

Medicinal garden behind the east wall.

A clinic wing.

Storage structures.

Training yards that looked, from outside, like they belonged to a well-funded private organization with too many disciplined guards.

Nothing about it said continental threat.

That did not make Hana feel better.

Haru studied the outer road.

“Traffic reduced.”

“Sera left.”

“Yes.”

“Public Top Ten left.”

“Yes.”

“Rhen unavailable.”

“Yes.”

Hana looked at him.

“You are enjoying saying yes.”

“No.”

“You sound like it.”

“I am confirming variables.”

“You are a deeply exciting person.”

Haru ignored her.

He had been feared since before most soldiers understood his name.

At twenty, the Hundred-Pace Courtyard made commanders notice him.

Later came the duel everyone repeated incorrectly.

Forty-seven thrusts.

An Established Paragon opponent had blocked or evaded every one.

People watching thought Haru was losing.

Then Carved Spear Dominion triggered the stored lines.

The opponent discovered the first forty-seven attacks had not been failed attempts.

They had been geometry.

Every thrust had left a temporary spear-qi line in the exact path it had traveled.

One by one, Haru re-fired the battlefield he had already built.

The forty-eighth thrust was the only one that mattered in the present.

Everything else came from the past.

That was why Haru was Shinsei No.4.

Not because he attacked fastest.

Because he measured until the battlefield became narrower than the opponent understood.

Hana looked at the wall again.

“What is bothering you?”

“Too few visible defenders.”

“That should be good.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Organizations with Sera’s known resources do not leave critical medical infrastructure this exposed.”

Hana’s eyes narrowed.

There.

That was the real Haru.

Not arrogance.

Pattern.

He tapped the report.

“Quaint’s public profile says medicine, private security, logistics.”

“Yes.”

“Sera took one hundred twenty north.”

“Yes.”

“If that is a meaningful fraction, more people should be visible here.”

Hana looked toward the tree line.

“Unless they’re hidden.”

“Or distributed.”

“Or the report is wrong.”

Haru folded it.

“That possibility is increasing.”

The mission was limited.

Probe the support architecture.

Damage command/logistics enough to force Wuyue’s expedition to consider returning.

Do not assault Stonecrown’s entire fixed army.

Do not turn a pressure operation into conquest.

Withdraw if the target proves materially stronger than assessed.

Tsubasa had been explicit.

Hana had appreciated that.

Shunto would have called caution weakness.

Tsubasa called it not being stupid.

They crossed the outer marker.

No alarm sounded.

That was the second problem.

A young man carrying two baskets of dried herbs looked at them.

Peak Marquis.

Hana felt it immediately.

Not a guard captain.

A herb carrier.

Peak Marquis.

He set the baskets down.

“Visitors?”

Haru looked at him.

“Move away from the building.”

The man glanced at Hana.

Then at Haru’s spear.

His expression did not become heroic.

Good.

He turned and shouted:

“RED TWO.”

The teahouse changed.

Not visibly at first.

Doors closed.

Two clinic assistants moved three patients through a rear corridor.

A bell rang once below ground.

Six people carrying medicine crates abandoned the crates and started clearing noncombatants instead.

No panic.

No mass rush toward the Paragons.

Hana stared.

“Prepared.”

“Yes.”

The herb carrier backed away with the civilians instead of fighting.

Haru’s eyes sharpened.

“Good organization.”

Then an iron pressure touched the road.

Hana stopped.

Haru did too.

A man walked through the outer gate wearing no ceremonial armor.

Broad shoulders.

Shorter dark hair.

Rugged face.

No visible weapon.

Tae Muyeon.

Haru recognized the name.

Everyone in Shinsei intelligence did.

Sera’s Sepal.

Former co-No.1 in older Wuyue records.

Sovereign-class historical data.

Dangerous.

Useful.

Not Paragon.

The intelligence model ended there.

Tae stopped twenty paces away.

Looked at Haru.

Then Hana.

Then behind them.

“Only two?”

Hana blinked.

Haru’s grip adjusted on his spear.

Tae sighed.

“I was hoping the intelligence service had become more ambitious.”

Hana looked at Haru.

“Do you know him?”

“No.”

Tae heard anyway.

“Rude.”

Haru stepped forward.

“We’re not here for civilians.”

“Good.”

“We are here for the support network.”

“Also rude.”

“Tae Muyeon.”

“Haru Ishikawa.”

“Hana Arakawa.”

Tae nodded once.

“High Paragon. Established Paragon.”

Hana’s eyes narrowed.

He had identified them cleanly.

Haru studied his circulation.

Dense.

Too dense.

Iron pressure under skin and bone.

Wrong for the old reports.

Haru’s expression changed.

Tae smiled.

There.

He saw it.

“The report said Sovereign?” Tae asked.

Hana said nothing.

Tae’s smile widened.

“That’s unfortunate.”

Then **Iron Chain Dominion** opened.

Haru’s first instinct was not fear.

It was recalculation.

The second was worse.

Because the Dominion was not weak.

Not new.

Not unstable.

High Paragon.

Haru looked at Hana.

She had already reached the same conclusion.

One hidden High Paragon.

Inside Sera’s house.

The operation had just become materially stronger than assessed.

They should have withdrawn.

Then Tae moved.

And Haru’s pride made the first mistake.

## Chapter 392 — Tae Muyeon Takes Both

Haru thrust once.

Tae stepped aside.

A chain formed around Haru’s wrist.

Not metal.

Compressed iron qi.

Haru felt the burden immediately.

Small.

Manageable.

Hana crossed right with Falling Sky Step.

Two more chains formed around her legs.

She changed direction.

Another.

Tae smiled.

“Keep moving.”

Hana swore.

Iron Chain Dominion did not stop action.

That would have been easier.

It charged for it.

Every meaningful hostile commitment created compressed iron-qi burden on the person making it.

Movement.

Attack.

Forceful dodge.

Qi release.

Committed block.

One action was nothing.

Ten became annoying.

Twenty became tactical.

Fifty became a body learning that every answer made the next answer heavier.

Haru understood in seconds.

He stabbed again anyway.

**Three-Length Spear.**

The reach changed at the last instant through grip, body extension and compressed spear qi.

Tae had measured the ordinary line correctly.

The tip extended beyond expectation.

It clipped his ribs.

First blood.

Tae looked down at the torn cloth.

Then at Haru.

“Good.”

Haru did not appreciate the tone.

Another chain settled over his forearm.

He triggered Carved Spear Dominion.

The first thrust’s line remained.

Stored.

Temporary.

Tae’s eyes moved once.

He saw it.

Haru attacked from a different angle.

Tae blocked with one forearm.

**Ironheart.**

The limb became brutally dense only at impact.

Spear met something that felt less like flesh and more like striking a moving iron pillar.

Haru’s hand numbed.

The blocked thrust left another stored line.

Hana entered from behind Tae.

Twin Gale Crescent.

Two wind-edged sword cuts.

The first forced Tae’s shoulder.

The second punished the guard shift.

Tae turned through it.

One blade opened his upper arm.

Hana smiled.

Then four new chains settled across her torso and thighs.

Her smile disappeared.

“Annoying.”

Tae nodded.

“That is usually the review.”

Haru triggered the first stored line.

The old thrust fired again along its exact path.

Tae had already moved.

The line clipped his coat, not body.

Second stored line.

Tae ducked.

Hana crossed low.

The fight accelerated.

That favored them.

At first.

Haru and Hana were excellent together despite not being a permanent pair.

Haru narrowed geometry.

Hana exploited the narrowing.

Spear removed straight exits.

Wind movement punished lateral correction.

Hollow Sky Dominion opened.

Low-pressure pockets formed around Tae.

Breathing became harder.

Sound changed.

Pressure boundaries tugged at footing.

Tae stepped through one.

His ear popped painfully.

Hana collapsed a pocket.

Air rushed back violently.

Tae’s stance shifted half a foot.

Haru had been waiting.

Nine Lines Collapse.

One.

Two.

Three spear lines removed retreat angles.

Tae blocked the fourth.

Chains formed on Haru.

Fifth.

Hana crossed.

Chains formed on Hana.

Sixth.

Tae gave ground.

Seventh.

He anchored.

**Anchor Step.**

Compressed iron qi bit through the stone beneath his foot.

Hana’s pressure equalization failed to move him fully.

Eighth.

Tae struck the spear shaft.

**Horizon Break.**

One physical impact.

Then compressed iron-pressure layers collapsed through the same vector in sequence.

Haru’s spear bent.

Not broke.

Bent enough.

Ninth line missed.

Haru backed off.

Too fast.

Chains accumulated.

His first retreat step felt normal.

Second heavier.

Third carried every previous commitment with it.

Tae advanced.

Now the Dominion’s real cruelty appeared.

Reducing aggression slowed new accumulation.

But reducing aggression surrendered initiative.

Haru hated that.

Hana understood it better.

“Stop forcing it,” she said.

“I know.”

“You’re still forcing it.”

“I know.”

Tae heard.

“Healthy relationship.”

“We are not together,” Hana snapped.

Tae blinked.

“I meant tactically.”

Hana looked furious.

Haru almost laughed.

That mistake cost him.

Tae appeared inside spear length.

Not teleportation.

Anchor Step from a broken pillar to the road.

Violent pivot.

Haru barely turned the spear shaft in time.

Tae’s fist landed on the guard.

Ironheart made the knuckles impossibly heavy at contact.

Haru flew backward.

Chains tightened.

He hit the ground hard.

Hana crossed through darkness beneath the eave—

No.

Not darkness technique.

Pure Wind-Qi movement.

Falling Sky Step changed her direction so sharply she appeared to fall sideways.

She caught Haru’s sleeve and pulled him out before Tae’s second strike landed.

More chains.

Hana’s breathing sharpened.

Hollow Sky Dominion was costing her.

Iron Chain Dominion was making every effort more expensive.

Tae was also bleeding from three cuts and one spear graze.

Good.

He was not invulnerable.

Haru stood.

Measured.

Recalculated.

One High Paragon.

Their combined pressure could hurt him.

Could possibly defeat him if they solved the Domain quickly enough.

Then a massive presence appeared behind Tae.

Haru’s eyes shifted.

A very large man stepped through the inner gate carrying a teapot.

Huo Wujin.

Historical Wuyue data: Sovereign.

Tae looked over his shoulder.

“You’re late.”

Huo looked at the broken outer wall.

Then at the teapot.

Then at Haru and Hana.

“I was making tea.”

Tae stared.

“During an attack.”

“It was steeping.”

Haru felt Huo’s circulation.

Nine rotating structures.

Deep.

Disciplined.

Wrong.

Again.

Hana whispered:

“No.”

Huo set the teapot on a stone post.

Then opened **Endless Dominion**.

High Paragon.

Haru’s face went still.

Two.

There were two hidden High Paragons inside the house.

Tae stepped back.

Haru noticed.

Not because Tae needed help.

Because the defenders were **rotating**.

Preserving one another.

The organization around them was still evacuating civilians.

Moving medicine.

Closing secondary approaches.

This was not three strong people waiting to fight.

It was a system.

Haru finally understood the size of the intelligence failure.

And they still had not seen who commanded it.

## Chapter 393 — Huo Changes the Answer

Huo Wujin’s first attack missed.

Haru knew because he dodged it.

Then realized he had dodged in the wrong direction.

His mind had chosen left.

His body resolved right.

Haru’s expression changed.

Huo grinned.

“There it is.”

**Endless Dominion.**

The Nine Meridian Wheels did not control intent.

Haru still knew exactly what he wanted to do.

That was the problem.

The Dominion rotated the relationship between intent, balance, movement timing and circulation response.

Want left.

Body resolves right.

Want retreat.

Momentum tips forward.

Prepare spear qi for the right arm.

Release arrives a fraction late through the wrong timing.

Attack becomes guard.

Guard opens into attack.

Not random.

Worse.

Structured change.

Haru corrected the first mismatch.

Huo’s smile widened.

Second Wheel overlapped.

The correction failed differently.

Hana entered from the flank.

She was harder to disrupt than Haru expected.

Wind-Qi movement had taught her body to survive changing pressure, unstable footing and violent directional shifts since childhood.

Her Falling Sky Step converted one wrong-body response into a new angle.

Huo’s forearm caught the first sword.

The second cut his shoulder.

Hana passed him.

“Not bad,” Huo said.

“Stop complimenting us.”

“No.”

She attacked again.

Hollow Sky Dominion returned.

Low-pressure pockets collided with Endless rhythm.

Huo inhaled once and felt breathing resistance change.

His body adapted.

Hana collapsed the pocket.

Air slammed sideways.

Huo moved with it rather than against it.

Then the Wheel changed.

Hana intended to continue left.

Her next step resolved slightly inward.

Only slightly.

Enough for Huo’s palm to strike her guard.

She flew through a wooden rail.

Haru’s spear arrived.

Three-Length Spear.

Huo read the body line.

The compressed reach changed.

The tip opened his side.

Haru stored the line.

Huo looked down at the blood.

“Good trick.”

Haru’s jaw tightened.

He triggered the stored thrust.

Huo stepped aside.

Except his own Dominion rotated something.

His left foot moved too slowly.

The stored line clipped his thigh.

Haru saw it.

“Your Domain affects you?”

Huo laughed.

“No.”

Then hit him.

Haru blocked.

Wrong.

He had intended to parry outward.

His spear came inward.

The shaft still intercepted the fist, but badly.

Huo’s second strike landed in the ribs.

Haru coughed.

“I let you think that,” Huo said.

Haru hated him instantly.

From the outer wall, Tae laughed.

“Everyone does.”

Huo looked back.

“You’re resting?”

“I’m managing perimeter.”

“You’re sitting.”

“Strategically.”

Hana emerged from the broken rail.

“Are they always like this?”

Haru answered before Tae could.

“Focus.”

She looked at him.

“That was not an answer.”

Huo came again.

The fight turned ugly.

Not because Huo was overwhelmingly faster.

Because solving him refused to stay solved.

Haru changed breathing count.

Worked for two exchanges.

Third Wheel answered.

He changed footwork cadence.

Worked once.

Fourth Wheel overlapped.

He stopped trusting reflex and moved through deliberate micro-commands.

Huo adjusted to the slower decision chain.

Hana tried to use pressure boundaries as external reference instead of internal rhythm.

Endless Dominion shifted the timing between her balance correction and Wind-Qi burst.

She remained functional.

Barely. Haru stored spear lines anyway.

One.

Two.

Three.

Even wrong timing could leave useful geometry if the thrust completed honestly.

He triggered them together.

Huo evaded the first.

Blocked the second.

The third hit his upper arm.

Haru saw blood.

Then Huo changed his answer.

Not the Dominion.

Huo himself.

He stopped trying to attack through Haru’s spear geometry.

He attacked Haru’s **corrections**.

Every time Haru solved one mismatch, Huo waited for the compensating posture.

Then hit that.

Haru’s precision became prediction.

His strength became a map Huo kept redrawing.

After seven minutes, Haru’s breathing changed.

Hana noticed.

“Enough.”

“Not yet.”

“Haru.”

“We can still—”

A low hum passed through the ground.

Everyone stopped.

Not from fear.

Because the whole compound seemed to return one heartbeat of sound.

Huo stepped back.

Tae stood.

Hana felt it first through pressure.

Haru through the spear shaft.

Vibration.

Every footstep.

Every broken board settling.

Every breath inside the clinic.

Every moving cart.

The battlefield suddenly contained too much information.

A blind man walked from the central building.

Qin Luo.

Second Inner Petal.

Operational deputy.

Historical data: Sovereign.

Haru’s stomach dropped before the cultivation was fully visible.

Because the pattern was now obvious.

The report had been wrong twice.

It was about to be wrong a third time.

Qin stopped at the inner courtyard.

Eyes closed.

One hand touching the wall.

High Paragon.

Hana whispered:

“Three.”

Haru said nothing.

Qin tilted his head.

“Civilians clear?”

Lu Weiran’s voice answered from inside.

“Primary clinic clear. Lower pharmacy secured. West stair still moving two apprentices.”

Qin waited.

Five breaths.

A bell rang once.

Lu again:

“Clear.”

Only then did Qin step forward.

Haru understood the final insult.

Qin had been here the whole time.

He had not been waiting for his turn to fight.

He had been running the evacuation.

Now the battlefield was secure enough for him to stop being a commander and become a Paragon.

Qin opened **Echo Dominion**.

## Chapter 394 — Qin Ends the First Attack

Sight stopped being useful first.

Hana still had eyes.

They simply ceased to provide a stable battlefield.

Every movement created overlapping resonance.

Footsteps returned through stone.

Sword motion disturbed air.

Qi releases pushed pressure into walls.

The Dominion caught all of it.

Returned it.

Multiplied not power, but information.

Hana’s vision said Qin stood ahead.

Her ears said movement came left.

Her qi perception found three overlapping pressure returns.

Her body chose none confidently.

She became functionally blind.

Qin had always been blind.

He looked completely comfortable.

Haru thrust.

Echo Dominion answered.

Not by copying the spear.

A resonance-force counterpart carrying comparable committed force returned toward Haru.

He shifted.

The shift created more resonance.

His planted foot gave Qin another location.

Haru blocked the echo-wave with his spear shaft.

The block itself became another committed action.

Another echo returned.

Haru’s face changed.

“Do less,” Hana said.

“I know.”

They reduced aggression.

Immediately.

Good decision.

Bad battlefield.

Qin advanced.

That was the trap.

Attack hard and feed Echo.

Move aggressively and lose orientation.

Reduce action and surrender initiative to someone who already knew where everything was.

Qin struck Haru once in the shoulder.

Simple palm.

No named technique.

Haru blocked late.

The force carried through.

Hana attacked from the side.

Qin turned before she arrived.

Not seeing.

Feeling every pressure change through the ground, walls and air.

Her first sword met his forearm.

The committed force returned as resonance.

Hana twisted with it.

Excellent.

Falling Sky Step converted recoil into a descending angle.

Qin’s head tilted.

He stepped half a pace.

She missed.

Not because he was faster.

Because he had known the line before sight could have described it.

Haru triggered three stored spear lines from Carved Spear Dominion.

Old attacks.

Different problem.

Echo Dominion read the discharges as force events when they activated.

Qin could not erase them.

Did not need to.

He moved between the fixed lines.

One grazed his sleeve.

Second broke stone.

Third he redirected with a resonance burst that spoiled its clean alignment enough to miss his ribs.

Haru saw that.

Important.

Domains were not automatic cancellation.

Carved Spear still functioned.

Hollow Sky still functioned.

Their problem was sustainable use inside Qin’s field.

Hana opened multiple low-pressure pockets.

Qin’s breathing changed.

His qi adjusted.

Pressure boundaries interfered with vibration.

For one moment, Hana created a patch where air gave Qin less reliable information.

Haru saw it.

He attacked through it.

Good.

Qin felt the spear only after the ground contact shifted.

Later than before.

The tip cut his side.

Hana followed.

Twin Gale Crescent.

Qin blocked one.

Second opened his shoulder.

They had him.

For two seconds.

Then Qin struck the ground.

Not hard.

Precisely.

A broad vibration pulse moved through the entire courtyard floor.

Walls.

Broken rails.

Clinic foundation.

Every physical structure answered.

Hollow Sky could disrupt air.

It could not erase stone.

Qin rebuilt the battlefield picture through the ground.

Haru understood too late.

Echo-wave force came back from his own last thrust.

He redirected.

Another from Hana’s cut.

She moved.

More resonance.

Their breathing worsened.

Not because Echo stole qi.

Because maintaining two Domains, constant correction and repeated defensive commitments inside a field designed to punish commitment was expensive.

Qin remained patient.

He did not chase a knockout.

That was worse.

Five minutes later, Hana dropped Hollow Sky Dominion first.

Her body needed air more than pride needed territory.

Haru kept Carved Spear active another thirty seconds.

Then one stored line collapsed before he could trigger it.

Not from Qin cancelling it.

From Haru’s own declining precision and reserve.

He stopped.

Silence did not return.

Echo Dominion still hummed through the compound.

Haru looked at Hana.

She nodded.

Withdraw.

Correct answer.

Finally.

Qin heard the shift in their posture.

He did not attack immediately.

“Leaving?”

Haru’s jaw tightened.

“Yes.”

“Good.”

Hana looked at him.

“You’re letting us?”

Qin tilted his head.

“Sera authorizes lethal action.”

“That does not prevent capture.”

“No.”

“Then why?”

Qin’s expression remained calm.

“Because you came to damage a support site, found three High Paragons you did not know existed, and are now going home to tell your government exactly how wrong its intelligence was.”

Haru became very still.

Qin continued.

“That information is useful to us too.”

Qin’s choice was strategy, not mercy.

Not only.

Strategy.

Hana looked toward Tae and Huo.

Both were still standing.

Injured.

Not badly.

The compound behind them remained functional.

Peak-Marquis and Duke-level Quaint members were already repairing outer barriers.

Patients had been moved.

Medicine saved.

No panicked mob.

No shattered organization.

The raid had failed completely.

Haru lowered his spear.

“Three High Paragons.”

Qin said nothing.

“Outside the public Top Ten.”

“Yes.”

“Living here.”

“Yes.”

Haru looked toward the inner building.

“And Rhen?”

Qin’s expression changed by almost nothing.

“Physician.”

Haru stared.

Tae coughed loudly.

Huo looked away.

Hana narrowed her eyes.

Nobody believed that answer anymore.

But they also knew they were not getting a better one.

Haru and Hana withdrew.

Fast.

Controlled.

No pursuit beyond the outer boundary.

The moment they vanished into the western route, Tae looked at Qin.

“You could have hit him harder.”

“Yes.”

“Hana too.”

“Yes.”

Huo picked up the teapot from the stone post.

Cold.

He looked devastated.

“They ruined it.”

Tae stared.

“We were attacked by two Paragons.”

“Yes.”

“You’re upset about tea.”

“It had timing.”

Lu walked into the courtyard holding three ledgers.

“Damage report.”

Huo looked hopeful.

“Teapot?”

“Not listed.”

“Why?”

“Because the clinic wall matters more.”

“Debatable.”

Qin rubbed one temple.

The compound began breathing again.

Then Lu opened the first ledger.

“Before anyone celebrates: this was a probe.”

Tae’s humor vanished.

“Yes.”

“They now know.”

Qin nodded.

“So do we.”

“Know what?” Huo asked.

Qin turned toward the continental map inside.

“That Shinsei is willing to attack Wuyue directly to change the Isgard war.”

He stepped toward the door.

“Open every regional defence map.”

Lu was already moving.

Tae followed.

Huo set the cold teapot down with visible sorrow.

Season 104’s first battle had ended.

The home-front war had just begun.
