## Chapter 321 — Too Much Medicine for a Black Market

Yun had been in Shinrin thirty-four days when the numbers stopped pretending to be criminal.

Black markets were messy.

That was one of the few reliable things about them.

They overordered what sold quickly, underordered what attracted patrol attention and lied badly enough that patterns emerged from the lies.

This did not look like that.

Yun sat in the attic room of a grain broker who believed she was comparing imported fever remedies. Spread around her were six copied manifests, two clinic requisition sheets and a warehouse rotation schedule she had paid far too much to borrow for one night.

None mentioned Axtaya.

That was the point.

The precursor compounds did.

Stimulant root extract.

Circulation stabilizer.

High-grade liver support.

Meridian cooling resin.

Recovery salts.

One shipment meant nothing.

Five meant commerce.

Thirty-seven, moving through three provinces under state-protected transport, meant planning.

Yun ran the figures again.

Then stopped halfway through because repeating the arithmetic would not make it smaller.

Too much.

Far too much.

The second Axtaya victim in Wuyue had bought one dose from a discreet seller who claimed he was moving rare foreign enhancement medicine.

Yun had initially imagined dozens of doses.

Then hundreds.

The records in front of her implied production capacity large enough that “black market” was becoming a ridiculous phrase.

She heard a footstep below.

One.

Then a pause.

The grain broker coming upstairs usually dragged his left heel.

This person did not.

Yun gathered the papers without haste.

Panic made noise.

She slid two sheets into the false bottom of her medical case and left one intentionally visible beneath a fever-treatment ledger.

The attic door opened.

A woman in a brown Shinsei inspection coat stepped in.

Yun looked up with mild irritation.

“Can I help you?”

The inspector’s eyes moved over the room.

“Foreign medical trader?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Yes.”

“Permit.”

Yun handed it over.

The woman checked the seal.

“What are you doing in a grain office?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Trying to understand why fever bark costs twelve percent more here than at the southern port.”

“That requires military transport schedules?”

The visible paper had one protected-route stamp on it.

Yun looked down as though noticing for the first time.

[[speaker:yun]]“Apparently grain brokers file everything together.”

The inspector did not smile.

Good.

People who smiled too much were harder.

The woman crossed the room.

Yun let her.

There were no active toxins within six feet.

She had learned from the peach-buying man that environments could be examined too.

The inspector lifted the fever ledger.

Found the transport sheet.

Read it.

“This is restricted.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Then your broker has poor filing discipline.”

“Where is he?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Downstairs, unless you’ve arrested him on the way up.”

The woman watched Yun for three breaths.

Then returned the permit.

“Stay out of protected logistics.”

Yun gave her the exact amount of offense a legitimate merchant would feel.

“I was comparing prices.”

“Compare less deeply.”

The inspector left.

Yun waited.

One hundred breaths.

Then another hundred.

Only after she heard the broker’s familiar dragging step did she move.

He poked his head into the attic.

“What did you do?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Read.”

“That is what I feared.”

Yun gave him the agreed coin and packed the remaining ledger copies.

[[speaker:yun]]“Did she ask about me?”

“Everyone asks about you now.”

Yun’s hands stopped.

The broker swallowed.

“I mean foreign merchants. Archives. Clinics. Nothing specific.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Specific enough.”

He looked miserable.

Yun softened by one degree.

[[speaker:yun]]“You did not see me again after tonight.”

“I would prefer not to.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Wise.”

She left through the rear stairs.

By dawn she had changed lodging, merchant coat and hairstyle.

She also changed the investigation.

Axtaya was no longer contraband she happened to be tracing inside Shinrin.

Its components moved through protected logistics.

Its scale exceeded any plausible illicit medical market.

Somewhere inside Shinsei’s institutional machinery, people were ordering enough material to dose formations.

Yun opened the coded notebook.

Luo’s private notation made the numbers look like a series of dosage corrections.

She wrote:

**Volume incompatible with smuggling model. State logistics implicated. Assume organized distribution.**

Then, after a moment:

**Need military denominator. How many bodies is this meant for?**

Outside, the city woke.

Carts rolled toward markets.

School bells rang.

A Shinsei patrol helped an old man lift a broken axle.

Yun watched from behind the shutter.

The same state that made the road safer was moving enough life-burning medicine to fill warehouses.

The contradiction was no longer philosophical.

It had inventory numbers.

## Chapter 322 — Peak Marquis Is Ordinary Here

The answer to Yun’s denominator question arrived wearing grey training clothes and complaining about breakfast.

She was sitting in the corner of a public noodle shop near a military district when twelve young cultivators entered together.

They looked like recruits.

Not children.

Early twenties, mostly. Fresh uniforms. One had a bandaged thumb. Another was arguing about whether fermented bean paste counted as breakfast food.

Ordinary.

Their cultivation was not.

Yun kept eating.

Peak Marquis.

All twelve.

Not hidden particularly well.

Not flaunted either.

They carried the quiet density of people trained to suppress qi around civilians because somebody had made that normal procedure.

The owner shouted at them for blocking the doorway.

They apologized and moved.

Yun’s chopsticks paused halfway to her mouth.

In Wuyue, twelve Peak Marquis in one room meant a serious sect delegation, a noble guard detachment or one of the more frightening Quaint company meetings.

Here they were arguing over noodles.

A corporal-looking woman slapped one of the men in the shoulder.

“Stop spending your pay before inspection.”

“I’m eating.”

“You ordered three bowls.”

“I’m cultivating.”

“So is everyone.”

The shop laughed.

Yun lowered her eyes.

That sentence stayed.

So is everyone.

She finished the noodles, paid and followed none of them.

Following recruits would be obvious.

Instead she spent two days following paper.

Training permits.

Barracks provisioning.

Uniform contracts.

Clinic capacity.

Nothing was fully public, but enough existed around the edges for an expert to reconstruct scale.

One district training registry listed intake by hundred.

Another showed rotation between active service and civil-defense reserve.

A clinic invoice contained treatment bands calibrated not for rare elite injuries but for continuous high-level martial training.

Yun copied no names.

Names were noise.

She copied counts.

By the third evening the conclusion had become uncomfortable.

Peak Marquis was not literally every soldier in Shinsei.

But within the trained elite apparatus, it was common enough to function as a floor rather than an apex.

Yun sat on the edge of a canal wall, watching barges pass beneath red lanterns.

Her original threat model died quietly there.

She had thought Shinrin might possess an advanced medical program attached to a competent state military.

Now she saw the inverse possibility.

A massive martial state possessed an advanced medical program because it had enough elite bodies to justify one.

The difference was enormous.

A voice beside her said, “You look like someone whose math offended her.”

Yun did not move.

An old fisherman had sat three paces away without asking permission.

He held a bamboo pole and smelled strongly of river water.

Yun said, “My math is usually polite.”

“Then perhaps the country is rude.”

She looked at him.

He grinned.

“Foreign?”

[[speaker:yun]]“Apparently obvious.”

“You stare at soldiers.”

[[speaker:yun]]“That seems unsafe.”

“Locals stopped staring years ago.”

Yun watched a patrol cross the bridge.

“Were there always this many?”

The fisherman snorted.

“Before Shinsei? Every clan had soldiers. More flags. Less discipline.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I meant cultivators.”

“Ah.”

He considered.

“No. Training got better when the guild stopped letting every house keep techniques locked in family vaults.”

Yun’s attention sharpened.

[[speaker:yun]]“Shared training?”

“Some. Standards. Medicine. Common basics. Big clans still hoard their pretty secrets.”

He cast the line.

“But a farmer’s son can join now and learn enough not to die to the first arrogant young master who dislikes his face.”

The line landed in the canal.

Yun looked at his hands.

Callused.

No martial density worth mentioning.

He had no reason to flatter Shinsei’s military program except that he believed what it had changed.

Again, the state refused to become simple for her convenience.

Yun stood.

“Catch anything?”

“Not yet.”

[[speaker:yun]]“How long have you been here?”

“Twenty years.”

Yun stared.

The old man laughed until he coughed.

She left before deciding whether to poison him.

Back in her room, she wrote:

**Elite military baseline substantially above Wuyue assumption. Peak Marquis common within trained apparatus.**

Then she began multiplying.

Food.

Clinics.

Training rotations.

Axtaya precursors.

The numbers did not yet give her an army size.

But they gave her something almost as bad.

Whatever Shinsei was preparing medicine for, it was not a handful of officers.

## Chapter 323 — The Aura That Would Not Stay Small

Rhen stopped sleeping because sleep made the room colder.

This annoyed him.

He liked sleeping.

Sera discovered the problem at three in the morning when she rolled over and found the blanket rim stiff with frost.

She opened one eye.

Rhen was lying on his back beside her, awake.

“Are you doing that?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“No.”

Sera lifted the frozen edge of the blanket.

“You understand why that answer is worse.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

She sat up.

The room was not cold overall.

That was the strange part.

The window remained warm from late-summer air. The floorboards were normal. Only a thin crescent around Rhen’s side of the bed had accumulated frost, as though his qi had become too dense to stay completely inside while his conscious suppression relaxed.

Sera touched his shoulder.

“No pain?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“No.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Pressure?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“More.”

[[speaker:sera]]“How much more?”

Rhen stared at the ceiling.

“Enough that I’m no longer sure dispersal is useful.”

Sera’s expression sharpened.

“You promised.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I’m telling you.”

[[speaker:sera]]“After I woke up frozen.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“The blanket is frozen.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You’re lucky you’re pretty.”

Rhen looked over.

“Am I?”

[[speaker:sera]]“Don’t ruin it.”

That got the small smile she wanted.

Then she put her hand over his sternum.

[[speaker:sera]]“Show me properly.”

Rhen hesitated.

Sera’s eyes narrowed.

He gave up.

The suppression loosened by an amount so small most cultivators would not have noticed.

The room changed.

Not with pressure.

Pressure would have been easier.

Sera felt depth.

An impossible sense that Rhen’s body contained a distance larger than the room, packed inward so tightly that her instincts could not find the bottom of it.

Then he closed it again.

Sera’s hand stayed on his chest.

“That is new.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Bad?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Not yet.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Rhen.”

He sighed.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Potentially.”

She lay back down, but not away from him.

Instead she pulled one leg over his and rested her chin on his shoulder.

[[speaker:sera]]“Explain like I’m not you.”

He considered.

[[speaker:rhen]]“The Garden keeps restoring what I spend.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I know.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“It also keeps refining it.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I know that too.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“The refinement increased recovery efficiency.”

Sera waited.

“So?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“So the reserve returns denser than before.”

[[speaker:sera]]“And?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“And I was already not short on reserve.”

[[speaker:sera]]“That is the understatement of your life.”

Rhen ignored that.

“Think of pouring water into a sealed jar.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Finally, a normal metaphor.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Then replacing the water with mercury.”

[[speaker:sera]]“There it is.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“The jar does not get larger.”

Sera’s amusement faded.

“You’re the jar.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“And the answer is what you said before. Make the qi take less space.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Condense it.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Eventually.”

She pressed her face into his shoulder for one irritated second.

[[speaker:sera]]“I dislike eventually.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“So do I.”

[[speaker:sera]]“When?”

Rhen looked toward the dark window.

“Not while Yun is overdue.”

Sera went still.

He felt it.

They had not spoken about Yun before bed because there had been nothing new to say.

Lu’s route work continued.

Xie had vanished toward another port.

Luo had become frighteningly quiet.

No message.

Sera said, “This is unrelated.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You’re not delaying because you think I’ll need you.”

Rhen turned his head.

She was watching him closely.

[[speaker:rhen]]“That would be a bad reason.”

[[speaker:sera]]“It would also be a very you reason.”

He thought about denying it.

[[speaker:rhen]]“Partly.”

Sera pinched his side.

Rhen did not flinch.

“That is cheating,” she said.

[[speaker:rhen]]“I’m durable.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You’re impossible.”

She let go.

Then, quieter:

[[speaker:sera]]“If you need to close the door, you close it.”

Rhen said nothing.

Sera continued.

“Yun went because she trusted the rest of us to exist while she was gone. You don’t get to make the opposite decision for yourself because you’re stronger.”

That landed harder than the pinch.

Rhen looked at her.

Sera raised one eyebrow.

“Leaving before anyone can depend on you is still a choice,” he said.

She blinked.

Then stared.

[[speaker:sera]]“You stole my line.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“It was good.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You heard that?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Xie complained about it downstairs.”

Sera covered her face with one hand.

“Of course he did.”

Rhen’s smile returned.

The room warmed slightly.

The problem remained; he was simply awake enough to control it again.

Sera settled back against him.

“Promise me something else.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“What?”

[[speaker:sera]]“When you know it’s time, don’t wait for a crisis to make the decision.”

Rhen looked at the frost dissolving along the blanket edge.

“Okay.”

Sera lifted her head.

“Okay as in yes?”

[[speaker:rhen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Good.”

She kissed him once.

Then pulled the blanket up.

It cracked.

Both looked down.

Sera sighed.

“You owe me bedding now too.”

Rhen closed his eyes.

“Add it to the teapot.”

[[speaker:sera]]“I have a list.”

[[speaker:rhen]]“I know.”

The frost did not return before morning.

Rhen still did not sleep.
