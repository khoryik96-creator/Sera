## Chapter 328 — The Arm He Could Not Feel

Shunto Takamori lost his left arm for eleven minutes.

Not physically.

That would have been simpler.

The limb remained attached, warm, alive and completely useless from shoulder to fingertips.

Yun knew the exact duration because she watched the clock tower.

She was hiding in the bellkeeper’s maintenance crawlspace while rain struck the roof above her and Shinsei search teams moved through the lower streets.

Nine minutes.

Ten.

Eleven.

At the far end of the square, Shunto stood beneath a covered walkway with his left arm hanging motionless at his side.

He had hidden the weakness from his soldiers at first.

Then hiding it became impossible.

One officer approached with a physician.

Shunto sent both away.

Yun approved reluctantly.

He knew the toxin was designed around intervention.

A conventional healer might purge through the wrong route and spread the numbness into the chest.

He waited instead.

Used the antidote slowly.

Reduced qi pressure.

Allowed the compromised channel to reopen by degrees.

Competent.

Extremely annoying.

At minute eleven, his little finger moved.

Yun exhaled.

She had wanted twenty.

She would take eleven.

The rain thickened.

Below, Shunto looked toward the clock tower.

Not at Yun.

Not exactly.

Still, she eased farther back into shadow.

The chase had changed.

Before, Shunto had treated her like a difficult arrest.

Now he knew she could meaningfully injure him.

That would make him more careful.

It would also make him more dangerous.

Yun opened the coded notebook on her knees.

The paper had begun to curl from humidity.

She wrote:

**Takamori circulation adaptation confirmed. Segmentation protects against broad spread but creates high-value dominant reroute under action demand. Exploited left shoulder/arm. Temporary functional loss ~11 min with prepared antidote.**

She paused.

Then added:

**Do not mistake success for parity. He remains far stronger.**

That sentence was for Luo.

It was also for herself.

A bell rope shifted below.

Yun froze.

The bellkeeper had returned early.

She closed the notebook.

The crawlspace had one exit.

Bad.

She looked through a gap in the boards.

The old bellkeeper entered carrying an umbrella and muttering about soldiers ruining market traffic.

He hung the umbrella.

Then looked directly up.

Yun held still.

The man squinted.

“Mouse?”

Yun did not answer.

“Big mouse.”

He dragged a stool beneath the crawlspace hatch.

Yun considered three options, all stupid.

The hatch opened.

The old man stared at her.

Yun stared back.

He looked at her torn sleeve.

Then at the blood drying near her collar.

Then through the tower window toward the Shinsei patrols.

“Are you the one they’re looking for?”

Yun said, “Possibly.”

“That is not reassuring.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I’m not here to hurt you.”

“Also what people say before hurting you.”

Fair.

Yun reached slowly for her coin pouch.

The bellkeeper raised one hand.

“No.”

She stopped.

He pointed toward the back stair.

“Roof drainage connects to the old shrine wall.”

Yun blinked.

“Why are you helping me?”

The old man looked offended.

“I am helping my bell tower.”

He glanced at the soldiers below.

“If they search properly, they’ll tear everything apart.”

Yun almost smiled.

“Of course.”

“Also,” he added, “Second Seat does not usually bring this many people for harmless merchants.”

Yun’s expression flattened.

The old man saw enough.

He looked away.

“Whatever you did, leave before they decide my roof is evidence.”

Yun moved.

At the back stair she paused.

[[speaker:yun]]“Thank you.”

“Do not come back.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Reasonable.”

She slipped into the rain.

Across the square, Shunto flexed his left hand again.

This time all five fingers responded.

His officer returned.

Shunto said something Yun could not hear.

The search pattern changed immediately.

Not wider.

Smarter.

They stopped checking every building.

Started checking exits.

Transit points.

Places a wounded foreigner would need rather than places a fugitive might hide.

Yun saw the shift and felt the narrowest thread of admiration.

Then irritation at herself for feeling it.

She disappeared down the shrine wall before the patrol reached the tower.

Eleven minutes.

That was what Pale Venom had bought against Shinsei No.2.

Eleven minutes and a lesson he would never let her teach twice.

## Chapter 329 — He Learns From Pain

Shunto stopped pursuing Yun for four days.

That frightened her more than pursuit.

The first day, she changed safehouses twice anyway.

The second, she abandoned a medical contact after noticing two unfamiliar food vendors across the street.

The third, nothing happened.

No patrol tightened.

No inspector appeared.

No smiling man bought fruit within sight.

By the fourth day, Yun understood.

He was studying.

So she did the same.

The best information came from inconvenience.

Shunto’s left arm injury had disrupted one district security deployment badly enough that several junior officers had been reassigned on short notice. The movement created paperwork.

Paperwork created witnesses.

Yun bribed one clerk with imported pain salve and learned that Second Seat had ordered revised capture protocols for “toxin-capable foreign infiltrators.”

Plural.

That was considerate.

The changes were not.

No shared canteens on pursuit teams.

No uncovered skin.

No healer intervention without toxin-specific clearance.

Two-person confirmation before touching seized objects.

Route officers separated from capture officers so poisoned pursuit teams could not compromise the exits.

And most importantly:

No unnecessary close engagement.

Shunto had taken Yun’s greatest weapon against him—the fact that his style wanted contact—and built procedure around denying her the opportunity to make that contact costly.

Yun read the copied directives in a rented room and felt a familiar anger.

His adaptation was not what angered her. His competence was.

Stupidity was easy to survive.

Competence made every success temporary.

She burned the copied directive over a wash bowl.

That evening, Shunto found her anyway.

Not in a trap.

In a tea house.

Yun sat alone near the back wall while rain ticked against paper windows.

Shunto entered through the front door, left both hooked blades peace-bound at his belt and ordered tea.

Nobody screamed.

Nobody even moved tables.

He was Shinsei No.2.

Here, that meant authority before it meant threat.

Yun hated the tactical disadvantage of civilization.

Shunto took a seat three tables away.

Did not look at her.

The server poured his tea.

Only after the woman left did he say, “The arm was clever.”

Yun kept her eyes on her own cup.

“You recovered.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“I did.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Tragic.”

A faint smile.

[[speaker:shunto]]“I changed six pursuit procedures because of you.”

[[speaker:yun]]“I should charge consulting fees.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You already cost the Guild enough.”

There.

Guild.

Not security office.

Not district command.

The hunt was institutional now.

Yun said, “If I’m so expensive, stop looking.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You are a foreign Sovereign moving through restricted medical and military infrastructure.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Still no cities on fire.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You keep returning to that defense.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It keeps being true.”

Shunto lifted his cup.

His left hand.

Deliberately.

He wanted her to see the recovered fingers.

Yun wanted him to know she did not care.

They both lied politely.

“What are you trying to find?” he asked.

[[speaker:yun]]“Good tea.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You came to the wrong district.”

Yun finally looked at him.

That was almost funny.

Shunto’s expression held no visible anger from the poisoning.

But his posture had changed.

Before, he had sat like someone who expected to control the room whenever he chose.

Now every part of him was positioned away from unknown surfaces.

Sleeves tucked.

Cup inspected.

Chair placed where nobody could pass behind him.

Pain had taught him.

Yun said, “You could have surrounded this place.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“I did.”

She listened.

No obvious boots outside.

Which meant he had learned that too.

[[speaker:yun]]“Then why are we talking?”

[[speaker:shunto]]“Because you keep behaving as though escape is more important than killing me.”

[[speaker:yun]]“It is.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“That suggests you have an objective beyond me.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Everyone has an objective beyond you.”

He smiled.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Not everyone.”

The answer was too revealing in a different direction.

Yun filed it away.

Shunto continued.

“You are not here to assassinate anyone. If you were, you would have tried already.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Comforting.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You are not here to sabotage infrastructure. Your routes avoid civilian systems whenever possible.”

[[speaker:yun]]“Also comforting.”

[[speaker:shunto]]“You are reading.”

Yun’s eyes cooled.

Shunto saw it.

[[speaker:shunto]]“Archives. Warehouses. movement. Medicine.”

He leaned back.

[[speaker:shunto]]“So I ask again. What are you trying to find?”

Yun finished her tea.

Then set the cup down.

[[speaker:yun]]“Why your state needs so much medicine.”

For the first time, Shunto’s expression truly changed.

Small.

But real.

Yun stood.

[[speaker:yun]]“You should fix your face.”

The door behind her opened.

Two plain-clothed officers entered.

Yun had expected that.

She flipped the table.

Not at them.

At the lamp.

Oil hit the floor.

The flame did not spread.

Because Yun had replaced the lamp oil twenty minutes earlier with a smoking compound that filled the room with dense, eye-stinging vapor instead of fire.

Civilians shouted.

Shunto did not chase blindly.

Exactly as his new procedure demanded.

He ordered exits controlled.

Yun did not use an exit.

She went through the kitchen wall.

Again.

Brick was becoming expensive around her.

Outside, she crossed a fish yard, entered a drainage culvert and changed direction twice.

No soldiers followed closely.

They were waiting ahead.

Learning.

Yun changed again.

By dawn she was outside the district entirely.

Shunto had not caught her.

But he had learned enough from one poisoned arm to make the next escape cost three safehouses and two routes.

Yun sat beneath an abandoned shrine and looked north.

She was winning exchanges.

He was winning space.

That difference was beginning to matter.

## Chapter 330 — Maps Point North

The first Isgard map Yun found was folded inside a shipping insurance file.

That was almost insulting.

She had spent weeks breaking routes, changing names and reading military-adjacent documents in rooms where being discovered could end the investigation.

Then an insurance clerk left a fleet-risk packet open on his desk.

Yun saw the northern coastline through the paper before she saw the title.

**SEASONAL LOSS PROJECTIONS — NORTHERN ROUTES.**

She walked past.

Did not stop.

Came back two hours later through a different office.

The map was gone.

Good.

That meant it mattered.

Over the next six days, she found five more pieces.

None alone proved anything.

A requisition for cold-weather wound dressings.

A bulk order of shipboard anti-nausea medicine sized far beyond merchant needs.

A naval repair schedule prioritizing deep-keel transports.

An artillery-equivalent shore-engine packing list marked for “external readiness exercise.”

And three Isgard harbor sketches embedded in a logistics workbook under generic geographic codes.

Yun sat in a rented storage room and laid her copied fragments in a half-circle.

North.

Everything pointed north.

Not Wuyue.

Isgard.

That made strategic sense.

Wuyue was still rebuilding from the last war but remained the dominant martial continent.

Isgard had been battered by that same war, then devastated further by epidemic.

Ports weakened.

Command networks thinner.

Population strained.

A military power looking outward would see opportunity there first.

Yun hated how reasonable it was.

She added the medical component.

Axtaya precursor stock.

Cold-weather deployment medicine.

High-volume trauma supplies.

Then manpower indicators.

Peak Marquis training density.

Protected logistics.

The picture became difficult to call anything except preparation.

Still not enough.

Preparation for what scale?

When?

Which force?

Yun rubbed her eyes.

She had been inside Shinrin more than six weeks now.

Double the trip she promised.

Luo would know.

The thought arrived without invitation.

She let it stay for one breath.

Then returned to the documents.

Going home with “I think Shinsei may someday attack Isgard” would trigger fear without useful timing.

Going home with nothing because she stayed too long would be worse.

She needed one more layer.

One.

The lie people always told themselves before dangerous work.

Yun knew it was a lie.

She also knew the evidence in front of her had changed the moral arithmetic.

If Shinsei was preparing a continental invasion, the problem was no longer whether her personal mission had exceeded three weeks.

It was whether Wuyue and Isgard received enough warning to matter.

A small scraping sound came from outside the storage room.

Yun extinguished the lamp.

Darkness.

Footsteps passed.

Stopped.

Continued.

She waited.

Then unfolded the largest copied map again.

One shoreline marker repeated beside three ports.

A black triangle.

Yun had seen the symbol before.

Not on maps.

On military clinic staging forms.

Mobilization medical point.

Her pulse slowed.

Three ports.

Three staging points.

All matched likely landing support for a northern campaign.

Yun opened Luo’s coded notebook.

She wrote:

**Evidence now indicates Isgard as probable external military objective. Not enough yet for timetable. Need deployment scale + launch window.**

Then she stopped.

A second thought occurred.

If she could reconstruct the launch window, she would have enough to leave.

Not solve Shinsei.

Not expose every secret.

Enough.

Yun closed the notebook.

Outside, somewhere beyond the warehouse roofs, a military bell marked shift change.

Far to the north, Isgard had no idea a woman it barely knew was sitting in a dark room tracing ships toward its coast.

Yun stood.

“One more layer,” she whispered.

Then hated herself slightly for saying it aloud.
