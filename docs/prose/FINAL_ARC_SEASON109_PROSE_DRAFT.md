## Chapter 441 — How Long?

“Six months.”

Lu Weiran stopped there.

The silence inside the stone chamber did not change, but something in his face did. He had spent half a year learning how to carry bad numbers without letting them show until the work was finished. This one still resisted him.

“Six months,” he repeated, quieter. “And twelve days.”

Rhen sat where Sera had left him.

The same low platform. The same folded traveling coat at his side. The same narrow lamp niche cut into the wall.

Everything else looked older.

The lamp had been replaced so many times that wax had built a pale ridge beneath it. A stack of medical ledgers sat against one wall where there had been none before. The doorframe carried three fresh reinforcement plates. Someone had repaired a crack near the ceiling and done a poor job disguising it.

Rhen looked down at his hands.

They looked exactly the same.

That was almost funny.

For months his body had felt too full, as though the qi inside him had forgotten that flesh required space. The pressure had grown until breathing, sleeping and even suppressing his presence demanded care. He had entered the chamber because the Garden was refilling him faster than his body could comfortably hold what it restored.

Now there was no pressure at all.

His meridians felt empty.

Not drained.

Empty in the way a winter sky looked empty when the stars were too distant to count.

Rhen turned one wrist.

A single frost-white petal formed above his palm.

Lu stared.

The petal was not the blue-white he remembered. Its edges were almost colorless, but deep in the frozen surface ran a faint violet radiance, so pale it seemed less like light than something trapped beneath ice.

Rhen watched it for a moment.

Then the petal vanished.

Lu did not ask what it meant.

That was one of the reasons he was Lu.

Rhen lifted his eyes.

“Where is Sera?”

“Isgard.”

The answer came too quickly.

Rhen's expression did not change.

“How long?”

“Most of the six months.”

“What happened?”

Lu breathed in through his nose.

There were men in the world who would have begun with armies. There were generals who would have reached for maps, rulers who would have asked which territories were lost, and martial artists who would have demanded the names of the strongest enemies first.

Rhen asked where Sera was.

Lu had expected that too.

“She is alive,” he said.

Rhen's gaze sharpened by a degree so small that another person might have missed it.

“That was not what I asked.”

“I know.”

Lu crouched beside the ledgers and took the top one.

He did not open it.

“Before I tell you the war, you need to know why it began.”

Rhen waited.

Outside the chamber, someone shifted weight and then became perfectly still again. The guard detail had heard his voice. Lu could feel that much without needing a martial sense.

Six months of waiting had ended, and suddenly nobody knew how loudly they were allowed to breathe.

Lu looked at Rhen.

“Yun Shizhen made it back from Shinrin.”

For the first time, Rhen moved before Lu finished.

Not far.

His fingers simply settled on his knee.

But Lu saw it.

Rhen remembered the physician with the dry mouth and the poisoner's eyes. Yun had argued with him about dosage twice, insulted his herb storage once, and then spent an afternoon correcting three apprentices because none of them understood that an antidote could become a second poison if circulation was wrong.

She had gone north chasing Axtaya.

Rhen had entered seclusion believing she was still away.

Lu continued.

“She reached Isgard with evidence. Shinrin's army strength. Their Paragons. Their shipping lanes. Their stimulant program. Enough to warn both continents.”

Rhen's eyes stayed on him.

“And?”

Lu's grip tightened around the ledger.

“She died there.”

The chamber became colder.

Not violently.

There was no burst of qi. No killing intent. No crack through the stone.

Frost simply appeared along the rim of the old lamp niche.

Lu looked at it.

Rhen looked too.

The frost stopped spreading.

A second later it withdrew.

“Sorry,” Rhen said.

Lu almost laughed.

Not because anything was funny.

Because that was the first thing Rhen chose to say after learning a friend had died while he slept.

Rhen looked toward the sealed door.

“When?”

“The day after you entered.”

That one landed harder.

Lu saw it because Rhen's face became completely still.

“One day.”

“Yes.”

If Sera had brought the news twenty-four hours earlier, Rhen would have been standing in Isgard before Yun's body cooled.

If Yun had arrived twenty-four hours later, nothing would have changed.

The timing had chosen its own cruelty.

Rhen did not curse it.

He sat with it.

Then he asked, “What is Axtaya?”

Lu finally opened the ledger.

“Something Shinrin should never have turned into a weapon.”

He gave Rhen the simplest version first.

A physician's emergency stimulant had been pushed past medicine. One and a half times normal combat throughput. Twice. Then three times. A sealed Redline dose could force roughly triple output for about five minutes.

At the cost of future life.

Rhen listened without interrupting.

“About five years for a standard three-times dose,” Lu said. “Healthy-adult average. More if redosed. Much more if repeated inside a day.”

Rhen's gaze dropped to the ledger.

“Can Aya heal the damage?”

“The physical damage, yes. She's a Paragon physician.”

“The lifespan?”

“No.”

Rhen was quiet for several breaths.

“Neither can I.”

Lu looked at him.

Rhen's tone was matter-of-fact.

“Not with ordinary healing.”

He did not elaborate.

Lu did not ask.

That was another reason he was Lu.

Rhen rose.

The movement was unremarkable.

That was what made Lu's skin tighten.

Before seclusion, even when Rhen suppressed himself, there had always been some sense that the room contained more than the room should have been able to hold. A pressure hidden so perfectly that only people who knew him well noticed the absence of normality.

Now there was nothing.

No weight.

No pressure.

No sense of depth.

Rhen stood three steps away, and Lu's instincts gave him no useful answer at all.

“Your coat,” Lu said.

Rhen glanced down at the folded dark green traveling coat.

It had been washed.

Repaired twice.

Sera had stitched one of the inner tears herself because she had said the apprentices made the seam too neat and he would complain.

Rhen put it on.

His fingers paused against the repaired lining.

“Did she do this?”

Lu considered lying.

It would have been kinder.

“She did it the night before you went in.”

Rhen's thumb rested against the stitch once.

Then he let the coat fall closed.

“Tell me everything.”

Lu looked toward the chamber door.

“Everything will take a while.”

Rhen stepped past him.

“I have six months to catch up on.”

The stone seal opened.

Outside, two Quiet Hand guards who had been standing watch for a season straightened so fast one nearly struck his head on the wall.

Both were Peak Marquis.

Both had survived the home-front attack.

Both had been told all their lives that Rhen was the harmless physician behind the tea counter.

Neither managed to speak.

Rhen looked at them.

“You should sit down.”

They sat.

Lu closed his eyes for half a second.

Some things, apparently, had not changed at all.

Rhen started down the mountain corridor.

Lu followed with the ledger under one arm.

Behind them, a frost-white petal drifted out of the open chamber.

Pale violet light moved once beneath its surface.

Then it dissolved before touching the floor.

## Chapter 442 — Six Months of Names

Lu did not begin with battles.

He began with names.

They walked through the mountain base while the first rumor moved faster than either of them.

Rhen is awake.

It passed from the chamber guards to a runner, from the runner to the lower infirmary, from the infirmary to the kitchens, from the kitchens to somewhere it absolutely did not belong. By the time Rhen and Lu reached the main passage, three apprentices were pretending not to wait around a corner.

Rhen glanced at them.

All three vanished.

“Your security has improved,” he said.

Lu kept walking.

“They were carrying soup.”

“They dropped it.”

“They were surprised.”

“It was good soup.”

Lu looked at him.

Rhen looked completely serious.

For half a corridor, the war felt very far away.

Then they reached the map room.

It had changed.

The old mountain table had once held local roads, herb routes and the kind of Quiet Hand logistics Sera insisted were boring until somebody needed medicine at three in the morning.

Now there were three continents' worth of markers.

Wuyue in black and silver.

Isgard in iron-grey.

Shinrin in red.

Lines crossed the northern sea like wounds.

Lu shut the door.

“Yun died on Day Zero,” he said.

Rhen stood at the table.

“Day Zero?”

“Jin's naming. Easier for planning.”

Rhen's eyes moved over the board.

“Of course it is.”

Lu pointed to Isgard's coast.

“Her warning reached Wuyue immediately. The defensive pact activated. You had entered seclusion the previous day, and Sera reached your chamber before the first full war council.”

Rhen's gaze stayed on the coast.

“She didn't wake me.”

“No.”

“Good.”

Lu looked at him.

Rhen finally looked back.

“I told her what would happen if she did.”

The answer was calm.

It still carried weight.

After condensation began, interruption could have torn the rebuilding meridians apart faster than even Rhen could repair them from inside the process. Sera had been given a choice between a war without him and gambling that opening the door would leave him alive enough to matter.

She chose not to gamble his life.

Rhen understood exactly what that choice had cost her.

He did not insult it by wishing she had chosen differently.

Lu moved to the next marker.

“Shinsei attacked in roughly two weeks. They originally had months. Yun's escape forced Tsubasa Kurokawa to accelerate.”

“Tsubasa.”

“Shinsei Number One. Peak Paragon.”

Rhen's eyes moved to the name.

“Strong?”

“Yes.”

“How strong?”

“Enough that nobody who has met him speaks casually about it.”

Rhen nodded once.

That was apparently sufficient.

Lu continued.

“The first landing force was around twenty-five thousand. Five official Paragons.”

He tapped five markers.

“Eirik Voss. Aya Katsuragi. Kenji Narukami. Jun Kajihara. Nao Shibasaki.”

Rhen's gaze stopped on the first.

“Voss.”

“Former ruler of Isgard.”

That made Rhen look up.

Lu explained.

The dead king who had not been dead. The shock when Aldric recognized his shieldwork. Eirik returning not as a lost ruler, but as a High Paragon commanding Shinsei troops against his former homeland.

Then the casualties.

Lu did not soften those either.

“Halvek Veyrhald died holding an evacuation road.”

A marker came off the board.

“Raska Dravaryn died covering a retreat.”

Another.

“Tor Veyrhald survived, but his meridians are destroyed badly enough that his frontline career is over.”

Another marker moved back from the line.

“Eldran Dravaryn survived. Crippled. He took a killing sequence meant for Maedra.”

Rhen's hand rested lightly on the table edge.

“Aldric?”

“Critically injured.”

“Maedra?”

“Same.”

“Sigrun?”

“Nearly died holding an evacuation corridor after becoming Paragon.”

Rhen looked at Lu.

“She reached Paragon?”

“Recently. Graven Dominion.”

A faint smile touched Rhen's mouth.

“Good for her.”

Lu almost told him that Sigrun had then used that new Dominion to keep moving while her body accumulated damage it should not have survived.

He decided that could wait five seconds.

Then he told him anyway.

The smile disappeared.

“Who ran Isgard?” Rhen asked.

“Someone had to.”

Lu moved a grey marker into the center.

“Solveig Skeldran. High Sovereign. Acting War Commander. Varok took mobile field command. Brynja held the coast. Astrid kept hidden roads and recon alive.”

Rhen nodded.

He knew Solveig only distantly.

That changed now.

“Remember her name,” he said.

Lu's eyes flicked up.

“I already did.”

Rhen glanced at him.

“Right.”

There were days when Lu suspected his entire purpose in life was making sure powerful people remembered that logistics existed before they urgently needed it.

He moved the Wuyue markers north.

“Wuyue arrived after roughly six weeks.”

“All ten?”

“All ten public Top Ten.”

“Sera?”

“Yes.”

“How many?”

“About sixty-four thousand public troops, plus Sera's one hundred twenty Quaint Hands.”

Rhen's eyes narrowed slightly.

“Only one hundred twenty?”

“She left the rest home.”

“Smart.”

“She also left Tae, Huo and Qin.”

Rhen looked toward the door.

“Smarter.”

Lu waited.

Then added, “Shinsei attacked Wuyue anyway.”

Rhen slowly looked back.

Lu told him about Haru Ishikawa and Hana Arakawa entering the Quaint home front believing Sera, the Top Ten and Rhen were gone.

He told him how they found Tae instead.

Then Huo.

Then Qin.

Rhen listened with increasing stillness.

“Two Paragons?” he asked.

“Yes.”

“And the house?”

“Standing.”

“The clinic?”

“Standing.”

“The tea?”

Lu stared at him.

Rhen waited.

“The tea survived.”

“Good.”

“Haru took three Redlines in a later engagement.”

Rhen's face changed.

Only slightly.

“Three?”

“In one day.”

“Three three-times doses?”

“Yes.”

Rhen did the arithmetic without asking.

Five years.

Then roughly double cost for the second.

Then roughly quadruple for the third.

“Thirty-five years,” he said.

“Approximately.”

“Idiot.”

Lu said nothing.

It was the harshest thing Rhen had said since waking.

Not murderer.

Not enemy.

Idiot.

Because a twenty-eight-year-old prodigy had burned decades of his own future because he could not accept a battlefield slipping away.

Lu moved to the Isgard front again.

“Kael reached Paragon before the war. He beat Jun.”

“Good.”

“Kai Moriyama arrived after that. High Paragon. Kael lost to him.”

Rhen nodded again.

“Also good.”

Lu frowned.

“He was badly injured.”

“I heard you.”

“Then why is that good?”

“Because Kael spent too long being the strongest person everyone around him expected to see.”

Rhen moved Kael's marker with one finger.

“Losing to someone better can be useful if it doesn't kill you.”

Lu considered this.

“You have an irritating way of making injuries sound educational.”

“I am a physician.”

“That is not what physicians do.”

“Bad physicians.”

Lu refused to dignify that.

He moved on.

He told Rhen about Aya healing Jun early because Shinsei needed him back before treatment was complete.

About the first mass Redline wave.

About Jin's Five-Minute Retreat.

About Kai adapting with staggered activation so one boosted wave came alive while another was already burning down.

About Sigrun returning before Aldric and Maedra.

About Wuyue winning battles and still losing people faster than the strategic balance improved.

About Shinsei sending thousands of wounded back to Aya and receiving Paragons again at full strength days later.

That made Rhen pause.

“Aya can restore them completely?”

“With enough time. Recoverable damage, yes.”

Rhen nodded.

“Good doctor.”

“She is also the physician whose work became Axtaya.”

“Still a good doctor.”

Lu looked at him.

Rhen's eyes remained on the map.

“Those can both be true.”

Lu said nothing for a moment.

Then he continued.

He told him Aya had begun fighting her own command over the drug.

That ordinary soldiers had not been told the real lifespan cost.

That she had started writing the years into medical records plainly.

That Hana and Nao had begun resisting from inside in their own ways.

That Shinsei was not one mind.

Rhen listened.

Then Lu reached the numbers he had been avoiding.

“Wuyue left with about sixty-three thousand eight hundred combat-ready public personnel.”

Rhen looked at the black markers.

“And now?”

“About fifty to fifty-one thousand combat-capable, depending on the day.”

“How many dead?”

“Current estimate between five and six thousand Wuyue personnel. More wounded, rotated out, missing, or temporarily unfit.”

Rhen said nothing.

“Isgard?”

“Worse proportionally.”

“Civilians?”

Lu did not answer immediately.

Rhen closed his eyes.

“I see.”

“No,” Lu said. “You don't. Not yet.”

Rhen opened them.

Lu's voice stayed level.

“Neither did I until the lists stopped fitting on one board.”

Rhen looked at him for a long second.

Then nodded.

“Fair.”

Lu had not expected the apology hidden in that word.

He turned to the last cluster.

“Sera is still commanding.”

Rhen's attention sharpened again.

“Injured?”

“Yes.”

“How badly?”

“Enough that Luo has ordered her to stop fighting twice.”

“And?”

“You know Sera.”

Rhen exhaled through his nose.

That was the closest he came to laughing.

“Is she walking?”

“Yes.”

“Eating?”

Lu hesitated.

Rhen looked at him.

“Lu.”

“She eats.”

“That was a suspicious pause.”

“She eats when someone places food directly in front of her and remains there until she finishes.”

Rhen stared.

Lu raised both hands.

“I am in Wuyue.”

“Who is doing that in Isgard?”

“Luo. Sometimes Arin. Once Jin.”

“Jin?”

“He threatened to classify lunch as a mandatory strategic resource.”

Rhen's mouth twitched.

“Effective?”

“Apparently.”

Rhen looked down at the northern map again.

Six months.

Tens of thousands.

Yun dead.

Sera still standing.

Everyone else carrying the space he had left behind.

He touched the edge of Isgard with one finger.

“I should go.”

“Yes.”

“But first—”

A heavy footstep stopped outside the map-room door.

Then another.

Then a third presence that made the wooden pins on the table tremble so faintly most people would never have noticed.

Rhen looked toward the door.

Lu did not need to.

“They heard.”

Rhen's expression softened.

“Good.”

The door opened.

Tae Muyeon stood first.

Huo Wujin filled most of the space behind him.

Qin Luo stood between them with his eyes closed, one hand resting lightly against the wall.

For a moment nobody said anything.

Then Tae looked Rhen up and down.

“You look annoyingly well-rested.”

Rhen nodded.

“You look louder.”

Huo grinned.

Qin sighed.

Lu quietly gathered his ledgers and moved toward the safest side of the room.

## Chapter 443 — The Three Who Stayed Home

Tae Muyeon had once been a man people described by the sandstorm that arrived before him.

Five years later, sand was no longer enough.

Iron lived in his circulation now.

Rhen saw it before Tae crossed the room.

Not literally as metal beneath skin. The qi ran denser than it had when Rhen entered seclusion, compressed into hard, disciplined channels that no longer wasted motion carrying strength from core to limb. Every step settled with the quiet certainty of a chain dropping into place.

High Paragon.

Still.

But better.

Huo Wujin was worse.

Or better, depending on who had to fight him.

The huge man had always possessed frightening reserves, but his qi used to feel like a flood trying to become a person. Now it turned through the body in distinct rotational patterns, nine disciplined wheels handing force from one meridian sequence to another.

High Paragon.

Deepened.

Sharpened.

Qin Luo was the strangest.

Blind from birth, eyes closed as always, he stood quietly enough that an ordinary cultivator might have mistaken him for the least dangerous person in the room.

Rhen felt the wall behind Qin humming.

Not visibly.

Not audibly.

The old stone simply carried back too much information.

Every foot shift. Every breath. Every tiny pressure change in the room reached Qin through vibration and returned through an internal-energy structure so efficient that Rhen had to smile.

Qin tilted his head.

“What?”

“You three got better.”

Tae folded his arms.

“You slept for six months.”

“That doesn't answer me.”

“It wasn't a question.”

Rhen considered this.

“True.”

Huo walked over and stopped directly in front of him.

He stared.

Rhen stared back.

Huo leaned closer.

“Your face is the same.”

“Most faces do that over six months.”

“Not yours. Yours is suspicious.”

Tae rubbed his forehead.

“Huo.”

“What? He disappeared into a rock for half a year and came out looking like he took a nap.”

Rhen glanced at Lu.

“Was I supposed to grow a beard?”

Lu did not look up from his ledger.

“I am not participating.”

Qin's mouth moved at one corner.

Then his expression changed.

He lifted one hand.

“Rhen.”

Rhen looked at him.

Qin's fingers hovered in the air as though feeling rain nobody else could sense.

“Do that again.”

“Do what?”

“The petal.”

Tae's posture changed.

Huo stopped smiling.

Rhen raised his hand.

A single petal formed.

Frost-white.

Pale violet inside.

The room went still.

Qin did not see its color.

He heard something worse.

Or rather, he failed to hear it.

His vibration sense could read the smallest ordinary interaction between qi and matter. An aura pressed the air. A technique changed pressure. A cultivated body had weight in the world beyond muscle and bone.

The petal barely registered.

Qin extended his perception further.

His brow tightened.

“Where is the rest of it?”

Rhen looked at the petal.

“In the petal.”

Qin's head turned toward him.

“All of it?”

“No.”

That answer did not help.

Tae stepped closer.

“How much?”

Rhen thought about it.

During seclusion he had taken the diffuse ocean that once filled every available part of his meridian network and forced it smaller.

Then smaller again.

Then smaller until what had once felt like a full reserve no longer occupied enough internal space to threaten his body.

He had not tried to release it yet.

Trying inside the mountain seemed discourteous.

“I don't know exactly.”

Tae stared.

“You spent six months doing it.”

“Yes.”

“And you don't know?”

“I know it worked.”

“That is not the same thing.”

“It usually is.”

“No, it isn't.”

Huo looked from one to the other.

“I missed this.”

“You were here,” Tae said.

“I missed him annoying you.”

Rhen dismissed the petal.

Qin's fingers slowly lowered.

“It's too quiet,” he said.

That silenced the room more effectively than Huo ever could.

Rhen looked at him.

Qin chose his words carefully.

“Before, even when you suppressed yourself, I could feel where the absence was. Like a bell wrapped in cloth. You hid the sound, but the shape was still there.”

He turned his face toward Rhen.

“Now I can't find the bell.”

Rhen absorbed that.

“Good.”

Tae threw both hands up.

“Of course that's your answer.”

Rhen looked at him.

“What did you want?”

“I don't know. Something normal. ‘Interesting.’ ‘Concerning.’ ‘Maybe I should test this somewhere that doesn't contain everyone we know.’”

Rhen nodded.

“That last one is sensible.”

Tae closed his eyes.

Huo laughed so hard the table pins moved.

Lu caught one before it fell.

“High Paragons,” he muttered. “All of you. Remarkable.”

Rhen's gaze returned to the three men in front of him.

The humor remained, but something underneath it changed.

“Lu told me about the attack here.”

Huo's grin faded.

Tae uncrossed his arms.

Qin went very still.

“Two of them,” Rhen said. “Haru and Hana.”

“Yes,” Qin replied.

“You fought them separately.”

Tae nodded.

“Mostly.”

“Why?”

It was Huo who answered.

“Because if we all jumped them at once, we'd learn less.”

Rhen looked at him.

Huo shrugged.

“And because Tae went first.”

“That second part sounds more accurate.”

“It is.”

Tae pointed at Huo.

“You volunteered me.”

“You were standing closest to the door.”

“That is not volunteering.”

“It is if I volunteer you.”

Rhen looked at Qin.

Qin sighed.

“They were trying to destroy the support hub. Tae engaged first. Huo replaced him when the pattern changed. I ended the fight when we had enough information and the objective shifted to forcing withdrawal.”

Rhen nodded.

That sounded like Sera's people.

Not three strong men chasing the satisfaction of winning.

Three officers using strength for an objective.

“What did you think of their Domains?” he asked.

Tae answered first.

“Haru is dangerous. Very dangerous. His precision is worse than his raw strength. If he gets to choose the line, he wastes almost nothing.”

“Hana?”

“Fast,” Huo said. “Actually fast. Not people-say-fast-because-they-lost-her fast.”

Qin added, “Her movement is physical. Darkness hides connected routes. Light accelerates the real body. No spatial tricks.”

Rhen nodded approval.

“And Yurushi?”

That changed the room.

Qin's expression softened first.

“Alive.”

“Lu told me.”

“Then he didn't tell you how close.”

Rhen looked toward Lu.

Lu pretended to be extremely interested in a ledger margin.

Tae answered.

“Haru took three Redlines fighting him. Yurushi stayed because abandoning the position meant civilians and command staff died behind him.”

Rhen's eyes lowered.

“Tae reached him during the third cycle,” Qin said. “Carried him home after.”

Rhen looked back at Tae.

Tae shrugged once.

“He was heavy.”

“He is smaller than Huo.”

“Everyone is smaller than Huo.”

Huo looked pleased.

Rhen's gaze stayed on Tae.

“Thank you.”

Tae's expression shifted.

He did not make a joke.

“Yeah.”

The room quieted again.

Rhen looked at all three.

For years, he had protected them without asking whether they wanted to be protected that way.

Then Sera had asked him to stop doing so much for her because she wanted strength that was hers.

He had listened.

Later, after nearly losing the core once, he had extended the Garden's long-term refinement back over the household—with consent this time, deliberately, carefully, never compressing them the way he compressed himself.

The result stood in front of him.

Not copies.

Not dependents.

People who had held Wuyue while the strongest public fighters were gone.

Rhen smiled faintly.

“What?” Tae asked suspiciously.

“Nothing.”

“I hate when you say that.”

“You hated when I said things too.”

“That is because most things you say are irritating.”

“Consistent.”

Qin turned toward the northern wall.

“You're going to Isgard.”

“Yes.”

Huo stepped forward immediately.

“We can—”

“No.”

Huo stopped.

Rhen's answer had not been loud.

It did not need to be.

“Sera left you here,” Rhen said. “She had a reason.”

“We're not children guarding the shop.”

“I know.”

“Then—”

“That is why she trusted you with the continent.”

Huo closed his mouth.

Rhen looked at Tae and Qin too.

“If all of you leave because I woke up, then her decision becomes meaningless the moment I return.”

Qin nodded first.

Tae took longer.

Then he exhaled.

“Fine.”

Huo frowned.

Rhen added, “Also someone should stop Huo from drinking the emergency tea stock.”

Huo looked offended.

“That happened once.”

Lu spoke without lifting his head.

“Four times.”

“Those were separate emergencies.”

“What emergencies?” Tae asked.

“I was thirsty.”

Rhen nodded gravely.

“Serious.”

Tae stared at him.

“You've been awake less than an hour and you're already making him worse.”

“Impossible.”

Huo pointed at Rhen.

“See? He understands me.”

Qin actually laughed.

It lasted only a second.

But after six months of casualty lists, it sounded strange enough that everyone noticed.

Rhen let the moment remain.

Then he asked, “Where is Yurushi?”

Lu finally closed the ledger.

“Lower west terrace. He thinks he's helping sort medical crates.”

“Is he?”

“No.”

“What is he doing?”

“Reorganizing them in a way that makes Hana's old Shinsei packing system obvious to anyone who knows it.”

Rhen looked at Lu.

“So helping.”

Lu sighed.

“Yes.”

Rhen turned toward the door.

Before he reached it, Tae spoke.

“Rhen.”

He looked back.

Tae's expression had lost all humor.

“Sera carried this.”

Rhen said nothing.

“For six months,” Tae continued. “Don't get there and make her feel like none of it mattered because you woke up.”

Huo looked at Tae, surprised.

Qin remained still.

Rhen's eyes softened.

“I won't.”

Tae held his gaze another moment.

Then nodded.

Rhen left the room.

Only after the door closed did Huo look at Tae.

“That was almost emotionally intelligent.”

Tae hit him.

The wall cracked.

Lu looked at the crack.

Then at both High Paragons.

“I am billing you.”

## Chapter 444 — Black Radiance and the Hidden Petal

Yurushi Amagiri was holding two boxes of dried feverroot when Rhen found him.

He was not supposed to be holding either.

The lower west terrace had become a temporary sorting yard during the war. Medicine from inland farms moved through one side. Isgard-bound shipments left through the other. Quiet Hand logistics officers had painted neat symbols on every crate so nobody had to open them to know what was inside.

Yurushi had apparently decided the symbols were insufficient.

He had arranged twelve crates into a completely different pattern.

A young quartermaster stood nearby wearing the expression of a man who had lost an argument but had not yet discovered how.

“You can't put the blue-marked crates beside the white-marked ones,” the quartermaster said.

“In Shinsei depots, blue-marked compounds were often stored one aisle from circulation stabilizers.”

“We are not Shinsei.”

“I noticed.”

“Then why are you reorganizing our stores like them?”

“So if anyone trained in their system looks at this yard, they'll know which assumptions are dangerous.”

The quartermaster opened his mouth.

Closed it.

Opened it again.

Rhen stopped several steps away.

“Is this normal?”

The quartermaster turned.

His face went white.

Yurushi looked over one shoulder.

Dark hair. Calm eyes. Lean build. Nothing about him advertised High Paragon unless one knew where to look, and even then his qi sat unusually balanced between two opposed expressions that should have made coexistence difficult.

Light.

Darkness.

Not metaphor.

Literal martial properties carried in one circulation without either consuming the other.

Yurushi set the boxes down.

“You're awake.”

Rhen nodded.

“So I've been told.”

The quartermaster looked between them.

Then at Lu, who had followed at a distance.

Then back at Rhen.

“Should I—”

“Yes,” Lu said.

The quartermaster vanished.

Yurushi watched him go.

“I think he dislikes me.”

“He likes systems,” Lu said.

“So do I.”

“You change his.”

“Only when they're wrong.”

Lu pinched the bridge of his nose.

Rhen looked at Yurushi.

“I like him.”

Lu's expression became pained.

“Of course you do.”

Rhen stepped onto the terrace.

The morning wind moved between them.

For several seconds he simply studied the man Sera had made Sixth Petal while Rhen was sealed inside a mountain.

Yurushi did the same.

Most people meeting Rhen after hearing the stories tried to reconcile two impossible images.

The healer who ran a tea shop.

The man who had flattened things armies could not.

Yurushi seemed less interested in either.

His eyes settled briefly on the faint frost-white petal that had formed near Rhen's shoulder without being summoned.

Pale violet flickered inside it.

Then he looked back at Rhen's face.

“You changed.”

Rhen glanced at the petal.

“So people keep saying.”

“I meant your circulation.”

“That too.”

Yurushi waited for more.

None came.

He nodded.

Rhen appreciated that.

“You helped Yun.”

The terrace quieted.

Yurushi's expression did not change much, but the balance in his qi did. Light receded. Darkness thickened slightly around the edges of his circulation before settling again.

“Yes.”

“Why?”

Yurushi looked at him.

“She needed help.”

Rhen waited.

Yurushi waited back.

“That is all?” Rhen asked.

“Was there supposed to be more?”

Lu looked away because he had already heard this exchange once from Sera and apparently the world had decided he deserved it twice.

Rhen smiled.

“No.”

Yurushi seemed mildly relieved.

Rhen stepped closer.

“Thank you.”

The words were simple.

No bow.

No title.

No promise of repayment.

Yurushi's face changed more than it had since Rhen arrived.

He looked down at the crate between them.

“I didn't save her.”

“No.”

That answer was immediate.

Yurushi looked up.

Rhen's voice remained gentle.

“You helped her get far enough to save everyone else.”

The wind moved again.

For months Yurushi had carried the fact that he had opened routes, changed patrol timings, burned safehouses, risked everything he still had—and Yun had died anyway.

People had thanked him for the warning.

Sera had thanked him for choosing correctly.

Lu had trusted him with work.

None of those things erased the body that reached Isgard too late to be healed.

Rhen did not try to erase it either.

He simply refused to let Yurushi call the attempt worthless because the person at its center had not survived.

Yurushi breathed out slowly.

“She poisoned me the first time I tried to help.”

Rhen's eyebrow lifted.

“Good.”

Yurushi blinked.

Lu closed his eyes.

Rhen continued, “She didn't know you.”

“I know.”

“What did she use?”

Yurushi looked almost offended.

“That's your question?”

“I am a physician.”

Lu muttered, “He keeps saying that as if it explains him.”

Yurushi's mouth twitched.

“Modified sleep toxin. Fast circulation trigger. She expected me to purge upward and used the purge route as the second delivery.”

Rhen's eyes brightened with professional interest.

“That's clever.”

“It was unpleasant.”

“Those are compatible.”

Yurushi looked at Lu.

“Is he always like this?”

“Yes.”

“I heard he was quiet.”

“He is. That is the problem.”

For the first time, Yurushi laughed around Rhen.

Not much.

Enough.

Rhen leaned against one of the crates.

“You're staying in Wuyue.”

It was not a question.

“Sera ordered it.”

“Good.”

Yurushi studied him.

“You don't want me in Isgard?”

“I want you where Sera decided you are useful.”

“That sounds suspiciously like obedience.”

Rhen considered it.

“She is usually right.”

Lu made a choking sound that might have been a laugh.

Rhen ignored him.

Yurushi looked north, toward a horizon that could not show Isgard from here.

“Tsubasa will come eventually.”

“To Isgard?”

“Yes.”

“He hasn't yet?”

“No. Not personally.”

Rhen absorbed that.

Yurushi continued.

“He prefers structure before presence. If he moves himself, he'll consolidate the rest around him. He won't keep the Ten scattered once he believes the war depends on one decisive theater.”

Rhen looked at him.

“You know him well?”

“Well enough to leave.”

“That is not what I asked.”

Yurushi's gaze lowered briefly.

“Well enough to know he genuinely believes Shinsei is necessary.”

Rhen waited.

“And?”

“Well enough to know that makes him more dangerous, not less.”

Rhen nodded.

A fanatic who wanted destruction was simple.

A powerful man who believed coercion was the price of peace could build institutions around the belief and call the results protection.

Rhen looked at the rearranged medical crates.

“What does he think of Axtaya?”

“That it is terrible.”

Lu's eyes narrowed.

Yurushi continued.

“And that terrible things may still be necessary.”

Rhen was quiet.

“That sentence has killed a lot of people.”

“Yes.”

“Does he know the lifespan cost?”

“The senior command knows severe long-term damage. Aya knows the numbers. Tsubasa likely knows enough to understand what he is authorizing.”

Rhen looked down at his hands again.

Five years for five minutes.

A trade made by commanders with soldiers' futures.

He disliked it more each time he heard it.

Yurushi watched him.

“You're angry.”

Rhen looked up.

“No.”

Lu and Yurushi both stared at him.

Rhen sighed.

“Fine. A little.”

Lu folded his arms.

“A little.”

“Yes.”

A frost-white petal formed near Rhen's collar.

Then another.

Then five more.

None carried killing intent.

The air on the terrace still became cold enough that Yurushi's breath fogged.

Rhen looked at the petals.

They vanished.

“Still adjusting,” he said.

Yurushi nodded slowly.

“I see.”

“You don't.”

“No.”

“Neither do I.”

That was somehow worse.

Lu stepped forward.

“Before either of you turn this into another conversation where nobody says what they mean, there is one more thing.”

Rhen looked at him.

Lu handed Yurushi a sealed packet.

“Your full account.”

Yurushi's expression hardened.

“For Sera?”

“Yes.”

Rhen's gaze moved between them.

“What account?”

Lu did not answer immediately.

Yurushi did.

“The parts of Yun's captivity that weren't in the battlefield report.”

Rhen went completely still.

Not cold.

Still.

Lu continued carefully.

“Sera knows there was abuse beyond interrogation. She does not have the complete private account yet. Yurushi only recently gave me enough to send it safely without putting it in normal military channels.”

Rhen's eyes lowered to the packet.

“What did Shunto do?”

Yurushi held his gaze.

“Something Sera should hear before the rest of the war council does.”

Rhen understood the boundary.

He did not push through it simply because he could.

“Send it.”

“I am,” Lu said.

“Today.”

“It leaves in an hour.”

Rhen nodded.

Yurushi looked at him for a long moment.

“You're not asking me.”

“No.”

“Why?”

“Because Yun's story is not mine to turn into curiosity.”

Yurushi's expression softened.

Then Rhen added, “If Sera decides I should know, she will tell me.”

Lu looked at him.

“That was almost emotionally healthy.”

Rhen frowned.

“Almost?”

“You still plan to cross a continent immediately after waking from six months of seclusion.”

“I stood up first.”

“That is not the standard.”

“It should be.”

Yurushi laughed again.

Rhen looked toward the north.

The humor left him gradually.

“Take care of this place.”

Yurushi followed his gaze.

“I have been.”

“I know.”

Rhen stepped away from the crates.

Yurushi spoke before he left.

“Rhen.”

He turned.

Yurushi hesitated only once.

“Sera did not wait for you.”

Rhen's expression softened.

“No.”

“She fought the whole time.”

“I know.”

“Good.”

Rhen nodded.

Then he looked at Lu.

“Is there anything else I need before I leave?”

Lu stared at him.

“Yes.”

“What?”

“Food.”

Rhen blinked.

“I just woke up.”

“That is precisely why.”

“I don't feel hungry.”

“I do not care.”

Yurushi quietly picked up one feverroot box and moved it out of the argument's path.

Rhen looked at Lu.

“You sound like Sera.”

“Someone had to.”

That ended the argument.

Rhen followed him toward the kitchens.

Behind them, Yurushi looked down at the sealed packet for Sera.

Then at the northern horizon.

For the first time since Yun died, the man who had built escape routes for everyone else allowed himself to believe one simple thing.

Someone had finally returned who could make the war stop.

Not today.

Not without cost.

But eventually.

And that was enough for one morning.
