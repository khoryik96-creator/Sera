## Chapter 324 — Shunto Takamori

Yun learned Shunto Takamori’s name from a wanted notice that was not about her.

That irritated her more than it should have.

She had spent six days identifying the smiling man from the market through indirect descriptions, registry references and the sort of careful questions that never asked who he was outright.

Then she walked past a public security board and found his face printed beside a notice congratulating the Ninth District on the capture of three fugitive martial kidnappers.

**SUPERVISING OFFICER: SHUNTO TAKAMORI — SHINSEI SECOND SEAT.**

Yun stopped exactly as long as any foreign merchant might stop to read local news.

Second Seat.

Not merely an investigator.

Not local enforcement.

Shinsei No.2.

The rest took another day.

A training manual footnote referred to the Takamori capture school.

A military ranking almanac gave the tier.

High Paragon.

Yun sat alone in a tea stall and read those two words three times.

Then folded the almanac shut.

Newly Sovereign.

High Paragon.

There was no clever way to make those two facts equal.

Poison could narrow gaps.

Terrain could narrow gaps.

Preparation could make stronger opponents regret confidence.

But cultivation still mattered.

If Shunto reached her in a clean fight, the correct tactical objective was not victory.

It was leaving.

Yun drank the tea.

It was poor.

That helped.

The stall owner noticed her expression.

“Foreigners always hate the smoked leaves.”

“Correctly.”

He laughed.

Yun tapped the closed almanac.

“Second Seat handles fugitives personally?”

“Depends on the fugitive.”

“Seems excessive.”

“Not if he wants them alive.”

That matched.

The owner leaned closer as if sharing gossip.

“Old saying from the border families.”

Yun waited.

“Don’t let Takamori touch you.”

She stared at him.

He grinned.

“Everyone knows that one.”

Apparently everyone except the foreign Sovereign who had learned it with half her body numb in a market lane.

Yun finished the tea and left.

By sunset she had changed rooms again.

The new room had one door, one window and a laundry chute large enough for a narrow person willing to regret several life choices.

She approved.

The coded notebook came out last.

Yun wrote:

**Pursuer identified: Shunto Takamori. Shinsei No.2. High Paragon. Capture specialist.**

Then:

**Direct power contest nonviable. Objective remains evidence + exit.**

She sat back.

The line mattered because admitting the power gap removed a temptation.

Yun had built a reputation partly on making stronger people discover that poison did not care about pride.

That could become its own pride.

Shunto was not a challenge sent by heaven to prove Pale Venom was clever.

He was an obstacle between Yun and Wuyue.

Obstacles did not need to be defeated.

They needed to be crossed.

A knock came at the door.

Three taps.

Pause.

Two.

Not her signal.

Yun’s fingers closed around a needle.

“Who?”

“Laundry.”

She looked at the laundry chute.

Then at the door.

“No laundry.”

Silence.

A man outside said, pleasantly:

“That is unfortunate. Your cover identity paid for the weekly service.”

Yun’s blood went cold.

Shunto.

He had learned the room.

She crossed the floor before the latch moved.

Window first.

Too obvious.

Laundry chute.

Also obvious now.

Yun kicked over the wash basin instead.

Water flooded the floor.

Powder from beneath her sleeve hit it.

A colorless film spread across the boards.

The latch clicked.

Shunto opened the door only two inches.

Stopped.

He looked down.

Yun smiled despite herself.

He had learned.

“Wet floor?” he asked.

“Dangerous.”

“I’ve heard.”

He did not enter.

That was worse than confidence.

He stepped back.

Outside, boots moved in the corridor.

Controlled exits.

Yun looked at the window again.

Then the ceiling.

Then the wall adjoining the next room.

Shunto’s voice came through the gap.

“You are making this more interesting than it needs to be.”

Yun drove a needle into the plaster seam beside the bed.

A prepared corrosive pellet cracked inside the wall.

Wood softened.

She kicked through.

The woman in the next room screamed.

Yun threw her a coin pouch.

“Apologies.”

Then kept moving.

Behind her, Shunto laughed once.

Not angry.

Genuinely entertained.

Yun disliked that too.

She hit the next corridor at a run.

No victory.

No duel.

Only distance. For now, distance was enough.

## Chapter 325 — Poison the Room, Not the Man

Shunto stopped breathing through his nose around Yun Shizhen.

She considered that flattering.

He had also started wearing thin gloves under his outer gloves, carrying his own drinking water and refusing food he had not watched being prepared.

Less flattering.

They met again in an abandoned dye warehouse near the eastern canal.

Yun had not chosen the location.

That was the first problem.

She had followed a requisition clerk there believing he sold diverted stimulant stock.

The clerk never arrived.

Shunto did.

He stepped through the far door with both hands visible.

No soldiers.

No hooks drawn.

Yun stood beside three empty vats.

“You’re improving,” she said.

“I’ve had a good teacher.”

“I charge more than peaches.”

Shunto smiled.

So that part had not changed.

“Yun Shizhen.”

Her face remained still.

He continued.

“Wuyue. Physician-adjacent poison specialist. Public rank uncertain until recently. Pale Venom.”

“You make me sound employable.”

“Are you denying it?”

“No.”

There was no point.

A false identity was useful until the enemy had the truth. Clinging to it afterward only wasted attention.

Shunto tilted his head.

“Why are you here?”

“Tourism.”

“You have visited six medical archives.”

“I like paperwork.”

“Three military-adjacent warehouses.”

“Architecture.”

“A river logistics office.”

“Good view.”

His smile widened.

Yun watched his feet.

He had not crossed the dark stain on the floor between them.

Good.

He knew it might be poisoned.

It was not.

The poisoned route was above him.

A slow-reacting powder clung to the rafters where heat from the old dye vats had carried it days earlier.

Yun had prepared the building as a contingency after finding the address in a clerk’s ledger.

She had not expected Shunto specifically. She had prepared the room because survival favored people who prepared before they needed to.

Shunto said, “Come with me.”

“No.”

“I am asking once.”

“How ceremonial.”

“You are a foreign ranked officer inside restricted Shinsei infrastructure.”

“And yet your streets remain standing.”

“That is not the standard.”

“It should be.”

His expression changed a little.

There was intelligence there.

That was inconvenient.

“You’re looking for Axtaya,” he said.

Yun’s pulse stayed steady.

Shunto watched anyway.

“Interesting,” he murmured.

“You say that often?”

“Only when people pretend not to react.”

Yun lifted one hand.

“Then react to this.”

She snapped her fingers.

A pellet dropped from the rafter behind him.

Shunto moved before it struck.

Not backward.

Sideways.

Exactly where Yun wanted.

His boot landed inside a harmless chalk ring.

He looked down.

Then up.

“Nothing?”

“Nothing.”

He understood one beat too late.

The movement had disturbed the powder above.

Fine particles descended through the shaft of window light.

Shunto stopped inhaling.

Already too late for skin exposure.

He crossed the room.

Fast.

Yun kicked the nearest vat.

Its wooden brace broke.

Not the vat itself.

The floor beneath it.

Stored rainwater from the roof gutter dumped through a hidden channel.

The powder hit moisture.

Changed.

Shunto’s sleeve darkened where the reaction landed.

He tore the outer layer off before the toxin could penetrate deeply.

That bought Yun the distance she needed.

She vaulted onto the second vat.

Shunto followed.

No direct qi projection.

No grand strike.

He wanted contact.

Yun wanted surfaces.

She touched the vat rim on the way over.

Shunto noticed.

Avoided it.

Good.

That made him choose the support beam instead.

Also prepared.

His gloved hand landed there.

The glove smoked.

Shunto released instantly.

Yun hit the upper walkway.

He was still faster.

A hook flashed from his sleeve.

Not to cut.

To catch cloth.

Yun twisted out of her outer robe and left it hanging from the hook.

Shunto’s second hand reached for her wrist.

She threw herself through the window.

Glass broke around her.

The canal was twelve feet below.

Yun hit badly.

Pain burst through her left shoulder.

She surfaced and swam under the dock rather than toward open water.

Above, Shunto appeared at the window.

He did not jump.

He looked down at the canal.

Then at the warehouse behind him.

Yun could almost see the calculation.

Water could carry poison.

The dock could be prepared.

The route after the canal could be prepared.

For once, caution worked in her favor.

He stepped back from the window.

Yun stayed under the dock until her lungs hurt.

Then moved.

Two streets away she climbed into a drainage access and reset her shoulder against the wall.

The joint went back with a wet click.

She breathed through the pain.

No victory.

But Shunto had learned another lesson.

Yun had too.

He would stop trusting empty space.

That meant next time she would need to poison his choices instead.

## Chapter 326 — The Gale Brings One More Ledger

Xie Wuchen returned to the Quaint Teahouse through a window.

Lu Weiran did not look up.

“Door.”

Xie put one boot on the floor.

“Window was open.”

“That is because air is allowed in.”

“So am I.”

“That remains under review.”

Xie stepped fully inside and placed a ledger on Lu’s desk.

Lu looked at it.

Then at him.

“What is this?”

“A ledger.”

“I can see that.”

“Good.”

Lu opened it.

The first page made him stop being annoyed.

Shipping transfers.

Foreign medical resin.

Shinrin origin stamps.

Three intermediary names already present in the Axtaya investigation.

“Where?” Lu asked.

“Eastern port.”

“How?”

Xie leaned against the wall.

“A courier was being beaten.”

“That does not explain how you acquired a commercial ledger.”

“The men beating him dropped it.”

“And then?”

“They continued to not have it.”

Lu stared.

Xie stared back.

Lu turned the page.

Shen Rui entered without knocking, saw Xie and stopped.

“You.”

Xie closed his eyes briefly.

“This house is very bad at privacy.”

Rui walked in.

“You vanished after the west road.”

“Yes.”

“You said you were going south.”

“I went east.”

“That is not south.”

“Strong geography.”

Rui’s eye twitched.

Lu kept reading.

The two of them could kill each other after the useful pages.

Rui folded his arms.

“Why did you bring it here?”

Xie gestured toward Lu.

“He likes paper.”

Lu said, “I do not like paper.”

Both looked at him.

Lu turned another page.

“I like accurate systems.”

Xie nodded gravely.

“Apologies.”

Rui stepped closer to the ledger.

“This connects to Yun?”

“Possibly,” Lu said.

That changed the room.

Rui’s irritation faded.

“How long overdue?”

“More than a week past her expected contact window.”

Xie’s expression went still.

He had known she was away.

He had not known that.

“Where?”

“Shinrin.”

“Alone?”

“Yes.”

Xie looked toward the open window.

Rui caught it.

“No.”

Xie glanced at him.

“No what?”

“You are thinking about disappearing again.”

“I’m always thinking about disappearing.”

“That is the problem.”

Lu closed the ledger.

“Both of you stop.”

They did.

Lu was not stronger; somehow everyone eventually obeyed the man holding the documents.

He tapped one line.

“This vessel unloaded in Wuyue three months ago under a fever-resin declaration. Same vessel departed for Shinrin twelve days later. Then it changed ownership on paper without changing crew.”

Xie said, “False transfer.”

“Yes.”

Rui frowned.

“Smuggling?”

“Maybe. But the customs clearance on the Shinrin side was unusually fast.”

Xie pushed away from the wall.

“You want the captain.”

“I want the records around the captain.”

“Same problem.”

“No. Captains lie. Routes lie less.”

Xie looked almost impressed.

Lu disliked that too.

Rui said, “I can send Azure people.”

“No visible sect detachment,” Lu said. “Not yet.”

“Why?”

“Because Yun left to investigate a pharmaceutical route, not to start an international incident.”

Xie glanced at the window again.

Lu pointed at him without looking.

“You also do not start one.”

“I have no affiliation. Very difficult to create an official incident.”

“You are a newly Sovereign Wind-Qi cultivator personally recognized by Wuyue’s No.5.”

Xie looked at Rui.

Rui smiled unpleasantly.

“Congratulations. You’re official enough.”

Xie sighed.

Lu pulled out a blank sheet.

“I need three things. The captain’s next port. Whether the crew changed after Shinrin. And whether any medical cargo left the ship under military protection.”

Xie took the sheet.

“You assume I’m doing this.”

“No.”

Lu dipped his brush.

“I assume you’ll complain, leave, and return with the answer.”

Rui coughed into one fist.

Xie looked offended.

That was new.

“I may not.”

“Then the sheet was inexpensive.”

For a long moment Xie said nothing.

Then folded the paper once and slid it into his sleeve.

“I am still not joining anything.”

Rui said, “Nobody asked.”

Xie vanished through the window.

Lu looked at Rui.

“Close that.”

Rui stared.

“I didn’t open it.”

“You are closest.”

Wuyue No.5, Peak Sovereign, master of Azure forces and one of the most feared swordsmen alive, walked over and closed the window.

Lu reopened the ledger.

“Thank you.”

Rui sat down.

“You enjoy this.”

“No.”

“You absolutely do.”

Lu turned a page.

Outside, somewhere above the roofs, wind changed direction.

## Chapter 327 — Shunto Changes His Bloodflow

Shunto brought antidotes the next time.

Yun knew because he let her see one.

The small ceramic vial hung from his belt beside the paired hooked blades he had finally stopped pretending not to carry.

They met beneath a stone aqueduct at dusk.

Yun had been following a military-clinic courier.

Shunto had been following Yun.

The courier vanished the moment the two of them noticed each other.

Professional.

Yun stood on the dry edge of the canal.

Shunto stopped fifteen paces away.

“No wet floors today?” he asked.

“Seasonal.”

He touched the ceramic vial.

“I brought a gift.”

“You should drink it now.”

“What is it?”

“Depends what I put here yesterday.”

Shunto’s eyes flicked once toward the stone under his boots.

Nothing visible.

That was enough to make him shift.

Yun watched the movement.

There.

His circulation pattern changed with it.

He had begun segmenting flow.

Instead of maintaining one clean whole-body circuit, Shunto was isolating regions in short controlled loops. If toxin entered one route, he could starve it before it spread.

Smart.

Annoying.

“You’ve been practicing,” Yun said.

“So have you.”

“Mine is more fun.”

“I disagree.”

He moved.

Yun threw three needles.

Not at him.

One struck the aqueduct wall.

One the canal bed.

One the wooden railing above.

Shunto ignored all three.

Also smart.

He crossed half the distance before Yun finished her next breath.

She retreated along the narrow canal edge.

His first hook came low.

Not to cut her leg.

To make her step where he wanted.

Yun jumped instead.

The second hook caught the hem of her coat.

She cut the cloth herself and kept moving.

Shunto’s left hand reached.

Yun twisted away.

His fingertips missed her wrist by less than an inch.

The air pressure from the movement was enough to make her skin crawl.

High Paragon.

The gap never stopped being real.

Yun kicked off the aqueduct pillar.

Her palm touched stone for half a heartbeat.

Shunto noticed.

Avoided that patch.

Exactly.

He chose the opposite side.

Yun snapped a thread hidden across the gap.

A packet burst above him.

Shunto held his breath and closed circulation to exposed skin channels.

The powder settled harmlessly against his outer coat.

Yun smiled.

Shunto saw it.

His eyes narrowed.

The powder was harmless.

Again.

The real toxin sat in the antidote logic.

Yun had spent two encounters learning which circulation routes Shunto isolated first when he anticipated skin exposure.

He closed forearms.

Neck.

Lungs.

Then redirected pressure through deeper shoulder and upper-arm channels to maintain weapon speed.

Predictable.

Not stupid.

Predictable because physiology offered only so many efficient answers.

Yun’s next needle struck his outer sleeve.

Shunto did not care.

The needle barely broke skin.

He rerouted immediately.

There.

The toxin entered the exact channel he had just made dominant.

Shunto’s left hand slowed.

Only slightly.

Yun saw it.

He saw her see it.

His expression changed.

No smile now.

Yun threw herself backward as he accelerated.

The aqueduct wall exploded where his hook struck.

Stone shards filled the canal.

Yun landed badly and rolled.

Shunto’s left arm followed slower than his right.

Numbness beginning.

Not enough.

Not yet.

He took the ceramic antidote vial.

Yun watched his thumb break the seal.

He did not drink it.

He smelled nothing.

Touched one drop to his tongue.

Then changed circulation again before swallowing.

Prepared broad-spectrum counteragent.

The numbness stopped spreading.

Yun clicked her tongue.

Shunto heard.

“Disappointed?”

“Deeply.”

“Good.”

He advanced.

Yun ran.

This time he did not chase immediately.

That was the most worrying thing he had done.

He stood beneath the aqueduct, flexing the compromised left hand while the antidote worked.

Learning.

Again.

Yun disappeared into the evening market.

Her own breath came too fast.

Her shoulder ached.

Two shallow cuts bled through her sleeve.

But she was smiling.

She had seriously poisoned Shinsei No.2.

Not defeated him.

Not even close.

But she had proved something important.

A High Paragon could still be made to choose circulation.

And every choice could become a battlefield.

Back beneath the aqueduct, Shunto stared at his slowly recovering left hand.

Then at the route Yun had taken.

His smile returned.

Smaller this time.

Less amused.

More focused.

Yun had taught him enough to survive her poison.

Now he intended to learn enough to survive her completely.
