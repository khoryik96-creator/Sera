## Chapter 421 — Liang Yue Uses Less

Nao Shibasaki expected Liang Yue to resist Stillwater Dominion by forcing more qi through it.

Most powerful cultivators did.

That was what the Domain punished.

The longer hostile circulation remained under Stillwater, the more ordinary output declined unless the cultivator overpressurized meridians to compensate.

Fight normally and become gradually weaker.

Force normal strength and injure yourself.

A cruel choice.

Liang Yue chose neither.

He simply used less.

Nao saw it in the first minute.

White Vein circulation became narrower.

Cleaner.

No attempt to reproduce his opening output.

No ego.

His body accepted that eighty percent now was better than damaging himself trying to pretend one hundred still existed.

Nao’s eyes narrowed.

[[speaker:nao]]“Efficient.”

Liang smiled.

[[speaker:liang]]“Usually.”

They were not fighting for a knockout. Nao held a broad retreat-control field over Shinsei’s right sector.

Liang’s assignment was to extract nearly three thousand wounded Wuyue and Isgard personnel from a corridor that was becoming untenable.

He did not need to beat her.

He needed her not to stop the evacuation.

Stillwater reduced his explosive qinggong.

He stopped using explosive qinggong.

Stillwater made large techniques require too much circulation.

He stopped using large techniques.

White Vein became surgical.

Small steps.

Precise parries.

Efficient body alignment.

Every action cost less than Nao expected.

That was irritating.

Nao attacked with Rain-Cut Sword.

Light precise cuts flowed around a stronger guard.

Liang did not produce a stronger guard.

He gave her less structure to flow around.

One blade line passed his sleeve.

Second he redirected with the smallest possible wrist movement.

Third opened a shallow cut.

He ignored it.

Behind him, casualty wagons moved.

Hundreds already clear.

Nao looked past him.

[[speaker:nao]]“Those are wounded.”

[[speaker:liang]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Why are you still here?”

[[speaker:liang]]“To keep you asking questions.”

She almost smiled.

Then used Mercy Current to redirect a heavy clash from one allied officer nearby and create an escape path.

Liang saw the choice.

Nao was doing the same thing on the other side.

Protecting people.

The war had made enemies out of two cultivators spending most of their talent minimizing damage.

Liang found that absurd.

“Do you believe in this invasion?” he asked.

Nao’s sword stopped for less than a breath.

[[speaker:nao]]“That is not a battlefield question.”

[[speaker:liang]]“It became one when you crossed the sea.”

Fair.

Nao attacked again.

Liang yielded another five percent of output instead of forcing it back.

His technique quality barely changed.

Nao understood the problem.

Stillwater was working.

Liang was simply too disciplined to make the Domain’s penalty dramatic.

That meant she could hold him.

Not cheaply defeat him.

Good.

For eighteen minutes they fought without either pretending victory required the other unconscious.

At the end, the last wounded column cleared.

Liang stepped back.

Nao could pursue.

Did not.

He looked at her.

[[speaker:liang]]“Why?”

[[speaker:nao]]“Your objective is done.”

[[speaker:liang]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Mine was to hold ground.”

[[speaker:liang]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Then continuing would be pride.”

Liang nodded.

[[speaker:liang]]“Good answer.”

Nao looked irritated.

[[speaker:nao]]“Stop grading me.”

He smiled and withdrew.

Later, both command reports described the engagement differently.

Shinsei:

**Nao held sector.**

Wuyue:

**Liang extracted 2,841 wounded.**

Both were true.

War had become full of victories that did not fit on one line.

## Chapter 422 — Jun Finishes Healing

Aya removed the last restriction from Jun Kajihara’s chart just after dawn.

He read the line twice, suspicious of good news on principle.

[[speaker:jun]]“Full?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Recoverable conventional damage: effectively full.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Effectively?”

[[speaker:aya]]“Medicine uses words carefully. Try it sometime.”

Jun ignored the insult and flexed the arm Kael had damaged weeks earlier.

No instability.

He rotated the shoulder through a slow circle. No pain. A full breath produced no catch beneath the ribs. Even the deep meridian bruise that had made Gatebreaker Halberd feel half a beat late was gone.

Aya had taken him from near-collapse to seventy-eight percent in days because Shinsei needed bodies back on the line. The rushed return had never been the same as healing. She had known it. Jun had known it. Command had known it and used him anyway.

Now enough uninterrupted treatment had finally passed for the remainder to close.

Jun looked at the clean chart.

[[speaker:jun]]“So I can go back.”

[[speaker:aya]]“I said you are medically cleared.”

[[speaker:jun]]“That means I can go back.”

[[speaker:aya]]“It means your body is no longer the reason you cannot.”

He looked at her.

Aya’s expression remained neutral.

Jun had learned that neutral was where she hid the most complicated answers.

He picked up the halberd resting beside the cot, tested its weight, then stopped before leaving.

[[speaker:jun]]“Kael?”

[[speaker:aya]]“What about him?”

[[speaker:jun]]“If I fight him again, I don’t make the same mistake.”

[[speaker:aya]]“That is not a medical observation.”

[[speaker:jun]]“No.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Good.”

Jun did not move.

Aya eventually looked up.

[[speaker:aya]]“What?”

He had spent his recovery reading battlefield summaries because lying still was intolerable and because the first loss to Kael had embarrassed him enough to become educational. The reports contained more than technique notes now.

Halvek’s road.

Raska’s river retreat.

Tor’s retirement.

Eldran’s ruined frontline future.

Sigrun returning after Kenji nearly killed her.

People on the other side kept paying costs that Aya could erase from Shinsei bodies but not from theirs.

“I used to think recovery meant we were harder to defeat,” Jun said.

[[speaker:aya]]“We are.”

[[speaker:jun]]“That is not what I mean.”

Aya put the brush down.

Jun continued. “Kael beat me. I came back before I was ready. Then I finished healing anyway. Kenji loses and you rebuild him. Eirik gets hurt and comes back. We keep treating the fact that we can stand again as proof we should keep standing in the same place.”

Aya watched him carefully.

[[speaker:aya]]“Do you want me to disagree?”

[[speaker:jun]]“No.”

[[speaker:aya]]“Good.”

He almost smiled.

Then his face settled again.

[[speaker:jun]]“Does getting cleared mean you think I should fight?”

[[speaker:aya]]“No.”

The answer came too quickly to misunderstand.

Aya tapped the chart.

[[speaker:aya]]“This says your shoulder will not fail because I released you. It says your meridians can sustain your own cultivation. It says the injuries Kael gave you are healed. It does not tell you what your orders mean, whether the invasion is right, or whether a second fight is worth another field full of wounded people.”

Jun’s grip shifted on the halberd.

[[speaker:jun]]“You make medicine sound annoyingly limited.”

[[speaker:aya]]“It is. That is why physicians become dangerous when governments start asking medicine to answer political questions.”

He looked at her for a long time.

[[speaker:jun]]“Thank you.”

Aya’s brush stopped.

“For healing me,” he added.

[[speaker:aya]]“You do not owe me obedience because I treated you.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I know.”

[[speaker:aya]]“You do not owe Shinsei more years because I returned your body.”

Jun went still at the wording.

Redline had made that sentence impossible to hear innocently.

Aya did not soften it.

[[speaker:aya]]“Medical clearance is not ownership.”

Jun nodded once.

Then left with his halberd and a body that felt as though the first fight had never happened.

The first fight had happened anyway.

On the Wuyue side, confirmation reached intelligence before noon.

**Jun Kajihara — returned to full conventional combat capacity.**

Kael read the report from a recovery bench while a physician retied the brace on his knee.

[[speaker:kael]]“Again?”

Luo nodded.

[[speaker:kael]]“I beat him.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kael]]“He was badly injured.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:kael]]“And now he is fine.”

[[speaker:luo]]“Yes.”

Kael looked personally offended by the concept of successful medicine.

Luo folded the report.

[[speaker:luo]]“Please do not take this personally.”

[[speaker:kael]]“I am absolutely taking it personally.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You were healed by Rhen from worse things.”

[[speaker:kael]]“That was different.”

[[speaker:luo]]“How?”

[[speaker:kael]]“He is unfair.”

Luo considered the argument.

[[speaker:luo]]“Valid.”

Then he added a new notation beside Jun’s name.

**Defeated once does not equal removed.**

Kael stopped joking.

The sentence was the real problem.

Aya did not need Shinsei to win every battle. She did not even need its Paragons to avoid losing.

She only needed the living ones to reach her with enough body left to repair.

## Chapter 423 — Shen Rui Holds the Breach

Jun returned smarter.

Shen Rui noticed immediately.

The first time Kael fought him, Jun had treated Breach Dominion like a decisive rule he could build everything around.

Now he used it like one tool inside a disciplined military system.

Worse.

The Azure formation held the eastern gap with layered shields, spear lines and rotating qi reinforcement.

Against ordinary enemies, excellent.

Against Breach Dominion, conventional defensive mitigation did nothing once Jun’s halberd connected cleanly.

Rui had planned for that.

[[speaker:rui]]“Do not block the blade.”

His officers repeated the order.

[[speaker:rui]]“Redirect shaft. Break angle. Give ground before contact.”

Jun still reached them.

First wall opened.

Second retreated.

Third held two breaths too long.

Gatebreaker Halberd hit.

The shield might as well have been paper.

Jun broke through.

Rui met him personally.

Peak Sovereign against Stable Paragon.

Bad arithmetic.

Necessary timing.

Jun stopped.

[[speaker:jun]]“Move.”

Rui smiled.

[[speaker:rui]]“No.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I do not need you dead.”

[[speaker:rui]]“Comforting.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Your troops are already withdrawing.”

[[speaker:rui]]“Exactly.”

Rui attacked.

He knew he could not win.

That freed him from trying.

Every exchange existed to buy seconds.

Jun’s halberd cut through one reinforced guard and opened Rui’s shoulder.

Pain went down his arm.

Rui changed hand.

Second Wall followed.

Jun shaped the next strike around the exposed timing.

Rui took it across the upper chest instead of the throat.

Armor broke.

He staggered.

Behind him, the final Azure companies crossed the secondary line.

Good.

Jun raised the halberd again.

Rui smiled through blood.

[[speaker:rui]]“Done.”

Jun saw the retreat was complete.

He stopped.

That was also new.

Old Jun might have spent one more attack because decisive violence ended uncertainty.

This Jun had lost to Kael, been healed by Aya, and returned with less interest in proving that every breach required a body beneath it.

“Go,” Jun said.

Rui laughed.

[[speaker:rui]]“You invaded us.”

[[speaker:jun]]“I know.”

[[speaker:rui]]“That was not permission.”

[[speaker:jun]]“No.”

Rui turned.

Then his injured shoulder failed.

He nearly fell.

Wind caught him.

Not magical wind.

A man.

Xie Wuchen landed beside him.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Convenient?”

Rui grimaced.

[[speaker:rui]]“You took your time.”

Xie looked at Jun.

Then at Rui.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“I had to become No.10. Apparently that includes rescuing people who once tried to arrest me.”

Rui almost smiled.

Then blood filled his mouth.

Xie’s expression changed.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Right. Jokes later.”

The Unseen Gale lifted him.

Jun watched them leave.

He did not pursue.

Not because Xie was stronger.

He was not.

Because the military objective was already achieved.

Jun had learned.

That made him more dangerous.

## Chapter 424 — The Unseen Gale Takes Number Ten Into Battle

Xie Wuchen had no Dominion.

This became extremely relevant while carrying Shen Rui through a collapsing war zone chased by people who did.

“Put me down,” Rui said.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“No.”

[[speaker:rui]]“I can run.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“You are leaking.”

[[speaker:rui]]“That is not technical language.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“It is accurate.”

Xie used Wind Qi the way Tempest Hall had taught him after he rebuilt the old flaw.

Not compressed destructively inside his meridians.

The atmosphere outside became the pressure chamber.

Air gathered.

Released.

His body accelerated without requiring the same suicidal internal compression that had stalled his cultivation for years.

Still Sovereign.

Still finite.

Carrying another Peak Sovereign while evading Paragon pursuit pressure was not free.

Xie hit the first ridge too fast.

Changed angle.

Meridian pain flashed along one leg.

Rui noticed.

[[speaker:rui]]“You’re overdoing it.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“You’re observant for a dying man.”

[[speaker:rui]]“I’m not dying.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Then stop making it convincing.”

Behind them, a Shinsei pursuit group gained.

No official Paragon in direct chase.

Good.

Duke/Peak-Marquis elite enough to matter.

Xie could handle them.

Probably.

Not while carrying Rui cleanly.

He changed route.

Wind pressure collapsed a loose awning behind them.

Not attack.

Obstacle.

Two pursuers lost line.

Xie cut through a narrow ravine.

The air there was better for compression.

He accelerated again.

Pain sharpened.

Rui said, “Xie.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“No.”

[[speaker:rui]]“You don’t know what I was going to say.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“You were going to tell me to leave you.”

Silence.

Xie smiled without humor.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Predictable.”

[[speaker:rui]]“You will damage yourself.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:rui]]“Why?”

Xie almost laughed.

Months earlier, Sera had asked him why he always helped and then left before anyone could depend on him.

Now Rui was asking why he stayed.

Apparently life had become irritatingly symmetrical.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Because I took the number.”

Rui looked at him.

Xie continued running.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“I’m not replacing Yun.”

[[speaker:rui]]“I know.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“I’m carrying the empty number.”

[[speaker:rui]]“I know.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“Then stop asking why I’m carrying people.”

Rui shut up.

Good.

They reached the allied secondary line with Xie’s breath ragged and Wind Qi stuttering around one ankle.

Medics took Rui.

Luo looked at Xie.

[[speaker:luo]]“You too.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“No.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You’re limping.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“I am dramatically walking.”

[[speaker:luo]]“You overstrained.”

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“I am Sovereign.”

[[speaker:luo]]“That does not make meridians decorative.”

Xie sighed.

Sat.

Luo examined him.

No catastrophic tear.

Inflammation.

Microdamage.

Restricted duty several days.

Xie looked offended.

[[speaker:xie_wuchen]]“This is what No.10 does?”

Luo did not look up.

[[speaker:luo]]“No. This is what you do.”

That was fair.

Later, the public report said:

**No.10 extracted Shen Rui from the eastern breach.**

No mention of Dominion.

No fake Paragon moment.

No invented army.

Just a Sovereign using exactly what he had until it nearly broke.

The number finally felt like his without taking anything from Yun’s history.

## Chapter 425 — Ilyra Burns a Redline Depot

Ilyra Serath had once been famous for leaving battlefields people did not enjoy describing.

The final arc gave her a better use for violence.

A Redline depot sat twelve miles behind Shinsei’s forward line.

Not a dramatic fortress.

A warehouse system.

Medical seals.

Shipping schedules.

Enough 3× stock to supply several thousand future minutes of borrowed life.

Ilyra wanted it gone.

Sera approved the raid with one condition.

[[speaker:sera]]“No civilian fire spread.”

Ilyra looked offended.

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I know.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Say it.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“No civilian fire spread.”

[[speaker:sera]]“Good.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I am not a child.”

[[speaker:sera]]“No.”

Sera paused.

[[speaker:sera]]“You were worse when younger.”

Ilyra glared.

Then left.

The raid used only enough people to stay invisible.

Quaint recon identified patrol gaps.

Astrid’s Isgard scouts provided the rear route.

Ilyra led the strike.

No vanity duel.

No hunting a Paragon because one existed nearby.

She killed two depot commanders who refused surrender and attacked first.

Disabled four others.

Her team opened the medical storage before ignition to separate legitimate medicine from Redline stock. The separation mattered because Axtaya had grown from medicine.

Not every vial in a Shinsei hospital deserved fire.

Then the depot burned.

Controlled trenches kept flame from spreading into the adjacent workers’ quarter.

Shinsei response arrived faster than expected.

Ilyra held the route.

Old instincts returned.

Brutality came easily.

She used it differently now.

Not punishment.

Protection.

Every savage strike was aimed at the people closing on the evacuation corridor, not civilians, not surrendering medics, not anyone merely wearing the wrong uniform.

One Shinsei spear entered under her ribs.

She broke the shaft.

Kept fighting.

Second strike damaged the hip.

Third almost took her leg.

Ilyra refused to fall until the last allied team cleared.

Then she did.

The Redline depot collapsed in flame behind them.

Restricted duty followed.

Weeks, not hours.

She hated it.

Sera visited the field hospital.

[[speaker:sera]]“You succeeded.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I know.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You’re alive.”

[[speaker:ilyra]]“I noticed.”

[[speaker:sera]]“You are not returning tomorrow.”

Ilyra looked at her.

[[speaker:ilyra]]“No.”

Sera raised one eyebrow.

Ilyra exhaled.

[[speaker:ilyra]]“Fine.”

Growth was ugly.

Sometimes it looked like a woman once known for brutality accepting that surviving the mission mattered more than bleeding dramatically on the next one.
