# Chapter 316 — A Country That Works
## Insert: Seven Bridges

**Preferred placement:** after Yun encounters the broken-circle marker / old Shinsei motto and before the chapter moves deeper into Shinrin.

The bun seller noticed Yun looking at the ribbons tied around the stone marker.

“Children still do that?” Yun asked.

“Every spring.”

“For Shinsei?”

The woman wiped flour from her hands.

“For the bridge.”

Yun looked at her.

“What bridge?”

The question earned the first expression of genuine surprise Yun had seen from her.

“You really are foreign.”

“So I have been told.”

The woman leaned one hip against the stall.

“Seven Bridges.”

Yun waited.

“Before Shinsei held the river country properly, three clans were fighting over the crossings. Not soldiers fighting soldiers. Everyone. Farmers getting searched. Ferries burned. Houses emptied because somebody's cousin wore the wrong crest.”

Her face had changed while she spoke.

Not into reverence.

Memory inherited from somebody else's fear.

“My mother was six.”

Yun said nothing.

“By the last night, six bridges were gone. Burned or broken. The clans were fighting over the seventh while people were still trying to cross.”

“Tsubasa?”

The woman nodded.

“He was young then. Not what he is now.”

That distinction interested Yun.

“What did he do?”

“Stayed.”

Yun waited for the rest.

The woman shrugged.

“That is the story.”

“He fought three clans.”

“Probably.”

“You don't know?”

“I know my mother crossed.”

The answer stopped Yun.

The woman pointed vaguely north.

“They say he held the bridge through the night. Kept the supports standing. Stopped anyone from reaching the families. Didn't chase the clans when they pulled away. Didn't leave when people started calling the road clear.”

Her voice softened.

“He stayed until the last family crossed.”

The market moved around them.

A cart wheel squeaked.

Someone shouted about onions.

Two children ran past the Shinsei marker with ribbon tangled around one wrist.

Yun looked at the broken circle joined by one line.

Propaganda could invent a hero.

It could polish one.

It could not easily manufacture the exact way an old merchant still measured a martial legend by whether her mother had reached the other bank alive.

That was more inconvenient.

A cruel government was easy to understand.

A government built by people who had once done something worth loving was much more dangerous.

Because people did not obey only from fear.

Sometimes they remembered the bridge.

# Chapter 335 — Luo's Private Notation
## Insert: the second layer is still them

**Preferred placement:** after Yun has established the notebook's military/medical code structure and before she closes it for the scene.

Yun finished the troop-rotation sequence and stared at the bottom margin.

There was room for one more line.

Not enough for anything useful.

Which was why she used it.

She wrote three apparently unrelated medical marks beneath the last supply notation:

**Low heat. Empty stomach. Repeated diagnostic fixation.**

A Shinsei clerk would read it as a symptom cluster.

A physician might assume it described one of the Axtaya cases.

Luo would know better.

It was an old joke from the second winter they had worked together.

He would skip meals while diagnosing difficult patients, then become irritated when his hands shook and insist the problem was inadequate tea.

Yun had once written the same three marks across his breakfast bowl.

Translated properly, they meant only:

**You forgot to eat again.**

Yun looked at the line.

Then added one final notation beside it.

**Treat before recurrence.**

Eat something, idiot.

Her mouth moved very slightly.

Then she turned the page and went back to the war.

# Chapter 349 — She Escapes on Her Own Feet
## Insert: what Yurushi does and does not know

**Preferred placement:** after Yurushi gets a clear look at Yun's condition but before the final extraction movement begins.

Yurushi caught her when one knee failed.

Only for a second.

Yun pushed away from him as soon as the leg remembered how to hold weight.

“I'm fine.”

“No.”

“Useful correction.”

He looked at her properly.

Not the blood.

Not the damaged circulation marks around her wrists.

The whole picture.

The too-careful way she kept one side away from contact. The medicinal smell of a detention compound. The bruising that did not match a clean arrest. The exhaustion beneath the poisoner's discipline.

Yurushi's face went still.

“What did Takamori do?”

Yun's eyes met his.

For one moment, something behind them became very cold.

Then she looked toward the route north.

“Later.”

He did not move.

Yun added, “If there is one.”

That answered enough.

Not everything.

Enough.

Yurushi's jaw tightened.

Yun pointed at the northern corridor.

“Isgard first.”

He nodded once.

No demand for details.

No promise to avenge her.

No attempt to turn her escape into his anger.

“Isgard first,” he agreed.

And they moved.

# Chapter 353 — Before the Door Closes
## Insert: one last ordinary problem

**Preferred placement:** before the chapter turns fully toward the medical danger of Rhen's seclusion.

The bedroom window would not close.

Rhen had been fighting it for six minutes.

Sera watched from the bed.

“You're losing.”

“I'm assessing it.”

“The window?”

“Yes.”

“It has two hinges.”

“One is misaligned.”

“You once stopped a mountain from falling on a village.”

“That mountain was structurally simpler.”

Sera covered her mouth.

Rhen looked over.

“Are you laughing?”

“No.”

“You are.”

“Petals Monarch defeated by carpentry.”

“Windows are not carpentry.”

“What are they?”

Rhen looked back at the frame.

“Currently hostile.”

Sera lost the fight and laughed into the pillow.

Rhen gave the window one final, deeply offended look.

Then he shut it with two fingers and an amount of precision qi that could probably have severed a fortress gate without disturbing the wall around it.

Sera stared.

“You cheated.”

“It is closed.”

“You used cultivation on a window.”

“It was hostile.”

She held out a hand.

Rhen crossed the room and took it.

Sera pulled him down beside her.

For a little while the coming seclusion was not a medical problem, or a strategic absence, or the beginning of six months neither of them could see.

It was simply tomorrow.

And tonight, Rhen had lost to a window.

- Yurushi's exchange establishes that he knows something terrible occurred without receiving a full firsthand account.
- The window beat restores the older domestic contrast immediately before Rhen's long absence.
