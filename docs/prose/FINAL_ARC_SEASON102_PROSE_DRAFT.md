## Chapter 371 — Crownless Against the Unbroken

Aldric Veyrhald opened the battle by trying to end it in one thrust.

That was not arrogance.

It was efficiency.

The spear left his hands in a straight line toward Eirik Voss's shield.

Then **Unbroken Dominion** took hold.

The air did not glow.

The ground did not split into some separate world.

Aldric simply committed.

And commitment, inside his Dominion, became difficult to stop.

The spear point struck Eirik's shield.

The first impact should have ended there.

It did not.

Force remained inside the contact.

Still pushing.

Still travelling.

Still demanding an answer.

Eirik's boots sank two fingers into frozen soil.

His shield arm bent.

His shoulder lowered.

**Winter Brace.**

Force travelled through shield rim.

Forearm.

Shoulder.

Hip.

Rear leg.

Into the earth.

Aldric's thrust kept going anyway.

The shield screamed.

Eirik slid backward one pace.

Then another.

Isgard soldiers roared.

Shinsei did not.

Their formation remained quiet.

Eirik's eyes stayed on Aldric.

[[speaker:eirik]]“So that is what you became.”

Aldric's jaw tightened.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You came home to find out.”

He twisted the spear.

The sustained pressure changed angle without truly ending.

Eirik's shield rim tore open.

Blood appeared along his knuckles.

High Paragon or not, Aldric had not spent five years becoming decorative.

Eirik answered by opening **Crownless Dominion**.

The difference was almost insulting in how little it looked like.

No wind.

No visible wall.

No crushing pressure.

Aldric simply inhaled after the first exchange and felt the battlefield give him nothing back.

He noticed immediately.

Ambient qi still existed.

He could sense it.

Could feel the world breathing around him.

His circulation simply could not meaningfully take it in.

The reserve already inside his body remained his.

Full strength.

Full ownership.

But once spent, it stayed spent.

Aldric's eyes sharpened.

Eirik lifted his damaged shield.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Do you understand?”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Then stop.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“No.”

Aldric attacked again.

Of course he did.

A horizontal spear sweep entered Unbroken Dominion and refused to lose force after the first three shield contacts.

Eirik caught it once.

Twice.

On the third, the spearhead cut through his upper arm.

Blood darkened Shinsei cloth.

Aldric reversed.

Eirik stepped inside the shaft.

Sword came low.

Aldric withdrew just enough.

Not enough to escape entirely.

The blade opened his side.

Then both men moved.

For ten minutes, everyone watching forgot what rank was supposed to look like.

Aldric struck like a man trying to prove that an honestly committed attack could still force history to move.

Eirik defended like a man who had spent half a lifetime learning that surviving the first answer mattered more than winning it beautifully.

The first five minutes belonged to Aldric.

The next three belonged to neither.

The ninth minute belonged to mathematics.

Aldric's breathing changed.

Only slightly.

His Domain remained active.

His techniques remained powerful.

But every Supreme-level commitment now cost exactly what it cost.

No natural recovery between exchanges.

No environmental replenishment through breath.

No slow return of what the body had spent.

Eirik's own reserve still touched the surrounding qi normally.

Not infinitely.

Not for free.

Just normally.

That was enough.

Aldric knew the shape of the trap before anyone else did.

He could conserve.

Fight smaller.

Wait.

And give Eirik the initiative.

Or he could spend harder and attempt to end the fight before Crownless turned endurance into defeat.

Aldric chose the second.

Eirik had expected that too.

“You always hated long arguments,” he said.

Aldric thrust.

[[speaker:aldric]]“You always made them long.”

The spear hit Eirik's shield again.

Unbroken pressure continued.

Eirik let it.

That was the trick.

Instead of fighting the sustained force head-on, he yielded one angle at a time, rotating his body and shield while Aldric kept feeding commitment into an attack that refused to end.

Aldric had created a strike that could continue.

Eirik turned continuation into expenditure.

Five heartbeats.

Eight.

Twelve.

The spear pressure remained terrible.

Aldric's reserve did not.

Then Eirik broke the angle.

Not the Dominion.

The body producing it.

His sword struck Aldric's forward shoulder.

The spear dipped.

Aldric forced it back up.

Eirik's shield crashed into his chest.

One rib broke.

Then another.

Aldric drove qi through the pain and stabbed from too close.

The spear point entered Eirik's side.

Shinsei soldiers shouted.

Eirik grunted.

Then caught the shaft beneath his shield arm.

Aldric tried to wrench free.

His body answered half a beat slower than he wanted.

Not because Crownless weakened him directly.

Because the strength he had spent was gone.

Eirik headbutted him.

The world flashed white.

Aldric staggered.

Eirik's sword hilt struck his sternum.

The third impact collapsed the damaged rib line inward.

Aldric went to one knee.

His Dominion flickered.

He still tried to stand.

Eirik stepped back.

That surprised everyone.

Aldric looked up.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Finish it.”

[[speaker:eirik]]“No.”

[[speaker:aldric]]“Why?”

[[speaker:eirik]]“Because I came here to make Isgard submit.”

Eirik's face was tired.

[[speaker:eirik]]“Not to prove I can kill you.”

Aldric laughed once and coughed blood.

[[speaker:aldric]]“Very civilized invasion.”

The words landed harder than the spear had.

Eirik's expression changed.

Aldric saw it.

Good.

He pushed himself upright anyway.

The Isgard line surged forward before he could fall again.

Maedra's people reached him first.

Eirik did not pursue through the extraction formation.

Not because he could not.

Because the battlefield objective had already shifted.

Aldric Veyrhald left the field alive.

Three broken ribs.

Severe internal bleeding.

Shoulder circulation damaged.

Reserve nearly emptied.

Unbroken Dominion intact in principle.

Its owner very much not.

Shinsei had defeated Isgard's supreme legal authority in the first major Paragon duel.

Nobody watching believed Isgard had become weak.

That was what frightened them.

## Chapter 372 — Halvek Holds the Road After the Gate Is Gone

Halvek Veyrhald understood roads better than most men understood swords.

That was why Jun Kajihara killed him at a gate that no longer mattered.

The road behind it did.

For six days, Halvek had turned retreat into architecture.

Civilians moved first.

Then medicine.

Then wounded.

Then replacement bowstrings and dried grain.

Then soldiers.

Always in that order unless the road itself was threatened.

Every bridge had a demolition team.

Every narrow pass had a second route.

Every wagon captain knew which ditch to abandon cargo into if wheels broke under fire.

Shinsei kept taking ground.

Halvek kept refusing to let ground become collapse.

Jun noticed.

On Day Twenty-One, he stood before the Seventh Road Gate and studied the fortress wall.

Stone.

Iron bracing.

Layered timber interior.

A normal siege commander might have admired it.

Jun did not care.

“Where does the road split behind it?” he asked.

An officer pointed.

“Two miles inland.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Evacuation traffic?”

“Continuous.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Then the wall is not the objective.”

The officer looked at the wall.

Jun lifted his halberd.

[[speaker:jun]]“The road is.”

The first swing struck the gate.

**Breach Dominion** activated on contact.

The iron bracing might as well have been decoration.

Not because Jun erased matter.

Not because the gate vanished.

Because conventional defensive mitigation stopped meaningfully reducing the force where his halberd actually landed.

Armour.

Shielding.

Reinforcement qi.

Thicker timber.

All of it still existed.

None of it did the job people had built it to do.

The impact travelled into the gate as if the protection were an argument Jun's Dominion had already declined to hear.

Wood exploded inward.

The second strike hit the damaged seam.

**Second Wall.**

The third broke through.

The gate was gone in less than a minute.

Halvek did not defend the opening.

He blew the withdrawal horn.

Jun's eyes narrowed.

Isgard soldiers fell back by companies.

Not panicking.

Executing a schedule.

“Push,” Jun ordered.

Shinsei moved through the breach.

Then the inner street collapsed.

Not magically.

Halvek's engineers dropped pre-cut support posts from two adjoining structures and buried the first pursuing formation under timber and stone.

Jun stopped the next rank before it fed into the blockage.

[[speaker:jun]]“Clear side route.”

The officer beside him looked furious.

“They destroyed their own town.”

[[speaker:jun]]“They destroyed our timetable.”

Jun watched Isgard withdraw.

[[speaker:jun]]“Respect the distinction.”

By sunset, the gate belonged to Shinsei.

The road did not.

Halvek had already moved the next evacuation column three miles inland.

The same thing happened at the Eighth Gate.

Then the Ninth.

Jun had become famous at Thirteen Gates because he could break sequential defence faster than defenders could rebuild certainty.

Halvek made him repeat the lesson in reverse.

A gate could fall and still buy time.

A road could retreat and still remain a road.

On Day Twenty-Four, Halvek reached the last fortified choke point before the interior split.

He checked the evacuation ledger himself.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Last medical wagon?”

“Passed.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Civilians?”

“Western villages clear.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Rear infantry?”

“Crossing the fork now.”

Halvek looked toward the gate.

Shinsei banners were already visible.

A young officer said, “Then we leave.”

Halvek looked at him.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Yes.”

The officer blinked.

He had expected something grander.

Halvek pointed inland.

[[speaker:halvek]]“You leave.”

The officer's face changed.

“Commander—”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Somebody has to make them believe this gate still matters for another ten minutes.”

“We can leave a company.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“No.”

“Then a squad.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“No.”

The young officer's jaw trembled.

Halvek softened.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Do not turn my job into other people's graves because you dislike the shape of it.”

The officer stared at him.

Then saluted.

Halvek hated salutes.

He returned it anyway.

The last Isgard soldiers withdrew.

Halvek stood alone inside the gatehouse with twelve signal cords, four demolition levers and a spear he had barely used all week.

Jun arrived nine minutes later.

The outer gate was open.

That made him stop.

He entered cautiously.

Halvek stood on the inner road.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Jun Kajihara?”

Jun held his halberd low.

[[speaker:jun]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Thirteen Gates.”

Jun's expression shifted.

[[speaker:jun]]“You know it.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“I study roads.”

[[speaker:jun]]“That was a siege.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Sieges are roads with worse manners.”

Jun almost smiled.

Almost.

[[speaker:jun]]“Move.”

Halvek looked behind him.

Nothing.

Good.

The last rear company had cleared the fork.

He lifted his spear.

[[speaker:halvek]]“No.”

Jun sighed.

[[speaker:jun]]“You know how this ends.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:jun]]“Then why?”

Halvek pulled the first signal cord.

A distant bridge collapsed.

Jun's eyes sharpened.

Halvek pulled the second.

Smoke rose from a supply shed Shinsei might have used.

Third.

The inland road markers rotated to reveal false distances.

Fourth.

The final gate dropped behind Jun's advance column, dividing the first companies from their support for several minutes.

Halvek looked at him.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Because ten minutes is still ten minutes.”

Jun moved.

The fight lasted thirty-one breaths.

Halvek was not Paragon.

He did not become one because death approached.

He did not reveal a secret technique saved for dramatic convenience.

He was an elite Isgard commander with a strong spear, excellent timing and no illusion about the difference between himself and Shinsei No.9.

He used the gatehouse.

Narrowed Jun's swing.

Dropped one beam.

Forced a sidestep.

Cut Jun's sleeve.

Bought six breaths.

Then Jun's halberd connected cleanly.

Breach Dominion did what it did.

Halvek's reinforced guard did not meaningfully soften the strike.

The spear broke.

The blade entered beneath his ribs.

Halvek stayed standing long enough to pull the final cord.

Far inland, the evacuation fork markers fell into their new position.

The road was closed behind the last column.

Jun caught Halvek before he hit the ground.

Halvek looked annoyed by this.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Put me down.”

[[speaker:jun]]“You'll die.”

[[speaker:halvek]]“That was already happening.”

Jun lowered him carefully against the wall.

Halvek's breathing shortened.

“Road?” he asked.

Jun looked toward the empty gate.

[[speaker:jun]]“Clear.”

Halvek's mouth moved.

Not quite a smile.

[[speaker:halvek]]“Then the gate was cheap.”

He died before Jun answered.

Shinsei took the Seventh Road system that evening.

They found almost nothing behind it worth capturing.

Halvek had spent gates to buy roads.

Then spent himself to buy ten more minutes.

Jun wrote the casualty report personally.

Under enemy commander, he did not write **eliminated**.

He wrote:

**Held objective until evacuation complete.**

Then stared at the sentence longer than regulations required.

## Chapter 373 — Raska's Last Charge

Raska Dravaryn had spent most of his career believing speed solved fear.

If a formation broke, arrive first.

If a flank opened, close it first.

If civilians were trapped, reach them before the enemy did.

Cavalry was not glory to him.

It was distance made negotiable.

Kenji Narukami understood that better than Raska expected.

Which was unfortunate.

They met on a broad snowfield east of Halvek's broken road network.

Raska's cavalry had one job.

Hold Shinsei long enough for two retreating infantry formations to cross the frozen river behind them.

Kenji's advance force had one job.

Do not let that happen cleanly.

Raska raised his sword.

“Two passes,” he told his riders.

His lieutenant looked at the Shinsei line.

“Only two?”

[[speaker:raska]]“After two, we are tired horses pretending otherwise.”

The lieutenant nodded.

Raska smiled.

[[speaker:raska]]“Good. Nice to be understood.”

They charged.

The first pass worked.

Raska hit Shinsei's left edge before Kenji could fully reposition.

Horsemen broke spear spacing, cut two supply wagons free from the line and withdrew before the response closed.

The second pass almost worked.

Kenji stepped into the snow.

Thunder Qi snapped around his legs.

**Stormstep Dominion.**

Raska felt the battlefield change.

One rider planted a stirrup foot after leaning too far through a cut.

A Thunder-Qi strike hit his position.

Another horse landed from a small ditch jump.

Lightning hit the ground beside it.

The animal screamed and veered.

Raska understood immediately.

[[speaker:raska]]“Disengage mounts!”

His lieutenant stared.

“What?”

[[speaker:raska]]“Off the horses!”

A cavalry commander ordering dismount in open ground sounded insane.

Then another planted contact called another thunder strike.

The riders obeyed.

Raska hit the ground running.

Thunder struck where his foot landed.

He twisted away.

Next step.

Another.

There was the Domain.

Not one impossible bolt.

The problem was that fighting normally required feet.

Kenji came through the storm laughing once from pure martial exhilaration.

Then caught himself.

This was not a trial ground.

Raska saw the correction in his face.

“You like this,” he said.

Kenji's sword flashed.

Raska blocked.

Thunder Qi discharged through the clash point and numbed three fingers.

“I like fighting,” Kenji answered.

[[speaker:raska]]“Bad place for it.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Yes.”

That honesty irritated Raska more than bravado would have.

They moved.

Raska learned fast.

He stopped using full running footwork.

Pivoted from planted positions.

Used sliding snow pressure to shift weight without committing clear steps.

Jumped once, then realized landing counted too.

Kenji smiled despite himself.

[[speaker:kenji]]“Good.”

[[speaker:raska]]“Stop complimenting me.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“No.”

Raska cut for his shoulder.

Kenji used **Flashstep**.

Physical acceleration.

No teleportation.

Just enough Thunder-Qi drive through nerve and muscle to make the distance collapse before Raska's next planted adjustment.

Sword hit armour.

Raska turned with it.

The cut glanced.

Thunder still entered.

His left leg spasmed.

He nearly fell.

Behind them, the first Isgard infantry formation reached the river.

Good.

Raska did not need to win.

That knowledge made him more dangerous.

He drove Kenji away from the crossing rather than toward death.

Used cavalry sabers thrown from two dismounted riders to clutter movement lanes.

Forced Kenji to step.

Stormstep did not punish its owner.

But every moment Kenji spent solving Raska was a moment the river kept filling with retreating Isgard troops.

Kenji saw it.

[[speaker:kenji]]“You're not trying to beat me.”

[[speaker:raska]]“Congratulations.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“You're trying to keep me here.”

[[speaker:raska]]“Second congratulations.”

Kenji's face tightened.

He changed rhythm.

**Sevenfold Thunder.**

Not seven magical copies.

Seven stored bursts released through one continuously changing-speed assault.

Raska blocked the first.

Missed the second.

Shoulder opened.

Third grazed thigh.

Fourth he caught on the flat of his sword.

Fifth broke the weapon.

Sixth passed through armour beneath his ribs.

The seventh never came at him.

Kenji used it to accelerate toward the river.

Raska threw the broken half of his sword.

Kenji turned his head.

Enough.

Raska tackled him.

Both hit the snow.

No planted step.

No fresh thunder.

Just two men crashing into frozen ground.

Kenji rolled first.

Raska did not.

The sixth cut had gone too deep.

Kenji rose.

Raska grabbed his ankle.

“You are very annoying,” Kenji said.

[[speaker:raska]]“Good.”

[[speaker:kenji]]“Let go.”

[[speaker:raska]]“No.”

The second Isgard infantry formation reached the river.

Kenji looked at it.

Then at Raska.

He understood.

His expression changed.

[[speaker:kenji]]“Damn you.”

Raska laughed blood into the snow.

[[speaker:raska]]“Third congratulations.”

Kenji could have cut the hand off.

He did not.

He struck Raska's wrist hard enough to break the grip.

By then the river crossing was covered by Isgard archers from the far bank.

The opportunity had closed.

Raska lay on his back.

Kenji stood over him.

[[speaker:kenji]]“Why didn't you retreat?”

Raska looked toward the river.

[[speaker:raska]]“They did.”

Not **we**.

They.

That was the answer.

Kenji's jaw tightened.

Raska's eyes closed.

He died before the snow stopped falling into his hair.

The Thousand-Step Trial had taught Kenji that movement meant arriving where people needed him.

Raska Dravaryn taught him the other half.

Sometimes staying where you were could move an entire army.

Kenji did not enjoy that lesson.

## Chapter 374 — Tor Wins the Retreat and Loses the Frontline

Nao Shibasaki told Tor Veyrhald exactly what her Domain was doing to him.

He hated her for the courtesy.

“Your output is falling,” she said.

Tor's axe hit her jian hard enough to bend the blade line sideways.

Nao rotated with the force.

Wrist.

Elbow.

Shoulder.

Waist.

Foot.

**Quiet Lake Guard.**

The collision spread through her body instead of breaking one point.

Tor drove forward.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You think I had not noticed?”

[[speaker:nao]]“No.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Then stop explaining.”

[[speaker:nao]]“No.”

He swung again.

The second strike was weaker.

Not much.

Five percent, perhaps.

Enough for a Sovereign to know his own body was lying.

**Stillwater Dominion** had been active for several minutes.

It did not steal Tor's qi.

It did not erase his cultivation.

The reserve remained inside him.

The problem was pressure.

The same circulation effort now produced slightly less output.

A little less weapon reinforcement.

A little less explosive drive through the legs.

A little less strength from techniques that had worked perfectly at the beginning.

Tor could accept the decline.

Or force more qi through the same channels until the old output returned.

He chose force.

Of course he did.

Behind him, three companies were withdrawing through a ravine too narrow to fight inside.

If Tor became weaker at the entrance, Nao's formation would enter the ravine with them.

So he drove circulation harder.

His axe regained full weight.

Nao's eyes changed.

[[speaker:nao]]“You'll tear something.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Probably.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Then reduce output.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“No.”

[[speaker:nao]]“You cannot hold the old level indefinitely.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Do I look like I need indefinitely?”

Nao glanced past him.

The retreating companies were still moving.

She understood.

That made everything worse.

Tor attacked.

Full output.

Again.

Again.

Every forced correction pushed harder against meridians Stillwater had made increasingly unwilling to sustain the same pressure.

Heat built along his forearms.

Then pain.

Then the unmistakable wet tearing sensation of internal bleeding along one circulation branch.

Tor grinned.

Nao looked furious.

[[speaker:nao]]“Stop.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You first.”

[[speaker:nao]]“This is unnecessary.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“It becomes unnecessary when the last company clears.”

Nao's sword paused for half a beat.

Nao recognized the pattern Kenji had found in Raska.

Shinsei had planned to defeat an army.

Isgard kept making individuals spend themselves on time.

Nao switched targets.

Not Tor.

The ground beside him.

A Rain-Cut sequence severed two wagon traces and collapsed the empty cart blocking one side of the ravine entrance.

Tor looked at her.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You missed.”

[[speaker:nao]]“No.”

Shinsei infantry behind Nao had to slow.

Tor understood.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You are helping me.”

[[speaker:nao]]“I am preventing my soldiers from entering a bad choke point.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“That was almost convincing.”

Nao's jaw tightened.

[[speaker:nao]]“You are still the enemy commander.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Yes.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Move.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“No.”

She sighed.

Then attacked him properly again.

Stillwater continued.

Tor forced circulation.

His left arm stopped responding cleanly.

He forced it harder.

Something tore near the shoulder junction.

The axe nearly fell.

He caught it with the right hand.

Nao's sword stopped a finger from his throat.

Tor looked at it.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Last company?”

Two notes.

Clear.

Gone.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Yes.”

Nao lowered the sword.

[[speaker:nao]]“Then leave.”

His left leg failed.

Tor looked down.

Annoyed.

Nao caught his shoulder before he hit the ground.

He stared at her.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“This is humiliating.”

[[speaker:nao]]“You're welcome.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Why?”

[[speaker:nao]]“Because the battle is over.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Not the war.”

[[speaker:nao]]“No.”

Nao raised one hand.

[[speaker:nao]]“Prisoner. Alive.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“Isgard will retrieve me.”

[[speaker:nao]]“Probably.”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You sound certain.”

[[speaker:nao]]“You are irritating enough that somebody will pay.”

Tor's meridians did not.

Inflammation.

Tears.

Internal bleeding.

One major junction permanently damaged.

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“That is what command is.”

[[speaker:nao]]“What?”

[[speaker:tor_veyrhald]]“You are thinking.”
