## Chapter 478 — Heaven Returns What It Receives

Sera hurt him properly three minutes later.

Not a touch.

Not a cracked rib.

Not a shallow Counter-Bloom accepted for position.

Properly.

Tsubasa committed to a shortened-chain crescent strike at her right flank.

Sera saw the bloom at his rear shoulder.

He changed the geometry through a broken pillar.

The opening shifted toward his ribs.

Sera changed with it.

He tried to shorten again.

Too late.

Her foot planted.

Her body turned.

**The Orchid Blooms Only Once.**

For one perfect instant, every fragment of movement she had engineered through the previous exchanges belonged to her.

The strike landed below Tsubasa's left ribs.

Peak-Paragon Orchid Qi did not explode outward.

It entered cleanly.

The impact folded him.

His feet left the ground.

He crossed twenty paces and struck stone hard enough to split it.

The armies reacted as one body.

Wuyue shouted.

Isgard shouted louder.

Even several Shinsei soldiers inhaled sharply.

Sera did not celebrate.

Something was wrong.

She had felt it at contact.

Not resistance.

Reception.

Tsubasa rose from the broken stone.

Blood ran from his mouth.

His left side was already darkening beneath torn cloth.

One rib had displaced.

Another was cracked.

The strike had damaged him exactly as it should have.

And yet his circulation carried something new.

Compressed force.

Not his own.

Sera's eyes narrowed.

Tsubasa wiped his mouth.

“Good.”

She stared at him.

“What did you do?”

He moved.

The answer arrived before the explanation.

The crescent crossed the basin in a direct downward line.

Sera stepped aside.

The blade missed.

The shock through the chain did not.

The ground beneath her erupted.

Sera's eyes widened.

The transmitted force was familiar.

Too familiar.

Her own impact rhythm lived inside it.

Tsubasa pulled the chain back.

The next strike came harder.

Not because he had suddenly become stronger.

Because part of what she had given him was coming back.

**Heaven Returns What It Receives.**

Tsubasa's Supreme passive did not erase damage.

His broken ribs remained broken.

The blood remained real.

His meridians still had to endure the collision.

But when genuine martial force reached him—impact, weapon collision, shockwave, qi transmitted through contact—his circulation captured part of that transmitted force and compressed it into the next committed attack.

He did not store it forever.

He could not build an infinite battery.

The force had to be spent quickly.

And if too much arrived, the excess injured him instead of becoming free power.

But the rule changed everything.

Hurting Tsubasa and arming Tsubasa could happen in the same instant.

Sera understood after the second returned strike.

She stopped throwing heavy force.

Tsubasa noticed immediately.

He smiled without warmth.

“You see it.”

“Yes.”

“You're quick.”

“I've been told.”

He released the crescent.

Sera evaded rather than blocked.

The weapon struck stone.

Tsubasa gained nothing from her.

He reversed the chain.

Sera used **Orchid Shadow Steps** and let the blade pass without offering a collision.

Tsubasa's eyes sharpened.

The fight changed again.

Sera stopped feeding him impact.

She used smaller touches.

Needle-like qi.

Footwork.

Joint disruption.

Shallow Counter-Blooms placed to alter structure rather than blast him backward.

Tsubasa adapted.

If she would not give him force, he would create situations where avoiding collision became expensive.

He anchored the chain to two stone ridges.

The crescent swept between them.

Sera ducked one pass.

The second forced a forearm block.

Metal struck qi-reinforced bone.

Tsubasa received part of the clash.

His next pull accelerated.

The crescent returned faster.

Sera twisted.

The blade cut across her side.

Blood darkened her clothing.

Rhen's fingers tightened once behind the Wuyue line.

Only once.

Kael saw it.

He said nothing.

Sera did not look toward Rhen.

That mattered.

She had asked for the duel.

He was letting her have it.

Tsubasa entered close range.

Sera met him there.

Palm.

Elbow.

Chain wrapped around forearm.

Knee.

Shoulder.

Crescent haft.

The exchange became too close for spectacle.

Sera struck his damaged ribs.

Tsubasa grunted.

He captured part of the impact anyway.

His elbow came back carrying her own force added to his.

It struck Sera's guard.

Her left forearm went numb.

She stepped inside before retreat pressure could catch her.

Two fingers touched his collarbone.

Small force.

Precise.

Tsubasa's shoulder weakened.

He used the weakness to change grip instead of resisting it.

The chain wrapped his wrist.

The crescent changed plane.

Sera ducked.

Tsubasa's knee hit her abdomen.

She folded.

His next attack committed.

Orchid Dominion opened a Counter-Bloom at his supporting hip.

Sera hit it from below.

Tsubasa lost balance.

The crescent buried itself in stone.

Sera kicked his injured ribs.

He took the blow.

Stored part of it.

Sera saw the circulation change.

Too late.

Tsubasa yanked the crescent free and released the stored force through the chain.

The black blade did not hit Sera.

The chain did.

It struck her across the back.

She went down hard.

Silence rolled across Wuyue's ridge.

Sera planted one palm.

Rose.

Blood touched her lips.

Tsubasa's own breathing was ragged now.

He was not winning cleanly.

That mattered too.

He looked at her.

“You stopped feeding me.”

“Yes.”

“And I still hit you.”

Sera wiped her mouth.

“You sound proud.”

“I am.”

“Good.”

She lowered her stance again.

“Keep that feeling.”

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

Sera smiled.

“I'm going to take it from you.”

The Pale Orchid moved.

## Chapter 479 — One Chain Binds Heaven / Ten Thousand Blooms

Tsubasa realized Sera's plan when the third anchor broke.

She was not trying to beat him directly anymore.

She was trying to make him reveal everything.

The first anchor failed when Sera redirected a Counter-Bloom into the stone seam around it instead of his body.

The second failed when she used his own chain tension to crack the pillar holding it.

The third failed because she simply kicked the already-damaged base until it gave way.

Tsubasa watched the stone collapse.

Sera stood behind it, breathing hard.

Blood marked one side of her waist.

Her left arm was slower than the right.

But her eyes remained clear.

Tsubasa understood.

If he continued fighting with ordinary anchor geometry, she would keep reducing the battlefield until his positional advantage became manageable.

If he wanted the duel back under his control, he had to expand the system.

Sera smiled faintly.

“There it is.”

Tsubasa's eyes narrowed.

“You wanted this.”

“Yes.”

“Why?”

“So I know what the strongest conventional cultivator alive looks like when he stops holding something back.”

The ridge behind Sera went silent.

Tsubasa looked toward Rhen once.

Rhen did not move.

Then Tsubasa looked back at Sera.

“Fine.”

He released the chain.

Not six feet.

Not twenty.

All of it.

The reinforced length hissed across the basin.

Tsubasa moved with terrifying speed.

Chain struck stone.

Wrapped a pillar.

Passed through a carved gap.

Hooked a broken arch.

Crossed a trench.

Looped a ridge spur.

Again.

Again.

Again.

Hundreds of physical anchor points formed across the battlefield.

No chain duplicated.

No weapon multiplied.

There was still only one man.

One chain.

One crescent.

But the uninterrupted physical connection became a web across the basin.

Body to hand.

Hand to chain.

Chain through anchor after anchor.

Chain to crescent.

Tethered Heaven's rule flowed through the entire connected structure.

The battlefield became his reach.

**One Chain Binds Heaven.**

The Ultimate opened fully.

The pressure hit everyone watching.

Not a magical barrier.

Not a spatial prison.

Martial geometry.

Every anchor changed the possible return path of the crescent.

Every connected line gave Tsubasa another route to redirect momentum without breaking physical continuity.

Near the chain network, retreat resistance propagated along the same connected structure.

The basin was no longer a place with Tsubasa standing inside it.

It was a machine his body had become the center of.

Jin stopped breathing for a second.

Xie Wuchen whispered, “That's absurd.”

Kael's eyes narrowed.

Liang Yue said nothing.

Across the opposite ridge, even Kai looked grimly impressed.

Aya watched Tsubasa's pulse climb.

The Ultimate was costing him.

Meridian strain.

Concentration.

Weapon load.

Body load.

No free miracles.

But what it bought was horrifying.

Tsubasa pulled.

The crescent disappeared.

Not literally.

It moved too fast through the first anchor turn for ordinary eyes to keep it.

Sera moved left.

The chain changed plane through a stone arch.

The crescent came from above.

She ducked.

A second anchor redirected it sideways.

Sera blocked.

Impact armed Tsubasa's passive.

The next return came heavier.

She slipped beneath it.

Another anchor.

Another angle.

Another hostile commitment.

Orchid Dominion began blooming everywhere.

That was the mistake.

Not Tsubasa's.

Not yet.

The battlefield simply reached the exact threshold Sera had been building toward.

Every committed attack created an opening.

Every new chain direction was still a real committed martial decision.

Every anchor transition that locked into an attack line gave Orchid Dominion something to record.

One bloom.

Then five.

Then twenty.

Then more than anyone except Sera could track.

Tsubasa saw the pale qi marks gathering.

His expression changed.

“What is that?”

Sera stood in the center of the chained basin.

Blood ran from her mouth.

Her breathing was ragged.

Her left arm hung lower than before.

And she smiled.

“You kept committing.”

Tsubasa pulled the crescent back.

Too late.

Sera opened her hands.

**Ten Thousand Blooms.**

The Peak-Paragon Ultimate did not create flowers from nothing.

It activated the entire debt Orchid Dominion had already earned.

Every stored Counter-Bloom opened at once.

And while Ten Thousand Blooms remained active, every new hostile commitment generated its answer immediately.

The basin erupted in pale Orchid Qi.

Not random explosions.

Precise punishments.

An anchor line cracked where Tsubasa's force had overcommitted through it.

A chain segment snapped against a damaged ridge.

Tsubasa's right shoulder took a Counter-Bloom exactly as he pulled.

His grip weakened.

He switched hands.

The switch became another commitment.

Another bloom opened against his stance.

His knee buckled.

He forced the next chain route anyway.

Three more blooms opened along the connected geometry.

Stone anchors shattered.

The network lurched.

Tsubasa changed the orbit.

Sera was already inside it.

Her palm hit his sternum.

He received part of the force.

His body still took the damage.

The next attack tried to return it.

Ten Thousand Blooms punished the commitment before the return completed.

His elbow dropped.

The crescent veered.

Sera struck again.

Tsubasa staggered.

For the first time in the duel, he gave ground unwillingly.

One step.

Then another.

The Wuyue ridge exploded in sound.

Jin forgot he hated cheering.

Shen Rui did not.

Ilyra shouted something obscene and supportive.

Kael's mouth moved faintly.

Across the Shinsei line, silence.

Tsubasa pulled the chain through twelve anchors at once.

Ten Thousand Blooms answered twelve commitments.

Four anchors failed.

Two chain sections slammed against stone and bent.

Tsubasa took three impacts across his torso.

Blood sprayed from his mouth.

He still did not fall.

Sera advanced.

This was her moment.

Not because Rhen had weakened him.

Not because someone else had solved his Domain.

Not because Tsubasa was on a timer created by Redline.

Her system had forced the strongest conventional cultivator alive to reveal his full battlefield art.

Then her system punished exactly what that art required him to do.

Tsubasa's Ultimate became a field of commitments.

Sera's Ultimate became a field of consequences.

For a meaningful stretch of time, Tsubasa Kurokawa was losing.

Everyone watching knew it.

Tsubasa knew it too.

That was when he stopped trying to overpower Ten Thousand Blooms.

He changed the question.

The chain went slack.

Sera's eyes narrowed.

No commitment.

No new bloom.

Tsubasa released the crescent from its attack path and let it fall into a low orbit.

He stopped feeding her Ultimate.

Ten Thousand Blooms still held every debt already earned.

But the flood of new answers slowed.

Sera moved.

Tsubasa defended.

No hostile commitment.

No immediate bloom.

She attacked again.

He absorbed the movement with footwork and chain positioning rather than countering.

Sera's eyes sharpened.

The strongest conventional cultivator alive had learned the same lesson she forced on everyone else.

Sometimes the answer to an offensive system was not a stronger offense.

It was patience.

Sera struck his shoulder.

Tsubasa took it.

Small force.

He stored almost nothing useful.

She attacked his ribs.

He retreated laterally, not directly away enough to trigger his own Dominion's worst geometry.

The chain network remained damaged but alive.

Tsubasa waited.

Sera's Ultimate continued burning reserve.

That was the cost.

Ten Thousand Blooms could make every earned opening flower at once.

It could not make Sera infinite.

Tsubasa watched her breathing.

So did Aya.

So did Rhen.

Rhen said nothing.

Sera knew exactly what Tsubasa was doing.

She smiled anyway.

“Coward.”

Tsubasa, bleeding from half a dozen serious wounds, answered:

“Strategist.”

Sera laughed.

Then attacked again.

## Chapter 480 — Two Peak Paragons Are Not Equal

Ten Thousand Blooms ended without ceremony.

No final explosion.

No collapsing sky.

The last stored Counter-Bloom opened against a damaged chain anchor and split it through the center.

Then the pale marks across the basin faded.

Sera remained standing.

Barely.

Tsubasa remained standing too.

Worse than barely.

His left ribs were broken in several places.

One shoulder had lost full range.

His right thigh had taken two deep structural strikes.

Blood covered his chest.

His chain network had lost nearly a third of its anchors.

One section of reinforced chain was visibly warped.

The crescent blade itself had a shallow fracture near the inner curve.

He looked like a man who should have stopped.

Sera looked like a woman who had spent everything trying to make sure he needed to.

Neither moved for several breaths.

The armies did not cheer.

The duel had gone beyond that.

Tsubasa's **One Chain Binds Heaven** remained active in reduced form.

Sera's **Orchid Dominion** remained open, but the Ultimate layer was gone.

Her reserve had fallen hard.

Tsubasa saw it.

Sera saw him see it.

“Don't look so happy,” she said.

Tsubasa coughed blood.

“I am not happy.”

“You have that face.”

“What face?”

Sera almost laughed.

Then winced.

“Never mind.”

Tsubasa tightened his grip.

The final phase began.

No new Ultimate.

No Redline.

No hidden realm.

Just two Peak Paragons with damaged bodies, incomplete reserves and enough understanding of each other to make every mistake expensive.

Sera attacked first.

Tsubasa defended.

A small Counter-Bloom formed only when he finally committed to a chain return.

Sera took it.

Her palm hit his wrist.

The crescent dipped.

She stepped inside.

Tsubasa used the chain itself as a brace and drove his shoulder into her.

Sera spun away.

Tethered Heaven made the retreat expensive.

Her legs slowed for half a beat.

That half-beat was enough.

The crescent haft struck her ribs.

Something cracked.

Sera's breath left her.

Tsubasa did not follow with a killing blow.

The terms forbade execution.

More importantly, he did not need one.

Sera forced herself upright.

Tsubasa stared.

“Enough.”

She raised one hand.

“No.”

“Sera.”

“Don't start sounding like him.”

Tsubasa glanced toward Rhen despite himself.

Rhen stood completely still.

Sera's eyes flashed.

“Eyes here.”

Tsubasa looked back.

She moved.

**Veiled Moon.**

Her approach disappeared inside a subtle shift of body rhythm rather than literal invisibility.

Tsubasa's chain reacted late.

Sera struck his damaged thigh.

He dropped to one knee.

The Wuyue ridge inhaled.

Sera followed.

Tsubasa planted the crescent into stone and used the chain as a pivot.

His body rotated from the ground.

Sera's next palm struck his guard.

Heaven Returns What It Receives captured part of it.

Not much.

Enough.

Tsubasa spent the force immediately through a short-range shoulder and chain-assisted turn.

Sera's balance broke.

He hooked her ankle with the chain.

No magical restraint.

Physical steel.

Sera cut the loop with qi before it tightened fully.

The detached edge weakened under Tethered Heaven.

Not enough.

The chain held.

Tsubasa pulled.

Sera was dragged one step.

She used the forced step to enter range.

Her fist struck his jaw.

Tsubasa's head snapped sideways.

He answered with an elbow.

Sera blocked.

The impact numbed both arms.

Both stumbled apart.

Neither had enough left for elegance.

Kael whispered, “She's still making him solve her.”

Jin did not look away.

“Can he?”

Kael's face tightened.

“Yes.”

That was the truth.

Sera had reached Tsubasa's level.

She had not reached his accumulation.

Peak Paragon described a tier.

It did not make every person inside it equal.

Tsubasa had lived at the top of the conventional system longer.

His body had adapted to larger loads.

His reserves were deeper.

His chain art gave him more ways to remain functional while damaged.

His Supreme passive converted even imperfect collisions into marginal advantage.

His Dominion made Sera pay for every attempt to reset distance.

And most importantly, he kept thinking.

He did not panic when Ten Thousand Blooms put him behind.

He survived the stretch where he was losing.

Then he forced the duel into the one place where experience mattered most.

The end.

Sera attacked again.

Tsubasa let the strike touch his guard.

Small force.

Captured.

He did not counter immediately.

Sera expected him to.

That was the trap.

He waited one half-beat.

Sera moved to punish the expected commitment.

There was none.

Her foot landed.

Tsubasa committed then.

The crescent came low.

Sera jumped.

Tethered Heaven made the backward escape inefficient.

She chose forward.

Exactly where Tsubasa wanted her.

He released the last stored force through the chain and rotated the crescent flat rather than edge-first.

The broad black metal struck Sera across the torso.

The impact launched her out of the near zone.

She hit stone.

Rolled.

Stopped.

The battlefield went silent.

Sera tried to rise.

One hand planted.

Her arm shook.

She got one knee beneath herself.

Tsubasa did not approach.

No execution.

No humiliation.

He stood where he was, chest heaving.

Sera pushed again.

Her knee slipped.

She laughed once.

Quietly.

“Annoying.”

Tsubasa almost collapsed hearing it.

“You are,” he answered.

Sera looked up.

“Was talking about you.”

“I know.”

She tried one more time.

Her circulation failed to answer cleanly.

That was enough.

The medical observers saw it.

Aya raised one hand on the Shinsei side.

Luo raised his on Wuyue's.

The duel was over.

Tsubasa exhaled.

Then looked toward Rhen.

Rhen was already moving.

Not before the end.

After it.

Exactly as promised.

He crossed the basin without hurry and reached Sera before she could fall sideways.

His arm caught her around the back.

Sera opened one eye.

“You took your time.”

Rhen looked down at her.

“You told me to.”

“Fair.”

His fingers touched her wrist.

Healing qi entered immediately.

Not the full thirty-mile Sanctuary.

Just her.

Broken ribs stabilized.

Internal bleeding stopped.

Torn muscle began repairing.

The overstrained meridians cooled.

Sera's breathing eased.

She looked past Rhen toward Tsubasa.

The Shinsei leader still stood alone among broken anchors.

Barely.

His Ultimate faded.

The remaining chain dropped to stone.

Tethered Heaven Dominion collapsed with it.

Tsubasa took one breath without the Domain.

Then another.

He looked at Sera.

“No one has pushed me that far.”

Sera's mouth moved faintly.

“Aethon?”

Tsubasa shook his head.

“No.”

That answer moved through the watching ranks faster than any official report ever would.

Sera had lost.

Nobody mistook that for weakness.

She had forced the strongest conventional cultivator of the era to unveil his Ultimate, lose anchors, break chain, bleed, retreat, adapt and survive a stretch where he was genuinely being beaten.

Base Tsubasa Kurokawa remained above her.

But now everyone knew how narrow the word above could become when the Pale Orchid was beneath it.

Rhen looked at Sera.

“Done?”

She closed her eyes.

“For today.”

“Good.”

“Don't sound relieved.”

“I am relieved.”

Sera smiled weakly.

“Terrible poker face.”

“I don't play cards.”

“I know.”

Across the basin, Aya reached Tsubasa.

She immediately checked his ribs.

He flinched.

Aya looked furious.

“You are an idiot.”

Tsubasa coughed.

“I won.”

“That is not a medical diagnosis.”

For the first time in hours, Sera laughed.

It hurt.

Rhen healed that too.

The armies did not move.

No general offensive began.

The champion engagement had prevented that much for one day.

But the war was not over.

Tsubasa had won the last conventional argument.

And now the man holding Sera in his arms looked across the battlefield at him.

Not angry.

Not jealous.

Interested.

Tsubasa saw the look.

For the first time since the duel began, something colder than exhaustion touched his spine.

Rhen said nothing.

He did not need to.

Season 112 ended with the strongest conventional cultivator alive still standing.

And the Petals Monarch preparing to make sure he stood at his very best.
