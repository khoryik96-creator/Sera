# Final Arc Literary Polish Audit

## Scope

This pass covers **Seasons 95–114 / Chapters 301–500** and is deliberately literary rather than structural. Canon, outcomes, power mechanics, chronology, relationships, rankings, deaths, and the locked ending remain unchanged.

The goal was to make the completed final arc read like finished fiction rather than a sequence of strong draft beats: reduce habitual rhetorical constructions, vary paragraph rhythm where repetition had become visible, remove editorial/source leakage, strengthen underwritten scenes, and preserve the clipped house style where it is doing real work.

## What changed

### Corpus-level rhetorical repetition

The diagnostic baseline showed several constructions recurring often enough to become audible as authorial habits. The polish pass reduced them selectively rather than mechanically:

| Construction | Baseline | Final audit |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `There it was` | 29 | 0 |
| `That mattered` | 47 | 13 |
| `That was enough` | 23 | 6 |
| `Not because ...` | 106 | 60 |
| `For the first time ...` | 61 | 49 |

The surviving instances were kept when the construction carried a specific dramatic, technical, comedic, or character-purpose rather than acting as default narration.

### Underwritten scenes expanded

Five late-Phase-II chapters were materially too skeletal and were expanded with scene-level content rather than filler:

- **Ch416 — Aya Gives Kenji Back His Body**: recovery privilege, medical ethics, and the distinction between healing and obedience.
- **Ch422 — Jun Finishes Healing**: medical clearance separated from moral/political permission; Aya's role becomes more than battlefield reset machinery.
- **Ch430 — We Beat Them Yesterday**: dramatizes why repeatedly wounding Shinsei apex fighters does not solve the attrition problem while Aya can restore conventional damage.
- **Ch432 — The Map That Refuses to Get Better**: Jin's conclusion that Wuyue is "winning too slowly" is earned through command-room arithmetic and accumulated battlefield facts.
- **Ch434 — Isgard Still Has a Government**: demonstrates rather than merely states why Isgard's own imperfect government remains a legitimate alternative to Shinsei's imposed order.

The corpus average moved from roughly **908 words to 922 words per chapter**. The increase comes mainly from these genuinely underwritten scenes; no global word-count target was imposed.

## Line-level polish

The pass also:

- removed source-facing lines such as **"Phase I ended..."** and **"End of Phase II"** from chapter prose;
- preserved the exact Ch440 → Ch441 wake-up seam while removing editorial framing around it;
- tightened Sera's Tsubasa duel so the prose emphasizes her decisions, mechanics, and earned achievement rather than repeatedly telling the reader that the moment matters;
- refined Rhen/Sera consent language around the final duel and Rhen's decision to intervene;
- polished Luo/Kael/Shunto medical-combat payoff language;
- strengthened Tsubasa's final conventional martial showing without changing his power, Redline state, Domain, or defeat;
- varied repeated staging lines where identical phrasing appeared across distant chapters;
- replaced the Ch465 draft-like ending **"The next phase had begun"** with Luo's direct realization that the next attack will be for him.

## Deliberate non-changes

The polish did **not** normalize every short paragraph. The final arc intentionally uses clipped paragraphs for:

- combat tempo;
- shock and injury;
- dry Quaint comedy;
- interrogation/survival sequences;
- tactical realization;
- Rhen's understated dialogue.

Likewise, repeated intelligence-report wording was preserved where characters are literally reading or transmitting the same information. Identical facts moving through the story are not treated as prose defects merely because the wording recurs.

Several intentionally short chapters also remain short. In particular, **Ch440 — How Long?** stays a compact wake-up cliffhanger because expanding it would weaken the Phase II → Phase III seam.

## Publication micro-pass

The last pass removed four remaining low-value repetitions / draft-like transitions:

- the Ch500 bed staging now differs from Ch351;
- Ch450 now gives Sera an active beat while she weighs Rhen's question rather than repeating an earlier generic look;
- Ch493 varies Tsubasa's table staging while preserving the symbolism of stepping away from executive authority;
- Ch465 ends on Luo realizing the next attack will be for him, directly handing off into Ch466.

## Editorial assessment

The final arc no longer has a systemic prose problem that requires another broad rewrite. Remaining repeated constructions are local stylistic choices and should be judged in context during ordinary reading, not eliminated by quota.

Further edits should therefore be **reader-discovered line edits only**. Another corpus-wide variation pass would risk sanding away the deliberate terse rhythm that distinguishes the series.
