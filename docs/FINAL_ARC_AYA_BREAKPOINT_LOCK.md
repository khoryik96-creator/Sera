# Final Arc — Aya Katsuragi Breakpoint Lock

> **Status: AUTHORITATIVE LATER CANON LOCK.**
>
> This file records the approved Phase II progression for Aya Katsuragi and the point at which Axtaya becomes an internal Shinsei moral fracture. Where this conflicts with older final-arc proposal wording, this file wins for Aya / Axtaya character progression.

---

## 1. Aya's starting position

Aya does **not** begin Phase II ignorant of Axtaya's danger.

She already knows that higher doses consume future vitality and that military use has moved well beyond the safe medical purpose of her original circulatory stimulant. Her existing flaw remains that she stayed close to the program because she believed a physician inside the system could reduce harm better than a physician who left.

She must therefore not receive a simplistic revelation that “Redline is dangerous.” She already knows that.

Her real Phase II break is realizing that Shinsei has converted emergency medicine into **industrialized disposable lifespan** and has begun using her medical credibility to normalize it.

---

## 2. Jun's treatment must justify why Aya stayed

After Jun Kajihara's first major defeat in Isgard, Aya spends several days to roughly a week repairing him.

She stabilizes damaged meridians, removes disruptive foreign qi, repairs internal injury and returns him to approximately 70–80% combat function before full recovery.

This scene must remind the reader why Aya stayed inside Shinsei:

- she is one of its best physicians;
- people genuinely survive because she remains;
- leaving would have real human cost.

The emotional principle is that Aya's old justification is not foolish on its face: **people die when the physician leaves first.**

That makes the later collapse of that justification meaningful.

---

## 3. First mass Redline activation — Aya's nightmare

Aya knows Redline exists and knows soldiers carry sealed 3× doses, but she does not support mass routine use.

When Shinsei command authorizes the first large formation-wide 3× Redline activation in Isgard, Aya objects but is overruled because the battlefield is judged strategically critical.

Thousands of already elite soldiers spike their combat throughput for the short Redline window.

For several minutes the tactic appears militarily brilliant.

Then the wounded return.

Aya activates **Hundred Pulse Dominion** in a treatment area.

The Domain's circulation perception turns the hospital into a horror for her because she can distinguish:

- torn meridians — repairable;
- internal bleeding — repairable;
- collapsed channels — difficult but repairable;
- consumed future vitality — **already gone**.

She feels hundreds of bodies carrying damage that ordinary healing cannot restore because the cost was not tissue; it was years already spent.

This is a required emotional / medical scene, not a briefing summary.

---

## 4. The betrayal: ordinary soldiers were not properly informed

Aya then discovers that many frontline soldiers were never given the real lifespan cost.

Ordinary briefings sanitize Redline as an emergency stimulant with serious recovery requirements and danger from repeated use.

They are not routinely told that one 3× dose can cost approximately five years of future life.

A young soldier should make this personal by revealing that he believed the consequence was months of recovery rather than years of life.

Aya understands that her screening procedures, medical terminology and institutional reputation have been used to make soldiers trust something whose true cost was withheld from them.

This is the point where her prior harm-reduction compromise becomes intolerable.

---

## 5. Aya does not defect immediately

Aya's first response is **medical resistance from inside Shinsei**, not an instant defection.

She begins to:

- classify far more soldiers as medically ineligible for Redline;
- refuse authorization for same-day redosing wherever her authority reaches;
- record actual vitality-loss estimates rather than sanitized recovery language;
- tell field physicians the real cost;
- place replacement stock behind medical seals;
- prioritize treatment of enemy wounded as well as Shinsei wounded when combat conditions permit;
- preserve records that command would prefer remain vague.

She still believes she may be able to stop the system from becoming worse.

That belief is now openly in conflict with the institution rather than quietly accommodating it.

---

## 6. Haru's three-dose incident — Aya's personal breaking point

After Haru Ishikawa uses repeated 3× Redline while fighting Yurushi and later Tae, Hana returns with him badly damaged.

Aya knows from his pulse / circulation that he took multiple same-day doses.

Under the current working Axtaya cost model:

- first Redline: ~5 years;
- second same-day: ~10 years;
- third same-day: ~20 years;
- total potential future-vitality loss: roughly **35 years**.

Aya's key realization is that Haru did not redose because he was dying.

He redosed because he was **losing**.

That distinction proves to her that Redline has crossed completely from emergency medicine into ego, doctrine and institutional dependency.

Hana explicitly tried to stop the later doses and did not take Axtaya herself.

Suggested emotional line / idea:

> “You weren't dying. You were losing.”

The exact wording is not mandatory, but the distinction is.

---

## 7. Aya confronts Tsubasa

Aya confronts Tsubasa after Haru's treatment.

Tsubasa is genuinely angry that Haru redosed so recklessly and orders tighter controls on repeat dosing.

However, he **does not discontinue 3× Redline**.

His reasoning is that:

- Wuyue has entered the war;
- Isgard continues resisting;
- Shinsei soldiers are dying;
- removing Redline now would reduce battlefield options during the largest crisis Shinsei has faced.

He promises reform after the war.

That answer finally breaks Aya's old logic because it is the same excuse she has used for years: remain through the current crisis, fix it afterward.

There is always another crisis.

The thematic line may be expressed as:

> “You keep promising to become better after the thing that requires you to become worse.”

Exact dialogue can change in prose, but the idea is locked.

---

## 8. Hana + Aya + Nao begin deliberately converging

Hana, Aya and Nao do **not** become a neat secret rebel faction overnight.

Their existing pattern of separate harm-reduction work now begins overlapping deliberately.

- **Hana** manipulates deployments, patrols and records to keep vulnerable soldiers / civilians away from the worst Redline decisions where she can.
- **Aya** controls medical access, tells people the real cost and blocks unsafe dosing within her authority.
- **Nao** protects frightened soldiers / civilians and begins openly questioning whether strategic objectives justify the human price.

For the first time, three Shinsei officers are consciously protecting people **from Shinsei's own machinery**.

This is the Phase II institutional crack.

---

## 9. What remains open

Aya's **final Phase III choice is not locked yet**.

Do not prematurely decide in Phase II whether she:

- formally defects;
- remains in Shinsei and forces reform;
- refuses a decisive battlefield order;
- directly aids Wuyue / Isgard at the climax.

Phase II only locks the progression from harm-reduction complicity to active internal resistance.

When Seasons 101–108 are drafted:

2. dramatize the first mass Redline aftermath through Hundred Pulse Dominion rather than a numbers-only explanation;
3. let Aya learn that ordinary soldiers were misled about lifespan cost in-scene;
4. show gradual medical resistance before the Haru incident;
5. make Haru's three doses the personal proof that Redline has become doctrine / ego rather than emergency medicine;
6. give Aya and Tsubasa a direct confrontation;
7. begin Hana / Aya / Nao coordination without prematurely turning them into an organized coup;
8. preserve Aya's uncertainty about her final institutional loyalty until Phase III.
