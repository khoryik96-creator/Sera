# Final Arc — Epilogue & Character Endings Canon Lock

> **Status: AUTHORITATIVE LATER CANON LOCK.**
>
> This file records the approved postwar / epilogue outcomes for the major Wuyue, Isgard, Shinsei and Quaint Teahouse characters after the final conflict. Where older ending proposals conflict with this file, this file wins.

---

## 1. General ending principle

The ending should feel lived-in rather than ceremonial. The final war ends, but the characters do not simply vanish after one victory scene. Each major surviving figure receives a sweet, bittersweet or quietly hopeful destination that follows from who they became during the final arc.

The final chapters should leave room for ordinary life, travel, rebuilding, romance, institutional reform and the sense that the world continues beyond the ranking board.

---

# 2. Kael Veyran — voluntary retirement

Kael's final major combat action is the brutal Shunto confrontation alongside Luo Wen.

Shunto uses 3× Redline and repeatedly targets Luo because Luo is the one medically solving his circulation and building the poison answer.

Kael protects Luo at extreme personal cost.

The fight should become unusually savage for Kael:

- Shunto smashes Kael's head / face into stone hard enough to crater the ground;
- repeatedly kicks or strikes his face while Kael is already down;
- breaks multiple limbs;
- destroys or heavily compromises one knee / leg;
- stacks Hundred Locks through injured regions;
- breaks ribs and causes catastrophic internal damage;
- repeatedly tries to throw Kael aside to reach Luo;
- Kael keeps physically putting himself between Shunto and Luo anyway;
- near the end Kael is close to death and may be functioning through one barely usable limb / hand while still buying Luo seconds to complete the medical-poison solution.

Kael is not humiliated by this. The point is that 3× High-Paragon Shunto is monstrously dangerous and Kael knowingly absorbs the punishment because Luo cannot survive a direct exchange.

Rhen later restores Kael's medically recoverable injuries back to full combat health.

Kael therefore does **not** retire because he is crippled or can no longer fight.

He retires because he is simply tired.

After years of carrying Wuyue's public No.1 burden, commanding armies and being expected to stand first whenever catastrophe arrives, Kael chooses ordinary life, travel and personal freedom.

He may continue to be physically capable of world-class combat, but he no longer wants a permanent public office or ranking obligation.

Kael begins travelling extensively after the war.

He gradually spends increasing amounts of time at the Quaint Teahouse / Second Spring Tea House.

Whether he ever becomes a formally inducted Quaint member remains deliberately ambiguous. The preferred joke is that everyone increasingly treats him as though he belongs there even if neither Sera nor Kael ever formally says so.

Lu Weiran may eventually keep a room prepared for him or record his status with dry ambiguity rather than a formal command seat.

# 3. Liang Yue — occasional companion to Kael

Liang Yue does not fully retire from Wuyue responsibilities.

However, Kael's choice changes Liang's relationship with rank and duty.

Liang increasingly delegates and periodically joins Kael on excursions.

The running tone may include Kael claiming he is travelling alone, only for Liang to appear with luggage and insist it is coincidence.

Liang remains a powerful Wuyue figure while gradually learning that not every chapter of life needs to be measured through cultivation and rank.

---

# 4. Qin Luo and Arin Vale — rekindled romance

Arin and Qin Luo gradually reconnect after the war.

The relationship must not snap back into place instantly because they once had history.

Instead:

- Arin begins visiting Qin more often under increasingly flimsy operational / reconstruction reasons;
- Qin recognizes the excuses and teases her;
- they discover they genuinely like who the other became after years of separation and war;
- affection returns slowly and naturally;
- they eventually rekindle their romance.

A preferred intimate detail is that when they travel together, Arin often describes what she sees to Qin—not because his blindness requires her to replace sight, but because he enjoys hearing how **she** sees the world, while Qin continues perceiving through his own vibration / resonance system.

Their ending should feel mature, warm and earned.

---

# 5. Jin Seoryu

Jin survives and remains one of Wuyue's principal strategic figures.

His ending is about no longer being the sole mind holding every operational thread.

After the six-month war nearly destroys him through exhaustion and responsibility, he builds stronger institutions, a command / strategy school or equivalent system capable of producing officers who can disagree with him and make correct decisions without waiting for his approval.

A meaningful payoff is Jin eventually receiving a correct field decision made without him and, for once, going home early.

---

# 6. Lei Zhen

Lei returns to Stormreach and increasingly invests in rebuilding communities rather than treating every peaceful year as preparation for another war.

He remains formidable and disciplined but becomes associated with protection, reconstruction and training the next generation rather than constant escalation.

---

# 7. Shen Rui

Shen Rui settles into a senior-master role within Azure traditions.

He continues training, mentoring and occasionally sparring with Xie Wuchen.

Their matches may remain destructive enough that younger disciples are horrified while both insist the session was controlled.

---

# 8. Ilyra Serath

Ilyra remains mobile rather than being domesticated into a static court role.

The Crownless Host gradually becomes a rapid-response force for:

- disasters;
- evacuations;
- bandit suppression;
- border emergencies;
- missing caravans;
- high-risk rescue operations.

Ilyra remains terrifying, just increasingly terrifying on behalf of civilians rather than conquest.

---

# 9. Mo Qingzhao

Mo remains politically important while deliberately strengthening institutions around himself so the state can function without depending on one exceptional ruler.

His postwar legacy should oppose the final arc's danger of permanent emergency authority.

---

# 10. Luo Wen

Luo survives the final arc and remains physician-first.

He never becomes 'the new Pale Venom.' There was only one Yun Shizhen.

His later medical / poison writings openly preserve Yun's contribution.

A preferred long-term development is that students eventually refer to the combined principles they developed as the **Luo–Yun Method**. Luo may dislike the name at first and later stop correcting it.

He keeps Yun's notebook.

His ending is bittersweet rather than replacing Yun with a new romance immediately.

---

# 11. Xie Wuchen

Xie's postwar growth is measured by reliability rather than stillness.

He continues travelling and disappearing for long periods, but now returns when he said he would.

People can depend on him.

This resolves his earlier flaw of leaving before anyone could rely on him without forcing him to become a stationary official.

---

# 12. Tae Muyeon and Huo Wujin

Tae and Huo do not require elaborate personality reinvention in the epilogue.

They remain, fundamentally, **Tae and Huo**.

They continue as major Quaint / Wuyue martial pillars, train people, argue, drink tea, handle difficult protection work and remain absurdly strong.

Do not manufacture a forced retirement, sudden political transformation or unrelated romance merely to give them an ending beat.

Their satisfaction comes from having a home, people they protect and a place in Sera's organization that fits who they already are.

---

# 13. Isgard — permanent loyal ally of Wuyue

Isgard remains politically independent.

Wuyue does not absorb, rule or culturally overwrite it.

Instead, the two continents / powers emerge from the final arc as extremely loyal long-term allies after surviving epidemic, invasion, shared casualties and mutual defense together.

The relationship should include:

- strong mutual-defense obligations;
- deep trade ties;
- medical / reconstruction cooperation;
- reliable cross-border military support in future crises;
- no assumption that either side commands the other.

Later generations may regard the Wuyue–Isgard partnership as normal even though it once seemed impossible.

---

# 14. Eirik Voss — return to Isgard without reclaiming rule

Eirik eventually returns to Isgard permanently to help rebuild.

He does **not** return as ruler and does not demand restoration of his former throne / authority.

At first, many Isgard citizens and officers distrust or hate him because of his history and Shinsei role.

Some may never fully forgive him.

Eirik accepts this and stays anyway.

He contributes through practical service:

- rebuilding fortifications;
- training soldiers;
- escorting supply routes;
- reconstruction work;
- protecting towns and civilian movements;
- helping restore regions he once ruled.

Over time, he is accepted back into Isgard's ranks as a senior protector / military figure rather than sovereign ruler.

A title such as **Warden of the Northern Shield** is acceptable if used later, provided it remains subordinate to Isgard's lawful government and does not recreate his old monarchy.

His redemption is service without ownership.

---

# 15. Aldric, Maedra, Sigrun and Solveig

Aldric remains a major legal / martial authority but does not simply absorb every wartime function back into himself after recovery.

He formally recognizes the emergency command structure that saved Isgard.

Solveig receives durable postwar authority—War Marshal / Defense Coordinator or equivalent—because she actually ran the theater when the stronger Paragons could not stand.

Maedra remains difficult, proud and powerful. She does not become soft. Her postwar relationship with Aldric matures into functional rivalry rather than destructive division, and she strongly resists any future attempt to reconcentrate Isgard under a single throne.

Sigrun becomes one of Isgard's future symbols: the Paragon who held evacuation lines while her own body was breaking. Her reputation centers on people surviving behind her, not on never being injured.

---

# 16. Shinsei survives, but the old structure does not

The Shinsei Guild / government is **reformed, not annihilated**.

Its original purpose—ending private clan wars, protecting civilians, stabilizing trade, resolving disputes and coordinating disaster response—still matters.

What ends is the extreme concentration of authority, secret lifespan-burning doctrine and the belief that Shinsei may sacrifice people indefinitely in the name of preserving itself.

---

# 17. Haru Ishikawa and Nao Shibasaki — postwar Shinsei leadership

After the political crisis dies down, **Haru and Nao become central to managing, redesigning and rebuilding Shinsei**.

They do not simply restore Tsubasa's old system under new names.

Their mandate is explicitly to make Shinsei less extreme, less coercive and less willing to treat soldiers / civilians as expendable strategic resources.

### Haru

Haru's earlier 3× abuse and enormous burned lifespan remain real consequences.

Aya can restore his physical combat damage, but not the decades of future life already consumed.

That experience changes him.

He helps rebuild Shinsei precisely because he now understands how institutional pressure, pride and emergency doctrine can turn self-destruction into normalized duty.

He remains a brilliant martial prodigy, but postwar he becomes far less interested in proving himself through rank or escalation.

### Nao

Nao becomes one of the strongest moral / civilian-facing anchors of the rebuilt Shinsei.

She helps ensure future peacekeeping, military deployment and emergency powers include meaningful protection for civilians and soldiers rather than abstract strategic necessity alone.

### Joint direction

Haru and Nao should be shown sharing high-level rebuilding responsibility rather than one becoming a new absolute ruler.

The preferred structure is distributed governance / council leadership with checks, not another single-person throne.

# 18. Aya Katsuragi — Axtaya / 3× Redline permanently destroyed

Aya remains central to Shinsei's medical reconstruction.

Her postwar non-negotiable line is that **3× Redline / military Axtaya must never survive as a hidden reserve, contingency stock or future emergency option**.

Haru and Nao support the abolition.

Aya personally ensures the program is dismantled.

Required outcome:

- all known 3× Redline stockpiles are collected;
- all military reserve caches are found;
- manufacturing instructions and active production records are seized;
- remaining prepared doses are publicly destroyed / burned;
- production equipment and infrastructure dedicated to 3× are dismantled;
- no secret 'just in case' arsenal is preserved;
- the government formally abolishes the military 3× doctrine;
- Aya verifies the destruction rather than trusting administrative declarations.

The final symbolic act should include the **burning / destruction of the remaining 3× stock**, with Aya present and ensuring it is permanently gone.

This does not require erasing legitimate medical knowledge that could save ordinary patients. Aya may preserve safe medical science, precursor research and the ethical lessons learned.

What is permanently abolished is the lifespan-burning 3× military weaponization.

# 19. Hana Arakawa and Yurushi Amagiri — romance rekindled

Once the war and Shinsei political crisis settle, Hana begins visiting Yurushi in Wuyue / at the Quaint Teahouse increasingly often.

This is not an occasional once-a-year unresolved tease.

The visits become noticeably more frequent because the political conditions that once forced them apart are finally fading.

Preferred progression:

- first visit is cautious and emotionally awkward;
- the next comes sooner;
- then she starts finding reasons to stay several days;
- then she stops pretending every trip is purely official;
- she becomes familiar with the Quaint Teahouse routines;
- Lu eventually keeps familiar supplies / a cup / room arrangement ready for her;
- Sera and the others notice long before either of them admits anything.

Yurushi is genuinely happy about her return.

He does not punish her endlessly for having stayed in Shinsei. Their separation was rooted in conflicting ideas of how to protect people, not lack of love.

Hana's reform work and final refusal of 3× prove she has changed through action.

They gradually rekindle their romance openly once politics no longer make the relationship impossible.

The relationship should become warm, domestic and hopeful again rather than remaining permanently ambiguous.

Do not rush directly into marriage unless later explicitly approved; the locked outcome is that they **reconnect and rekindle the relationship**, with both of them happy to have found their way back.

---

# 20. Tsubasa Kurokawa

Tsubasa permanently steps down from Shinsei executive rule after the war.

He is not restored as supreme leader once reforms begin.

He accepts responsibility for the war, Redline doctrine and the concentration of authority that allowed Shinsei to become what it did.

He may cooperate with investigations / reform and provide records needed to dismantle his own old system.

His precise legal punishment and long-term civilian / martial status may be finalized later, but he must not simply reclaim command.

---

# 21. Kael, Liang and the Quaint Teahouse as recurring epilogue comedy

The Quaint Teahouse gradually becomes an accidental gathering point for terrifying retired / semi-retired figures.

Possible recurring beats include:

- Kael staying longer than intended;
- Liang arriving during one of Kael's 'solo' trips;
- Arin visiting Qin;
- Hana visiting Yurushi more and more frequently;
- Xie passing through;
- Isgard figures occasionally visiting;
- Tae and Huo acting as though none of this is unusual;
- Lu quietly adapting logistics around all of them.

Sera may eventually complain that this was supposed to be a quiet tea shop.

Rhen may sincerely insist that it is quiet while chaos is happening in the background.

---

# 22. Final Rhen / Sera destination

Rhen and Sera do not accept permanent continental rule, a new throne or a numeric rank.

Their reward is ordinary life together.

The final emotional destination remains the tea house: customers, medicine, friends, arguments, travel stories, rebuilding lives and the ability to choose peace after having proved they could dominate war.

The world around them changes because of what they did, but the ending should return to the human scale that started the story.
