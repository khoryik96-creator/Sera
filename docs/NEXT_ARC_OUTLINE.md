# Next Arc — Ten-Season Planning Skeleton

> **Status:** Pre-prose planning only. Nothing introduced in this document becomes canon merely by appearing here. `docs/STORY_SKELETON_LOCK.md` and implemented reader prose remain authoritative wherever they conflict with this planning file.
>
> **Proposed reader range:** Seasons 95–104 · Chapters 301–400.
>
> **Working-title status:** Unchosen. Title candidates appear in the approval gates near the end of this file.
>
> **Central promise — proposed, not locked:** The first arc after the Wuyue–Isgard war should be driven by consequences rather than by immediately introducing a continent that is simply stronger than the last one. Wuyue returns exhausted, Isgard is politically fractured, Sera is a new Paragon, the public Top Ten now know what has been living beneath the crooked sign, and Tae, Huo and Qin can no longer be treated as harmless hidden auxiliaries. The working route is a cross-border conspiracy that attempts to restart the war through forged orders, stolen authentication methods, manipulated casualty records and targeted killings. Sera must protect the peace without becoming a ruler, while Jin, Lu and the hidden network determine who is manufacturing evidence that both continents are trained to believe.
>
> **Important:** The conspiracy route below is a complete working skeleton so the next arc has something concrete to revise. It remains an approval gate. If the user chooses a different central threat, the inherited continuity and structural rules remain useful while the proposed season/chapter beats may be replaced.

## 1. Inherited Continuity That Is Already Locked

The next arc begins only after the completed end state of **When Wuyue Marches North**. The following are inherited facts, not new proposals:

1. The two-year Wuyue–Isgard war is over. Wuyue demobilizes without annexing Isgard territory.
2. Wuyue's Black March reached sixty percent strategic activation and then returned to its constituent authorities rather than becoming one permanent imperial army.
3. Sera is now a true **entry-level Paragon** and possesses the stable Supreme Domain **Orchid Dominion**.
4. Sera is not automatically the strongest Paragon in the world. Her first true Paragon battle ended in an honest two-against-one defeat after she forced Aldric and Maedra to cooperate, wounded both and extracted Wuyue's army from their overlapping Domains.
5. Rhen remains beyond the public ranking system. He defeated Aldric and Maedra only after Sera's condition crossed his medical veto.
6. Rhen's war-finale offensive tools remain his already-established Transcended **Petal Convergence** and **Meridian Bloom**. No new finale technique was created.
7. Rhen remains primarily healer, husband and hidden protector rather than campaign commander.
8. Tae Muyeon, Huo Wujin and Qin Luo are each **Peak Sovereign**.
9. Qin Luo remains Sera's **Second Inner Petal** and operational deputy.
10. Tae Muyeon remains **Senior Sepal / Offensive General**.
11. Huo Wujin remains **Sepal / Second Offensive General** under Sera's authority and existing conditional-custody framework.
12. Lu Weiran remains a **non-combatant Sepal / Chief Steward and logistics-intelligence authority**. He did not gain martial cultivation from Rhen's wartime passive extension.
13. The Top Ten now know Tae, Huo and Qin hold formal positions beneath Sera and reached Peak Sovereign while the northern armies were away. Future arcs must not reset this reveal or make the Top Ten surprised by the same information twice.
14. Rui previously encountered Huo acting under Sera's authority before the full post-war disclosure, but did not then understand Huo's complete restored strength or permanent place under the crooked sign.
15. The 160 Quiet Hands retained in Wuyue improved during the home defence. Eight cell captains reached Early Duke class, thirty-two operatives reached Peak Marquis and the remaining force became substantially harder to disable in Sovereign-scale combat.
16. The forty Quiet Hands who travelled north paid real cost: the hidden force is exceptional, not consequence-proof.
17. Wuyue's public armies suffered severe losses. The previous arc's demobilization ledger records 26,160 public troops dead or missing, 35,200 alive but inactive and 69,640 still combat-capable from 131,000 cumulative public deployment.
18. Arin Vale's Silver Horizon Sword Sect no longer exists as a three-thousand-person field army. Only 611 remain fit for formation; 1,486 survive wounded, retired or otherwise outside field formation; 903 are dead or missing.
19. Aldric Veyrhald survives.
20. Maedra Dravaryn survives, remains dangerous and does not automatically become friendly merely because Rhen spared her.
21. Eirholt remains a narrow medical bridge rather than a Wuyue client.
22. Tor Veyrhald remains a procedural bridge rather than a Wuyue loyalist.
23. Raska Dravaryn remains an Isgard nationalist rather than a convert.
24. Sigrun Veyrhald, Halvek Veyrhald, Solveig Skeldran and other surviving Isgard senior figures remain meaningful powers.
25. Wuyue and Isgard both know genuine Paragons exist. Paragon is no longer a theoretical realm unknown to the continental leadership.
26. Sera returned to Wuyue without accepting a new public numerical rank.
27. Rhen and Sera ended Chapter 300 beneath the reopened crooked sign rather than on a forced cliffhanger.
28. The previous outline explicitly leaves the next disturbance undecided. No Chapter 300 event may be retroactively invented merely to force this arc to begin.
29. Rhen's two-year emergency extension of **Frozen Petals Garden** to Tae, Huo and Qin must be reviewed after the war. Whether it ends immediately, tapers or remains in some limited emergency form is an approval gate for this arc; no automatic permanent extension is assumed.
30. The five-year reconstruction that stopped Sera's aging remains unique to her completed body. Tae, Huo and Qin do not become ageless merely because they received a two-year wartime extension.

## 2. Narrative and Spoiler Discipline

This arc should inherit the prose rules that kept the war arc from becoming an outline disguised as fiction.

### 2.1 Present-Scene Knowledge

- Characters may act only upon information they plausibly possess in the current chapter.
- Do not narrate future recruitment, betrayal, promotion, death, technique or political outcome before it happens.
- Avoid lines such as “none of them yet knew,” “this would later become,” “for now,” or other retrospective spoilers that reveal the outline instead of dramatizing the scene.
- Planning documents may state future outcomes. Chapter prose may not.

### 2.2 No Power-Scale Reset

- The Top Ten remember Sera's Paragon battle.
- The Top Ten remember Rhen defeating two Paragons without a Domain.
- The Top Ten remember Tae, Huo and Qin are Peak Sovereigns under Sera.
- Isgard seniors remember the Black March and the final three-Domain battle.
- Nobody sensible treats the Quaint Teahouse as an ordinary tea shop once the post-war disclosures circulate among leadership.
- Secrecy can still protect exact numbers, locations, identities and methods. It cannot erase what named leaders personally witnessed.

### 2.3 No Automatic Bigger Villain

The next antagonist does **not** need to be “above Paragon” merely because the previous arc contained Paragons.

A weaker fighter may be more dangerous through:

- authentication access;
- political legitimacy;
- intelligence networks;
- command of evidence;
- knowledge of war dead and displaced units;
- the ability to make two sides attack each other;
- control of roads, food, medicine or legal recognition;
- hostages or protected civilian systems;
- a martial method that is specialised rather than numerically stronger.

Do not introduce a new realm above Paragon in this arc unless explicitly approved.

### 2.4 Chapter Ending Rule

End chapters on one present consequence, revelation, decision, arrival or reversal. Do not summarize the next chapter.

### 2.5 Word-Count Rule

Target the same useful chapter density as current production: roughly 2.0k–2.8k words when the material supports it. Do not pad a chapter merely to reach a number.

## 3. Post-War World Baseline

### 3.1 Wuyue

Wuyue returns victorious in the narrow sense that the invasion threat was neutralized, but the continent is not healthy.

- Tens of thousands are dead, missing, disabled or unwilling to continue military life.
- Each Sovereign must rebuild institutions that spent two years abroad.
- Jin's Compact survived its greatest test, but emergency authentication systems are now known to far more people than before the war.
- Kael's Stonecrown remained home and proved institutional independence from Kael himself.
- Liang's White Vein proved that two thousand specialists can dismantle an army's circulation without needing mass slaughter.
- Arin returns as a sect leader whose institution survived but can no longer field the army it marched north with.
- Mo must account to the civil monarchy for what the imperial army paid during his regency.
- The public now has veterans, refugees, widows, damaged trade routes and competing stories about what the war meant.

### 3.2 The Quaint Teahouse

The crooked sign is no longer merely a hidden centre known to a handful of insiders.

Leadership now knows that beneath Sera's roof exist:

- one new Paragon;
- Rhen, who remains outside the ranking system;
- three Peak Sovereigns with formal offices;
- a hidden elite network that defended Wuyue during the war;
- Lu's intelligence and logistics system;
- a command structure that repeatedly refuses formal rule.

The political problem is not that the Top Ten suddenly want to attack Sera. That would ignore everything they survived together. The more plausible pressure comes from institutions beneath them: courts, sect councils, military deputies, bereaved families, rival intelligence houses and regional officials asking why one private household contains enough strength to destabilize a continent if its intentions ever changed.

### 3.3 Isgard

Isgard is fractured rather than conquered.

- The Gate Seal survives in revised form.
- Several War Banner functions were dismantled or politically neutralized during the campaign.
- Aldric and Maedra survive with damaged authority.
- Eirholt retains credibility because it protected medicine rather than victory.
- Some Isgard leaders accept the war's end; others accept only that continued fighting became impossible.
- Thousands of veterans return to guilds whose command legitimacy has collapsed.
- Any actor capable of manufacturing believable evidence can exploit people who already lost family to Wuyue.

### 3.4 The Ranking System

The public ranking remains administratively useful, but its meaning is strained.

- Sera is a Paragon and refuses a new number.
- Rhen remains beyond numerical placement.
- Tae, Huo and Qin are stronger than many publicly ranked leaders but do not automatically claim Top Ten seats.
- The existing Top Ten still represent regional command, public legitimacy, armies and institutions in addition to personal cultivation.

Do not solve this tension by casually replacing half the Top Ten list in one chapter.

## 5. Character Spines the Next Arc Should Protect

### 5.1 Sera

Sera should remain the narrative centre.

Her next challenge is not simply learning to hit harder. She must decide what it means to be a Paragon who does **not** want a throne while governments, armies and terrified institutions now know how much force answers to her household.

Required Sera functions:

- lead the main investigation or central strategic response;
- make decisions the Top Ten cannot make for her;
- continue refining Orchid Dominion through judgement and efficiency rather than an immediate new realm;
- confront the political consequences of The Quiet Hand's existence;
- protect Tae, Huo, Qin and Lu from being reduced to assets others may requisition;
- remain capable of being wrong and revising a plan;
- receive help without becoming secondary to Rhen.

### 5.2 Rhen

Rhen should not become the shortcut that solves a political-thriller arc.

Useful functions:

- healer of war survivors;
- quiet domestic counterweight to Sera's public burden;
- investigator only where his medical perception genuinely matters;
- protector when a condition crosses a clear boundary;
- source of humour through his refusal to treat terrifying people as ceremonial figures.

Default rule: **no new Rhen technique in this arc** unless separately approved.

### 5.3 Tae, Huo and Qin

Their war breakthroughs now need consequences rather than another secret reveal.

- Tae's issue: a Peak Sovereign who could command armies but deliberately accepts Sera's hierarchy.
- Huo's issue: the world knows a feared former threat is now one of Sera's formal offensive commanders.
- Qin's issue: the blind former #6 is now Peak Sovereign, operational deputy and the person most capable of hearing hidden movement across Wuyue.

The three should disagree sometimes. Peak Sovereign does not make them interchangeable personalities.

### 5.4 Lu Weiran

A conspiracy built around documents, routes, casualty ledgers and authentication gives Lu genuine central relevance without turning him into a fighter.

Lu should be allowed to win important conflicts by:

- identifying impossible supply movement;
- noticing forged or duplicated orders;
- tracking who benefits from reconstruction contracts;
- proving that a message reached a place it physically could not have reached;
- protecting records from martial people who think evidence is secondary to strength.

### 5.5 Jin

Jin's war arc proved he can command a continental campaign. The next arc should challenge a different part of him: what happens when his own authentication systems are used to manufacture orders he never issued.

He should not become omniscient. A strategist can be dangerous precisely because an enemy understands the rules he trusts.

### 5.6 The Top Ten

Do not turn the Top Ten into jealous antagonists merely because Sera's household is stronger than expected.

More plausible reactions:

- Kael dislikes unaccountable force but respects what defended Wuyue.
- Liang wants systems capable of proving whether hidden fighters are acting under lawful authority.
- Jin wants authenticated command boundaries.
- Lei values direct trust but may underestimate institutional fear.
- Rui is sensitive to medical neutrality and civilian routes after his own defeat.
- Ilyra distrusts permanent structures that try to own fighters.
- Mo must answer civil questions the stronger cultivators can ignore.
- Arin understands what institutions owe weaker members after catastrophic loss.
- Luo and Yun see how false records can kill patients without one blade being drawn.

## 6. Working Conflict Route — NOT YET CANON

### 6.1 The Core Idea

Someone wants Wuyue and Isgard to believe the other side has already violated the peace.

The antagonist does not begin by attacking a Sovereign. The first weapon is an **authentic-looking order**.

The network has access to pieces of wartime bureaucracy from both continents:

- retired couriers;
- captured seals;
- casualty rolls;
- abandoned War Banner contracts;
- Black March routing conventions;
- medical-corridor credentials;
- regency and military paperwork;
- dead officers whose names still authorize dormant systems.

The enemy's strength is turning true fragments into false conclusions.

### 6.2 Why This Fits the Existing Story

This route grows directly from unresolved consequences:

- Jin's Black March depends upon authenticated shared truth.
- Tor's Duskvein evidence proved documents can change a war.
- Lu kept a sealed home ledger for two years.
- Mo's authority depends upon lawful seals rather than personal strength.
- Eirholt's legitimacy depends upon neutral medical recognition.
- Isgard lost institutions without losing every member who served them.
- Wuyue's hidden household has just become politically impossible to ignore.

### 6.3 Antagonist Model

No antagonist identity, name or final cultivation is locked yet.

Recommended structure:

| Slot | Proposed function | Suggested power ceiling |
|---|---|---|
| **The Architect** | Designs the evidence war and understands both continents' authentication habits. | Could be noncombatant, Duke or Sovereign. Does not need Paragon strength. |
| **The Blade** | Protects the network and creates physical crises when evidence alone fails. | Peak Sovereign by default; veteran Paragon only if explicitly approved. |
| **The Forger** | Reconstructs seals, timing, courier habits and dead officers' command signatures. | Noncombatant or low martial level is acceptable. |
| **Wuyue Sponsor** | Provides legitimate internal access. | Political importance matters more than realm. |
| **Isgard Sponsor** | Provides War Banner / Gate access. | Political importance matters more than realm. |

Do **not** assume the Architect and Blade are the same person.

### 6.4 Motive Options

One motive should be selected before prose:

1. **Permanent emergency power:** the conspirators profit politically and economically only while both continents remain mobilized.
2. **Revenge through institutions:** survivors believe peace rewards the leaders who destroyed their families and want the Compact/Gate systems themselves discredited.
3. **Forced unification:** an extremist faction believes only renewed war can create one authority strong enough to prevent future invasions.
4. **Hidden territorial goal:** renewed war is a distraction while the network seizes a specific route, archive, mine, port or medical system.
5. **Combination:** political emergency is the public motive while one smaller hidden objective is the real prize.

## 8. Season 95 — The Order Jin Never Gave

### Chapter 301 — Tea Before Reports

The Quaint Teahouse reopens without a victory ceremony. Sera refuses to let the first day home become a council session. Veterans and neighbours arrive for tea, establishing that the continent's greatest concentration of hidden force still has ordinary customers.

### Chapter 302 — No Number Fits Sera

Regional messengers ask what public rank should now describe Sera. She declines a number again. Kael argues that refusing rank does not remove political consequence; Sera answers that accepting one would falsely imply the ranking council owns her authority.

### Chapter 303 — Six Hundred Eleven Swords Stand

Arin begins rebuilding Silver Horizon around the 611 members still capable of formation while wounded survivors decide individually whether to remain disciples. No healing scene erases the fact that the old field army is gone.

### Chapter 304 — Three Peaks at One Table

Sera, Rhen, Tae, Huo, Qin and Lu hold the first private post-war command review. The wartime extension of Frozen Petals Garden becomes an explicit unresolved question rather than silently continuing forever.

### Chapter 305 — The Black Strip Returns on a Dead Courier

A courier is found carrying a Black March authentication strip even though the emergency protocol has ended. The strip is genuine material from the war, but the attached order directs a Wuyue border unit to detain an Isgard medical convoy.

### Chapter 306 — Jin Never Sent It

Jin verifies every visible command convention on the order and then states he never issued it. The frightening detail is not that the forgery is crude; it is that someone knew which obsolete wartime habits Jin would no longer use.

### Chapter 307 — Eirholt Sends One Name

Eira reports that the detained convoy contained a witness from the final Gate-Seal negotiations. The witness disappeared during the confusion without any large battle occurring.

### Chapter 308 — Sera Chooses Investigation

Several Wuyue officers want to seal the border. Sera refuses to treat fear as proof and assigns Quiet Hand cells to find the missing witness while keeping the organization itself under her authority rather than lending it wholesale to Jin.

### Chapter 309 — Lu Finds an Impossible Arrival Time

Lu proves the counterfeit order reached the border before the courier carrying its supposed source copy left the eastern relay. Someone inserted the message inside an authenticated route rather than merely copying the paper.

### Chapter 310 — The Second Order Bears Mo's Seal

A separate command reaches an imperial garrison ordering seizure of an Isgard grain shipment. It carries Mo's lawful regency seal. Mo is standing beside Jin when they read it.

## 9. Season 96 — Two Seals, One Lie

### Chapter 311 — The Regent Locks His Own Office

Mo closes the regency archive and submits his staff to investigation before anyone can accuse him of protecting prestige. The child monarch's council supports the decision but warns that prolonged closure will stall reconstruction payments.

### Chapter 312 — A Genuine Seal Can Sign a False Order

Lu and Jin establish that the conspirators are not manufacturing perfect replicas from nothing. They possess genuine obsolete seal surfaces, courier codes and partial wartime templates.

### Chapter 313 — Liang Reads the Hand That Held the Paper

Liang examines circulation residue left by repeated handling and identifies that several documents passed through the same trained courier network even though their official routes never intersected. This is martial forensics, not supernatural memory reading.

### Chapter 314 — Tae Walks Into a Public Council

Tae attends a regional command session openly as Sera's Senior Sepal. The room already knows he is Peak Sovereign, so the tension comes from what his presence means politically rather than another strength reveal.

### Chapter 315 — Huo Refuses to Become a Threat Display

A frightened official asks Sera to place Huo visibly at a border gate as deterrence. Huo refuses before Sera answers: his office does not make him a weapon other institutions can position for theatre.

### Chapter 316 — Qin Hears the Courier Chain

Qin maps messenger rhythms across roads, floors and relay houses. He finds one route where footsteps repeat with identical timing under different names, suggesting one trained group has been rotating identities.

### Chapter 317 — Kael Wants the Door Broken

Kael identifies the relay house and proposes immediate seizure. Sera delays long enough for Lu to preserve records first. Kael dislikes the delay but accepts that destroying evidence would hand the enemy exactly what it wants.

### Chapter 318 — The Relay House Is Empty but Warm

The site has been abandoned minutes before the raid. Food is warm, ash is fresh and every official ledger is intact except the pages that would show who authorized replacement couriers during the war.

### Chapter 319 — The Missing Witness Is Attacked Twice

Quiet Hands locate Eirholt's witness. A first attacker tries to kill him; a second prevents the killing and then tries to abduct him. The network is not one perfectly unified faction.

### Chapter 320 — War Did Not End for Everyone

The witness says the people hunting him care less about Wuyue or Isgard than about what the peace will invalidate: wartime contracts, emergency offices and command privileges that disappear if normal law returns.

## 10. Season 97 — Two Continents Accuse Each Other

### Chapter 321 — Isgard Receives the Same Lie

An Isgard Gate garrison receives an authenticated-looking order claiming Wuyue has reopened Black March mobilisation. Aldric does not believe it immediately, but commanders beneath him begin moving before verification finishes.

### Chapter 322 — Aldric Does Not Ask Sera to Trust Him

Aldric sends the disputed order to Wuyue through Tor and asks only that Sera verify whether Jin issued it. Their relationship remains adversarial but procedural.

### Chapter 323 — Maedra Has No Army to Command

Maedra discovers one Dravaryn remnant has received orders in her old wartime command style. She cannot simply countermand it because she no longer possesses the coalition authority she once abused.

### Chapter 324 — Eirholt's White Road Burns Without Fire

A medical corridor collapses when its food, escort and replacement-physician schedules are redirected by separate valid-looking orders. No spectacular attack is required; patients are endangered because ordinary systems arrive at the wrong places.

### Chapter 325 — Rui Protects a Convoy Instead of Chasing Blame

Rui escorts emergency supplies across the corridor and refuses pursuit when the saboteurs retreat, preserving the medical route first.

### Chapter 326 — Arin Finds a Dead Instructor's Name

One false order carries the authorization mark of a Silver Horizon instructor who died in Isgard. The signature is genuine because it was copied from wartime formation records after her death.

### Chapter 327 — Sera Crosses North as an Investigator

Sera enters Isgard with a small approved party rather than another army. The journey itself demonstrates how much harder trust is after war: every gate recognizes her and still questions why a Paragon is crossing.

### Chapter 328 — Rhen Stays With the Healed Who Cannot Sleep

Rhen remains in Wuyue for part of the investigation to treat veterans whose bodies are repaired but whose war has not ended. This keeps him important without converting him into Sera's detective shortcut.

### Chapter 329 — Tae, Huo and Qin Divide the Home Command

Qin coordinates information, Tae handles territorial response and Huo remains the approved offensive reserve. None is presented as Sera's replacement while she travels.

### Chapter 330 — Someone Publishes the Crooked Sign's Names

A list naming Qin, Tae and Huo as Sera's hidden senior officers appears simultaneously in Wuyue and Isgard. Leadership already knew. The dangerous change is that ordinary institutions now know enough to become afraid.

## 11. Season 98 — The Names Beneath the Crooked Sign

### Chapter 331 — Wuyue Learns What the Top Ten Already Knew

Rumours exaggerate The Quiet Hand into a private army of thousands. Sera refuses to publish a complete roster merely to calm people who would then possess the identities of covert operatives and their families.

### Chapter 332 — Fear Is Not Treason

Regional officials demand oversight. Sera distinguishes legitimate fear from conspiracy instead of treating criticism as hostility.

### Chapter 333 — Sera Owns the Authority Publicly

She confirms that Tae, Huo and Qin serve under her command and that lethal Quiet Hand deployments require her authority. She does not reveal locations, cell numbers or methods.

### Chapter 334 — Tae Declines a Ranking Challenge

A respected commander challenges Tae publicly to prove whether the rumours about his realm are true. Tae refuses the duel because winning would answer the wrong question: capability does not determine lawful authority.

### Chapter 335 — Huo's Past Becomes Someone Else's Weapon

Old records of Huo's feared history are circulated without the conditions of his recovery or current custody. Huo expects Sera to hide him. She instead explains the actual framework and lets him answer for his own choices.

### Chapter 336 — Qin Steps Into a Room That Expected a Ghost

Qin attends a public inquiry. His blindness and calm presence clash with stories describing an invisible assassin commander. He gives exactly the information Sera authorized and no more.

### Chapter 337 — Lu Follows the Printer, Not the Rumour

Lu traces identical paper fibre, ink procurement and distribution timing across supposedly independent pamphlets. The panic has logistical coordination.

### Chapter 338 — A False Ledger Names Sera

A fabricated casualty ledger claims Quiet Hands killed surrendered Isgard soldiers outside recorded battles. The document includes four genuine Quiet Hand dead from the war, making the false sections harder to dismiss.

### Chapter 339 — The Top Ten Do Not Pretend Ignorance

Kael, Liang, Jin, Lei and Rui publicly confirm what they personally witnessed about Sera's authority and the home defence. They do not endorse every secret she keeps, but they refuse to let manufactured ignorance become evidence.

### Chapter 340 — Jin Calls the Ledger a Weapon

Jin demonstrates three timing contradictions proving the ledger could not be contemporaneous with the battles it describes. The conspiracy fails to prove Sera guilty but succeeds in making ordinary people ask who has the power to certify truth.

## 12. Season 99 — The First False Peace

### Chapter 341 — A Summit Needs More Than Strong Guards

Wuyue and Isgard agree to a limited post-war summit focused on medical corridors, prisoner records and trade routes. Sera attends as an independent party rather than Wuyue's ruler.

### Chapter 342 — Aldric and Maedra Enter Through Different Doors

Aldric attends under Gate authority. Maedra arrives without coalition command and without pretending reconciliation. Their separate entrances make clear Isgard remains politically divided.

### Chapter 343 — Sigrun Guards a Room She Does Not Own

Sigrun accepts responsibility for exterior security while Wuyue controls none of the gates. Kael cooperates without rewriting their old battlefield defeat.

### Chapter 344 — The First Attack Is a Schedule

Three delegations receive different start times from apparently valid clerks. The summit nearly begins with each side believing the others intentionally excluded them.

### Chapter 345 — Sera Makes Everyone Wait

Sera refuses to begin negotiations until the contradictory orders are displayed side by side in one room. The delay is politically embarrassing and strategically correct.

### Chapter 346 — One Wuyue Sect Walks Out

A bereaved sect delegation leaves rather than share a table with Isgard. Their grief is genuine and is not automatically linked to the conspiracy.

### Chapter 347 — Quiet Hands Catch a Courier Alive

A courier attempting to replace the summit's medical schedule is captured without public spectacle. He carries legitimate credentials issued during the second year of the war.

### Chapter 348 — The Courier Believes His Employer Is Lawful

Interrogation reveals compartmentalization. The courier thinks he serves a post-war claims authority, not a secret army.

### Chapter 349 — The Enemy Possesses Legitimate Access

Lu proves at least one current reconstruction office is feeding old credentials into the network. The conspiracy cannot be dismissed as a collection of surviving enemy agents.

### Chapter 350 — The Summit Signs One Small Thing

Instead of a grand peace treaty, the delegations agree upon one shared casualty-verification process. The modest agreement survives precisely because it is narrow enough to verify.

## 13. Season 100 — The Ledger of the Dead

### Chapter 351 — Dead Soldiers Are Still Being Paid

Mo finds military stipends issued under names confirmed dead in Isgard. The money disappears through reconstruction contractors rather than obvious criminal accounts.

### Chapter 352 — Living Veterans Have Been Declared Dead

Luo discovers wounded soldiers denied treatment funding because altered records list them as deceased. Administrative fraud becomes a physical threat.

### Chapter 353 — The Royal Treasury Was Not Robbed Once

Mo learns small false payments were distributed across hundreds of legitimate accounts for two years. The network was built during the war rather than after it.

### Chapter 354 — Arin Reads Names She Buried

Silver Horizon dead appear as authorizers on equipment transfers after their deaths. Arin refuses to let anger destroy the records before Lu copies them.

### Chapter 355 — Luo's Medical Dead Do Not Match the Army Dead

Medical and military ledgers disagree in patterns too regular to be clerical error. Someone needed certain names to remain legally alive.

### Chapter 356 — Yun Finds Poison Bought by Hospitals That Never Ordered It

Procurement records show controlled compounds diverted through institutions with real treatment authority. The amounts are too small for mass poisoning and large enough for targeted assassinations.

## 21. Casualty and Recovery Discipline

The previous war's damage remains real throughout this arc.

- Rhen may heal surviving bodies; he cannot resurrect the dead.
- A healed limb does not automatically restore two lost years, a destroyed unit or desire to keep fighting.
- Silver Horizon's 903 dead or missing do not disappear from the story's emotional arithmetic.
- Wuyue's public armies do not instantly return to pre-war numbers.
- Isgard's damaged guilds do not instantly rebuild because the plot needs another large army.
- Veterans may retire permanently even after successful treatment.
- Reconstruction money, disability records and medical access may matter to the conspiracy because these systems now affect hundreds of thousands of lives.
